# Scheduler decisions and implementation

Implemented October 12, 2026, following the approved decisions below. This is a single-user application; assessments and ordinary reviews use activity history across courses and study modes.

The scheduler build covers the ten categories in this report. **No scheduler policy question remains open.** Review timing starts with a **0.5 retention threshold and 1-day base half-life**, using the existing fractional encompassing updates. Further timing calibration and the difficulty controller are later refinements. Diagnostic delivery remains outside this build.

Question authoring remains a content dependency: preparation waits for fresh stock, automatic inventory jobs invoke existing generators or the generator creator, and bounded unsupported/finite-template outcomes remain visible. This does not claim that every captured KP already has a validated generator or enough variants.

## Confirmed choices

- **Ordinary assessment:** offer at 120 qualifying XP; require at 150, subject to enough eligible material. Both boundaries are enforced by the scheduler and start/resume APIs.
- **Unavailable assessment pool:** as an exceptional fallback, allow continued ordinary study if a due assessment cannot be assembled. Preserve the existing signed practice-XP accounting, replenish missing fresh questions, and recheck eligibility on refresh. Once ready, make the assessment required when the current XP sum meets the 150-XP boundary, with assessment remediation still taking precedence. Record the reason for unavailable supply or eligibility so it can be investigated.
- **Assessment XP accounting:** derive cadence from the signed XP actually earned on finalized qualifying practice since the previous ordinary assessment. Include negative awards; do not clamp each award to zero or maintain a separate positive-only progress balance. For example, 140 qualifying XP followed by a -1 award becomes 139. Assessment and retake rewards remain outside the practice sum, and a retake does not reset it.
- **Diagnostics — deferred:** diagnostics are optional and outside the current implementation scope. If supported later, their earned XP should count toward assessment pacing. Do not build a diagnostic flow, require one before study, or delay the scheduler work for diagnostic or first-cycle research.
- **Ordinary reviews:** consider all learned topics in the learner's activity history, without restricting by enrolled course, in automatic and self-directed study. Retention state and qualifying direct or implicit practice determine due dates. Becoming due does not impose a required-study gate or later grace-period promotion. Required review obligations arise from wrong or unanswered assessment questions; ordinary review timing alone does not create them.
- **Review queues and ordinary failures:** all five recommendations may be reviews; do not permanently reserve a lesson slot. Failed ordinary reviews update the learner's accuracy and retention evidence, then end without forcing another activity. Subsequent ordinary reviews follow normal due-date and priority scheduling. Add no separate retry cooldown or automatic full-lesson replacement. These updates affect learner state, not the shared question-difficulty selection weights.
- **Periodic lesson availability:** after three ordinary review attempts finish without a lesson being offered, include an eligible optional lesson in the next ordinary queue. No separate backlog-size threshold is needed. Required assessment remediation takes precedence and does not count toward this ordinary-review streak.
- **Failed remediation reviews:** require the full lesson on the failed review's topic, whether the review originated from an assessment or repeated lesson failure at the same KP. Carry its original obligation through the lesson. Passing that linked lesson clears the topic's remediation obligation without an extra automatic review.
- **Key-prerequisite escalation:** two consecutive lesson failures at the same KP without further progress assign reviews of that KP's key prerequisite topics. If one of those reviews fails, require the corresponding prerequisite lesson before retrying the original lesson. This blocks that learning path; it does not by itself hide all unrelated work.
- **Ordinary lesson retry:** after a first failed lesson, admit an automatic fresh-question retry after five other successfully completed practice activities on other topics: lessons, reviews, and assignments/multisteps. Exclude failed, unfinished, aborted, or supply-deferred work. Derive the count from finalized history; add no separate stored balance, XP threshold, or elapsed-time minimum. Normal ranking, prerequisites, supply, and required-work gates still apply. This is an automatic-offer rule, not a new restriction on voluntary study.
- **Assessment remediation:** after the assessment completes or expires, every topic with an incorrect answer or question left unanswered by the deadline requires review. Deduplicate by topic. Show only ready required remediation and block unrelated starts and resumes while that work is ready. Passing the linked review resolves the topic obligation. If it fails, immediately offer the required full lesson on that topic. The gate follows the obligation, subject to the failed-lesson break below.
- **Failed required lesson:** preserve its obligation and allow a break until five other successful practice activities finish. Other ready required remediation takes precedence; if all remaining required work is waiting for retries, temporarily allow ordinary study. After five successful activities, the fresh-question lesson retry becomes required again when eligible. The related assessment retake stays unavailable until all its remediation obligations resolve. Passing the linked lesson clears its topic obligation without an extra automatic review. Repeated failure at the same KP without further progress instead invokes the key-prerequisite escalation.
- **Unanswered questions:** apply the same unsuccessful-score and required-review consequences as wrong answers, including questions never reached on the reserved route. Preserve the factual distinction in history: an unanswered slot is not a submitted incorrect answer. An open question while time remains is not yet an expiry failure. Keep assessment feedback hidden until the assessment ends.
- **Assessment navigation:** allow free movement among the reserved questions without requiring an answer. Leaving a question unanswered is navigation, not a skip or an incorrect outcome. Skipping is a diagnostic behavior, not an assessment answer class. This rule also applies to assessment retakes.
- **Assessment submission:** keep answers as editable drafts and submit the entire assessment once, when the learner selects Submit or the time limit expires. Freeze the latest drafts and grade all reserved questions together. Unanswered questions receive the same score and remediation consequences as wrong answers, while retaining their unanswered history status. Keep correctness hidden until finalization; apply this to ordinary assessments and retakes.
- **Assessment retakes:** offer one optional retake when the original assessment scores **below 75%**, after required remediation succeeds, with no additional XP countdown or time delay. At 75% or higher, missed topics still require reviews but produce no automatic retake. Wrong and unanswered questions count identically in the score; expiry has no separate eligibility override. Lesson-retry pacing is a separate rule discussed below.
- **Assessment assembly:** reserve at least one easy, one moderate, and one hard question from distinct eligible topics, then sample additional topics without replacement toward a 15-minute estimated budget. Use provisional 1/2/3-minute difficulty estimates and the database difficulty weights for additional draws, restricted to available bands that fit the remaining time. Shuffle and freeze the route before start; the actual timer remains 15 minutes. Minimums change the realized difficulty proportions.
- **Retake assembly:** keep one question per original topic, preserving the original question's KP, and use fresh questions with the remedial 45/40/15 weights. Before each draw, restrict choices to those that leave a feasible completion covering every remaining original topic within 15 estimated minutes, with at least one easy, one moderate, and one hard overall. This prevents over-budget draws during assembly. Missing fresh supply triggers replenishment rather than relaxed requirements.

These are the implemented rules. The engine reads difficulty weights from the captured database value; prepared and started routes stay frozen.

| 4. Topic sampling | Weighted sampling without replacement; 4:1 never-tested preference and continuous encompassing discount |

| 7. Assessment sizing | One easy/moderate/hard minimum, 1/2/3-minute estimates, 15-minute budget and wall timer; fixed-pool retake lookahead |
| 8. Review timing | Retain threshold 0.5 and base half-life 1 day; direct and fractional encompassing practice postpone due dates |
| 9. Failed lessons | Five other successful activities before automatic retry; repeated same-KP failure assigns prerequisite repair; path and origin-aware gates |

Lifecycle, break counts and XP cadence are derived from finalized task history. The only new task facts are the active assessment item and an actual ordinary lesson queue-offer timestamp. Generator registration adds an identity and a script path; existing remediation and retake links carry origins.

The final build is deployed in the active `course-academy.service`; live endpoint and browser checks confirm the five-activity queue and automatic inventory handoff. The four new attributes committed at **EDB basis 1694** with source `:application/ca-engine`; readback at that exact basis confirmed their types and cardinalities. The database already contains the approved threshold, half-life, ordinary assessment **50/40/10**, and remedial **45/40/15** weights.

Native isolated fixtures cover grading, navigation, expiry, signed XP, retry breaks, prerequisite gates, failed remediation and fresh original-KP retakes. ## 1. Required remediation

**Current:** Wrong and unanswered slots create deduplicated, origin-linked review obligations at batch submission or expiry. Required queue filtering and API start/resume gates apply while repairs are ready; failed reviews escalate to linked lessons.

**Desired:** At assessment completion or expiry, immediately require reviews on every affected topic and display no unrelated activities in the queue. Incorrect answers and unanswered reserved questions have the same remediation consequence. Moving away from an unanswered question while time remains creates no outcome or remediation obligation. Passing a linked review after the triggering assessment clears its obligation. If the review fails, carry the obligation through a required full lesson on the same topic. Apply the chosen break if that lesson fails. Enforce the gate and its break consistently in automatic study, self-directed study, direct topic starts, and resumes. Preserve an already running assessment's deadline.

**Investigate:** Trace original assessment → missed topics → assigned reviews → outcomes → next queue using the existing captures. Separate submitted incorrect answers from unanswered expiry slots; the latter require practice without invented incorrect-answer evidence. Inspect failed-review replacement paths and whether one review can satisfy multiple outstanding obligations. Audit every API entry point that can start or resume work. Preserve legacy evidence without allowing old skip representations to define the new assessment behavior.

**Enough confidence:** Each obligation has an identifiable origin and an explicit successful resolution. Fixtures show that prior or unrelated practice cannot clear it, and retries or course/mode switches cannot bypass the gate or duplicate work. A failed remediation review requires its linked lesson; passing that lesson clears the topic obligation without another automatic review. A failed or unfinished replacement lesson retains the obligation. A supply shortage stays visible with a defined recovery path.

**Failed remediation review — chosen:** Immediately offer the full lesson on the failed review's topic, with fresh practice questions and no added cooldown or 48-hour age wait. Link it to the original remediation obligation. For assessment-origin remediation, unrelated work remains hidden while required work is ready; a failed replacement lesson receives the break below. Passing the linked lesson resolves that topic's obligation without an extra automatic review. For remediation after two lesson failures at the same KP, require the prerequisite lesson before resuming the original learning path while permitting unrelated eligible study. The reason for remediation determines the gate; both use the same review-failure-to-lesson transition. Keep failed attempts and their outcomes in history.

**Required lesson failure — chosen:** Keep the original remediation obligation pending and defer the lesson retry until five other successful practice activities finish. If other required remediation is ready, show it first; successful practice on other topics counts toward the break. If all remaining required remediation is waiting for retries, temporarily reopen ordinary study. Failed ordinary reviews update learner state but do not advance the five-success count. At five successes, restore the fresh-question required lesson when eligible and hide unrelated work again. The associated assessment retake remains unavailable throughout the break. Passing the linked lesson resolves the topic obligation; unrelated practice merely advances the break. If another lesson attempt fails at the same KP without further progress, assign its key-prerequisite remediation instead of another ordinary retry. Derive the break from finalized history, preserving it across restart and queue refreshes. Prerequisite-origin remediation keeps its original-learning-path gate while permitting unrelated study.

**Observational evidence for that branch:** The [assessment-review failure check](reference/analysis/review-queue-behavior-2026-10-12/assessment-review-failure-check.json) examines 50 distinct assessment-linked reviews from the existing retake audit: 45 have explicit passed status, and five older records have successful completion messages without an outcome field. None records a failure. None of the seven explicitly failed reviews in the queue audit appears directly in a captured assessment's follow-up queue or matches a recorded missed assessment topic in the corresponding checked account. Fourteen newer assessment captures likewise contain no missed-topic overlap with those failed reviews. Thus we have observations of assessment remediation succeeding and reviews failing elsewhere, but no directly traced assessment-required review failure. The ordinary-failure observations cannot establish its next activity, timing, or queue gate.

The current [Math Academy FAQ](https://mathacademy.com/faq) describes immediate reviews after incorrect quiz answers or exceeding the time limit, followed by an optional fresh-question retake after reviews. Our choice to hide all unrelated queue activities and handle every unanswered reserved slot is explicit; these exact details should be verified in our implementation rather than inferred from a numerical priority.

The [existing plan](reference/analysis/assessment-selection-2026-10-11/retake-scheduling-plan.md) already specifies this lifecycle. The [schema](schema/learner/2-1-learner-task.edn) already defines `:learner-task/remediation-for`; the scheduler uses it for origin tracking and clearing obligations.

## 2. Optional retakes

**Current:** The retake scheduler uses database remedial weights and `:learner-task/retake-of`, preserving original topics/KPs and waiting for linked repairs to pass. Retakes remain optional and do not reset cadence.

**Desired:** After successful required remediation, offer one optional retake using fresh questions from the original KPs, including previously correct ones. Add no cooldown, new XP requirement, or 48-hour wait. Retake mistakes require practice again, without an automatic endless retake chain. Retakes must not reset ordinary assessment cadence or prevent the next ordinary assessment.

**Evidence already available:** The [eight captured pairs and queue comparisons](reference/analysis/assessment-selection-2026-10-11/retake-behavior.md) contain 67 fresh questions, all on original KPs: 34 recheck previously correct topics and 33 recheck topics recorded as missed, including eight unanswered questions from an expired original. Four complete automated sequences offer the retake in the queue immediately after the last remedial review. Three retakes with further mistakes produce reviews again; no second retake appears after those reviews in the captured queues.

**XP delay:** There is no observed extra XP requirement after remediation. The reviews themselves earn XP, but that is work required to resolve the weaknesses, not an additional waiting counter. The four traced workers start the retake about 47–58 seconds after the last review's completion capture, and 7–13 minutes after the original completion capture. Those times include capture and interaction overhead and do not establish a prescribed cooldown. Use **zero additional XP and no added delay**, offering the retake on the next refresh after all obligations clear. The [official FAQ](https://mathacademy.com/faq) supports this sequence without publishing an XP delay.

**Eligibility chosen:** Offer one optional retake after remediation when the original score is **strictly below 75%**. At exactly 75% or above, require reviews for every missed topic but offer no automatic retake. Score against all assigned questions, with unanswered slots counting the same as wrong answers. Expiry has no separate eligibility override; this replaces the earlier plan's expiry exception. Four fully traced originals scoring 63.64–71.43% produce offers, while 22 scoring 76.92–90.91% show no offer after reviews. Those observations support our chosen cutoff without identifying Math Academy's exact threshold. Covering every original KP is our initial content rule; captures sometimes omit KPs and do not explain that omission.

**Investigate:** Verify the chosen score boundary, equal treatment of wrong and unanswered slots, failed-remediation replacements, and ordinary-versus-retake XP accounting. Reuse the existing evidence before collecting more. Keep named assessment retakes separate from repeating a failed lesson or review.

**Enough confidence:** Replay the fully traced sequences and test boundary scores, unresolved remediation, failed reviews, absent alternatives, duplicate refreshes, and retake mistakes. A ready retake remains optional alongside normal study, uses the remedial database weights, preserves its original link, and leaves ordinary cadence unchanged. More captures are needed only where an unresolved case would change these rules.

## 3. Assessment pacing and required status

**Current:** Offer at 120 signed finalized practice XP; require at 150 when the route is feasible. The queue and APIs enforce the gate; insufficient material preserves XP and permits continued study. Retakes do not reset the cadence.

The counter is derived during a chronological history read, not stored as another learner balance. Finalized lesson, review and assignment tasks contribute their signed `:learner-task/xp-earned` values. Only ordinary assessment completion resets the sum; retake completion leaves it intact.

**Desired:** Offer at 120 qualifying XP and require at 150 when a suitable assessment can be assembled. Sum signed finalized practice awards across courses from the existing earned-XP facts, including negative awards, since the previous ordinary assessment. Remove the per-award positive clamp. Assessment and retake rewards do not advance this practice sum, and completing a retake does not reset it. No additional stored XP counter is needed.

**Implemented:** `engine/rust/retrieval.rs` sums signed recorded awards directly; the per-award positive clamp is removed. Earned-XP formula limits remain unchanged. Fixtures verify negative awards, ordinary resets and unchanged cadence after retakes.

The current local [earned-XP formulas](engine/rust/activities.rs) have a minimum award of **-1 XP per activity**. Penalty frequency has not been quantified here; the signed-sum decision does not depend on treating penalties as frequent or severe. Preserve observed historical awards as recorded rather than rewriting them to fit current formulas.

The 120/150 distinction is deliberate: **120 is availability, 150 is the required boundary**. Whole-activity awards can make the first observed offer occur at 122 or 129 rather than exactly 120. At the required boundary, show the ordinary assessment instead of unrelated study; assessment-remediation obligations still take precedence. Optional retakes never inherit this countdown.

**Unavailable pool — chosen fallback:** If the assessment cannot be assembled at the required boundary, allow continued ordinary study and retain the ordinary-assessment cadence derived from signed practice XP. Do not reset the cycle or relax learning-age, fresh-question, or minimum-difficulty requirements. Trigger replenishment for missing questions; otherwise wait for normal topic eligibility. Recheck on refresh and require the assessment once it is ready and the current signed XP sum meets the boundary. Existing required remediation takes precedence. Treat this as an exceptional fallback and retain its reason for investigation, rather than designing an additional ordinary-study phase around it.

**Diagnostics — outside current scope:** Do not implement or require diagnostics as part of scheduler readiness. Future diagnostic earned XP should count toward the same assessment cadence, but diagnostic support and diagnostic-specific first-cycle investigation are deferred. Ordinary study can begin without a diagnostic.

**Investigate:** Reconstruct the signed XP ledger between original assessments, comparing the first offer and displayed optional countdown. Verify negative awards and failed practice using the recorded amounts; inspect assignments/multisteps, ordinary-assessment finalization resets, and retake rewards. Verify the exceptional unavailable-pool fallback without imposing an impossible assessment obligation. Diagnostic and first-cycle research are not prerequisites for this implementation.

The [existing availability audit](reference/mathacademy/capture-audits/2026-10-11-assessment-patterns/report.md) has 19 fully covered intervals matching a 150-XP boundary, with offers near 120. Math Academy's first-cycle and diagnostic rules are less certain, but those investigations are deferred. Confirm the signed ledger against the covered intervals; the report does not establish Math Academy's precise penalty-accounting rule.

**Enough confidence:** The chosen ledger reproduces the complete intervals and is checked against later independent intervals. Test study without a diagnostic, crossing both boundaries in one award, restart recovery, ordinary completion/expiry, optional-retake completion, and an unavailable eligible pool. Required status must be enforced by the API, not just a queue label or priority.

## 4. Assessment topic sampling

**Current:** Topic selection is weighted and random without replacement. Previously untested topics receive base weight 4, previously tested topics 1, multiplied by the chosen continuous encompassing discount.

**Desired:** Random sampling across the learner's eligible studied topics, reducing priority for already tested topics and topics encompassed by another topic in the current eligible assessment pool. This avoids redundant test coverage; it is distinct from implicit practice that updates retention and review timing. Apply no course preference. Freeze the selected route and preserve original KPs for retakes.

**Evidence gathered:** The [topic-sampling audit](reference/analysis/assessment-topic-sampling-2026-10-12/README.md) analyzes 56 dated ordinary assessments with 466 attributed questions across four separate accounts. Of these, 135 appearances revisit previously assessed topics. Forty-five reconstructed pre-offer pools contain 6,230 candidate appearances and 305 matched selections. Prior testing and encompassing coverage are associated with lower selection rates, including after matching learning age and course membership. Repeats can occur within two hours while untested aged topics remain available; the preference should be soft. The published explanation specifically refers to coverage by other **quizzable** topics. Neither the captures nor the published sources identify exact numerical penalties.

**Chosen:** Base topic weight 4 when never assessed, 1 when previously assessed; multiply by `0.05 + 0.95 * (1 - c)^2`, where `c` is the strongest existing encompassing coverage from another currently eligible topic. This single smooth curve decreases throughout the coverage range and reaches its 5% minimum only at full coverage; 80% coverage gives a multiplier of 0.088. Use weighted random draws without replacement and existing database question-difficulty weights. Keep retakes on their approved original-KP route. These topic-priority values are initial application choices, not recovered Math Academy parameters. The audit retains replay results, account differences, and evidence limits. The sampler is implemented and reads question-difficulty weights from the database; these topic-priority constants require no new attributes.

**Enough confidence:** Choose an inspectable rule that behaves sensibly across realistic pools, has reproducible seeded draws, and does not permanently exclude lower-weight topics. Check later captures for major contradictions without requiring exact quiz reproduction. Verify that empty question banks remain visible to inventory before supply filtering. Use the [topic-pool evidence](timed-delay-report.md) and [encompassing analysis](encompassing-report.md) as starting points.

## 5. Spaced reviews across study modes

**Current:** Ordinary spaced reviews consider all learned topics across courses and study modes, remain optional and use the existing FIRe due-date/ranking logic. Required remediation has separate origin-aware gates; the next ordinary queue offers an eligible optional lesson after three ordinary review attempts.

**Desired:** Ordinary reviews follow all learned topics in the learner's actual activity history, without course filtering, including self-directed study. They remain optional even when overdue: no global start/resume gate and no promotion to required after a grace period. Assessment remediation remains required in every mode.

**Meaning of learned:** A topic admitted to retention tracking with `:progress/learned true`: normally after successfully completing its lesson, or after diagnostic credit or explicit initialization establishes it as already known. It need not have a completed lesson in this application. Being queued, targeted, unlocked, partially studied, or merely receiving implicit accuracy evidence does not by itself mark a topic learned. Learned status is distinct from current recall: retention can decay and reviews can fail. See the [progress attribute](schema/learner/1-3-learner-progress.edn), [lesson completion](engine/rust/learning.rs), and [diagnostic initialization](engine/rust/runtime.rs).

**Queue and failure handling:** Keep recommendations limited to five items; all five may be reviews. Do not permanently reserve a lesson slot. After an ordinary review fails, use its actual outcomes to update retention evidence, then let the ordinary due-date and priority rules offer another fresh-question review. If it remains due, it can be offered on the next refresh; otherwise it returns when due. Add no separate retry cooldown, global gate, or automatic full-lesson replacement. Unrelated eligible study remains available. Repeated ordinary review failures alone do not trigger the two-lesson-failure prerequisite rule.

**Implemented change:** Blanket failed-review relearning is replaced with origin-aware handling: ordinary review → update learner state and end; remediation review → required lesson on the same topic, preserving the assessment or original-lesson obligation. Ordinary scheduling can subsequently offer another review when due. Keep failed attempts in history and reserve fresh questions for follow-up work. Review freshness, shortages, and the existing pass criterion still apply. Failed practice must not be counted as a successful review or silently reset retention to a mastered state.

**Math Academy evidence:** Justin Skycak, its Director of Analytics, explicitly says dashboards can temporarily contain only ordinary reviews. During a backlog, a lesson should become available after roughly 3–4 reviews; quiz follow-up reviews are excluded from that expectation. This supports intermittent lesson availability rather than a lesson occupying every queue. His reply was published in January 2025, so it describes stated behavior then, not a guarantee of today's private implementation. [Public staff explanation](https://www.reddit.com/r/mathacademy/comments/1hv9twt/reviews_gone_wild_on_ma/). The primary-source book also describes a minimum average opportunity to do lessons of roughly 25%; it does not publish the exact counter or reset rules. [Gamification, Progress vs XP](<reference/The Math Academy Way/III-COGNITIVE-LEARNING-STRATEGIES/22-Gamification/22-Gamification.md#progress-vs-xp>).

The [capture audit](reference/analysis/review-queue-behavior-2026-10-12/README.md) finds 351 all-review snapshots among 2,685 saved legacy queues, including 154 containing five reviews and 344 distinct account/task-list combinations. These are observations, not independent episodes; they include quiz follow-up queues whose origins are not fully classified. Seven explicitly failed reviews have captured follow-ups. In the three modern cases, two later repeat as reviews; one later returns as a lesson and eventually passes. None of the seven immediate queues offers a lesson on the failed topic, and six offer unrelated lessons. Together with the book's narrower escalation after failed **key-prerequisite remedial reviews**, this supports our chosen ordinary review-retry default. Retention-based ordinary retries are our best-supported implementation choice. These observations do not settle recovery after a failed assessment-required review.

**Periodic lesson availability — chosen:** After three ordinary review attempts finish without a lesson being offered, include an eligible lesson among the next queue's five recommendations. Count both passed and failed terminal attempts; unfinished, aborted, or supply-deferred work does not count. Reset the streak when an eligible lesson is offered in the ordinary queue, whether or not the learner selects it. No separate backlog-size threshold or permanent lesson slot is needed. If no lesson is eligible, keep the offer due until one becomes available. Existing required-activity gates take precedence; assessment-remediation reviews do not count toward this streak. The offered lesson remains optional. Derive the streak from activity and queue history rather than storing another progress balance. Three is our chosen starting rule, informed by the published 3–4-review behavior.

**Established due behavior:** A learned topic is due when its estimated retention reaches or falls below the database policy's `:policy/review-threshold`. Estimate retention from `:progress/memory`, `:progress/memory-at`, and `:progress/interval-days` using exponential half-life decay: `memory_now = stored_memory * 2^(-elapsed_days / interval_days)`. The retained provisional threshold is 0.5. This is a retention-model value, not a direct assertion that the learner has exactly a 50% probability of answering correctly. There is no fixed 48-hour age floor for ordinary reviews.

**Encompassing credit:** A successful activity on a source topic can provide fractional practice to its already learned encompassed topics. The effective graph coverage weight enters the credit calculation, together with the activity's quality, early-practice discount, and the receiving topic's learning speed. With the current decay-before-add convention, decay the target memory to the activity time, add the discounted coverage dose, add speed-adjusted fractional repetitions, and recompute its half-life and due date. Very early practice earns less credit; the current slow-implicit gate can suppress retention credit for a receiving topic with learning speed below 1. A prerequisite relationship alone supplies no encompassing dose.

For illustration only, assume the seeded timing defaults, speed and quality 1, and no slow gate: a newly learned topic with memory 1 and one repetition has a 48-hour initial due point. Successful 0.5-coverage practice at hour 24 earns about 0.293 fractional repetitions and moves that point to about hour 82.8. This is a calculation from our engine, not a recovered Math Academy rule. The [review timing investigation](reference/analysis/review-threshold-2026-10-11/README.md) records the example and the still-provisional calibration.

**Implementation and calibration:** Course/mode suppression is removed from scheduler filtering and candidate ordering. Ordinary reviews create no required-work gate or promotion. The three-review lesson-offer rule records actual offers and handles passed and failed attempts, temporarily unavailable lessons and required-activity precedence. Audit how incorrect answers and failed attempts affect retention so ordinary scheduling uses failure evidence without granting successful-review credit. Keep targeted prerequisite repair from repeated lesson failure distinct from ordinary review scheduling. Confirm that committed encompassing weights reach the live progress-update path and that postponement changes eligibility on the next refresh, including for an already prepared but unstarted review. Use the chosen transitions now; further capture can refine timing without reopening the basic recovery policy.

**Enough confidence:** The agreed scope and optionality have mode-switch, course-switch, pause/resume, overdue, failed-review, and completion fixtures. Assessment-required work cannot disappear because its course or study mode changed. Confirm that queue composition permits reasonable progress when eligible lessons exist and that implicit practice can postpone ordinary due dates.

## 6. Automatic replenishment and supported difficulties

**Current:** Candidate discovery is integrated with native captured-basis inventory and server hooks. Durable jobs deduplicate overlapping KP/band requests, retain lineage and cursor, run registered generators or the creator, and recheck preparation after stock arrives. Retake supply includes exact original-KP shortages. Unsupported and finite-limit outcomes stop repeated generation instead of claiming filled stock.

**Desired:** Replenish five unseen questions per supported difficulty at the appropriate KP or topic scope. Distinguish an exhausted supported band from an unobserved band. Investigate uncertain capabilities instead of repeatedly demanding an unsupported template. Preserve fresh retake alternatives during remediation.

**Investigate:** Use the [KP difficulty report](kp-difficulty-report.md) to find existing source templates and capture questions missing from EDB. Audit existing generator capabilities, the generator-ref interface, and finite variant limits. Difficulty absence scores are sampling heuristics, not probabilities of impossibility. Newly observed evidence must overturn an earlier absence estimate.

Exercise queue, target, unlock, due-review, and assessment-selection triggers through restart and overlapping events. Count distinct validated, gradable, unseen, unreserved questions at one database basis using efficient [$edb](skills/edb/SKILL.md) practices. Test urgency tiers against real shortages, using activity demand and existing planner priority to break ties.

**Enough confidence:** Exhausted supported levels replenish without changing their difficulty or skill. Uncertain or unproducible levels have explicit outcomes and no endless retry. If fewer than five genuinely distinct variants are possible, document a bounded exception and verify actual activity supply rather than silently claiming readiness. Required reviews and original-KP retakes have a viable supply path. Retries cannot duplicate questions or jobs, and committed replenishment resumes pending preparation.

## 7. Assessment sizing and workload estimates

**Current:** Assessments use 1/2/3-minute estimates, mandatory easy/moderate/hard seeds on distinct topics, weighted budget filling and an independent 15-minute wall-clock deadline. Retakes use feasible-completion lookahead over the fixed original topics and KPs.

**Chosen assembly:**

1. Check that the eligible pool can supply one unseen, validated question at each difficulty from three distinct topics. Respect supported difficulty bands; a topic need not support every band. Each selection must leave a feasible assignment for the remaining required slots.
2. Reserve one easy, one moderate, and one hard question using the chosen topic-priority weights. These consume six estimated minutes, leaving nine.
3. Sample another eligible topic using the agreed topic weights, then a difficulty using the database's ordinary-assessment 50/40/10 weights redistributed across its available bands that fit the remaining budget. The sampling unit is a **topic**, not each KP within it: one question per selected topic, chosen from an eligible KP and difficulty within that topic. A topic with several KPs does not automatically contribute several questions. Retakes keep those original topics and preserve the KP of each original question, rather than drawing a new topic pool.

Do not choose a difficulty-count combination in advance. ## 9. Failed lessons and key-prerequisite remediation

**Current implementation:** History-derived retry eligibility, repeated same-KP failure detection, linked key-prerequisite reviews/lessons and learning-path gates are implemented. Ordinary review failure updates state without automatic forced relearning. Required lesson failure retains its origin and temporarily releases the global gate until five other successful practice activities finish.

**What the documentation says:** Math Academy describes allowing unrelated learning after the first failed lesson, then assigning targeted prerequisite reviews if the next attempt stalls at the same place without further progress. It identifies the relevant prerequisites by lesson segment, even several graph steps back. The public FAQ broadly describes a lesson returning within a few days; neither page specifies an XP delay. [Algorithm description](https://mathacademy.com/how-our-ai-works), [FAQ](https://mathacademy.com/faq).

The local primary-source book is more specific: two consecutive failures at the same KP trigger **reviews of its key prerequisites**; if those reviews fail, the learner receives the corresponding **full lessons**. This is an escalation from reviewing known foundations to relearning them, followed by re-attempting the original lesson. See [Targeted Remediation](<reference/The Math Academy Way/III-COGNITIVE-LEARNING-STRATEGIES/21-Targeted-Remediation/21-Targeted-Remediation.md>) and [The Practice Experience, remediation section](<reference/The Math Academy Way/VI-FREQUENTLY-ASKED-QUESTIONS/FAQ-The-Practice-Experience/FAQ-The-Practice-Experience.md#remediation>), also published in [The Math Academy Way](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf).

These are different scheduling cases:

| Trigger | Follow-up | Implementation and calibration |
|---|---|---|
| Failed ordinary review | Update learner state and end; later reviews follow ordinary retention scheduling | Implemented without forced follow-up; failure-state calibration remains provisional |
| Wrong or unanswered-at-expiry assessment question | Immediate required review; required full lesson on that topic if the review fails | Implemented; passing the review or replacement lesson clears the topic obligation |
| First failed ordinary lesson attempt | Pause that learning path; permit unrelated work; automatic retry eligibility after five other successful practice activities | Implemented; later observations may refine pacing |
| Failed required replacement lesson | Keep obligation pending; five-successful-activities break; other ready required work first, otherwise ordinary study; restore required retry afterward | Implemented with history-derived counts and origin-aware gates |
| Second consecutive lesson failure at the same KP without progress | Targeted reviews of that KP's key prerequisites before retrying the original lesson | Implemented from existing key-prerequisite links; the gate applies to the original learning path |
| Failed prerequisite remedial review | Required full lesson on that prerequisite topic before retrying the original lesson | Implemented; failed required lessons use the five-success break while retaining their origin |
| Eligible original assessment after successful remediation | Optional fresh-question assessment retake, after linked reviews or replacement lessons pass | Implemented: below 75%, no added delay, one optional retake |

**Recommendation for investigation:** Reconstruct repeated lesson attempts from captured task IDs and question-to-KP membership. Identify the first failed KP and the furthest completed KP on each attempt; exclude supply deferrals from actual failures. Compare the next offered tasks, intervening XP/activities, timestamps, prerequisite reviews, failed-review replacements, and eventual return to the original lesson. Audit coverage and identity of the existing key-prerequisite links; do not substitute every ancestor for the targeted set.

The chosen escalation is repeated lesson failure at the same KP without further progress → its key-prerequisite reviews before retry → prerequisite lesson if a remedial review fails. Passing the assigned prerequisite repair allows the original lesson to return once all its required repairs are complete. Block the original lesson's start and resume until then, while permitting unrelated eligible work. The first ordinary lesson retry becomes eligible after five successful other practice activities. Assessment-origin required lessons use the same five-activity break after failure, with the temporary gate release defined in category 1. Required work resumes when its retry is eligible; its obligation remains pending throughout the break.

**Lesson-retry evidence and chosen rule:** The [captured retry investigation](reference/analysis/lesson-retry-2026-10-12/README.md) finds 15 explicit lesson failures and 14 observed returns. The closest three return brackets show no offer after four activities and an offer after five, despite differing practice awards of 13, 25, and 40 XP. Another return follows six attempts containing one failure, supplying five successful practice activities. Several returns occur within the same hour; the documentation's return within a few days is not a universal multi-day minimum. Chosen rule: after the first failed ordinary lesson, admit an automatic fresh-question retry after five other successful practice activities, derived from history, with no additional XP or elapsed-time minimum. Normal ranking, prerequisites, supply, and required-work gates can postpone its offer. Failed required replacement lessons use the approved break in category 1, allowing unrelated work when no required remediation is ready. This rule is implemented; capture-based timing refinement remains possible.

**Enough confidence:** A few fully traced examples establish each transition, while fixtures cover repeated versus different failure locations, forward progress, fresh questions, multiple prerequisites, successful repair, failed repair, missing links, and retry recovery. Required prerequisite repair cannot be bypassed by direct study or a mode switch. Preserve the failed attempts and derive the failure location from their actual route and outcomes where possible.

Finally, [capture post-processing](capture/docs/4-post-activity-processing.md) instructs our capture worker to make its next lesson/review repeat all-correct after negative XP. That is a capture-agent policy. It does not prove Math Academy imposes an all-correct retake rule, and it is not implemented as a learner-scheduler requirement.

## 10. Assessment navigation and unanswered questions

**Current:** The assessment player supports free navigation and editable saved drafts. Submit or expiry finalizes the entire route atomically; unanswered slots receive zero score and required review without fabricated incorrect-answer events. Assessment skip and per-question submission are rejected.

**Chosen:** The learner can visit any question in the frozen assessment, leave it unanswered, and return to edit its answer while time remains. Diagnostic skipping remains a separate behavior.
