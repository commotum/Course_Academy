# MTH 255 — Vector Calculus II

## Course Description

Vector Calculus II develops the calculus of vector fields and integration along curves and over surfaces. Building on Vector Calculus I, the course connects local descriptions of a field with accumulated quantities such as work, circulation, and flux. Its central tools include conservative fields, divergence, curl, and the integral theorems of Gauss and Stokes, with applications involving force, work, mass, and charge.

## Course Overview

The course revisits vector functions, space curves, gradients, and directional derivatives before extending integration to curves and surfaces. Line integrals connect vector fields with work and circulation. Conservative fields, potential functions, and path independence explain when an integral can be determined from endpoint information rather than the details of a path.

Surface geometry and oriented surface elements provide the foundation for surface integrals and flux. Divergence and curl describe local field behavior, while the Divergence Theorem and Stokes’ Theorem connect these local quantities to integrals over regions and their boundaries. The archived course materials emphasize geometric understanding and the use of vector differentials to connect the various integration methods.

## Course Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, students will have mastered the following:

### Vector Geometry and Field Interpretation

- Express vectors in standard coordinate systems and bases and interpret dot and cross products geometrically.
- Explain the relationship among gradients, directional derivatives, and level sets.
- Construct line and surface elements appropriate to a curve or surface, accounting for its geometry and orientation.

### Line Integrals and Conservative Fields

- Evaluate scalar and vector line integrals and interpret their use in calculating quantities such as mass, work, and circulation.
- Analyze conservative fields, find potential functions when they exist, and use path independence to evaluate line integrals.

### Surface Integrals and Flux

- Set up and evaluate scalar surface integrals for quantities distributed over a surface, including mass or charge.
- Calculate flux through an oriented surface and interpret the role of the field’s normal component.

### Differential Operators and Integral Theorems

- Calculate divergence and curl in standard coordinate systems and interpret them in relation to flux and circulation.
- Explain and apply the Divergence Theorem to connect flux through a closed surface with a volume integral.
- Explain and apply Stokes’ Theorem to connect circulation around a boundary curve with a surface integral of curl.
