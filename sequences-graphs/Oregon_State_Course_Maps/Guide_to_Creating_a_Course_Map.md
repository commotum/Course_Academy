# Guide to Creating a Course Map

## Purpose

Use the template to describe a course’s purpose, scope, progression, and intended learning outcomes. The completed course map should contain:

- Course title
- Course Description
- Course Overview
- Course Outcomes

Do not include a Course Content section or a numbered outline of units, sections, and topics.

## Understanding the Placeholders

Square-bracketed text identifies content to replace. Repeated placeholders illustrate a repeatable structure; they do not specify how many entries the completed course map should contain.

**The number of placeholder entries must not determine the number of actual entries.**

For example, the template displays two outcome categories with three outcomes beneath each. This does not require two categories, three outcomes per category, or six outcomes overall. It also does not establish minimums, maximums, or a requirement that categories contain equal numbers of outcomes.

Instead:

- Add or remove outcome categories according to the course’s scope and organization.
- Add or remove outcome bullets according to the distinct capabilities students are expected to develop.
- Allow different categories to contain different numbers of outcomes.
- Omit category headings when a single, ungrouped outcome list is more appropriate.

Likewise, a single `[Course description]` or `[Course overview]` placeholder does not impose a one-sentence or one-paragraph limit.

Do not invent, duplicate, split, combine, or omit substantive material merely to match the template’s visible placeholder counts. Any actual numerical requirements must come from explicit instructions or authoritative course requirements—not from the template.

**Preserve the document’s structure, not the number of example entries.**

## 1. Establish the Course’s Scope

Before filling in the template, identify the course’s central subject, major areas of study, intended level, progression, and expected learning outcomes.

When working from existing course materials, use those materials as the basis for the map. Preserve their terminology, meaning, scope, and emphasis. Do not silently add topics, prerequisites, applications, or learning outcomes that the materials do not support.

When designing a new course, establish its intended scope before writing the map. Keep proposed design choices distinguishable from any existing requirements.

## 2. Write the Course Title

Replace `[Course Title]` with the course’s name.

Use a single level-one Markdown heading:

`# Course Title`

Retain an established title when one is provided. For a new course, choose a title that accurately reflects its subject and scope.

## 3. Write the Course Description

Under `## Course Description`, explain what the course is and why it is offered.

Describe its central subject, overall scope, and educational purpose. Where established, explain the knowledge it builds on and the further study or applications it prepares students for.

Keep the description focused on the course as a whole rather than cataloging individual topics. Include only supported details about prerequisites, audience, level, or preparation for later courses.

Use enough text to define the course clearly without repeating the overview.

## 4. Write the Course Overview

Under `## Course Overview`, explain what students will study and how the major areas connect.

Present the course’s organization or progression in coherent prose. Where the sequence is established, describe how earlier concepts support later work. Mention important methods, applications, or perspectives when they are part of the course’s intended emphasis.

The distinction between these sections is:

**Course Description:** What is this course, and what is its purpose?

**Course Overview:** What does the course cover, and how does that coverage fit together?

The overview may be brief or extended, depending on the course. Do not invent a teaching sequence when none is established, and do not turn the overview into the excluded Course Content outline.

## 5. Write the Course Outcomes

Under `## Course Outcomes`, retain the introductory sentence:

Upon successful completion of this course, students will have mastered the following:

Then state the capabilities students should possess after completing the course.

Write each outcome as a bullet describing an identifiable capability, rather than merely naming a topic. Use verbs appropriate to the intended learning, such as explain, calculate, apply, analyze, construct, interpret, or evaluate.

Keep outcomes at the course level. Include enough detail to communicate meaningful expectations without converting the list into an inventory of individual lessons or exercises.

When deriving outcomes from existing materials, preserve the intended level of achievement. Do not turn introductory exposure into advanced mastery or add capabilities unsupported by the course.

### Organizing Outcomes

Use optional level-three headings to group outcomes into meaningful subject areas or competency categories:

`### Outcome Category`

Choose category names that reflect the actual course. Categories need not correspond one-to-one with units, and they need not contain equal numbers of outcomes.

Use a flat bullet list when categories would add unnecessary structure.

### Determining How Many Outcomes to Include

Let the course’s distinct learning expectations determine the number of outcomes.

Add an outcome when it represents an important capability not already covered. Combine closely related statements when doing so preserves their meaning and improves clarity. Remove redundancy without losing substantive expectations.

Do not stop because every placeholder has been filled, and do not continue because unused placeholders remain.

## 6. Review the Completed Map

Check that the description, overview, and outcomes represent the same course. Major capabilities should be consistent with the stated scope, and the overview should not promise unsupported coverage.

Confirm that all bracketed placeholders have been replaced or removed. Remove empty categories and unnecessary repetition.

Preserve the Markdown hierarchy: one `#` course title, the three required `##` section headings, optional `###` outcome categories, and `-` outcome bullets.

Finally, verify that the number of categories, outcomes, and paragraphs follows the course’s actual needs—not the number of placeholders shown in the template.
