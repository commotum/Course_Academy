# MTH 341 — Linear Algebra I

## Course Description

Linear Algebra I introduces the computational and conceptual study of linear systems, matrices, and subspaces. The course connects methods for solving equations with the structure of linear transformations and vector spaces. Determinants, linear independence, bases, dimension, and introductory eigenvalue analysis provide complementary ways to understand how a linear system or transformation behaves.

## Course Overview

Work with linear systems and Gaussian elimination establishes a foundation for matrix operations, invertibility, and determinants. The course then connects these calculations with linear independence, spanning sets, subspaces, bases, and dimension. Row, column, and null spaces describe important features of a matrix, while rank–nullity relates those spaces to the structure of a system’s solutions.

Matrix representations make linear transformations concrete through examples such as rotation, reflection, projection, and scaling. Eigenvalues and eigenvectors introduce directions preserved by a transformation, including examples with complex eigenvalues. The archived section used here also introduces diagonalization and illustrates eigenvalue methods through simple systems of differential equations.

## Course Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, students will have mastered the following:

### Linear Systems and Matrix Computation

- Use Gaussian elimination to solve linear systems and describe their complete solution sets.
- Perform matrix operations and determine whether a square matrix has an inverse, calculating it when one exists.
- Calculate determinants and use their properties to assess invertibility and the uniqueness of solutions to square linear systems.

### Subspaces, Bases, and Dimension

- Determine whether a subset of \(\mathbb{R}^n\) satisfies the requirements for a subspace.
- Analyze linear dependence and independence and determine the span of a collection of vectors.
- Find bases and dimensions for row, column, and null spaces.
- Apply the rank–nullity relationship to reason about solution sets and matrix invertibility.

### Linear Transformations

- Construct and use the standard-basis matrix representation of a linear transformation.
- Interpret matrix transformations geometrically and relate composition of transformations to matrix multiplication.

### Eigenvalues, Eigenvectors, and Introductory Applications

- Calculate eigenvalues and eigenvectors, including examples involving nonreal complex values, and determine the associated eigenspaces.
- Relate algebraic and geometric multiplicities to the availability of an eigenvector basis and diagonalize a matrix when such a basis exists.
- Explain, at an introductory level, how eigenvalue methods can be used in solving simple systems of linear differential equations.
