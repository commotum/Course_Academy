# MTH 254 — Vector Calculus I

## Course Description

Vector Calculus I extends differentiation and integration to vector-valued functions and functions of several variables. Building on single-variable calculus, the course develops mathematical tools for describing curves, surfaces, motion, and quantities distributed across regions in two or three dimensions. Geometric interpretation and physical applications connect vector operations, multivariable derivatives, and multiple integrals.

## Course Overview

Vectors and coordinate systems provide a language for describing geometry in the plane and in space. Vector-valued functions represent curves and motion, while derivatives and integrals of these functions describe velocity, acceleration, arc length, and curvature. Equations of surfaces and their level sets extend graphical reasoning to functions of several variables.

Multivariable differentiation includes limits, continuity, partial derivatives, the chain rule, directional derivatives, and gradients. These concepts support tangent-plane approximations and optimization, including constrained problems using Lagrange multipliers. Multiple integration addresses accumulated quantities over planar and spatial regions. Rectangular, polar, cylindrical, and spherical coordinates—and more general changes of variables—allow students to match an integral’s representation to the geometry of its region.

## Course Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, students will have mastered the following:

### Vectors, Coordinates, and Spatial Geometry

- Describe points and regions using rectangular, polar, cylindrical, and spherical coordinates and convert between appropriate coordinate systems.
- Perform vector operations, including dot and cross products, and interpret their geometric meaning.
- Represent and analyze lines, planes, surfaces, and level sets using equations and geometric descriptions.

### Vector-Valued Functions and Curves

- Differentiate and integrate vector-valued functions and use them to describe position, velocity, and acceleration.
- Calculate arc length and curvature and interpret tangent and normal vectors as descriptions of a curve’s local geometry.

### Multivariable Differentiation and Optimization

- Evaluate limits and analyze continuity for functions of several variables.
- Calculate partial derivatives and apply the multivariable chain rule.
- Use gradients and directional derivatives to determine rates and directions of change and relate them to level sets.
- Construct tangent planes and use linear approximations to estimate nearby function values.
- Find and classify critical points and solve unconstrained and constrained optimization problems, including problems requiring Lagrange multipliers.

### Multiple Integration

- Set up and evaluate double and triple integrals over specified regions.
- Select suitable coordinates and apply changes of variables, including the appropriate area or volume scaling factor.
- Use multiple integrals to calculate geometric and physical quantities, including volume and mass.
