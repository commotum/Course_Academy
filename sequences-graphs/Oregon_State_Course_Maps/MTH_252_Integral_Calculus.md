# MTH 252 — Integral Calculus

## Course Description

Integral Calculus studies accumulation and integration for functions of one variable. Building on differential calculus, the course develops definite and indefinite integrals and their applications. Graphical, numerical, and symbolic approaches support the interpretation of integrals and their use in mathematical modeling and problem solving.

## Course Overview

The course connects Riemann sums, definite integrals, and antiderivatives through the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus. Students develop integration techniques and use numerical approximation alongside analytical methods. These tools support applications to areas, volumes, curve lengths, motion, force, and work. Logarithmic and exponential functions, polar coordinates, improper integrals, and elementary growth and decay models extend the range of problems addressed.

## Course Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, students will have mastered the following:

### Foundations of Integration

- Interpret Riemann sums and definite integrals graphically and as signed accumulation.
- Find antiderivatives and distinguish indefinite integrals from definite integrals.
- Apply the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus to evaluate definite integrals and differentiate integrals with variable limits.

### Integration Methods

- Select and apply substitution, integration by parts, trigonometric integration methods, and partial fractions.
- Approximate definite integrals using basic numerical integration rules.
- Evaluate improper integrals using limits and determine whether they converge.

### Geometric and Physical Applications

- Calculate areas, including areas described in polar coordinates, and volumes using slicing or cylindrical shells.
- Use integration to calculate curve lengths and physical quantities, including displacement, total distance, force, and work.
- Formulate and solve elementary first-order differential equations describing growth and decay.
