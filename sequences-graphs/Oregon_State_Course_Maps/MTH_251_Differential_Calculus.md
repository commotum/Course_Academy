# MTH 251 — Differential Calculus

## Course Description

Differential Calculus introduces the mathematics of change for real-valued functions of one variable. Building on algebra and trigonometry, the course develops the concepts and methods needed to interpret derivatives, analyze functions, and solve problems involving changing quantities. Graphical, numerical, and symbolic representations connect computational techniques with mathematical reasoning and applications.

## Course Overview

Limits and continuity provide the conceptual framework for studying derivatives. Students connect average rates of change with instantaneous rates, then use limit laws and differentiation rules—including the chain rule—to calculate and interpret derivatives. The emphasis is on understanding what these calculations represent, rather than treating differentiation solely as algebraic manipulation.

Applications connect derivatives to motion, the shape of a graph, and maximum and minimum problems. Students use calculus to analyze velocity, speed, and acceleration; investigate how functions change; and solve optimization problems. Mathematical modeling includes identifying relevant variables, making appropriate assumptions, and evaluating whether a method and its conclusions make sense in context.

## Course Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, students will have mastered the following:

### Limits, Continuity, and Derivatives

- Evaluate limits using algebraic limit laws and interpret limiting behavior through graphical, numerical, and symbolic representations.
- Analyze the continuity of a single-variable function at a point or over an interval.
- Calculate average and instantaneous rates of change and explain how the derivative represents an instantaneous rate.
- Differentiate single-variable functions using standard differentiation rules, including the chain rule.

### Function Analysis and Applications

- Use derivatives to analyze motion, including relationships among position, velocity, speed, and acceleration.
- Determine the shape and behavior of a graph using derivative information, including increasing and decreasing behavior, extrema, and concavity.
- Formulate and solve maximum and minimum problems using differential calculus.

### Mathematical Modeling and Reasoning

- Recognize situations that can be modeled mathematically, identify relevant quantities, and calculate or estimate the relationships needed to solve a problem.
- Evaluate the suitability of a mathematical approach and the validity of its conclusions, accounting for the assumptions used in constructing the model.
