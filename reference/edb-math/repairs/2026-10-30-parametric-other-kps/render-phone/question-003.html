Given that a curve is defined parametrically as

x=−3cos⁡θ+3,y=−2sin⁡θ+3,π2<θ≤2π,

what are the ranges of x and y? Use < or ≤ in the comparison blanks.

The range of x is x .

The range of y is y .

CorrectPreview only
Solution:

Answer 1 · correct value:

0

Answer 2 · correct value:

≤

Answer 3 · correct value:

≤

Answer 4 · correct value:

6

Answer 5 · correct value:

1

Answer 6 · correct value:

<

Answer 7 · correct value:

≤

Answer 8 · correct value:

5

Consider each coordinate separately.

For x:

Check the quarter-turn points in the parameter interval and its endpoint limits: θ=π2 gives cos⁡θ=0 (excluded endpoint); θ=π gives cos⁡θ=−1 (included); θ=3π2 gives cos⁡θ=0 (included); θ=2π gives cos⁡θ=1 (included).

The value −1 is attained at θ=π.

The value 1 is attained at θ=2π.

Multiplying by −3 reverses both comparisons; translate by 3, then write the bounds in increasing order.

−1≤cos⁡θ≤13≥−3cos⁡θ≥−36≥−3cos⁡θ+3≥00≤x≤6.

Thus 0≤x≤6.

For y:

Check the quarter-turn points in the parameter interval and its endpoint limits: θ=π2 gives sin⁡θ=1 (excluded endpoint); θ=π gives sin⁡θ=0 (included); θ=3π2 gives sin⁡θ=−1 (included); θ=2π gives sin⁡θ=0 (included).

The value −1 is attained at θ=3π2.

The value 1 is approached at an excluded endpoint and is not attained elsewhere in the interval.

Multiplying by −2 reverses both comparisons; translate by 3, then write the bounds in increasing order.

−1≤sin⁡θ<12≥−2sin⁡θ>−25≥−2sin⁡θ+3>11<y≤5.

Thus 1<y≤5.

Continuity gives every value between the stated bounds. The coordinate extrema need not occur at the same parameter value.
