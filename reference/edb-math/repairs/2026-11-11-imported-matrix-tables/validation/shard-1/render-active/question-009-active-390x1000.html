Let's denote f1(x,y)=4x(1−2x)−xy and f2(x,y)=−3y+3xy.

Now, we compute the partial derivatives:

∂f1∂x=∂∂x(4x(1−2x)−xy)=4−16x−y,∂f1∂y=∂∂y(4x(1−2x)−xy)=−x∂f2∂x=∂∂x(−3y+3xy)=3y,∂f2∂y=∂∂y(−3y+3xy)=−3+3x

Thus, the Jacobian matrix is

J=[4−16x−y−x3y−3+3x].

Next, we evaluate the Jacobian at the equilibrium x∗=[00] to get

J(x∗)=[4−16(0)−0−03(0)−3+3(0)]=[400−3].

The eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix are λ1=4 and λ2=−3. Since the eigenvalues are real with opposite signs, our equilibrium is an unstable saddle.
