Recall that if A=UΣVT is a singular value decomposition of a 3×2 matrix A, then

  • Σ is a 3×2 matrix with nonnegative elements on the main diagonal and zeroes outside the main diagonal,

  • U is an orthogonal 3×3 matrix,

  • V is an orthogonal 2×2 matrix.

From the matrix Σ, we deduce that σ1=4 and σ2=0. Also, the columns v1 and v2 of V give us the right singular vectors of A.

Therefore, we can compute the first column of U as follows:

u1=1σ1Av1=14[22−2−200][1212]=14[42−420]=[12−120]

Since the second singular value is σ2=0, and we do not have the third singular value, we can't use the same formula to find the second and third columns of U. But we know that U must be orthogonal. Hence, we must find vectors

u=[x1x2x3]

such that u1⋅u=0 and ‖u‖=1.

Solving u1⋅u=0, which is equivalent to

12x1−12x2=0⟹x1−x2=0,

we get the general solution

[x1x1x3]=x1[110]⏟b1+x3[001]⏟b2,

where x1 and x3 are free variables.

Notice that the two linearly independent solutions b1 and b2 are mutually orthogonal. Therefore, we only need to normalize them. This gives the second and third columns of U:

u2=[12120]u3=[001]

So, the matrix U could be the following:

U=[12120−12120001]
