Consider the function L:R3×2→R defined by L(A)=(Ax−b)T(Ax−b), where

x=[44],b=[802].

What is ∇AL at A=[11−103−2]?

∇A=
0
−32
16
CorrectPreview only
Solution:

Answer 1 · correct value:

0

Answer 2 · correct value:

−32

Answer 3 · correct value:

16

The gradient of L with respect to A is a matrix of the same shape as A, with (i,j)th entry given by

∂L∂aij,

the partial derivative of L with respect to the (i,j)th entry of A.

We can compute ∂L∂aij by applying the chain rule.

Let y=Ax−b∈R3. Then, L(A)=yTy and

∂L∂aij=∑k=13∂L∂yk⋅∂yk∂aij

Now, since

∇yL=∇y(yTy)=2y,

we have that

∂L∂yk=2yk.

Writing out y=Ax−b in component-wise notation as

yi=∑j=12aijxj−bi,

we have

  • ∂yk∂aij=xj if i=k, and

  • ∂yk∂aij=0 if i≠k.

This means that

∂L∂aij=∑k=13∂L∂yk⋅∂yk∂aij=∂L∂yi⋅∂yi∂aij=2yi⋅∂yi∂aij=2yixj.

The (i,j)th entry of ∇AL is 2 times the ith entry of y multiplied by the jth entry of x, so

∇AL=2y⋅xT.

Finally, evaluating ∇AL at A=[11−103−2], we get

∇AL=2y⋅xT=2(Ax−b)⋅xT=2([11−103−2][44]−[802])⋅[44]=2([8−44]−[802])⋅[44]=[0−84]⋅[44]=[00−32−321616].
