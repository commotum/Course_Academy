If f(x,y,z)=4(yzcosx) and r(t)=(t+1)2i+e(t+1)j+e(t+1)k, compute ddt[f(r(t))].

Solution:

Answer · correct value:

4(2e2(t+1)(cos((t+1)2)−(t+1)sin((t+1)2)))

The chain rule in vector form states that

ddt[f(r(t))]=∇f(r(t))⋅r′(t).

First, we need to compute ∇f(x,y,z):

∇f(x,y,z)=∂∂x(4(yzcosx))i+∂∂y(4(yzcosx))j+∂∂z(4(yzcosx))k=(4(−yzsinx))i+(4(+zcosx))j+(4(+ycosx))k

Next, we substitute x=(t+1)2,y=e(t+1), and z=e(t+1) into the above to find ∇f(r(t)):

∇f(r(t))=(4(−e2(t+1)sin((t+1)2)))i+(4(+e(t+1)cos((t+1)2)))j+(4(+e(t+1)cos((t+1)2)))k

Now, we calculate the derivative of r(t), which is

r′(t)=ddt((t+1)2)i+ddt(e(t+1))j+ddt(e(t+1))k=(2(t+1))i+(+e(t+1))j+(+e(t+1))k.

Finally, applying the chain rule, we get

ddt[f(r(t))]=∇f(r(t))⋅r′(t)=((4(−e2(t+1)sin((t+1)2)))i+(4(+e(t+1)cos((t+1)2)))j+(4(+e(t+1)cos((t+1)2)))k)⋅((2(t+1))i+(+e(t+1))j+(+e(t+1))k)=4(−2(t+1)e2(t+1)sin((t+1)2)+e(t+1)cos((t+1)2)⋅e(t+1)+e(t+1)cos((t+1)2)⋅e(t+1))=4(−2(t+1)e2(t+1)sin((t+1)2)+2e2(t+1)cos((t+1)2))=4(2e2(t+1)(cos((t+1)2)−(t+1)sin((t+1)2))).
