Given the set of functions $\{ e^x, \, \sin x \}$ defined on $x \in \mathbb{R},$ explain why they are linearly independent.

SOLUTION:
Recall that the set of functions $\{ f_1(x), f_2(x), \ldots, f_n(x) \},$ which are defined on some interval $I,$ is *linearly dependent* if there exist real scalars $k_1,k_2,\ldots,k_n$ not all zero such that the linear combination $k_1 \cdot f_1 + k_2 \cdot f_2 + \ldots + k_2 \cdot f_2$ is identically zero, i.e, 

$$
k_1 \cdot f_1(x) + k_2 \cdot f_2(x) + \ldots + k_2 \cdot f_2(x) = 0
$$

for all $x \in I.$ Otherwise, if $k_1 = k_2 = \cdots = k_n = 0$ is the only solution, the set is *linearly independent*.

Now, suppose for all $x \in \mathbb{R},$ we have that 

$$
k_1 e^x + k_2 \sin x = 0.
$$

For example:

- If $x=0,$ we get 

$$
\begin{aligned} & {k}_{1}({e}^{0})+{k}_{2}(\operatorname{sin}⁡0)=0 \\  & {k}_{1}\cdot 1+{k}_{2}\cdot 0=0\,⇒\,{k}_{1}=0.\end{aligned}
$$

- If $x=1,$ we get 

$$
\begin{aligned} & {k}_{1}({e}^{1})+{k}_{2}(\operatorname{sin}⁡1)=0 \\  & e{k}_{1}+(\operatorname{sin}⁡1)\,{k}_{2}=0\,⇒\,e{k}_{1}+(\operatorname{sin}⁡1)\,{k}_{2}=0.\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, we obtain the following system of linear equations:

$$
\left\{\begin{aligned}{k}_{1} &=0 \\ e{k}_{1}+(\operatorname{sin}⁡1)\,{k}_{2} &=0\end{aligned}\right.
$$

The only solution for this system is $\bbox[4pt, border: solid 1pt gainsboro]{k_1=k_2=0}.$

Therefore, since $\bbox[4pt, border: solid 1pt gainsboro]{\text{all}}$ the scalar coefficients in the linear combination must be zero, the given set of functions is linearly $\bbox[4pt, border: solid 1pt gainsboro]{\text{independent}}.$

=== 17592186151905 e-21680 ===
Compute the Wronskian $W(y_1, y_2)$ for $y_1(x) =2x$ and $y_2(x) = x^2.$

SOLUTION:
The Wronskian $W(y_1,y_2)$ of two functions $y_1(x)$ and $y_2(x)$ is defined as

$$
W(y_1,y_2) = 	
\left|
		{\begin{array}{cc}{y}_{1} & {y}_{2} \\ {y}_{1}^{\prime } & {y}_{2}^{\prime }\end{array}}
	\right|	
= y_1 y_2'-  y_2 y_1'.
$$

In our case, we have $y_1(x) = 2x$ and $y_2(x) = x^2.$ Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}W({y}_{1},{y}_{2}) & =2x{({x}^{2})}^{′}-{x}^{2}{(2x)}^{′} \\  & =4{x}^{2}-2{x}^{2} \\  & =2{x}^{2}.\end{aligned}
$$
