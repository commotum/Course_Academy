KP 17592186147436 Solving a Cauchy-Euler Equation with a Repeated Root

QUESTION 17592186147353 e-6627
Find the general solution to the equation

$$
2x^2 y'' + 6 xy' + 2y = 0, \qquad x>0.
$$
SOLUTION
This is an instance of the Cauchy-Euler equation. So, we assume the solutions take the form $y=x^\lambda.$ Differentiating $y$ with respect to $x$ gives

$$
y' = \lambda x^{\lambda -1}, \qquad y'' = \lambda(\lambda-1)x^{\lambda-2}.
$$

Substituting the above to our differential equation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}2{x}^{2}\cdot \lambda (\lambda -1){x}^{\lambda -2}+6x\cdot \lambda {x}^{\lambda -1}+2{x}^{\lambda } & =0 \\ 2\lambda (\lambda -1){x}^{\lambda }+6\lambda {x}^{\lambda }+2{x}^{\lambda } & =0 \\ {x}^{\lambda }[2\lambda (\lambda -1)+6\lambda +2] & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have the following characteristic equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}2\lambda (\lambda -1)+6\lambda +2 & =0 \\ 2{\lambda }^{2}+4\lambda +2 & =0 \\ 2({\lambda }^{2}+2\lambda +1) & =0 \\ 2(\lambda +1)^{2} & =0\end{aligned}
$$

The characteristic equation has a double root $\lambda = -1.$ Therefore, the general solution is

$$
\begin{aligned}y(x) & ={c}_{1}{x}^{-1}+{c}_{2}{x}^{-1}\operatorname{ln}⁡x \\  & =\frac{{c}_{1}}{x}+\frac{{c}_{2}\operatorname{ln}⁡x}{x}.\end{aligned}
$$

QUESTION 17592186148185 q-64777
Find the general solution to the equation

$$
{x}^{2}{y}^{″}-x{y}^{′}+y=0,\,x>0.
$$
SOLUTION
The given equation is an instance of the Cauchy-Euler equation. So, we assume that the solutions take the form $y={x}^{\lambda }.$ Differentiating $y$ with respect to $x$ gives

$$
{y}^{′}=\lambda {x}^{\lambda -1},\,{y}^{″}=\lambda (\lambda -1){x}^{\lambda -2}.
$$

Substituting the above to our differential equation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}{x}^{2}\cdot \lambda (\lambda -1){x}^{\lambda -2}-x\cdot \lambda {x}^{\lambda -1}+{x}^{\lambda } & =0 \\ \lambda (\lambda -1){x}^{\lambda }-\lambda {x}^{\lambda }+{x}^{\lambda } & =0 \\ {x}^{\lambda }[\lambda (\lambda -1)-\lambda +1] & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the auxiliary equation is

$$
\begin{aligned}\lambda (\lambda -1)-\lambda +1 & =0 \\ {\lambda }^{2}-2\lambda +1 & =0 \\ (\lambda -1)^{2} & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, we have a double root $\lambda =1.$
Therefore, the general solution is

$$
y(x)={c}_{1}{x}^{1}+{c}_{2}{x}^{1}\operatorname{ln}⁡x={c}_{1}x+{c}_{2}x\operatorname{ln}⁡x.
$$
