QUESTION 17592186151994 e-6861
Given that the differential equation

$$
y'' + y - 2y =0
$$

has the general solution

$$
y = Ae^{x} + Be^{- 2 x},
$$

solve the initial value problem

$$
y'' + y - 2y =0,
\ \ y(1) = e, \ \ y'(1) = e ^{-2}.
$$
SOLUTION
We find the constants $A$ and $B$ using the initial conditions.

- Substituting $y(1)=e$ into our general solution gives

$$
\begin{aligned}e & =A{e}^{1}+B{e}^{-2(1)} \\ e & =Ae+B{e}^{-2} \\ A & =1-B{e}^{-3}.\end{aligned}
$$

- To apply the condition $y'(1) = e^{-2},$ we first differentiate $y$ to get

$$
y' = Ae^x - 2Be^{-x}.
$$

Then, we substitute $y'(1)=e^{-2}$ into the above to get

$$
\begin{aligned}{e}^{-2} & =A{e}^{1}-2B{e}^{-2(1)} \\ {e}^{-2} & =Ae-2B{e}^{-2}.\end{aligned}
$$

Putting the two equations from above together, we have the following system:

$$
\left\{\begin{aligned}A &=1-B{e}^{-3} \\ {e}^{-2} &=Ae-2B{e}^{-2}\end{aligned}\right.
$$

Substituting $A = 1 - B e ^{-3}$ in the second equation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}{e}^{-2} & =(1-B{e}^{-3})e-2B{e}^{-2} \\ 1 & ={e}^{3}-B-2B \\ B & =\frac{{e}^{3}-1}{3}.\end{aligned}
$$

Then, substituting $B= \dfrac {e ^ 3 - 1} 3$ back into the equation $A = 1-Be^{-3}$ gives

$$
A = 1 -  \dfrac {(e ^ 3 - 1) e ^ {-3}} 3 =   \dfrac {2 +  e ^ {-3}} 3.
$$

Therefore, the particular solution of the initial value problem is

$$
y =  \dfrac {  e ^ {-3} + 2} 3  e^{x} + \dfrac {e ^ 3 - 1} 3 e^{-2x}.
$$

QUESTION 17592186152401 q-130343
Solve the initial value problem

$$
{y}^{″}-{y}^{′}-2y=0,\,y(0)=1,\,{y}^{′}(0)=-4.
$$
SOLUTION
Let $y={e}^{\lambda x}.$ Differentiating $y$ with respect to $x$ gives

$$
{y}^{′}=\lambda {e}^{\lambda x},\,{y}^{″}={\lambda }^{2}{e}^{\lambda x}.
$$

Substituting the above into our differential equation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}{e}^{\lambda x}-\lambda {e}^{\lambda x}-2{e}^{\lambda x} & =0 \\ {e}^{\lambda x}({\lambda }^{2}-\lambda -2) & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

The auxiliary equation is therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}-\lambda -2 & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

Factoring the auxiliary equation results in

$$
(\lambda +1)(\lambda -2)=0,
$$

and so $\lambda =-1$ or $\lambda =2.$

Therefore, the general solution to the differential equation is

$$
y=A{e}^{-x}+B{e}^{2x}.
$$

Now let us find constants $A$ and $B.$ 

Substituting $y(0)=1$ into the general solution gives

$$
A+B=1.
$$

We now need to apply the condition ${y}^{′}(0)=-4.$ First, we differentiate $y$ to find ${y}^{′}$:

$$
{y}^{′}=-A{e}^{-x}+2B{e}^{2x}
$$

Substituting ${y}^{′}(0)=-4$ into the above gives

$$
-A+2B=-4.
$$

Therefore, we have the following system of equations for $A$ and $B$:

$$
\left\{\begin{aligned}A+B &=1 \\ -A+2B &=-4\end{aligned}\right.
$$

Solving this system gives $A=2,B=-1.$

Therefore, the solution to the initial value problem is

$$
y=2{e}^{-x}-{e}^{2x}.
$$

$$
y=A{e}^{3x}+B{e}^{5x}.
$$

- Substituting $y(0)=2$ into the general solution gives

$$
A+B=2.
$$

- To apply the condition ${y}^{′}(0)=2,$ we first differentiate $y$ to get

$$
{y}^{′}=3A{e}^{3x}+5B{e}^{5x}.
$$

Then, we substitute ${y}^{′}(0)=2$ into the above to get

$$
3A+5B=2.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}+2\lambda +10 & =0 \\ ({\lambda }^{2}+2\lambda +1)^{2}-1+10 & =0 \\ (\lambda +1)^{2}+9 & =0 \\ (\lambda +1)^{2} & =-9 \\ \lambda +1 & =\pm 3\text{i} \\ \lambda  & =-1\pm 3\text{i}\end{aligned}

$$
