QUESTION 17592186078337 e-6587
Consider the equation $y''-3y'-4y = 0$ where $y=y(t).$ If $y = e^{\lambda t}$ is a solution, then what is $\lambda?$
SOLUTION
Assuming $y=e^{\lambda t}$ and differentiating $y$ with respect to $t$ gives

$$
y'= \lambda e^{\lambda t},\qquad y''= \lambda^2 e^{\lambda t}.
$$

Substituting the above into our differential equation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}{e}^{\lambda t}-3\lambda {e}^{\lambda t}-4{e}^{\lambda t} & =0 \\ {e}^{\lambda t}({\lambda }^{2}-3\lambda -4) & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have the following characteristic equation:

$$
\lambda^2 -  3\lambda - 4 = 0
$$

Factoring, we get

$$
(\lambda -4) (\lambda +1) = 0.
$$

Therefore, the roots of the characteristic equation are $\lambda = 4$ and $\lambda =  -1.$

QUESTION 17592186265781 q-129836
Consider the equation ${y}^{″}-{y}^{′}-2y=0$ where $y=y(x).$ If $y={e}^{\lambda x}$ is a solution, then $\lambda =$
SOLUTION
Assuming $y={e}^{\lambda x}$ and differentiating $y$ with respect to $x$ gives

$$
{y}^{′}=\lambda {e}^{\lambda x},\,{y}^{″}={\lambda }^{2}{e}^{\lambda x}.
$$

Substituting the above into our differential equation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}{e}^{\lambda x}-\lambda {e}^{\lambda x}-2{e}^{\lambda x} & =0 \\ {e}^{\lambda x}({\lambda }^{2}-\lambda -2) & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

The auxiliary equation is therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}-\lambda -2 & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

Factoring the auxiliary equation results in

$$
(\lambda +1)(\lambda -2)=0,
$$

and so the solutions of the auxiliary equation  are $\lambda =-1$ or $\lambda =2.$

$$

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}x} = \lambda e^{\lambda x}, \qquad \dfrac{\textrm{d}^2y}{\textrm{d}x^2} = \lambda^2 e^{\lambda x}.
$$

Substituting the above into our differential equation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}{e}^{\lambda x}+\lambda {e}^{\lambda x}-2{e}^{\lambda x} & =0 \\ {e}^{\lambda x}({\lambda }^{2}+\lambda -2) & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have the following characteristic equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}+\lambda -2 & =0 \\ (\lambda -1)(\lambda +2) & =0\end{aligned}
$$

The roots of the characteristic equation are $\lambda = 1$ and $\lambda = -2.$ Therefore, the general solution is

$$
y = Ae^{x} + Be^{- 2 x}.
$$

Given that $y = y(x)$ satisfies the equation $y''+7y'+6y = 0,$ which of the following could **not** be $y(x)?$

I. $-3e^{-6x}$

II. $2e^{-6x}-4e^{-x}$

III. $e^{-6x}+2e^{x}$
SOLUTION
This is a second-order homogeneous ODE. So, we assume the solutions take the form $y=e^{\lambda x}.$ Differentiating $y$ with respect to $x$ gives

$$
y'= \lambda e^{\lambda x},\qquad y''= \lambda^2 e^{\lambda x}.
$$

Substituting the above into our differential equation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}{e}^{\lambda x}+7\lambda {e}^{\lambda x}+6{e}^{\lambda x} & =0 \\ {e}^{\lambda x}({\lambda }^{2}+7\lambda +6) & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have the following characteristic equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}+7\lambda +6 & =0 \\ (\lambda +6)(\lambda +1) & =0\end{aligned}
$$

The roots of the characteristic equation are $\lambda = -6$ and $\lambda = - 1.$

Therefore, the general solution is

$$
y = Ae^{-6x} + Be^{- x}.
$$

Finally, we need to determine whether each of the given functions takes the form shown above.

- Function I takes the above form with $A=-3$ and $B=0.$ So, it is a solution to the given ODE.

- Function II takes the above form with $A=2$ and $B=-1.$ So, it is a solution to the given ODE.

- Function III does *not* take the above form. It contains $e^x,$ which is not included in the above form. So, it is *not* a solution to the given ODE.

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only".
