QUESTION 17592186087595 e-6595
Find the general solution to the equation

$$
\frac{\textrm{d}^2 y}{\textrm{d} x^2} + 25y =0.
$$
SOLUTION
Assuming $y=e^{\lambda x}$ and differentiating $y$ with respect to $x$ gives

$$
\frac{\textrm{d} y}{\textrm{d} x}  = \lambda e^{\lambda x}, \qquad \frac{\textrm{d}^2 y}{\textrm{d} x^2}  = \lambda^2 e^{\lambda x} .
$$

Substituting the above into our differential equation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}{e}^{\lambda x}+25{e}^{\lambda x} & =0 \\ {e}^{\lambda x}({\lambda }^{2}+25) & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

The corresponding characteristic equation is

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}+25 & =0 \\ {\lambda }^{2}+25 & =0 \\ {\lambda }^{2} & =-25 \\ \lambda  & =\pm 5\text{i}.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the general solution to the differential equation is

$$
y = P\cos{5x} + Q\sin{5x}.
$$

QUESTION 17592186088351 q-7816
Find the general solution to the equation

$$
\frac{{\text{d}}^{2}y}{\text{d}{x}^{2}}+16y=0.
$$
SOLUTION
Let $y={e}^{\lambda x}.$ Differentiating $y$ with respect to $x$ gives

$$
\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}=\lambda {e}^{\lambda x},\,\frac{{\text{d}}^{2}y}{\text{d}{x}^{2}}={\lambda }^{2}{e}^{\lambda x}.
$$

Substituting the above into our differential equation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}{e}^{\lambda x}+16{e}^{\lambda x} & =0 \\ {e}^{\lambda x}({\lambda }^{2}+16) & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

The auxiliary equation is therefore

$$
{\lambda }^{2}+16=0.
$$

 

The auxiliary equation has pure imaginary roots:

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}+16 & =0 \\ {\lambda }^{2} & =-16 \\ \lambda  & =\pm 4\text{i}\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the general solution to the differential equation is

$$
\begin{aligned}y=P\operatorname{cos}⁡4x+Q\operatorname{sin}⁡4x.\end{aligned}
$$

QUESTION 17592186265845 q-130153
Find the general solution to the equation

$$
\frac{{\text{d}}^{2}y}{\text{d}{x}^{2}}+11y=0.
$$
SOLUTION
Let $y={e}^{\lambda x}.$ Differentiating $y$ with respect to $x$ gives

$$
\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}=\lambda {e}^{\lambda x},\,\frac{{\text{d}}^{2}y}{\text{d}{x}^{2}}={\lambda }^{2}{e}^{\lambda x}.
$$

Substituting the above into our differential equation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}{e}^{\lambda x}+11{e}^{\lambda x} & =0 \\ {e}^{\lambda x}({\lambda }^{2}+11) & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

The auxiliary equation is therefore

$$
{\lambda }^{2}+11=0.
$$

 

The auxiliary equation has pure imaginary roots:

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}+11 & =0 \\ {\lambda }^{2} & =-11 \\ \lambda  & =\pm \sqrt{11}\,\text{i}\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the general solution to the differential equation is

$$
\begin{aligned}y=P\operatorname{cos}⁡(\sqrt{11}x)+Q\operatorname{sin}⁡(\sqrt{11}x).\end{aligned}

QUESTION 17592186087597 e-6597
Consider the equation $y'' + 10y' + 34y  = 0,$ where $y = y(t).$ If $y = e^{\lambda t}$ is a solution, then what is $\lambda?$
SOLUTION
Assuming $y = e^{\lambda t}$ and differentiating $y$ with respect to $t$ gives

$$
y' = \lambda e^{\lambda t}, \qquad y'' = \lambda^2 e^{\lambda t} .
$$

Substituting the above into our differential equation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}{e}^{\lambda t}+10\lambda {e}^{\lambda t}+34{e}^{\lambda t} & =0 \\ {e}^{\lambda t}({\lambda }^{2}+10\lambda +34) & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

The corresponding characteristic equation is

$$
\lambda^2 + 10\lambda + 34  = 0,
$$

and we can solve it by completing the square:

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}+10\lambda +34 & =0 \\ ({\lambda }^{2}+10\lambda +25)-25+34 & =0 \\ (\lambda +5)^{2}+9 & =0 \\ (\lambda +5)^{2} & =-9 \\ \lambda +5 & =\pm 3\text{i} \\ \lambda  & =-5\pm 3\text{i}\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,  $\lambda = - 5 \pm 3\textrm{i}$ are the complex roots of the auxiliary equation.

Find the general solution to the equation

$$
\frac{{\text{d}}^{2}y}{\text{d}{x}^{2}}+4\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}+20y=0.
$$
SOLUTION
Let $y={e}^{\lambda x}.$ Differentiating $y$ with respect to $x$ gives

$$
\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}=\lambda {e}^{\lambda x},\,\frac{{\text{d}}^{2}y}{\text{d}{x}^{2}}={\lambda }^{2}{e}^{\lambda x}.
$$

Substituting the above into our differential equation gives 

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}{e}^{\lambda x}+4\lambda {e}^{\lambda x}+20{e}^{\lambda x} & =0 \\ {e}^{\lambda x}({\lambda }^{2}+4\lambda +20) & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

The auxiliary equation is therefore

$$
{\lambda }^{2}+4\lambda +20=0.
$$

The auxiliary equation can be solved by completing the square:

$$
\begin{aligned}{\lambda }^{2}+4\lambda +20 & =0 \\ ({\lambda }^{2}+4\lambda +4)-4+20 & =0 \\ (\lambda +2)^{2}+16 & =0 \\ (\lambda +2)^{2} & =-16 \\ \lambda +2 & =\pm 4\text{i} \\ \lambda  & =-2\pm 4\text{i}\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the general solution to the differential equation is

$$
\begin{aligned}y & ={e}^{-2x}(P\operatorname{cos}⁡4x+Q\operatorname{sin}⁡4x).\end{aligned}
$$
