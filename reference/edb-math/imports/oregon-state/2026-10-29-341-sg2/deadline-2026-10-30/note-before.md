    type: text

id: q-4
content: Geometrically describe the span of $\mathbf u=(-2,3,2)^T$, $\mathbf w=(2,1,0)^T$, and $\mathbf p=(-2,5,3)^T$,
  using an equation of a line or plane, or another appropriate geometric description.
fields:
- key: work
  type: free
  correct:
    type: text
    value: The span is the plane through the origin $$\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf u,\mathbf w,\mathbf p\}=\{(x,y,z)^T\in\mathbb
      R^3:x-2y+4z=0\}.$$ Indeed, $\mathbf p=\frac32\mathbf u+\frac12\mathbf w$, so the third vector adds no new
      direction. The first two vectors are independent. Their cross product is $\mathbf u\times\mathbf w=(-2,4,-8)^T$,

      $s,t\in\mathbb R$.
feedback: A nonzero normal is orthogonal to all three vectors, so each lies in the stated plane. The first two independent
  vectors span a two-dimensional set, proving that their span equals the whole plane, rather than merely lying within
  it. The set is not a line, and the third vector does not make the span all of $\mathbb R^3$.
