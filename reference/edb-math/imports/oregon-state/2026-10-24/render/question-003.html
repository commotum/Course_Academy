Reading definition (unnumbered source item question_59459086, immediately before Question 2): Let v→1,…,v→k and u→ be column vectors. The vector u→ is a linear combination of {v→1,…,v→k} if scalars c1,…,ck exist such that

u→=∑i=1kciv→i.

Write in your own words what the span of a set of vectors is. Is there a geometric way that you think about spans?

Answer: answer · reference answer:

The span is every vector I can reach by scaling the given vectors and adding them together, allowing any real coefficients. In symbols,

span{v→1,…,v→k}={∑i=1kciv→i:c1,…,ck∈R}.

Geometrically, one nonzero vector spans a line through the origin. Two nonparallel vectors span a plane through the origin. Three linearly independent vectors in R3 span all of R3. A set consisting only of the zero vector spans just the origin.

A span contains all linear combinations, rather than just the original vectors or one particular sum. The coefficients may be positive, negative, or zero, so a single nonzero vector gives the entire line through the origin, rather than only a ray or segment. With more vectors, the geometric size depends on how many independent directions they supply, rather than just how many vectors are listed.
