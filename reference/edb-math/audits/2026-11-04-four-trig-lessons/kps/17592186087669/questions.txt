SOLUTION Use the difference of squares, then the Pythagorean identity.  $$ \begin{aligned} &12\cos^{4}\left(\frac{\pi}{31}\right)-12\sin^{4}\left(\frac{\pi}{31}\right) \\ &=12\left(\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{31}\right)-\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{31}\right)\right) \\ &\qquad\cdot\left(\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{31}\right)+\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{31}\right)\right) \end{aligned} $$  Since $\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{31}\right)+\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{31}\right)=1$, this reduces to  $$ 12\left(\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{31}\right)-\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{31}\right)\right). $$  Recall the **double-angle formula for cosine**:  $$ \cos^2 x-\sin^2 x=\cos(2x). $$  Substitute $x=\frac{\pi}{31}$ and double the angle:  $$ \begin{aligned} &12\left(\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{31}\right)-\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{31}\right)\right) \\ &=12\cos\left(2\cdot \frac{\pi}{31}\right) \\ &=12\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{31}\right). \end{aligned} $$  Only the angle doubles; the common coefficient stays outside the cosine.
