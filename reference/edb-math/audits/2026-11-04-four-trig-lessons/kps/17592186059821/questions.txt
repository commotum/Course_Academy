SOLUTION Use $\cos(u-v)=\cos u\cos v+\sin u\sin v$ with $u=x$ and $v=60^\circ$.  $$ \begin{aligned} \cos(x-60^\circ)&=\cos x\cos 60^\circ+\sin x\sin 60^\circ\\ &=\cos x\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)+\sin x\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)\\ &=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sin x+\frac{1}{2}\cos x \end{aligned} $$  Use $\cos(u+v)=\cos u\cos v-\sin u\sin v$ with $u=x$ and $v=240^\circ$.  $$ \begin{aligned} \cos(x+240^\circ)&=\cos x\cos 240^\circ-\sin x\sin 240^\circ\\ &=\cos x\left(- \frac{1}{2}\right)-\sin x\left(- \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)\\ &=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sin x-\frac{1}{2}\cos x \end{aligned} $$  Distribute the outside factors, then collect the sine and cosine terms. Let $E$ denote the original expression.  $$ \begin{aligned} E&=2\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sin x+\frac{1}{2}\cos x\right)\\ &\quad +2\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sin x-\frac{1}{2}\cos x\right)\\ &=\left(2\cdot\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)+2\cdot\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)\right)\sin x\\ &\quad+\left(2\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)+2\cdot\left(- \frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\cos x\\ &=2 \sqrt{3}\sin x \end{aligned} $$  The first blank is $2 \sqrt{3}$ and the second is $0$.
