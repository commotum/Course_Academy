## Why these additions and exclusions

- Keep concrete column-space, null-space, basis, dimension and coordinate lessons: those concepts are genuinely unstudied, and the early questions make the later combined lessons easier. A later repeated definition is not sufficient reason to remove its instructional foundation.
- Add geometric subspaces, null-space basis/dimension, matrix and transformation rank-nullity, kernel, and transformation IMT. These are actual skills in 4.10.4/4.10.5/5.7 or useful foundations of retained lessons.
- Add reflections, rotations, composition and inversion for textbook 5.3–5.4. Add shear/stretch because the full retained composition lesson actually tests those matrices, even though the named textbook 5.4 focuses on rotations/reflections.
- Skip the whole Orthogonal Matrices lesson for WHW 4’s determinant proof: the problem gives AᵀA=I, and retained determinant transpose/product laws give det(A)^2=1. The whole orthogonal lesson adds unrelated inverse-system and conceptual eigenvalue work. No eigenvalue/characteristic-polynomial/quadratic-solving chain is justified here.
- Skip abstract-domain subspaces/axiom lessons: the actual exam tasks and textbook 5.7 use subspaces of finite-dimensional real coordinate spaces. Polynomial/function/matrix-space closure practice would add overhead. This does not mean abstract notation or general proofs are unimportant.
- Skip unstudied Dot Product Using Components: the already studied Vectors in N-Dimensional Euclidean Space teaches and practices both direct component dot products and unknown-coordinate orthogonality. Keep the distinct dot-linearity lesson because numeric component mastery does not establish proof fluency.
- Skip both general set-proof lessons. The replacement is mandatory complete original course proof practice, below; proof proficiency has not been inferred from numeric mastery.
