## Lesson content

### tutorial 17592186111919 — Introduction

Many quadratic equations have two solutions. However, it's also possible for a quadratic equation to have only one solution or no real solutions at all.

For example, consider the following quadratic equation:

$$
(3x-6)^2=0
$$

We can solve this equation using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}(3x-6)^{2} & =0 \\ \sqrt{(3x-6)^{2}} & =\sqrt{0} \\ |3x-6| & =0\end{aligned}
$$

Normally, at this stage, we would obtain two solutions by setting the inside of the absolute value equal to positive or negative $(\pm)$ the right-hand side. However, in this case, the right-hand side is zero, and $+0$ and $-0$ are both just $0.$ 

So, we have only one solution:

$$
\begin{aligned}|3x-6| & =0 \\ 3x-6 & =0 \\ 3x & =6 \\ x & =2\end{aligned}
$$



### example 17592186110346 — Cases When the Perfect Square is Isolated

Solve $-2(3k-12)^2 = 0.$

We can solve this equation using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}-2(3k-12)^{2} & =0 \\ (3k-12)^{2} & =0 \\ \sqrt{(3k-12)^{2}} & =\sqrt{0} \\ |3k-12| & =0 \\ 3k-12 & =0 \\ 3k & =12 \\ k & =4\end{aligned}
$$

### example 17592186110348 — Finding an Unknown Parameter

Which value of $k$ gives precisely one solution to the equation $(x+3)^2+10=k?$

First, we solve for $x$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}(x+3)^{2}+10 & =k \\ (x+3)^{2} & =k-10 \\ \sqrt{(x+3)^{2}} & =\sqrt{k-10} \\ |x+3| & =\sqrt{k-10}\end{aligned}
$$

The equation has only one solution if the right-hand side is equal to zero. So, we solve for the value of $k$ that makes the right-hand side equal to zero:

$$
\begin{aligned}\sqrt{k-10} & =0 \\ k-10 & =0 \\ k & =10\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $k=10$ will give only one solution to the equation.

### example 17592186110349 — Factoring a Perfect Square

Given that $g^2 -18g + 81 = 0$, what are the values of $g?$

First, notice that the left-hand side is a perfect square. It just needs to be factored:

$$
\begin{aligned}{g}^{2}-18g+81 & =0 \\ {g}^{2}-2\cdot 9\cdot g+{9}^{2} & =0 \\ (g-9)^{2} & =0\end{aligned}
$$

From here, we solve the equation using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}\sqrt{(g-9)^{2}} & =\sqrt{0} \\ |g-9| & =0 \\ g-9 & =0 \\ g & =9\end{aligned}
$$

### tutorial 17592186111920 — Perfect Square Quadratic Equations With No Real Solutions

Let's try and solve the equation $(x+1)^2=-1$ using the square root method.

$$
\begin{aligned}(x+1)^{2} & =-1 \\ \sqrt{(x+1)^{2}} & =\sqrt{-1} \\ |x+1| & =\sqrt{-1}\end{aligned}
$$

We have a problem here. We cannot take the square root of a negative number. For that reason, the equation above has *no real solutions*.



### example 17592186110350 — Equations With No Real Solutions

Consider the equation $(4x+1)^2 +2=3k.$ For which values of $k$ does this equation have no real solutions?

Let's try and solve using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}(4x+1)^{2}+2 & =3k \\ (4x+1)^{2} & =3k-2 \\ \sqrt{(4x+1)^{2}} & =\sqrt{3k-2} \\ |4x+1| & =\sqrt{3k-2}\end{aligned}
$$

The equation will have no real solutions if the expression under the square root is negative, which gives the following condition:

$$
3k-2 < 0
$$

We solve this inequality for $k\mathbin{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}3k-2 & <0 \\ 3k & <2 \\ k & <\frac{2}{3}\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, any value of $k$ that is less than $\dfrac 2 3$ will give an equation with no real solutions.

$$
\begin{aligned}{x}^{2}+q+3 & =7 \\ {x}^{2} & =7-q-3 \\ {x}^{2} & =4-q \\ \sqrt{{x}^{2}} & =\sqrt{4-q} \\ |x| & =\sqrt{4-q}\end{aligned}
$$

The equation has only one solution if the right-hand side is equal to zero, and the right-hand side is zero only if the expression underneath the radical is zero. So, we solve for the value of $q$ that makes the expression under the radical equal to zero:

$$
\begin{aligned}4-q & =0 \\ q & =4\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $q=4$ will give only one solution to the equation.
