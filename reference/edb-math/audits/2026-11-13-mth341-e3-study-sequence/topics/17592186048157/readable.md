## Lesson content

### tutorial 17592186069945 — Introduction

Suppose we have a quadratic equation like
$x^2-25 = 0$.
We can solve this equation using the **difference of squares** formula, shown below:

$$
{\color{red}a}^2 - {\color{blue}b}^2 = ({\color{red}a}+{\color{blue}b})({\color{red}a}-{\color{blue}b})
$$

Using the difference of squares formula, we can factor the left-hand side of our quadratic equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} - 25 &= 0 \\
x^{2} - 5^{2} &= 0 \\
(x + 5)(x - 5) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we apply the zero product rule to get

$$
x+5=0 \qquad\text{or}\qquad x-5=0
$$

which in turn gives

$$
x=-5 \qquad\text{or}\qquad x=5
$$

Therefore, the solutions are $x=-5,5$. We can write this even more concisely as $x=\pm 5$.

**Note:** We could have solved the equation $x^2-25=0$ using the square root method. However, the factoring method introduced in this lesson will become very useful later when we move on to more complicated quadratic equations. Make sure that you learn it!



### example 17592186068484 — Solving Perfect Square Quadratic Equations

Use the difference of squares method to solve the equation $a^2 - 100 = 0$.

Using the difference of squares formula, we factor the left-hand side of our quadratic equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^{2} - 100 &= 0 \\
(a)^{2} - (10)^{2} &= 0 \\
(a - 10)(a + 10) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Applying the zero product rule, we have

$$
a-10=0 \qquad\text{or}\qquad a+10=0
$$

which in turn gives

$$
a=10 \qquad\text{or}\qquad a=-10
$$

Therefore, the solutions are

$$
a = -10, 10
$$

We can write this even more concisely as $a=\pm 10$.

### example 17592186068485 — Solving Perfect Square Quadratic Equations With Leading Coefficients

Solve the equation $4z^2 - 25 = 0$.

Using the difference of squares formula, we factor the left-hand side of our quadratic equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4z^{2} - 25 &= 0 \\
(2z)^{2} - (5)^{2} &= 0 \\
(2z - 5)(2z + 5) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Applying the zero product rule, we have

$$
2z-5=0 \qquad\text{or}\qquad 2z+5=0
$$

Then, we solve each equation separately:

$$
\begin{aligned}2z-5 & =0 & \hspace{2em} & \hspace{2em} & 2z+5 & =0 \\ 2z & =5 & \hspace{2em} & \hspace{2em} & 2z & =-5 \\ z & =\frac{5}{2} & \hspace{2em} & \hspace{2em} & z & =-\frac{5}{2}\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solutions are

$$
z = -\dfrac 5 2, \dfrac 5 2
$$

We can write this even more concisely as

$$
z=\pm \dfrac 5 2
$$

### example 17592186068488 — Solving Perfect Square Quadratic Equations by Reorganizing and Factoring

For which values of $t$ is $-2t^2 -16 = -5 -3t^2$?

First, we move all of the terms to the left-hand side and collect the like terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2t^{2} - 16 &= -5 - 3t^{2} \\
(-2t^{2} + 3t^{2}) + (-16 + 5) &= 0 \\
t^{2} - 11 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, using the difference of squares formula, we factor the left-hand side of our quadratic equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
t^{2} - 11 &= 0 \\
t^{2} - (\sqrt{11})^{2} &= 0 \\
(t - \sqrt{11})(t + \sqrt{11}) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Applying the zero product rule, we have

$$
t-\sqrt{11}=0 \qquad\text{or}\qquad t+\sqrt{11}=0
$$

which in turn gives

$$
t=\sqrt{11} \qquad\text{or}\qquad t=-\sqrt{11}
$$

Therefore, the solutions are

$$
t = -\sqrt{11}, \sqrt{11}
$$

We can write this even more concisely as

$$
t=\pm \sqrt{11}
$$
