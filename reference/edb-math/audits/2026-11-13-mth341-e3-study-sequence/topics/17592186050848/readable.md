### tutorial 17592186185313 — Introduction

When we take a power of a trigonometric function, we write the power after the function but before the argument. 

For instance $\sin^2 30^\circ$ is the same as $(\sin30^\circ)^2.$ Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}{\sin }^{2}⁡{30}^{∘} & =(\operatorname{sin}⁡{30}^{∘})^{2} \\  & ={\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}^{2} \\  & =\frac{1}{4}.\end{aligned}
$$

Similarly, $\tan^4 \left(\dfrac\pi 3\right)$ is the same as $\left(\tan \left(\dfrac\pi 3\right)\right)^4.$ Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}{\tan }^{4}⁡\left(\frac{\pi }{3}\right) & ={\left(\operatorname{tan}⁡\left(\frac{\pi }{3}\right)\right)}^{4} \\  & ={(\sqrt{3})}^{4} \\  & ={(\sqrt{3})}^{2}\cdot {(\sqrt{3})}^{2} \\  & =3\cdot 3 \\  & =9.\end{aligned}
$$



### example 17592186184051 — Evaluating an Expression Involving Special Angles in Degrees

Evaluate $\sin^2\left(150^\circ\right) + 2\csc^2\left(240^\circ\right)$.

Let's remind ourselves of the special trigonometric ratios, as given by the unit circle.

![](images/d9/d93c490adbb5cdbc0e9a09b60eea9b82ab93be8e871e334423abc17ddd62955e.png)

- Let's first consider the angle $150^\circ$. From the unit circle, we see that the angle $150^\circ$ corresponds to the point $$
\displaystyle \left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2} \right).
$$ Since the sine of the angle corresponds to the $y$-coordinate, we have $\sin 150^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
- Now, let's consider the angle $240^\circ$. From the unit circle, we see that the angle $240^\circ$ corresponds to the point $$
\displaystyle \left(-\dfrac{1}{2}, -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right).
$$ Since the sine of the angle corresponds to the $y$-coordinate, we have $\sin 240^\circ = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

Using the fact that

$$
\csc{\theta} = \dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\csc 240^{∘} &= \frac{1}{\sin 240^{∘}} \\
&= (1)/\left(\left(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)\right) \\
&=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we combine the results:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin^{2} (150^{∘}) + 2\csc^{2} (240^{∘}) &= \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2} + 2\left(-\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{4} + 2\left(\frac{4}{3}\right) \\
&= \frac{1}{4} + \frac{8}{3} \\
&= \frac{35}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

### example 17592186184106 — Evaluating an Expression Involving Special Angles in Radians

Evaluate $$
\displaystyle 4\cos^2 \left(\dfrac \pi 6\right) - \sin \left(\dfrac \pi 4\right).
$$

Let's remind ourselves of the special trigonometric ratios, as given by the unit circle.

![](images/db/dbf36192ac332dc49ed75278838e989a37ac7d888311be1b8b059a7b59667aa6.png)

- Let's first consider the angle $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. From the unit circle, we see that the angle $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ corresponds to the point $$
\displaystyle \left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2} \right).
$$ Since the cosine of the angle corresponds to the $x$-coordinate, we have $$
\displaystyle \cos\left(\dfrac {\pi} {6}\right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.
$$
- Now, let's consider the angle $\dfrac \pi 4$. From the unit circle, we see that the angle $\dfrac \pi 4$ corresponds to the point $$
\displaystyle \left(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right).
$$ Since the sine of the angle corresponds to the $y$-coordinate, we have $$
\displaystyle \sin\left(\dfrac {\pi} {4}\right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}.
$$

Finally, we combine the results:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4\cos^{2} \left(\frac{π}{6}\right) - \sin \left(\frac{π}{4}\right) &= 4\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^{2} - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \\
&= 3 - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
