### tutorial 17592186070019 — Introduction

When an equation contains an $x^2$ term and a constant, it can be solved using the **square root method**. The square root method makes use of the fact that

$$
\sqrt{x^2} = \vert x \vert \,.
$$

To illustrate, we can solve the equation $2x^2-18=0$ using the following three steps:

1\. Isolate the $x^2$ on one side of the equation:

  $$
\begin{aligned}2{x}^{2}-18 & =0 \\ 2{x}^{2} & =18 \\ {x}^{2} & =9\end{aligned}
$$

2\. Take the square root of both sides of the equation

  $$
\begin{aligned}\sqrt{{x}^{2}} & =\sqrt{9} \\ |x| & =3\end{aligned}
$$

3\. Solve the resulting absolute value equation:

  $$
\begin{aligned}x & =3\,\text{or}\,x=-3\end{aligned}
$$

So, there are two solutions, $x=3$ and $x=-3.$ We can write this more concisely as $x= \pm 3,$ where the "$\pm$" symbol stands for "plus or minus."



### example 17592186068475 — Solving Equations With Integer Solutions

Solve $2x^2 - 27=5.$

First, we isolate the $x^2$ on one side of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}2{x}^{2}-27 & =5 \\ 2{x}^{2} & =32 \\ {x}^{2} & =16\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we take the square root of both sides of the equation and solve the resulting absolute value equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}\sqrt{{x}^{2}} & =\sqrt{16} \\ |x| & =4 \\ x & =\pm 4\end{aligned}
$$

### example 17592186068476 — Solving Equations With Fractional Solutions

Solve for $p$ where $4p^2 - 11 = 14.$

First, we isolate the $p^2$ on one side of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}4{p}^{2}-11 & =14 \\ 4{p}^{2} & =25 \\ {p}^{2} & =\frac{25}{4}\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we take the square root of both sides of the equation and solve the resulting absolute value equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}\sqrt{{p}^{2}} & =\sqrt{\frac{25}{4}} \\ |p| & =\frac{\sqrt{25}}{\sqrt{4}} \\ |p| & =\frac{5}{2} \\ p & =\pm \frac{5}{2}\end{aligned}
$$

### example 17592186068477 — Solving Equations With Radical Solutions

Find $t$ if $2t^2 = 36.$

First, we isolate the $t^2$ on one side of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
		2t^2 &= 36\\
		t^2 &= 18
	\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we take the square root of both sides of the equation and solve the resulting absolute value equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}		
		\sqrt{t^2} &= \sqrt{18} \\
		\vert t \vert &= \sqrt{18} \\
		\vert t \vert &= \sqrt{9 \cdot 2} \\
		\vert t \vert &= \sqrt{9} \cdot \sqrt{2} \\
		\vert t \vert &= 3\sqrt{2} \\
		t &= \pm 3\sqrt{2}
	\end{aligned}
$$
