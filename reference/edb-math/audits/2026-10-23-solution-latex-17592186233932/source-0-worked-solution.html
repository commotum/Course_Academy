First, the slope of $y = f(x)$ at $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ is given by $f'\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right).$

We will calculate $f'(x)$ using the chain rule. We express $f(x)$ as the composite function \[ f = \sec{u}, \qquad u = \pi x^{2}. \]

Differentiating gives \[ \dfrac{\textrm d f}{\textrm d u} = \sec{u}\tan{u}, \qquad\dfrac{\textrm d u}{\textrm d x} = 2\pi x. \]

We now apply the chain rule, and get \begin{align*} \dfrac{\textrm d f}{\textrm d x} & = \dfrac{\textrm d f}{\textrm d u} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm d u}{\textrm d x}\\[5pt] &= \sec{u}\tan{u} \cdot 2\pi x \\[5pt] &= 2\pi x \sec{\left(\pi x^{2}\right)}\tan{\left(\pi x^{2}\right)}. \end{align*}

Evaluating at $x=\dfrac{1}{2}$, we find \begin{eqnarray} \eqalign{ f'\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right) &= 2\pi \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}\cdot \sec{\left[\pi\cdot\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\right]}\tan{\left[\pi\cdot\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\right]} \\[5pt] &= \pi \sec{\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)} \tan{\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)} \\[5pt] &= \pi \cdot \sqrt{2}\cdot 1 \\[5pt] &= \pi\sqrt{2}.} \end{eqnarray}

Therefore, the slope of $y = f(x)$ at $x=\dfrac{1}{2}$ is $\bbox[4pt, border: 1px solid lightgray]{\pi\sqrt{2}}.$
