ENTITY 17592186096451 :question/problem
Consider the picture below. Given that $\mathbf{m}=\lambda\mathbf{n}+\mu\mathbf{k}$, find $\lambda$ and $\mu$.

![](images/b8/b8abf22d14f93a38c4d01f368b8171e4ccfe90086d932f5b58453baa46391405.png)

ENTITY 17592186096451 :question/worked-solution
![](images/76/76b9867a7c1ef484922241ebe44272f0104e406c31310bf36227a13850f50d8b.png)

In $\bigtriangleup ONM$ shown above, we have:

- $ON=2|\mathbf{n}|$;

- $\overrightarrow{ON}$ and $\mathbf{n}$ have the same direction

Hence, $\overrightarrow{ON}=2\mathbf{n}$.

Similarly:

- $NM=2|\mathbf{k}|$

- $\overrightarrow{NM}$ and $\mathbf{k}$ have the same direction

Hence, $\overrightarrow{NM}=2\mathbf{k}$.

Additionally, 

$$
\begin{aligned}m & =\overset{\to }{ON}+\overset{\to }{NM} \\  & =2n+2k.\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently, $\mathbf{m}=\lambda\mathbf{n}+\mu\mathbf{k}=2\mathbf{n}+2\mathbf{k}$. 

Since $\mathbf{n} \nparallel \mathbf{k},$ the linear combination can only be written in one way. Equating the coefficients of $\mathbf{n}$ and $\mathbf{k}$, we get:

- $\lambda=2$ and $\mu=2$

ENTITY 17592186096452 :question/problem
Consider the following diagram, where $\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf{a},$ $\overrightarrow{OB}=\mathbf{b},$ and $X$ divides $AB$ in the ratio $3:1.$ Find $\overrightarrow{OX}.$

![](images/67/678ea2936b8a5b1ded74cb8a0875c629e8be0f7002c98492ebf2e8109fc603ef.png)

ENTITY 17592186096452 :question/worked-solution
From the diagram, we see that

$$
\overrightarrow{OX} = \overrightarrow{OA}+\overrightarrow{AX}.
$$

We're told that $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a},$ so we just need to work out $\overrightarrow{AX}.$

First, from $\triangle OAB,$ we have

$$
\begin{aligned}\overset{\to }{AB} & =\overset{\to }{AO}+\overset{\to }{OB} \\  & =-\overset{\to }{OA}+\overset{\to }{OB} \\  & =-a+b \\  & =b-a.\end{aligned}
$$

Since $AX:XB=3:1,$ we have

$$
\overrightarrow{AX}=\dfrac{3}{4}\overrightarrow{AB}=\dfrac{3}{4}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}).
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}\overset{\to }{OX} & =\overset{\to }{OA}+\overset{\to }{AX} \\  & =a+\frac{3}{4}(b-a) \\  & =\frac{1}{4}a+\frac{3}{4}b.\end{aligned}
$$

Consider the picture below.

![](images/f2/f2518c2e998e7c7f551548a4cc7283720a66428ea8fa9872494a81cc3848ba9e.png)

Given that $\mathbf{c} = \lambda  \mathbf{a} + 2 \mathbf{b}$, find $\lambda.$

ENTITY 17592186096495 :question/worked-solution
Recall that when two known vectors are not parallel, a linear combination of them is unique. So, once we rewrite $\mathbf c$ in terms of $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b,$ we can match coefficients to determine the unknown scalar.

To find $\lambda,$ we want to match the expression
$$
\mathbf{c} = \lambda  \mathbf{a} + 2 \mathbf{b} .
$$

We are given that one of the components is $2 \mathbf{b} .$ So, we first draw the vector $2 \mathbf{b}$ from the origin. Then we complete the path to the point $C.$

![](images/ea/ea517ac4ca8e0bcf7ba48f23e66f100323d5485f1221219aacfbea9148f4a1af.png)

Note that in $△ O D C$, we have:

- $D C = 2 | \mathbf{a} |$

- $\overrightarrow{D C}$ and $\mathbf a$ have the same direction

Hence, $\overrightarrow{D C} = 2 \mathbf{a} .$

Additionally,
$$
\begin{array}{cc}\mathbf{c} &  = \overrightarrow{O D} + \overrightarrow{D C} \\  &  = 2 \mathbf{b} + 2 \mathbf{a} .\end{array}
$$

Consequently, $\mathbf{c} = \lambda  \mathbf{a} + 2 \mathbf{b} = 2 \mathbf{b} + 2 \mathbf{a} .$

Since $\mathbf{a} \nparallel \mathbf{b},$ the linear combination can only be written in one way. Equating the coefficients of $\mathbf a,$ we get:
$$
\lambda=2
$$

ENTITY 17592186097837 :tutorial/content
Given a fixed **origin** in the plane (often denoted $O$), and another point $X,$ the vector $\overrightarrow{OX}$ is called the **position vector** of the point $X.$

So, the position vector tells us how to move from the origin to the point.

For example, suppose $\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{A X} = \mathbf{b} .$ How can we find the position vector $\overrightarrow{OX}$ in terms of $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf{b}?$

![](images/e6/e61003fb62534b04cfbbac07a64e61f6c16e2d464eb35bc5b98654b0603b24db.png)

From the diagram, we can trace a path from $O$ to $X$ by first moving from $O$ to $A,$ and then from $A$ to $X.$ Therefore,
$$
\overrightarrow{OX} = \overrightarrow{OA}+\overrightarrow{AX}.
$$

Since $\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{A X} = \mathbf{b} ,$ we get
$$
\overrightarrow{O X} = \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} .
$$

So, to find a position vector, we can trace a convenient path from the origin to the point and add the vectors along that path.

ENTITY 17592186234976 :question/worked-solution
To find $\lambda,$ we want to match the expression
$$
\mathbf{c} = \lambda  \mathbf{a} - 2 \mathbf{b} .
$$

We are given that one of the components is $- 2 \mathbf{b} .$ So, we first draw the vector $- 2 \mathbf{b}$ from the origin. Then we complete the path to the point $C.$

![](images/65/65a6b5743ab9b6ef820744d0d6601b7dfd8de08a3f895661d96d2fe2cab8e0a7.png)

Let $P$ be the endpoint of the vector $- 2 \mathbf{b} .$ Then

$$
\mathbf{c} = \overrightarrow{O P} + \overrightarrow{P C} .
$$

In $△ O P C ,$ we have:

- $O P = 2 | \mathbf{b} |$

- $\overrightarrow{OP}$ and $\mathbf b$ have opposite directions

Hence,
$$
\overrightarrow{O P} = - 2 \mathbf{b} .
$$

Similarly,

- $P C = | \mathbf{a} |$

- $\overrightarrow{P C}$ and $\mathbf a$ have opposite directions

Hence,
$$
\overrightarrow{P C} = - \mathbf{a} .
$$

Additionally,

$$
\begin{array}{cc}\mathbf{c} &  = \overrightarrow{O P} + \overrightarrow{P C} \\  &  = - 2 \mathbf{b} + ( - \mathbf{a} ) \\  &  = - \mathbf{a} - 2 \mathbf{b} .\end{array}
$$

Consequently,
$$
\mathbf{c} = \lambda  \mathbf{a} - 2 \mathbf{b} = - \mathbf{a} - 2 \mathbf{b} .
$$

Since $\mathbf{a} \nparallel \mathbf{b},$ the linear combination can only be written in one way. Equating the coefficients of $\mathbf a,$ we get

$$
\lambda=-1.
$$

$$

$$

Let $O$ be the origin, and let $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf c$ end at points $A$ and $C,$ as shown in the diagram below.

Since one term in the vector sum is $2 \mathbf{b} ,$ we first draw the vector $2 \mathbf{b}$ from the origin. Then we complete the path to the point $C.$

![](images/bc/bc976b12b4610c69953545d95662bf5535ef2a92eb9d9cbd63e087c85f3b98e7.png)

$$
\mathbf{c} = \overrightarrow{O P} + \overrightarrow{P C} .
$$

From the construction, we have

$$
\overrightarrow{O P} = 2 \mathbf{b} .
$$

Also, the vector $\overrightarrow{P C}$ has length $2 | \mathbf{a} | ,$ but points in the opposite direction to $\mathbf a.$ Therefore,

$$
\overrightarrow{P C} = - 2 \mathbf{a} .
$$

$$

$$

$$
\mathbf{c} = \lambda  \mathbf{a} + 2 \mathbf{b} = - 2 \mathbf{a} + 2 \mathbf{b} .
$$

Since $\mathbf{a} \nparallel \mathbf{b},$ the linear combination can only be written in one way. Equating the coefficients of $\mathbf a,$ we get

$$
\lambda=-2.

Suppose we have two vectors $\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB}=\mathbf{b},$ and a parallelogram $OAXB_1,$ where $\overrightarrow{O {B}_{1}} = 2 \overrightarrow{O B} .$ How can we find the position vector $\mathbf{x} = \overrightarrow{O X}$ in terms of $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf{b}?$

Given that $\mathbf{c}=\lambda\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}$, find $\lambda$.

ENTITY 17592186735395 :question/worked-solution
Construct the known component $\mathbf{b}$ from $O$, ending at $B$. Count signed horizontal and vertical grid displacements:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{OB} &=1\,(-5, 0)=(-5, 0). \\
\overrightarrow{BC} &=(0, -7).
\end{aligned}
$$

![](images/ea/ea6e3ab1951e7aebfcce1c3ed7a26696966ba3bb0a7bb8be86e888e062e3e94e.png)

Compare the remaining displacement with $\mathbf{a}$, whose grid displacement is $(0, -2)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Vertical scale} &=\frac{-7}{-2}=\frac{7}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The scale is positive, so the remaining vector has the same direction as $\mathbf{a}$. Thus $\overrightarrow{BC}=\frac{7}{2}\mathbf{a}$. By the triangle law,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{c} &=\overrightarrow{OB}+\overrightarrow{BC} \\
&=\mathbf{b}+\frac{7}{2}\mathbf{a} \\
&=\frac{7}{2}\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The vectors $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are not parallel, so their coefficients are unique. Matching the coefficient of $\mathbf{a}$ gives $\lambda=\frac{7}{2}$.

Given $\mathbf{c}=\lambda\mathbf{a}+2\mathbf{b}$, find $\lambda$.

ENTITY 17592186735569 :question/worked-solution
First trace $2\mathbf{b}$ from the common origin $O$ to a point $P$: keep its direction and scale its length. The remaining path from $P$ to the endpoint $C$ must be the unknown multiple of $\mathbf{a}$.

![](images/54/549775db5d66d443394d386f4d1918cab7f5ba9e121baac177be4c7778ef48e1.png)

Following the grid direction of $\mathbf{a}$ takes $3$ steps, while the parallel path $PC$ has $3$ steps. Their length ratio is $\frac{3}{3}=1$. The remaining path points in the opposite direction to $\mathbf{a}$, so
$$
\overrightarrow{PC}=-\mathbf{a}.
$$
Adding the path vectors gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{c}&=\overrightarrow{OP}+\overrightarrow{PC}\\
&=2\mathbf{b}+(-\mathbf{a})\\
&=-\mathbf{a}+2\mathbf{b}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Because $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are nonparallel, these coefficients are unique. Comparing with $\mathbf{c}=\lambda\mathbf{a}+2\mathbf{b}$ gives $\lambda=-1$. The minus sign records the reversed direction.
