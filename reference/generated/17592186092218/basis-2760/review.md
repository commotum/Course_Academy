We notice that the point $P$ is described using polar coordinates $(r,\theta )$, where

$$
r=1,\,\theta ={315}^{∘}.
$$

To convert from polar coordinates to Cartesian coordinates, we use

$$
x=r\operatorname{cos}⁡\theta ,\,y=r\operatorname{sin}⁡\theta .
$$

First, we calculate the $x$-coordinate of $P:$

$$
\begin{aligned}x & =r\operatorname{cos}⁡\theta  \\  & =1\cdot \operatorname{cos}⁡({315}^{∘}) \\  & =1\cdot (\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}) \\  & =\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we calculate the $y$-coordinate of $P:$

$$
\begin{aligned}y & =r\operatorname{sin}⁡\theta  \\  & =1\cdot \operatorname{sin}⁡({315}^{∘}) \\  & =1\cdot (-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}) \\  & =-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the Cartesian coordinates of $P$ are $(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}).$
