Find the general solution to the differential equation (in implicit form) $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}=\frac{3 x^{2} - 1}{y^{2}}.$ Work on an interval where $y\ne 0.$

- $\frac{y^{3}}{3}=x^{3} - x+C$
- $\frac{y^{3}}{3}=x^{3}+C$
- $y^{2}=x^{3} - x+C$
- $\frac{y^{3}}{3}=- x^{3} + x+C$
- $\frac{y^{3}}{3}=2 x^{3} - 2 x+C$

To find the general solution, we first separate the variables: 

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} & =\frac{3 x^{2} - 1}{y^{2}} \\ (y^{2})\,\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} & =3 x^{2} - 1\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we integrate both sides with respect to $x:$

$$
\begin{aligned}\int (y^{2})\,\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}\,\text{d}x & =\int (3 x^{2} - 1)\,\text{d}x \\ \int (y^{2})\,\frac{\text{d}y}{\cancel{\text{d}x}}\,\cancel{\text{d}x} & =\int (3 x^{2} - 1)\,\text{d}x \\ \int (y^{2})\,\text{d}y & =\int (3 x^{2} - 1)\,\text{d}x \\ \int y^2\,\text{d}y & =\int 3x^2\,\text{d}x-\int 1\,\text{d}x \\ \frac{y^3}{3} & =\frac{3x^3}{3}-1x+C\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the general solution to the differential equation (in implicit form) is

$$
\frac{y^{3}}{3}=x^{3} - x+C.
$$
