These are authored question difficulty bands, not topic difficulty, score weights, or XP weights. Our engine assembles each assessment as a fixed set, so the lesson/review initial-versus-remedial split is not a useful way to classify its assessment questions.

The mix is a baseline rather than a universal MA policy. Automated captures account for 441 of the 556 rated questions; the human-only sample is smaller. Eligibility and which difficulty bands exist in a topic's bank can change the delivered proportions. The engine renormalizes the positive weights over available rated bands: with only easy and moderate questions, 50/40/10 becomes about 55.6/44.4/0. It falls back to unrated questions only if no positively weighted rated band is available.

MA currently describes adjusting the next quiz's difficulty toward about 80% accuracy; the saved book FAQ calls 80–85% a useful range. This means the observed pooled mix cannot establish one exact weight vector for every learner. Neither source discloses a numerical adaptation function. Published selection versus our engine

MA describes random selection from previously learned topics, preference for the enrolled course, and reduced priority for previously assessed or encompassed topics. The FAQ explains that the pool includes older learned material once sufficient practice supports an expectation of automaticity. It does not disclose a fixed waiting period, required review count, or numerical topic-selection weights. Official algorithm description; saved practice FAQ.

The implementation is visible in retrieval.rs, schedule/history/bank and question_selection.rs, weights/select. New assessments require 150 positive finalized practice XP, automatic mode, and no pending assessment. Topic readiness relies on locally timestamped started and completed task history; incomplete imported history does not establish a mastery date. Recorded retrieval includes a completed review or a previously answered assessment item, even an incorrect or skipped assessment answer.

Within eligible topics, eligible.sort() orders (ever_assessed, topic_entity_id, question_entity_id). Never-assessed topics come first. Within each group the numeric storage ID decides selection and presentation order. There is no time-since-last-assessment ranking, random topic draw, coverage penalty, or final presentation shuffle. If many topics are already assessed, lower IDs can recur until their usable question supply runs out.

The 24-hour local-lesson gate and mandatory retrieval gate are local implementation choices, not recovered MA thresholds. The existing scheduling proposal is broader: it allows diagnostic placement and meaningful encompassing retrieval, recommends time-based topic rotation, and proposes adaptive difficulty. Its example adaptation window and thresholds are design choices, not observations.

The EDB policy currently has question-weight children only for lesson and review. Assessments directly pass [0.5, 0.4, 0.1] to the selector. Therefore the database weight update from earlier in the conversation changes lesson/review selection but does not change assessment selection.

Recommended direction

  1. Keep 50/40/10 as the assessment baseline and make that baseline configurable through EDB.
  2. Add learner-specific difficulty adaptation using finalized quiz performance. Specify and validate the adaptation separately; this sample does not recover MA's private formula.
  3. Replace database-ID sorting with reproducible weighted random topic sampling from practiced learned material. Favor the active course, reduce recent assessment priority, and reduce redundant encompassed coverage. Retain older topics in the pool. The data supports these directions but does not identify numerical multipliers.
  4. Use genuine readiness evidence, including appropriate placement and implicit practice, instead of requiring a local lesson for every topic. Keep exact readiness thresholds explicit as product choices.
  5. Preserve one question per topic, balance KP/case coverage, and fill a time budget using question duration estimates when available. Allow quiz size to vary with workload.
