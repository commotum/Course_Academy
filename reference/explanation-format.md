## Example: Solving Systems of Equations When One Value Is Given

**Question 1**

$$
\begin{cases}
y = -2x^{2} - 2x + 1 \\
y = -11
\end{cases}
$$

If pairs $(x_{1}, y_{1})$ and $(x_{2}, y_{2})$ with $x_{1} < x_{2}$ are the solutions of the system above, what is the value of $x_{1}y_{2}$?

- [ ] A. $0$
- [ ] B. $-55$
- [ ] C. $-12$
- [x] D. $33$
- [ ] E. The system has no real solutions.

---

**EXPLANATION**

First, we set the equations equal and solve for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2x^{2} - 2x + 1 &= -11 \\
-2x^{2} - 2x + 12 &= 0 \\
x^{2} + x - 6 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Now factor the quadratic equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + x - 6 &= 0 \\
(x + 3)(x - 2) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

So,

$$
x = -3 \qquad \text{or} \qquad x = 2
$$

Since $x_{1} < x_{2}$, we have

$$
x_{1} = -3, \qquad x_{2} = 2
$$

Both solutions have

$$
y = -11
$$

so

$$
y_{1} = -11, \qquad y_{2} = -11
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1}y_{2} &= (-3)(-11) \\
&= 33
\end{aligned}
$$

The value of $x_{1}y_{2}$ is $\boxed{33}$.
