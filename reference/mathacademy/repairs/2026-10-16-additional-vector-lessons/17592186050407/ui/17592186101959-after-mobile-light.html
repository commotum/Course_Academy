Introduction

When multiplying two matrices in general, we apply the same approach as usual:

  • First, we verify that the two matrices are conformable for multiplication.
  • Then, we multiply each row in the first matrix by each column in the second.

For example, let's find AB, where

A=[−2211−13]
B=[−522−2−41].

First, we verify the conformability for multiplication:

A2×3⋅✓B3×2=C2×2

So the matrices are conformable for multiplication, and the product is a 2×2 matrix. Let's set up the multiplication:

[−2211−13]⏟A[−522−2−41]⏟B=[∗∗∗∗]⏟C

Now, we calculate the entries of the product matrix (shown as ∗'s in the equation above).

To find c11, the entry at the intersection of the first row and first column, we calculate the dot product of the first row of A and the first column of B:

[−2211−13][−522−2−41]=[10∗∗∗]

10=(−2)⋅(−5)+2⋅2+1⋅(−4)

To find c12, we calculate the dot product of the first row of A and the second column of B:

[−2211−13][−522−2−41]=[10−7∗∗]

−7=(−2)⋅2+2⋅(−2)+1⋅1

To find c21, we calculate the dot product of the second row of A and the first column of B:

[−2211−13][−522−2−41]=[10−7−19∗]

−19=1⋅(−5)+(−1)⋅2+3⋅(−4)

Finally, to find c22, we calculate the dot product of the second row of A and the second column of B:

[−2211−13][−522−2−41]=[10−7−197]

7=1⋅2+(−1)⋅(−2)+3⋅1

Therefore, we have

AB=[10−7−197].
