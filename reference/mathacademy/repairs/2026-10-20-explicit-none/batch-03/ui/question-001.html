Consider the matrix A and vector v shown above. Which of the following statements are true?

true

A generalized eigenvector vn of rank n corresponding to the eigenvalue λ of a matrix A is a nonzero vector such that

vn→A−λIvn−1→A−λI…→A−λIv1→A−λI0,

where vn−1,vn−2,…,v1≠0. An eigenvector is simply a generalized eigenvector of rank 1.

First, we compute A+I:

A+I=[−32−2−11−210−1]+[100010001]=[−22−2−12−2100]

  • Next, we find the image of v under the action of A+I:

(A+I)v=[−22−2−12−2100][111]=[−2−11]⏟w≠0

  • Since we got a nonzero vector, we now compute the image of w under the action of A+I:

(A+I)w=[−22−2−12−2100][−2−11]=[0−2−2]⏟x≠0

  • Again, since we got a nonzero vector in the last step, we now compute the image of x under the action of A+I:

(A+I)x=[−22−2−12−2100][0−2−2]=[000]=0

So, we obtain the following chain of maps:

[111]⏟v→A+I[−2−11]⏟w→A+I[0−2−2]⏟x→A+I[000]⏟0

Here, we see that A+I maps v to 0 in 3 steps.

Therefore:

v is a generalized eigenvector of rank 3 corresponding to the eigenvalue λ=−1 of A.

So, the correct answer is "None of the statements are true."
