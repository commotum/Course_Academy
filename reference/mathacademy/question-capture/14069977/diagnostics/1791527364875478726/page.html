Two cars drive toward an intersection. The cars are traveling at right angles to each other, and each car's speed is 30mph. If car A is 12 miles from the intersection and car B is 35 miles from the intersection, how fast is the distance between the cars decreasing? Give your answer to one decimal place.

a
36.5mph
b
28.3mph
c
38.1mph
d
30.8mph
e
32.3mph

Correct
EXPLANATION

We start by drawing a diagram of the problem.

In the diagram,

  • x is the distance between car B and the intersection,

  • y is the distance between car A and the intersection, and

  • z is the distance between the cars.

As shown in the diagram, x, y, and z form a right triangle with z as the hypotenuse. We wish to find the rate of change of z, which is dzdt.

We know that y=12mi and x=35mi. We can find z using the Pythagorean Theorem, as follows:

x2+y2=z2352+122=z21369=z2z=37mi

We also know that:

  • dydt=−30mph when x=35mi and y=12,

  • dxdt=−30mph when x=35mi and y=12.

Note that the rates of change are both negative because the distance to the intersection is decreasing for both cars.

Finally, we differentiate both sides of x2+y2=z2 with respect to t and then substitute the known values, as follows:

ddt(x2+y2)=ddt(z2)2xdxdt+2ydydt=2zdzdt2(35)(−30)+2(12)(−30)=2(37)dzdt−2820=74dzdtdzdt=−38.1 to one decimal place.

Therefore, the distance between the cars is decreasing at a rate of 38.1mph.
