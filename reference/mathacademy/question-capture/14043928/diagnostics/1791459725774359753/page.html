A critical point of f(x) is a point x=c in the domain of f such that either f′(c)=0 or f′(c) does not exist.

In part 1, we obtained

f′(x)=7(5−4x)(2x2−5x−3)2.

We now proceed to find the critical points:

  • First, we compute the points where f′(x)=0. We set the derivative equal to zero, and solve for x: 7(5−4x)(2x2−5x−3)2=07(5−4x)=05−4x=04x=5x=54 Therefore, f(x) has a stationary point at x=54.

We see that f′(x) does not exist at x=−12 and x=3. But these points do not lie in the interval (−12,3). So f(x) has no non-stationary critical points.

Therefore, we conclude that the only critical point of f(x) is x=54.
