## q-124137

Template: `q-124403`.

The given expression is a sum of two factorials.

- Computing $3!$ using the definition, we get

$$
3! = 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 6.
$$

- By definition, the factorial of zero equals one:

$$
0! = 1
$$

Finally, we can evaluate the given expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3! + 0! &= 6 + 1 \\
&= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

## q-124138

Template: `e-9519`.

The given expression is a quotient of two factorials.

- Computing $2!$ using the definition, we get

$$
2! = 2 \cdot 1 = 2.
$$

- By definition, the factorial of zero equals one:

$$
0! = 1
$$

Finally, we can evaluate the given expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2!}{0!} &= \frac{2}{1} \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

## q-19838

Template: `q-5371`.

First, notice that $7! = 7 \cdot 6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4!$ and $3! = 3 \cdot 2!$. Then, we can cancel the $4!$ and the $2!$ that occur in both the numerator and the denominator:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{7!3!}{4!2!} &= \frac{(7 \cdot 6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4!)(3 \cdot 2!)}{4! \cdot 2!} \\
&= \frac{(7 \cdot 6 \cdot 5 \cdot \cancel{4!})(3 \cdot \cancel{2!})}{\cancel{4!} \cdot \cancel{2!}} \\
&= (7 \cdot 6 \cdot 5) \cdot 3 \\
&= 630
\end{aligned}
$$
