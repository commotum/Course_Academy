A function f(x,y) has a local maximum point at (a,b) if f(x,y)≤f(a,b) for all points (x,y) in a neighborhood of (a,b).

In other words, we can take a neighborhood of (x,y)-values such that the point (x,y,f(x,y)) is the highest point on the graph in the neighborhood.

Such region exists for the point A. Hence, A is a local maximum point.

Note: For the point A(xA,yA,zA), there are points (x,y) in the surrounding regions such that f(xA,yA)=f(x,y). However, since the definition of local extrema is non-strict, f(x,y)≤f(a,b), the point A does satisfy the conditions for a local maximum.
