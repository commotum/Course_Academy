We have a population of size N=60, and p=0.35 represents the proportion of the population of left-footed children.

Therefore,

  • the number of left-footed children equals Np=60(0.35)=21,

  • the number of right-footed children equals N(1−p)=60(1−0.35)=39.

We select two children of the population at random. We need to find P(X2=1|X1=0), the probability that the second child is left-footed given that the first child is right-footed.

If the first child is right-footed, we have

N−1=60−1=59 children remaining, and

Np=21 children remaining who are left-footed.

Therefore, P(X2=1|X1=0)=NpN−1=2159.
