Recall that if X and Y are independent random variables and a and b are constants, then

Var[aX+bY]=a2Var[X]+b2Var[Y].

Also, the variance of X is determined by the formula Var[X]=E[X2]−E[X]2.

First, let's find Var[X]. If X is defined on a sample space SX, then

E[X]=∑x∈SXx⋅f(x)=10⋅0.25+15⋅0.35+20⋅0.15+25⋅0.25=17

and

E[X2]=∑x∈SXx2⋅f(x)=100⋅0.25+225⋅0.35+400⋅0.15+625⋅0.25=320.

Therefore,

Var[X]=E[X2]−E[X]2=320−172=31.

Similarly, if Y is defined on a sample space SY, then

E[Y]=∑y∈SYy⋅f(y)=12⋅0.33+18⋅0.67=16.02

and

E[Y2]=∑y∈SYy2⋅f(y)=144⋅0.33+324⋅0.67=264.6.

Therefore,

Var[Y]=E[Y2]−E[Y]2=264.6−16.022=7.9596.

Finally, we obtain

Var[1.5X+2Y]=1.52Var[X]+22Var[Y]=2.25(31)+4(7.9596)≈101.59.
