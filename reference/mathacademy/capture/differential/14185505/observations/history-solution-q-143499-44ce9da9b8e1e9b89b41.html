Suppose that X1,X2,…,Xn is a random sample of size n from a population with population mean μ and population standard deviation σ. The central limit theorem (or CLT) states that for sufficiently large n, the distribution of the sample mean X― can be approximated as

X―∼N(μ,σ2n).

In our case:

  • the sample size is n=40,

  • the population mean is μ=E[X]=∫−∞∞xf(x)dx=∫01x⋅1dx=∫01xdx=x22|01=12=0.5,

  • the population variance is σ2=0.08.

Therefore, by the CLT, we have that the distribution of the sample mean can be approximated as

X―∼N(μ,σ2n)∼N(0.5,0.0840)∼N(0.5,0.002).

We want to find P(0.51<X―<0.55). So, we convert X― to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring:

P(0.51<X―<0.55)=P(0.51−0.50.002<Z<0.55−0.50.002)≈P(0.22<Z<1.12)=P(Z<1.12)−P(Z<0.22)=Φ(1.12)−Φ(0.22)

From the tables, we have Φ(0.22)=0.5871,Φ(1.12)=0.8686.

Therefore,

P(0.51<X―<0.55)=Φ(1.12)−Φ(0.22)=0.8686−0.5871=0.2815.
