EXPLANATION

Suppose that X1,X2,…,Xn is a random sample of size n from a population with population mean μ and population standard deviation σ. The central limit theorem (or CLT) states that for sufficiently large n, the distribution of the sample mean X― can be approximated as

X―∼N(μ,σ2n).

In our case:

  • the sample size is n=84,

  • the population mean is μ=E[X]=∑i=1nxif(xi)=0⋅0.2+1⋅0.3+2⋅0.3+3⋅0.2=0+0.3+0.6+0.6=1.5,

  • the population variance is σ2=Var[X]=E[X2]−E[X]2=(02⋅0.2+12⋅0.3+22⋅0.3+32⋅0.2)−1.52=(0+0.3+1.2+1.8)−2.25=1.05.

Therefore, by the CLT, we have that the distribution of the sample mean can be approximated as

X―∼N(μ,σ2n)∼N(1.5,1.0584)∼N(1.5,0.0125).

We want to find P(X―<1.75). So, we convert X― to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring:

P(X―<1.75)≈P(Z<1.75−1.50.0125)=P(Z<2.24)=Φ(2.24).

From the table, we have Φ(2.24)=0.9875.

Therefore, P(X―<1.75)=Φ(2.24)=0.9875.
