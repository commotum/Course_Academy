EXPLANATION

Suppose that X1,X2,…,Xn is a random sample of size n from a population with population mean μ and population standard deviation σ. The central limit theorem (or CLT) states that for sufficiently large n, the distribution of the sample mean X― can be approximated as

X―∼N(μ,σ2n).

In our case:

  • the sample size is n=116,

  • the population mean is μ=E[X]=∑i=1nxif(xi)=0⋅0.6+5⋅0.1+10⋅0.2+15⋅0.1=0+0.5+2+1.5=4,

  • the population variance is σ2=Var[X]=E[X2]−E[X]2=(02⋅0.6+52⋅0.1+102⋅0.2+152⋅0.1)−42=(0+2.5+20+22.5)−16=29.

Therefore, by the CLT, we have that the distribution of the sample mean can be approximated as

X―∼N(μ,σ2n)∼N(4,29116)∼N(4,0.25).

We want to find P(3<X―<5). So, we convert X― to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring:

P(3<X―<5)≈P(3−40.25<Z<5−40.25)=P(−2<Z<2)=Φ(2)−Φ(−2).

Therefore, we conclude that

P(3<X―<5)=Φ(2)−Φ(−2).
