The conditional probability mass function fX|Y(x|y) is defined as

fX|Y(x|y)=P(X=x|Y=y).

Therefore, we're required to compute the following set of conditional probabilities:

fX|Y(x|0)=P(X=x|Y=0)

By the multiplication law for conditional probability, we have

P(X=x|Y=0)=P(X=x,Y=0)P(Y=0)=f(x,0)fY(0)

where f(x,y) is the joint probability mass function of X and Y, and fY(y) is the marginal distribution of Y.
