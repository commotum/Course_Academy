A manufacturing company has three sites, factory 1, factory 2, and factory 3. They all produce two light bulb types: A and B.

  • Factory 1 produces 25% of the entire production, and 2% of those bulbs are defective.
  • Factory 2 produces 50% of the entire production, and 3% of those bulbs are defective.
  • Factory 3 produces 25% of the entire production, and 1% of those bulbs are defective.

If 40% of all light bulbs are of type A, and 1% of type A light bulbs are defective, what is the probability that a randomly chosen defective light bulb is of type A? Round your answer to 3 decimal places.

EXPLANATION

Let A be the event that a light bulb is of type A, D be the event that a light bulb is defective, and F1,F2,F3 be the event that a light bulb is produced in factory 1, 2, or 3, respectively.

Let's break down the information given in the problem statement:

  • From the bullet points, we have the following information: P(F1)=0.25P(D|F1)=0.02P(F2)=0.5P(D|F2)=0.03P(F3)=0.25P(D|F3)=0.01 The disjoint union F1⊔F2⊔F3 covers the entire sample space, and we have P(F1)+P(F2)+P(F3)=0.25+0.5+0.25=1.

  • Since 40% of all light bulbs are of type A, we have P(A)=0.4.

  • Since 1% of type A light bulbs are defective, we have P(D|A)=0.01.

We want to find the probability that a randomly chosen defective light bulb is of type A, which is represented as P(A|D).

Using Bayes' theorem combined with the law of total probability, we have

P(A|D)=P(D|A)P(A)P(D)=P(D|A)P(A)P(D|F1)P(F1)+P(D|F2)P(F2)+P(D|F3)P(F3)=(0.01)(0.4)(0.02)(0.25)+(0.03)(0.5)+(0.01)(0.25)=0.0040.0225≈0.178,

rounded to 3 decimal places.
