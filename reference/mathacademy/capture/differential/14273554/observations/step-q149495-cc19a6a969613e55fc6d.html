In a nature park, there are 4 species of squirrels, species 1, species 2, species 3, and species 4. Each species has the same population.

  • 8% of squirrels of species 1 are born in captivity.
  • 9% of squirrels of species 2 are born in captivity.
  • 11% of squirrels of species 4 are born in captivity.

If 42% of the squirrels are adult females, 8% of the adult female squirrels are born in captivity, and 36% of the squirrels born in captivity are adult females, what is the probability that a randomly chosen squirrel from species 3 is born in captivity? Round your answer to 3 decimal places.

EXPLANATION

Let F be the event that a squirrel is an adult female, C be the event that a squirrel is born in captivity, and S1,S2,S3,S4 be the event that a squirrel is of species 1, 2, 3, or 4, respectively.

Let's break down the information given in the problem statement:

  • Each species has the same number of individuals, so we have the following information: P(S1)=P(S2)=P(S3)=P(S4)=0.25

  • From the bullet points, we have the following information: P(C|S1)=0.08P(C|S2)=0.09P(C|S4)=0.11 The disjoint union S1⊔S2⊔S3⊔S4 covers the entire sample space, and we have P(S1)+P(S2)+P(S3)+P(S4)=0.25+0.25+0.25+0.25=1.

  • Since 42% of the squirrels are adult females, we have P(F)=0.42.

  • Since 8% of adult females are born in captivity, we have P(C|F)=0.08.

  • Since 36% of squirrels born in captivity are adult females, we have P(F|C)=0.36.

We want to find the probability that a randomly chosen squirrel from species 3 is born in captivity, which is represented as P(C|S3).

Using Bayes' theorem combined with the law of total probability, we have

P(F|C)=P(C|F)P(F)P(C)0.36=P(C|F)P(F)P(C|S1)P(S1)+P(C|S2)P(S2)+P(C|S3)P(S3)+P(C|S4)P(S4)0.36=(0.08)(0.42)(0.08)(0.25)+(0.09)(0.25)+P(C|S3)(0.25)+(0.11)(0.25)0.36=0.03360.07+0.25P(C|S3).
