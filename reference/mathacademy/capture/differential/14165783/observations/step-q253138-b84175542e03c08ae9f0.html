Consider the following set. A={ncos⁡(nπ):n∈N}For this set, we have that
inf(A)=−∞
inf(A)=−1∈A

Correct
EXPLANATION

The infimum of A is the greatest lower bound of A.

To find the infimum of this set, we must find the set containing all lower bounds of A (i.e., the set of lower bounds) and take the maximum of this set.

The elements of A are the terms of the sequence an, given by

an=ncos⁡(nπ),n∈N={1,2,3,4,…}.

Let's draw some points of our sequence on the real line:

We see that an is an oscillating, non-convergent sequence. Moreover, A is unbounded below. In other words, we can always find a sufficiently large n so that an is smaller than x for any real number x. Therefore, A has no lower bounds and inf(A)=−∞.
