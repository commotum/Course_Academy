For the polynomial f(x)=5x3+2x2−x+12, how many potential roots are given by the rational roots theorem?

a
0
b
3
c
6
d
12

24
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

Given a polynomial with integer coefficients, the rational roots theorem states that any rational roots of the polynomial must take the form

±factor of the constant termfactor of the leading coefficient.

For the given polynomial:

  • The constant term is 12, and its factors are ±1,±2,±3,±4,±6 and ±12.

  • The leading coefficient is 5, and its factors are ±1 and ±5.

First, we list out all the possibilities for the numerator and denominator:

±11,±15,±21,±25,±31,±35,±41,±45,±61,±65,±121,±125.

Next, we reduce the fractions to lowest terms:

±1,±15,±2,±25,±3,±35,±4,±45,±6,±65,±12,±125.

Notice that none of these potential roots are duplicates. Listing those potential roots in order from least to greatest in absolute value, we have

±15,±25,±35,±45,±1±65,±2,±125,±3,±4,±6,±12.

This makes a total of 24 potential roots. (Remember that each ± sign accounts for 2 roots, and 2⋅12=24.)
