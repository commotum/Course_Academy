Given that X∼Bernoulli(0.6) and Y∼Bernoulli(0.6) are independent, calculate P(X=1∪Y=0).

a
0.70
b
0.77
c
0.66
d
0.67

0.76

Correct
EXPLANATION

By the addition law, we have

P(X=1∪Y=0)=P(X=1)+P(Y=0)−P(X=1∩Y=0).

Using the multiplication law for independent events, we have

P(X=1∩Y=0)=P(X=1)⋅P(Y=0).

Therefore,

P(X=1∪Y=0)=P(X=1)+P(Y=0)−P(X=1)⋅P(Y=0).

Now, since X∼Bernoulli(0.6), we have

P(X=1)=0.6,P(X=0)=0.4,

and since Y∼Bernoulli(0.6), we have

P(Y=1)=0.6,P(Y=0)=0.4.

Therefore,

P(X=1∪Y=0)=P(X=1)+P(Y=0)−P(X=1)⋅P(Y=0)=0.6+0.4−0.6⋅0.4=1−0.24=0.76.
