The chain rule for a multivariable function f(x,y,z) with two independent variables s and t, where x=x(s,t),y=y(s,t) and z=z(s,t), can be expressed as follows: ∂f∂t=∂f∂x⋅∂x∂t+∂f∂y⋅∂y∂t+∂f∂z⋅∂z∂t.

First, we find the partial derivatives of f with respect to x,y and z: ∂f∂x=3,∂f∂y=−2,∂f∂z=1.

Next, we take the derivatives of x, y, and z with respect to t, and we get ∂x∂t=1,∂y∂t=−2t,∂z∂t=0.

Substituting all of our derivatives into the multivariable chain rule, we get

∂f∂t=(3)⋅1+(−2)⋅(−2t)+(1)⋅0=4t+3.
