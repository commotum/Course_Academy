Let D be the event that a selected cow is a dairy cow, and

  • let A be the event that the cow is from ranch A,

  • let B be the event that the cow is from ranch B,

  • let C be the event that the cow is from ranch C.

Then the disjoint union A⊔B⊔C covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability that the randomly selected cow is a dairy cow, we use the law of total probability:

P(D)=P(D∩A)+P(D∩B)+P(D∩C)=P(D|A)P(A)+P(D|B)P(B)+P(D|C)P(C)

Let's go through the problem statement and translate each piece of information into mathematical notation.

  • Since 34% of all the rancher's cows are on ranch A, 36% are on ranch B, and 30% are on ranch C, we have
    P(A)=0.34,P(B)=0.36,P(C)=0.3.

  • Since 25% of all cows on ranch A and 20% of all cows on ranch B are dairy cows, we have P(D|A)=0.25,P(D|B)=0.2.

  • Since the probability that a randomly selected cow from any of the three ranches is a dairy cow is 37%, we have P(D)=0.37.

Substituting the above information into the law of total probability and solving for P(D|C), we get

P(D)=P(D|A)P(A)+P(D|B)P(B)+P(D|C)P(C)0.37=(0.25)(0.34)+(0.2)(0.36)+P(D|C)(0.3)0.37=0.085+0.072+0.3P(D|C)0.3P(D|C)=0.213P(D|C)=0.71

Therefore, the probability that a randomly selected cow from ranch C is a dairy cow is 71%.
