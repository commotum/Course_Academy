Let D be the event of selecting a defective bulb, and

  • let A be the event that the bulb is from box A,

  • let B be the event that the bulb is from box B

  • let C be the event that the bulb is from box C.

Then the disjoint union A⊔B⊔C covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability of selecting a defective bulb at random from one of the three boxes, we can use the law of total probability:

P(D)=P(D∩A)+P(D∩B)+P(D∩C)=P(D|A)P(A)+P(D|B)P(B)+P(D|C)P(C)

Let's go through the problem statement and translate each piece of information into mathematical notation.

  • Since a box is selected at random, we have P(A)=P(B)=P(C)=13.

  • Since there are 2 defective bulbs out of 15 in box A, 1 defective bulb out of 10 in box B, and 2 defective bulbs out of 10 bulbs in box C, we have P(D|A)=215,P(D|B)=110,P(D|C)=210.

Substituting the above information into the law of total probability, the probability of selecting a defective bulb at random from one of the three boxes is

P(D)=P(D|A)P(A)+P(D|B)P(B)+P(D|C)P(C)=(215)(13)+(110)(13)+(210)(13)=245+130+230=1390.
