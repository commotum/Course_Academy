A company manufactures electronic components. Of the component population, 45% are produced by machine A, 25% by machine B, and 30% by machine C. If 5% of the components produced by machine A and 3% of those produced by machine B are defective, and the probability that a randomly selected component is defective is 0.042, what is the probability that a randomly selected component produced by machine C is defective?

EXPLANATION

Let D be the event that a randomly selected component is defective, and

  • let A be the event that the component was produced by machine A,

  • let B be the event that the component was produced by machine B,

  • let C be the event that the component was produced by machine C.

Then the disjoint union A⊔B⊔C covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability that a randomly selected component is defective, we can use the law of total probability:

P(D)=P(D∩A)+P(D∩B)+P(D∩C)=P(D|A)P(A)+P(D|B)P(B)+P(D|C)P(C)

Let's go through the problem statement and translate each piece of information into mathematical notation.

  • Since machine A produces 45% of the components, machine B produces 25% of the components, and machine C produces 30% of the components, we have P(A)=0.45,P(B)=0.25,P(C)=0.3.

  • Since 5% of the components produced by machine A and 3% of the components produced by machine B are defective, we have P(D|A)=0.05,P(D|B)=0.03

  • Since the probability that a randomly selected component is defective is 0.042, we have P(D)=0.042.

Substituting the above information into the law of total probability and solving for P(D|C), we get

P(D)=P(D|A)P(A)+P(D|B)P(B)+P(D|C)P(C)0.042=(0.05)(0.45)+(0.03)(0.25)+P(D|C)(0.3)0.042=0.0225+0.0075+0.3P(D|C)0.3P(D|C)=0.012P(D|C)=0.04

Therefore, the probability that a randomly selected component produced by machine C is defective is 0.04.
