EXPLANATION

If X and Y are discrete random variables with the joint probability mass function f(x,y), then the corresponding joint CDF is given by

F(x,y)=P(X≤x,Y≤y)=∑xi≤x∑yi≤yf(xi,yi).

Note the following:

  • We can think of each point where f(x,y)≠0 as a point mass in the plane, where the "probability mass" at each point equals the probability associated with that point.

  • The joint CDF F(x,y)=P(X≤x,Y≤y) is the total "probability mass" that lies in the infinite rectangular region that has its top-right corner at (x,y) and whose sides are parallel to the coordinate axis.

Given the supports of X and Y, the joint support S is a subset of

SX×SY={2,3,4}×{2,3,4}.

Let's sketch the points in SX×SY, which contains the joint support. Let's also consider a point (x,y) where 2<x<4 and y<2, and a region Δ that lies below and to the left of this point.

Since every value in the support of Y is at least 2 and y<2, no possible value of Y satisfies Y≤y. Therefore, there are no points in the joint support that lie in the region Δ.

Hence,

F(x,y)=P(X≤x,Y≤y)=0.
