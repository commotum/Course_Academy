The direction vectors of the lines are v1=⟨−2,−2,−2⟩ and v2=⟨1,1,1⟩, respectively. But v2=−2v1. So, our lines are parallel.

Despite this fact, two distinct parallel lines still determine a plane. Now:

  • from the equation of the first line, we get the point P with the position vector p=⟨−1,5,7⟩ that lies on it, and

  • from the equation of the second line, we get the point Q with the position vector q=⟨0,3,−19⟩ that lies on it.

The vector u=q−p will be parallel to the required plane.

Computing the coordinates of u, we obtain

u=q−p=⟨0,3,−19⟩−⟨−1,5,7⟩=⟨1,−2,−26⟩

Finally, since PQ→∦v1, we can find the equation of our plane.

We know that the point C(1,4,−14) lies on the plane and its position vector is c=⟨1,4,−14⟩.

So, the corresponding parametric equation of the plane is given by

r=c+sv1+tur=⟨1,4,−14⟩+s⟨1,1,1⟩+t⟨1,−2,−26⟩,s,t∈(−∞,∞).
