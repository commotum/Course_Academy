Let the random variable X be equal to the number of arrows that hit the bull's eye.

Here, we have the following information:

  • there are n=16 shots

  • the probability of hitting the bull's eye for each arrow is p=25%=0.25

So, X follows a binomial distribution, where X∼B(16,0.25).

For a binomial random variable X, the variance is given by Var[X]=np(1−p), where n is the number of experiments, and p is the probability of success on each experiment.

So, the variance of the number of arrows hitting the bull's eye is

Var[X]=np(1−p)=(16)(0.25)(1−0.25)=(4)(0.75)=3.
