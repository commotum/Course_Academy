Given a population in which a proportion p of the population has a particular characteristic, the sampling distribution of the sample proportion p^ can be approximated as

p^∼N(p,p(1−p)n),

where the sample size n is much smaller than the population size, np>5, and n(1−p)>5.

In our case

  • the sample size is n=100, and

  • the population proportion is p=0.6.

Now, since np=100(0.6)=60>5 and n(1−p)=100(1−0.6)=40>5, and n=100 is much smaller than the population size, we can apply the above approximation.

The sampling distribution of the sample proportion, in this case, can be approximated as

p^∼N(p,p(1−p)n)∼N(0.6,0.6(1−0.6)100)∼N(0.6,0.0024).

The probability we wish to find is P(0.55≤p^≤0.65). So, we convert p^ to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring:

P(0.55≤p^≤0.65)=P(0.55−0.60.0024≤Z≤0.65−0.60.0024)≈P(−1.02≤Z≤1.02)=P(Z≤1.02)−P(Z≤−1.02)=Φ(1.02)−Φ(−1.02)
