The chain rule for a multivariable function f(x,y) with two independent variables s and t, where x=x(s,t) and y=y(s,t), can be expressed as follows: ∂f∂s=∂f∂x⋅∂x∂s+∂f∂y⋅∂y∂s

First, we find the partial derivatives of f with respect to x and y: ∂f∂x=1,∂f∂y=−1

Writing the above results in terms of s and t, we get ∂f∂x=1,∂f∂y=−1.

Next, we take the derivatives of x and y with respect to s, and we get ∂x∂s=−cos⁡t,∂y∂s=sin⁡t.

Finally, substituting all of our derivatives into the multivariable chain rule, we get ∂f∂s=∂f∂x⋅∂x∂s+∂f∂y⋅∂y∂s=(1)(−cos⁡t)+(−1)(sin⁡t)=−(cos⁡t+sin⁡t).
