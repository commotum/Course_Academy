Let A be the event that a muffin is made with yeast A, G be the event that a muffin turns out well, and M1,M2,M3 be the event that a muffin is cooked with mode 1, 2, or 3, respectively.

Let's break down the information given in the problem statement:

  • From the bullet points, we have the following information: P(M1)=0.3P(G|M1)=0.9P(M2)=0.4P(G|M2)=0.85P(M3)=0.3P(G|M3)=0.88 The disjoint union M1⊔M2⊔M3 covers the entire sample space, and we have P(M1)+P(M2)+P(M3)=0.3+0.4+0.3=1.

  • Since 20% of muffins are made with yeast A, we have P(A)=0.2.

  • Since 87% of muffins made with yeast A turn out well, we have P(G|A)=0.87.

We want to find the probability that a randomly chosen good muffin is made with yeast A, which is represented as P(A|G).

Using Bayes' theorem combined with the law of total probability, we have

P(A|G)=P(G|A)P(A)P(G)=P(G|A)P(A)P(G|M1)P(M1)+P(G|M2)P(M2)+P(G|M3)P(M3)=(0.87)(0.2)(0.9)(0.3)+(0.85)(0.4)+(0.88)(0.3)=0.1740.874≈0.199,

rounded to 3 decimal places.
