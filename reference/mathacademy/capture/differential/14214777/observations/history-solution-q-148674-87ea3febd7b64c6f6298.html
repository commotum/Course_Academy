Let A be the event that a muffin is made with yeast A, G be the event that a muffin turns out well, and M1,M2,M3 be the event that a muffin is cooked with mode 1, 2, or 3, respectively.

Let's break down the information given in the problem statement:

  • From the bullet points, we have the following information: P(M1)=0.45P(G|M1)=0.8P(M2)=0.35P(G|M2)P(M3)=0.2P(G|M3)=0.9 The disjoint union M1⊔M2⊔M3 covers the entire sample space, and we have P(M1)+P(M2)+P(M3)=0.45+0.35+0.2=1.

  • Since 65% of muffins are made with yeast A, we have P(A)=0.65.

  • Since 82% of muffins made with yeast A turned out well, we have P(G|A)=0.82.

  • Since 68% of good muffins were prepared with yeast A, we have P(A|G)=0.68.

We want to find the probability that a randomly chosen muffin cooked with mode 2 turned well, which is represented as P(G|M2).

Using Bayes' theorem combined with the law of total probability, we have

P(A|G)=P(G|A)P(A)P(G)P(A|G)=P(G|A)P(A)P(G|M1)P(M1)+P(G|M2)P(M2)+P(G|M3)P(M3)0.68=(0.82)(0.65)(0.8)(0.45)+P(G|M2)(0.35)+(0.9)(0.2)0.68=0.5330.35P(G|M2)+0.54
