A travel agency offers three itineraries, itinerary 1, itinerary 2, and itinerary 3. For each itinerary, customers can decide to travel using their own car or the agency's bus.

  • Itinerary 1 is chosen by 60% of the customers, and 10% of those customers travel by bus.
  • Itinerary 2 is chosen by 20% of the customers, and 25% of those customers travel by bus.
  • Itinerary 3 is chosen by 20% of the customers.

If 30% of all customers travel with children, 50% of customers with children choose the bus, and 83% of customers that choose the bus travel with children, what is the probability that a randomly chosen customer that chooses itinerary 3 takes the bus? Round your answer to 3 decimal places.

Let C be the event that a customer has children, B be the event that a customer takes the bus, and I1,I2,I3 be the event that a customer chooses itinerary 1, 2, or 3, respectively.

Let's break down the information given in the problem statement:

  • From the bullet points, we have the following information: P(I1)=0.6P(B|I1)=0.1P(I2)=0.2P(B|I2)=0.25P(I3)=0.2 The disjoint union I1⊔I2⊔I3 covers the entire sample space, and we have P(I1)+P(I2)+P(I3)=0.6+0.2+0.2=1.

  • Since 30% of customers have children, we have P(C)=0.3.

  • Since 50% of customers with children choose the bus, we have P(B|C)=0.5.

  • Since 83% of customers that choose the bus have children, we have P(C|B)=0.83.
