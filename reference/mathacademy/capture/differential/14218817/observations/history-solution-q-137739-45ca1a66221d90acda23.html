Recall that the unit eigenvectors of the covariance matrix C are called the principal components of the data. If

λ1>λ2>⋯>λn

are the eigenvalues of C written in descending order, then:

  • the 1st principal component is the unit eigenvector u1 corresponding to the 1st eigenvalue λ1,

  • the 2nd principal component is the unit eigenvector u2 corresponding to the 2nd eigenvalue λ2,

  • ⋯

and so on.

In our case, the eigenvalues of the covariance matrix C, in descending order, are λ1=10 and λ2=5. So, we need the unit eigenvector that corresponds to the eigenvalue λ1=10:

C−10I=[9226]−10[1001]=[−122−4]

Seeking a non-zero solution of (C−10I)x=0 gives the eigenvector v1=[21].

Finally, dividing v1 by its norm ‖v1‖=5, we get

u1=v1‖v1‖=15[21].
