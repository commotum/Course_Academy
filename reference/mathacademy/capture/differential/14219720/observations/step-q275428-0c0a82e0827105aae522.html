Let X and Y be discrete random variables, where Y takes values p, q, and r with probabilities 0.5, 0.3, and 0.2, respectively. You are given the following conditional expectations:

E[X∣Y=p]=8,E[X∣Y=q]=14,E[X∣Y=r]=10.

What is E[X]?

a
10.2
b
9.2
c
10.8
d
8.8
e
11.4

Incorrect
EXPLANATION

We are given the conditional expectations of X given different values of another random variable Y, along with the probabilities of those values. This is a direct application of the law of total expectation, which states:

E[X]=∑yE[X∣Y=y]⋅P(Y=y).

In this problem, Y takes three values:

  • Y=p with probability 0.5, and E[X∣Y=p]=8

  • Y=q with probability 0.3, and E[X∣Y=q]=14

  • Y=r with probability 0.2, and E[X∣Y=r]=10

So, we calculate our expected value as follows:

E[X]=E[X∣Y=p]⋅P(Y=p)+E[X∣Y=q]⋅P(Y=q)+E[X∣Y=r]⋅P(Y=r)=8⋅0.5+14⋅0.3+10⋅0.2=4+4.2+2=10.2
