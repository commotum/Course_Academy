A surveyor is sent to one of three cities: A, B, or C. She is sent to City A with a probability of 0.4, City B with a probability of 0.35, and City C with a probability of 0.25.

If the expected number of households visited is 12 in City A, 18 in City B, and 10 in City C, what is the expected number of households visited on a given day?

The expected number of households visited is
14
.

Incorrect
EXPLANATION

We are given the probabilities of being assigned to one of three cities and the expected number of households visited in each. This is a direct application of the law of total expectation, which tells us how to compute the overall expected value by conditioning on which city the surveyor is sent to.

Let X be the number of households visited in a day, and let Y be the city to which the surveyor is sent.

  • Y=A with probability 0.4, and E[X∣Y=A]=12.

  • Y=B with probability 0.35, and E[X∣Y=B]=18.

  • Y=C with probability 0.25, and E[X∣Y=C]=10.

So, we calculate our expected value as follows:

E[X]=E[X∣Y=A]⋅P(Y=A)+E[X∣Y=B]⋅P(Y=B)+E[X∣Y=C]⋅P(Y=C)=12⋅0.4+18⋅0.35+10⋅0.25=4.8+6.3+2.5=13.6
