Let X and Y be discrete random variables, where Y takes values a, b, and c with probabilities 0.25, 0.5, and 0.25, respectively. You are given the following conditional expectations:

E[X∣Y=a]=10,E[X∣Y=b]=4,E[X∣Y=c]=6.

What is E[X]?

a
6.5
b
7.0
c
5.5
d
5.0
e
6.0

Correct
EXPLANATION

We are given the conditional expectations of X given different values of another random variable Y, along with the probabilities of those values. This is a direct application of the law of total expectation, which states:

E[X]=∑yE[X∣Y=y]⋅P(Y=y).

In this problem, Y takes three values:

  • Y=a with probability 0.25, and E[X∣Y=a]=10

  • Y=b with probability 0.5, and E[X∣Y=b]=4

  • Y=c with probability 0.25, and E[X∣Y=c]=6

So, we calculate our expected value as follows:

E[X]=E[X∣Y=a]⋅P(Y=a)+E[X∣Y=b]⋅P(Y=b)+E[X∣Y=c]⋅P(Y=c)=10⋅0.25+4⋅0.5+6⋅0.25=2.5+2+1.5=6.0
