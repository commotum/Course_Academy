A ride-share driver starts every day in either the City Center or the Suburban Area. The driver starts the day in the City Center with probability 0.55, where the expected number of rides given per day is 14.3. The driver starts the day in the Suburban Area with probability 0.45, where the expected number of rides is 11.7.

What is the expected number of rides given in a day?

a
12.87
b
13.87
c
13.13
d
11.13
e
11.34
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

We are asked to compute the expected number of rides given in a day. This is a classic application of the law of total expectation, which allows us to calculate an overall expected value by conditioning on a related variable — in this case, the driver's starting location.

Let X be the number of rides completed in a day, and Y be the location from which the driver started.

  • Y=City with probability 0.55, and E[X∣Y=City]=14.3.

  • Y=Suburban with probability 0.45, and E[X∣Y=Suburban]=11.7.

So, we calculate our expected value as follows:

E[X]=E[X∣Y=City]⋅P(Y=City)+E[X∣Y=Suburban]⋅P(Y=Suburban)=14.3⋅0.55+11.7⋅0.45=7.865+5.265=13.13
