Let A be the event that a randomly chosen patient is an allergy sufferer, and let T be the event they tested positive. Then, we require P(A|T), which we can compute using Bayes' theorem.

P(A|T)=P(T|A)P(A)P(T)=P(T|A)P(A)P(T|A)P(A)+P(T|A′)P(A′)

From the problem statement, we have the following:

  • Since the probability of a person who has the allergy testing positive is 96%, and the probability of a non-allergic person testing positive is 3%, we have P(T|A)=0.96,P(T|A′)=0.03.

  • Since only 4% of the population suffers the allergy, we have P(A)=0.04,P(A′)=1−0.04=0.96.

Therefore, using Bayes' theorem, we have

P(A|T)=P(T|A)P(A)P(T|A)P(A)+P(T|A′)P(A′)=(0.96)(0.04)(0.96)(0.04)+(0.03)(0.96)≈0.57 rounded to 2 decimal places.
