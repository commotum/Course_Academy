Which of the following random variables could be modeled as a binomial distribution?

  1. The number of correct guesses at 50 true-false questions if all questions are answered at random
  2. The number of winning lottery tickets in a set of 10 tickets of the same kind
  3. The temperature, in Celsius, of children in a class of 30 students during a day
a
I and II only
b
 I only
c
 II and III only
d
II only
e
 I and III only
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

A binomial distribution models the number of successes in a sequence of n independent experiments (where each outcome is either success or failure).

With that in mind, let's consider each of the given random variables.

  • Random variable I could be modeled as a binomial distribution. The number of correct guesses at 50 true-false questions can be interpreted as the number of successes in a sequence of 50 independent experiments.

  • Random variable II could be modeled as a binomial distribution. The number of winning lottery tickets (out of 10 tickets in total) can be interpreted as the number of successes in a sequence of 10 independent experiments.

  • Random variable III could not be modeled as a binomial distribution. The temperatures cannot be interpreted as "success" or "failure."

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and II only."
