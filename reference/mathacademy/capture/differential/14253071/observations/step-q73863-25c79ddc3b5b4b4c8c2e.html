The Cartesian equation of the tangent plane to the surface z=f(x,y) at the point (x0,y0,z0) on the surface can be written as

z−z0=(x−x0)∂f∂x(x0,y0)+(y−y0)∂f∂y(x0,y0),

where ⟨∂f∂x(x0,y0),∂f∂y(x0,y0),−1⟩ represents a normal vector to the tangent plane.

First, we find the partial derivatives:

∂f∂x=∂∂x(x2+2x+y2+2y)=2x+2∂f∂y=∂∂y(x2+2x+y2+2y)=2y+2

Hence, the normal vector to the tangent plane is

⟨2x+2,2y+2,−1⟩

Our tangent plane should be perpendicular to ⟨−4,0,1⟩. So, the normal vector of that plane must be parallel to that vector. In other words, for some constant c, we must have

c⋅⟨2x+2,2y+2,−1⟩=⟨−4,0,1⟩.

−(2x+2)=−4⟹x=1.

And substituting c=−1 into the second equation, we have −(2y+2)=0⟹y=−1.

Therefore, the tangent plane is perpendicular to the vector v at (1,−1,2).
