A factory produces shovels, each consisting of a handle and a blade. The length of a handle is normally distributed with a mean of 120 centimeters and a variance of 0.14, while the length of a blade is normally distributed with a mean of 30 centimeters and a variance of 0.02. If one shovel is randomly selected, what is the probability that its length is more than 150 centimeters, but less than 150.5 centimeters? You may assume that the lengths are independent.

EXPLANATION

Let X denote the length of a handle, and let Y denote the length of a blade. We want to find

P(150<X+Y<150.5).

Since X and Y are independent random variables such that X∼N(120,0.14) and Y∼N(30,0.02), then

X+Y∼N(120+30,0.14+0.02)∼N(150,0.16).

So, the variable X+Y is normally distributed with mean μ=150 and standard deviation σ=0.16=0.4.

In order to find P(150<X+Y<150.5), we convert X+Y to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring: P(150<X+Y<150.5)=P(150−1500.4<Z<150.5−1500.4)≈P(0<Z<1.25)=P(Z≤1.25)−P(Z≤0)=Φ(1.25)−Φ(0)

Using the z-table, we see that

Φ(1.25)≈0.8944, and we know that Φ(0)=0.5.

Therefore,

P(150<X+Y<150.5)≈Φ(1.25)−Φ(0)≈0.8944−0.5≈0.3944.

So, we conclude that the probability that the length of a randomly selected shovel is more than 150 centimeters, but less than 150.5 centimeters is 0.3944.
