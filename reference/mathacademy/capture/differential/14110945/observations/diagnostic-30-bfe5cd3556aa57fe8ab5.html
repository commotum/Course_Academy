EXPLANATION

First of all, we need to find the slope of the normal line at the point (1,0). Remember that the slope of the normal line, m′, is the negative reciprocal of the slope of the tangent line:

m′=−1m

To find the slope of the tangent line, we will calculate the derivative using implicit differentiation, assuming that y=y(x).

ddx(xy+x2)=ddx(1)ddx(xy)+ddx(x2)=0xdydx+y+2x=0xdydx=−(2x+y)dydx=−2x+yx

So, the slope of the tangent at the point (1,0) is

m=(dydx)(1,0)=(−2x+yx)(1,0)=−2⋅1+01=−2.

Therefore, the slope of the normal line is m′=−1m=−1−2=12. We can write the equation of the normal at the point (1,0) as follows:

(y−y1)=m′(x−x1)(y−0)=12(x−1)y=12(x−1)

Now, we just need to find where this normal line intersects the y-axis. At the point P the x-coordinate is 0, so we set x=0 and solve for y:

y=12(x−1)y=12(0−1)y=−12

Therefore, the coordinates of P are (0,−12).
