The weights of apples in a certain orchard are normally distributed with a mean of 150g and a standard deviation of 5g. What is the probability that a randomly selected apple weighs more than 158g? Round your answer to two decimal places.

Hint: A table of values of Φ(z) for different values of z is given below, where Φ(z) is the cumulative distribution function for the standard normal distribution.

z 0.00 0.01 0.02
1.5 0.9332 0.9345 0.9357
1.6 0.9452 0.9463 0.9474
1.7 0.9554 0.9564 0.9573
The probability that a randomly selected apple weighs more than 158g is approximately
0.05

Correct
EXPLANATION

Let X represent the weight (in grams) of an apple. Then we have X∼N(150,52).

We want to find P(X>158). Thus, we convert X to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring:

P(X>158)=P(Z>158−1505)=P(Z>1.6)=1−P(Z≤1.6)=1−Φ(1.6)

From the z-table, we see that

1−Φ(1.6)=1−0.9452≈0.05.

Therefore, the probability that a randomly selected apple weighs more than 158g is approximately 0.05.
