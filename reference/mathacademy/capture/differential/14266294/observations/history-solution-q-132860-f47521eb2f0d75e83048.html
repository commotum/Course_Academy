Let X represent a randomly selected admission fee. Then we have X∼N(μ,0.752), and we want to find P(X<5). But first, we need to find the mean μ.

We know that P(0.7μ<X<1.3μ)=0.94. First, we convert this to a cumulative probability:

P(0.7μ<X<1.3μ)=0.942P(μ<X<1.3μ)=0.94P(μ<X<1.3μ)=0.47P(X<μ)+P(μ<X<1.3μ)=0.5+0.47P(X<1.3μ)=0.97

Next, we convert X to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring. We get

P(X<1.3μ)=0.97P(Z<0.3μ0.75)=0.97Φ(0.3μ0.75)=0.970.3μ0.75=Φ−1(0.97)0.4μ=Φ−1(0.97).

From the table,

Φ−1(0.97)≈1.8808.

Therefore, we have

0.4μ=1.8808μ=1.88080.4≈4.702.

Finally, we can solve for P(X<5) as follows:

P(X<5)=P(Z<5−4.702.0.75)≈P(Z<0.40)=Φ(0.40)=0.6554≈65.5%

Therefore, approximately in 65.5% of cases, the admission fee is lower than $5.
