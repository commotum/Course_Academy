EXPLANATION

Two discrete random variables X and Y with a joint probability mass function f(x,y) are independent if and only if f(x,y)=fX(x)⋅fY(y) for all possible values x and y, where fX(x) and fY(y) are the marginal probability mass functions for X and Y, respectively.

Computing the marginal probability mass functions for X and Y, we obtain the following:

f Y=2 Y=4 fX
X=1 0.7 0.1 0.8
X=3 0.1 0.1 0.2
fY 0.8 0.2 1

With that in mind, let's examine our statements.

  • Statements I and II are false. From the table, we obtain that P(X=3,Y=2)=f(3,2)=0.1. However, P(X=3)⋅P(Y=2)=fX(3)⋅fY(2)=0.2⋅0.8=0.16.× Therefore, P(X=3,Y=2)≠P(X=3)⋅P(Y=2), and P(X=x,Y=y)≠P(X=x)⋅P(Y=y) for all possible x and y.

  • Statement III is false. Since statement II is false, our random variables X and Y are not independent.

Therefore, the correct answer is "None of those listed."
