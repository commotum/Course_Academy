EXPLANATION

Two discrete random variables X and Y with a joint probability mass function f(x,y) are independent if and only if

f(x,y)=fX(x)⋅fY(y)

for all possible values x and y, where fX(x) and fY(y) are the marginal probability mass functions for X and Y, respectively.

With that in mind, let's examine our statements.

  • Statements I and II are false. From the table, we obtain that P(X=0,Y=1)=f(0,1)=0.32. However, P(X=0)⋅P(Y=1)=fX(0)⋅fY(1)=0.7⋅0.6=0.42.× Therefore, P(X=0,Y=1)≠P(X=0)⋅P(Y=1), and P(X=x,Y=y)≠P(X=x)⋅P(Y=y) for all possible x and y.

  • Statement III is false. Since statement II is false, our random variables X and Y are not independent.

Therefore, the correct answer is "None of the statements are true."
