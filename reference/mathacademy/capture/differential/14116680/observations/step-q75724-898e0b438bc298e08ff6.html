Which of the following statements are true?

  1. v=[−11] is an eigenvector of A=[−2−143] that corresponds to the eigenvalue λ=1.
  2. v=[−11] is an eigenvector of A=[1540] that corresponds to the eigenvalue λ=5.
  3. If v is an eigenvector of a matrix A corresponding to the eigenvalue λ=−3, then 2v is an eigenvector of A corresponding to λ=−3.
a
II only
b
None
c
I and II only
d
II and III only
e
III only
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

Remember that the eigenvectors of A are the non-zero vectors v such that Av=λv. With that in mind, let's examine the statements one by one.

  • Statement I is false. We have Av=[−2−143][−11]=[1−1]=−1⋅[−11]=−1v. Since Av=v, we conclude that v is an eigenvector of A and the corresponding eigenvalue is λ=−1 (but not 1).

  • Statement II is false. We have Av1=[1540][−11]=[4−4]=−4⋅[−11]=−4v1. Since Av=−4v, we conclude that v is an eigenvector of A and the corresponding eigenvalue is λ=−4 (but not 5).

  • Statement III is true. We have A(2v)=2Av=2(−3v)=−3(2v). Since, A(2v)=−3(2v), and 2v≠0, this implies that 2v is an eigenvector of A and the corresponding eigenvalue is λ=−3.

Note: In general, any non-zero multiple of an eigenvector is again an eigenvector, and the corresponding eigenvalue is the same.

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only".
