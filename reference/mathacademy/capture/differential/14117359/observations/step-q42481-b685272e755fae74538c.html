Given that det(A)=14, and I is the 3×3 identity matrix, which of the following statements are true?

  1. det(A)−det(3I)=−13
  2. −det(AT)+det(I)=−13
  3. det(A)−det(AT)=−28
a
I and II only
b
I and III only
c
All
d
II and III only
e
II only
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

From the properties of determinants, we know that det(AT)=det(A)=14 and det(I)=1.

Now, notice that

3I=3[100010001]=[300030003].

The determinant of a diagonal matrix is equal to the product of the entries on the main diagonal. Therefore, we have

|300030003|=33=27.

With that in mind, let's analyze each statement in turn.

  • Statement I is true. Indeed, det(A)−det(3I)=14−27=−13

  • Statement II is true. Indeed, −det(AT)+det(I)=−14+1=−13

  • Statement III is false. We have det(A)−det(AT)=14−14=0≠−28

In conclusion, the correct answer is "I and II only".
