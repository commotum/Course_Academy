The gradient of L with respect to A is a matrix of the same shape as A, with (i,j)th entry given by ∂L∂aij, the partial derivative of L with respect to the (i,j)th entry of A.

We can compute ∂L∂aij by applying the chain rule.

Let y=Ax−b∈R2. Then, L(A)=12yTy and

∂L∂aij=∑k=12∂L∂yk⋅∂yk∂aij

Now, since ∇yL=∇y(12yTy)=y,

we have that

∂L∂yk=yk.

Writing out y=Ax−b in component-wise notation as yi=∑j=13aijxj−bi, we have

  • ∂yk∂aij=xj if i=k, and

  • ∂yk∂aij=0 if i≠k.

This means that

∂L∂aij=∑k=12∂L∂yk⋅∂yk∂aij=∂L∂yi⋅∂yi∂aij=yi⋅∂yi∂aij=yixj.

The (i,j)th entry of ∇AL is the ith entry of y multiplied by the jth entry of x, so

∇AL=y⋅xT.

Finally, evaluating ∇AL at A=[120400], we get

∇AL=y⋅xT=(Ax−b)⋅xT=([120400][01020]−[78])⋅[01020]=([200]−[78])⋅[01020]=[13−8]⋅[01020]=[01302600−80−160].
