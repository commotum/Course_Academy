Suppose that X1,X2,…,Xn is a random sample of size n from a population with population mean μ and population standard deviation σ. The central limit theorem (or CLT) states that for sufficiently large n, the distribution of the sample mean X― can be approximated as

X―∼N(μ,σ2n).

In our case:

  • the sample size is n=80,

  • the population mean is μ=E[X]=∑i=1nxif(xi)=−1⋅0.3+0⋅0.5+1⋅0.1+2⋅0.1=−0.3+0+0.1+0.3=0,

  • the population variance is σ2=Var[X]=E[X2]−E[X]2=((−1)2⋅0.3+02⋅0.5+12⋅0.1+22⋅0.1)−02=(0.3+0+0.1+0.4)−0=0.8.

Therefore, by the CLT, we have that the distribution of the sample mean can be approximated as

X―∼N(μ,σ2n)∼N(0,0.880)∼N(0,0.01).

We want to find P(X―>0.1). So, we convert X― to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring:

P(X―>0.1)≈P(Z>0.1−00.01)=P(Z>1)=1−Φ(1).

Therefore, we conclude that

P(X―>0.1)=1−Φ(1).
