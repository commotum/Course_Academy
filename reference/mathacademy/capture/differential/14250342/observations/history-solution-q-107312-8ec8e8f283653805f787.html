Remember that the eigenvectors of A are the non-zero vectors v such that Av=λv. With that in mind, let's examine the statements one by one.

  • Statement is false. By the definition of an eigenvector, we have Av=13v. Multiplying it by A−1 from the left, we obtain v=13A−1v. Multiplying both sides by 3, we get A−1v=3v. So, v is an eigenvector of A−1 that corresponds to the eigenvalue 3.

Note: In general, an eigenvector of an invertible matrix A that corresponds to an eigenvalue λ is an eigenvector of A−1 that corresponds to the eigenvalue 1λ.

  • Statement II is true. According to the statement above, an eigenvector of A that corresponds to the eigenvalue λ=−2 is an eigenvector of A−1 that corresponds to λ=−12.

Note: In general, any non-zero multiple of an eigenvector is again an eigenvector, and the corresponding eigenvalue is the same.
