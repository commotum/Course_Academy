Let D be the event that a person has the disease, and

  • let A be the event that the person is from city A,

  • let B be the event that the person is from city B,

  • let C be the event that the person is from city C.

Then the disjoint union A⊔B⊔C covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability that a randomly selected person has the disease, we use the law of total probability:

P(D)=P(D∩A)+P(D∩B)+P(D∩C)

First, notice that there are a total of

100000+200000+500000=800000

people in all three cities.

  • To calculate P(D∩A), we take the number of people in city A who have the disease (500) and divide by the total number of people in the three cities (800000). So, we have P(D∩A)=500800000.

  • To calculate P(D∩B), we take the number of people in city B who have the disease (800) and divide by the total number of people in the three cities (800000). So, we have P(D∩B)=800800000.

  • To calculate P(D∩C), we take the number of people in city C who have the disease (1100) and divide by the total number of people in the three cities (800000). So, we have P(D∩C)=1100800000.

Finally, substituting the above information into the law of total probability, the probability that a randomly selected person from any city has the disease is

P(D)=P(D∩A)+P(D∩B)+P(D∩C)=500800000+800800000+1100800000=2400800000=248000=31000.
