Let V be the event that a randomly selected plant is poisonous, and

  • let A be the event that the plant is from forest A,

  • let B be the event that the plant is from forest B,

  • let C be the event that the plant is from forest C.

Then the disjoint union A⊔B⊔C covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability that a randomly selected plant is poisonous, we can use the law of total probability:

P(V)=P(V∩A)+P(V∩B)+P(V∩C)=P(V|A)P(A)+P(V|B)P(B)+P(V|C)P(C)

Let's go through the problem statement and translate each piece of information into mathematical notation.

  • Since forest A occupies 40% of the total park land and forest B occupies 25%, then forest C occupies the remaining 100%−(40%+25%)=35%. As a result, we have P(A)=0.4,P(B)=0.25,P(C)=0.35.

  • Since 30% of the plants in forest A and 20% of the plants in forest B are poisonous, we have P(V|A)=0.3,P(V|B)=0.2

  • Since the probability that a randomly selected plant from the park is poisonous is 0.24, we have P(V)=0.24.

Substituting the above information into the law of total probability and solving for P(V|C), we get

P(V)=P(V|A)P(A)+P(V|B)P(B)+P(V|C)P(C)0.24=(0.3)(0.4)+(0.2)(0.25)+P(V|C)(0.35)0.24=0.12+0.05+0.35P(V|C)0.35P(V|C)=0.07P(V|C)=0.2

Therefore, 20% of plants from forest C are poisonous.
