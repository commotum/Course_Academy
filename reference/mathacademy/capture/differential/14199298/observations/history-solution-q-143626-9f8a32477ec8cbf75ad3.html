Let B be the event that a randomly selected car breaks down during the first year, and

  • let C1 be the event that the car is of model C1,

  • let C2 be the event that the car is of model C2,

  • let C3 be the event that the car is of model C3.

Then the disjoint union C1⊔C2⊔C3 covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability that a randomly selected car will break down during the first year, we can use the law of total probability:

P(B)=P(B∩C1)+P(B∩C2)+P(B∩C3)=P(B|C1)P(C1)+P(B|C2)P(C2)+P(B|C3)P(C3)

Let's go through the problem statement and translate each piece of information into mathematical notation.

  • Since 40% of the cars produced by the company are model C1, and 50% are model C2, the model C3 cars make up the remaining 100%−(40%+35%)=25%. As a result, we have P(C1)=0.4,P(C2)=0.35,P(C3)=0.25.

  • Since 6% of the cars of model C1, 4% of those of model C2, and 2% of the cars of model C3 break down during the first year, we have P(B|C1)=0.06,P(B|C2)=0.04,P(B|C3)=0.02.

Substituting the above information into the law of total probability, the probability that a randomly selected car breaks down during the first year is

P(B)=P(B|C1)P(C1)+P(B|C2)P(C2)+P(B|C3)P(C3)=(0.06)(0.4)+(0.04)(0.35)+(0.02)(0.25)=0.024+0.014+0.005=0.043.
