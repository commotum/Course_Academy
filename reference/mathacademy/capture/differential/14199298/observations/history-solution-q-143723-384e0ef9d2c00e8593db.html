Let F be the event that a person selected was a female.

  • let A1 be the event that the person was under 18,

  • let A2 be the event that the person was at least 18 but under 30,

  • let A3 be the event that the person was at least 30 or older.

Then the disjoint union A1⊔A2⊔A3 covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability that a randomly selected person was a female, we use the law of total probability:

P(F)=P(F∩A1)+P(F∩A2)+P(F∩A3)

We have

P(F∩A1)=0.25,P(F∩A2)=0.2P(F∩A3)=0.15.

Finally, substituting the above information into the law of total probability, the probability that a randomly selected person is a female is

P(F)=P(F∩A1)+P(F∩A2)+P(F∩A3)=0.25+0.2+0.15=0.6.
