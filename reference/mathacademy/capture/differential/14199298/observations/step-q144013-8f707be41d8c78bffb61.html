Three similar bags, A,B, and C, contain balls. Bag A contains 4 red and 6 green balls, and bag B contains 6 red and 4 green balls. A bag is selected at random, and a ball is randomly selected from the bag. If the probability that the selected ball is green is 35, what proportion of balls from bag C are green?

a
45
b
25
c

EXPLANATION

Let G be the event of selecting a green ball at random from one of the three bags, and

  • let A be the event that the ball is from bag A,

  • let B be the event that the ball is from B,

  • let C be the event that the ball is from bag C.

Then the disjoint union A⊔B⊔C covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability of selecting a green ball, we can use the law of total probability:

P(G)=P(G∩A)+P(G∩B)+P(G∩C)=P(G|A)P(A)+P(G|B)P(B)+P(G|C)P(C)

Let's go through the problem statement and translate each piece of information into mathematical notation.

  • Since a bag is selected at random, we have P(A)=P(B)=P(C)=13.

  • Since there are 6 green balls out of 10 in bag A, and 4 green balls out of 10 in bag B, we have P(G|A)=610,P(G|B)=410.

  • Since the probability of choosing a green ball is 35, we have P(G)=35.

Substituting the above information into the law of total probability and solving for P(G|C), we get

P(G)=P(G|A)P(A)+P(G|B)P(B)+P(G|C)P(C)35=(610)(13)+(410)(13)+P(G|C)(13)35=15+215+(13)P(G|C)13⋅P(G|C)=35−15−21513⋅P(G|C)=415P(G|C)=45.

Therefore, 45 of the balls in bag C are green.
