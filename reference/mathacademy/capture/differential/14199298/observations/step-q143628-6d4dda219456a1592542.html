Mike has a total of 100 candies divided into three bags. The first bag contains 18 mint-flavored candies, while the second bag contains 16 mint-flavored candies. If 55% of all of Mike's candies are mint-flavored, what is the probability that a randomly selected candy is a mint-flavored candy from the third bag?

20%

EXPLANATION

Let M be the event that the selected candy is mint-flavored, and

  • let A be the event that the selected candy is from the first bag,

  • let B be the event that the selected candy is from the second bag,

  • let C be the event that the selected candy is from the third bag.

Then the disjoint union A⊔B⊔C covers the entire sample space.

Recall that the law of total probability states that

P(M)=P(M∩A)+P(M∩B)+P(M∩C).

We wish to find P(M∩C):

  • To calculate P(M∩A), we take the number of mint-flavored candies in the first bag (18) and divide by the total number of candies (100). So, we have P(M∩A)=18100.

  • To calculate P(M∩B), we take the number of mint-flavored candies in the second bag (16) and divide by the total number of candies(100). So, we have P(M∩B)=16100.

  • Also, we're given that P(M)=55%=55100.

Finally, substituting the above information into the law of total probability and solving for P(M∩C), we get the following:

P(M)=P(M∩A)+P(M∩B)+P(M∩C)55100=18100+16100+P(M∩C)P(M∩C)=55100−18100−16100P(M∩C)=21100P(M∩C)=21%
