Let V be the event that the plant is poisonous, and

  • let A be the event that the plant is from forest A,

  • let B be the event that the plant is from forest B,

  • let C be the event that the plant is from forest C.

Then the disjoint union A⊔B⊔C covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability that a randomly selected plant is poisonous, we can use the law of total probability:

P(V)=P(V∩A)+P(V∩B)+P(V∩C)=P(V|A)P(A)+P(V|B)P(B)+P(V|C)P(C)

Let's go through the problem statement and translate each piece of information into mathematical notation.

  • Since forest A occupies 40% of the park area and forest B occupies 25%, then forest C occupies the remaining 100%−(40%+25%)=35%. As a result, we have P(A)=0.4,P(B)=0.25,P(C)=0.35.

  • Since 30% of the plants in forest A, 20% of the plants in forest B, and 6% of the plants in forrest C are poisonous, we have P(V|A)=0.3,P(V|B)=0.2,P(V|C)=0.06.

Substituting the above information into the law of total probability, the probability that a randomly selected plant in that park is poisonous is

P(V)=P(V|A)P(A)+P(V|B)P(B)+P(V|C)P(C)=(0.3)(0.4)+(0.2)(0.25)+(0.06)(0.35)=0.12+0.05+0.021=0.191.
