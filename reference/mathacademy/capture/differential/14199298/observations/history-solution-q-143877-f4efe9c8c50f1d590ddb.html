Let B be the event that a randomly selected car breaks down during the first year, and

  • let M1 be the event that the car is model M1,

  • let M2 be the event that the car is model M2,

  • let M3 be the event that the car is model M3.

Then the disjoint union M1⊔M2⊔M3 covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability that a randomly selected car will break down during the first year, we can use the law of total probability:

P(B)=P(B∩M1)+P(B∩M2)+P(B∩M3)=P(B|M1)P(M1)+P(B|M2)P(M2)+P(B|M3)P(M3)

Let's go through the problem statement and translate each piece of information into mathematical notation.

  • Since 40% of cars are model M1,, 35% are model M2, and 25% are model M3, we have P(M1)=0.4,P(M2)=0.35,P(M3)=0.25.

  • Since 6% of model M1 and 4% of model M2 break down during the first year, we have P(B|M1)=0.06,P(B|M2)=0.04.

  • Since the probability that a randomly selected car breaks down during the first year is 0.045, we have P(B)=0.045.

Substituting the above information into the law of total probability and solving for P(B|M3), we get

P(B)=P(B|M1)P(M1)+P(B|M2)P(M2)+P(B|M3)P(M3)0.045=(0.06)(0.4)+(0.04)(0.35)+P(B|M3)(0.25)0.045=0.024+0.014+0.25P(B|M3)0.25P(B|M3)=0.007P(B|M3)=0.028=2.8%

Therefore, 2.8% of model M3 cars break down during the first year.
