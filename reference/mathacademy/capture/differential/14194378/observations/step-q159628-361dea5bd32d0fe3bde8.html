Consider a sample of size n1=54 from a population with standard deviation σ1=3.7 and a sample of size n2=77 from a population with standard deviation σ2=1.2. Given that the samples are independent and the sample means are x―1=23 and x―2=12, find a 99% confidence interval for the difference μ1−μ2 between the corresponding population means.

EXPLANATION

Notice that

  • the samples are independent,

  • both sample sizes are "sufficiently large," i.e., ≥30, and

  • both population variances are known.

As a result, given a value α between 0 and 1, the corresponding [100(1−α)]% confidence interval for the difference μ1−μ2 between the corresponding population means can be written as

(x―1−x―2)±E

where

  • E=zα/2σ12n1+σ22n2 is the margin of error,

  • x―1 and x―2 are the sample means, μ1 and μ2 are the population means, σ12 and σ22 are the population variances, n1 and n2 are the sample sizes,

  • P(Z>zα/2)=α2 and Z∼N(0,1).

Now, to construct the confidence interval, we proceed as follows:

  • Step 1. Find the point estimate: x―1−x―2=23−12=11

  • Step 2. Find the margin of error. Since we are interested in finding a 99% confidence interval, we have α=1−0.99=0.01⟹α2=0.005. We are given that P(Z>2.576)=0.005. As a result, zα/2=z0.005=2.576. Now, we can compute the margin of error: E=2.576⋅3.7254+1.2277≈1.344

  • Step 3. Construct the 99% confidence interval for the difference between the population means: 11±1.344
