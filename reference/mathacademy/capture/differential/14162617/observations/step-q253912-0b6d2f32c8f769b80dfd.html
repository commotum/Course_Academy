Consider the following set. A={−1+(710)n:n∈N}For this set, we have that
inf(A)=−1∉A
inf(A)=−310∈A
inf(A) does not exist
inf(A)=−1∈A
inf(A)=−1∉A
inf(A)=−310∉A
.

Correct
EXPLANATION

The infimum of A is the greatest lower bound of A.

To find the infimum of this set, we must find the set containing all lower bounds of A (i.e., the set of lower bounds) and take the maximum of this set.

The elements of A are the terms of the sequence an, given by

an=−1+(710)n,n∈N={1,2,3,4,…}.

Let's draw some points of our sequence on the real line:

We see that an is decreasing and converges to −1.

So, in our case, we have that

  • any number in the set x≤−1 is a lower bound of A,

  • any number in the set x>−1 is not a lower bound of A. In other words, we can always find a sufficiently large n so that −1+(710)n is smaller than x for any x>−1.

Therefore, (−∞,−1] is the set of lower bounds of A, and

inf(A)=max(−∞,−1]=−1. Notice that −1 is not the minimum of A (the set A has no minimum). Therefore, we conclude that

inf(A)=−1∉A.
