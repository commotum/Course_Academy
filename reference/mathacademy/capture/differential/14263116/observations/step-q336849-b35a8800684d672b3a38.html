EXPLANATION

Given a normal random variable X∼N(μ,σ2), we have

P(a<X<b)=P(a−μσ<Z<b−μσ)

where Z∼N(0,1) is a standard normal random variable. In our case, we have μ=−3 and σ=10.

First, we transform X into a standard normal random variable Z by z-scoring:

P(−0.7<X<16.3)=P(−0.7−(−3)10<Z<16.3−(−3)10)=P(0.23<Z<1.93).

Now, we express the required probability in terms of Φ(z):

P(0.23<Z<1.93)=P(Z<1.93)−P(Z≤0.23)=P(Z≤1.93)−P(Z≤0.23)=Φ(1.93)−Φ(0.23).

Φ(1.93)=0.9732Φ(0.23)=0.5910

Therefore, we have

P(0.23<Z<1.93)=Φ(1.93)−Φ(0.23)=0.9732−0.5910=0.3822.

P(−0.7<X<16.3)=0.3822.
