Which of the following probabilities could be approximated using the normal approximation of the binomial distribution?

  1. The probability of finding more than 120 people with blood type O+ in a randomly selected sample of 200 if 53% of the population has blood type O+.
  2. The probability of finding less than 4 defective items in a random sample of 100 items produced by a machine if 2% of the items made are defective.
  3. The probability that a biased coin lands on heads 9 times out of 15 coin flips if the probability of a single flip landing on heads is 0.25.
a
II and III only
b
II only
c
I only
d
III only
e
I and II only

Correct
EXPLANATION

Given a binomial random variable X∼B(n,p), if

  • np>5, and

  • n(1−p)>5,

we can approximate X as a normal random variable Y with mean μ=np and variance σ2=np(1−p), that is,

Y∼N(np,np(1−p)).

Let's now examine each situation.

  • In situation I, we have n=200 and p=0.53. Therefore, np=200⋅0.53=106>5✓n(1−p)=200⋅(1−0.53)=94>5.✓ So, we can use the normal approximation of the binomial distribution.

  • In situation II, we have n=100 and p=0.02. Therefore, np=100⋅0.02=2≯5.× So, we cannot use the normal approximation of the binomial distribution.

  • In situation III, we have n=15 and p=0.25. Therefore, np=15⋅0.25=3.75≯5.× So, we cannot use the normal approximation of the binomial distribution.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only."
