The weights of some chocolate cookies are normally distributed with a mean of 10g and a variance of 1g2. On the other hand, the weights of some oatcakes are normally distributed with a mean of 15g and a variance of 2g2, while the weights of some macaroons are normally distributed with a mean of 14g and a variance of 1.5g2.

EXPLANATION

Let X1, X2, and X3 denote the weight of the chocolate cookie, the oatcake, and the macaroon, respectively. Then, we have

X1∼N(10,1),X2∼N(15,2),X3∼N(14,1.5).

Let the random variable Y denote the difference between the sum of 6 times the weight of the cookie and 5 times the weight of the oatcake, and 10 times the weight of the macaroon. Then, we have Y=6X1+5X2−10X3, and we want to find

P(Y<0).

The random variable Y is normally distributed, where

E[Y]=(6⋅10+4⋅15−10⋅14)=−5Var[Y]=62⋅1+52⋅2+102⋅1.5=36+50+150=236.

Therefore, Y∼N(−5,236).

In order to find P(Y<0), we convert Y to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring: P(Y<0)=P(Z<0−(−5)236)≈P(Z<0.33)=Φ(0.33)

Using the z-table, we see that

Φ(0.33)≈0.6293.

Therefore,

P(Y<0)≈Φ(0.33)≈0.6293.
