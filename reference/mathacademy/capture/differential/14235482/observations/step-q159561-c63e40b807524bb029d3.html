EXPLANATION

We use the following notations:

  • y=β0+β1x is the regression line that best fits the population, and

  • y^=β^0+β^1x is the estimate of the "true" regression line given by the data in our sample.

In this case, we have that

y^=β^0+β^1x=2.4+0.2x.

We are told that all assumptions for a simple regression model are held. So, given a value α between 0 and 1, the corresponding [100(1−α)]% confidence interval for the y-intercept β0 of our model can be written as

β^0⏟pointestimate±tn−2,α/2⋅SE[β^0]⏞margin of error.

Note the following:

  • β^0 is our point estimate for the intercept, computed using the data points from our sample.

  • P(T>tn−2,α/2)=α2, where T has a student's t-distribution with n−2 degrees of freedom.

  • SE[β^0] is the standard error of β^0, given by SE[β^0]=MSE⋅(1n+x―2Sxx) where we have:

    • MSE is the mean squared error of the model: MSE=1n−2∑i=1n(yi−y^i)2 where y^i is the estimate of yi given by our regression model.

    • Sxx is the usual sum of squares formula: Sxx=∑i=1n(xi−x―)2

  • Step 1. Find the point estimate. From the regression equation y^=2.4+0.2x, our point estimate is
    β^0=2.4.

  • Step 2. Find the margin of error. Since we are interested in finding a 95% confidence interval, we have α=1−0.95=0.05⟹α2=0.025. We are given that P(T>2.228)=0.025. As a result, tn−2,α/2=t10,0.025=2.228. Now, we can compute the margin of error: tn−2,α/2⋅SE[β^0]=2.228⋅0.7⋅(112+1.2218.9)≈0.7
