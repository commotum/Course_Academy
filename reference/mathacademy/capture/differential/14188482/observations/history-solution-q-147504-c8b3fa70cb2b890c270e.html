Given a population in which a proportion p of the population has a particular characteristic, the sampling distribution of the sample proportion p^ can be approximated as

p^∼N(p,p(1−p)n),

where the sample size n is much smaller than the population size, np>5, and n(1−p)>5.

In our case

  • the sample size is n=65, and

  • the population proportion is p=0.35.

Now, since np=65(0.35)=22.75>5 and n(1−p)=65(1−0.35)=42.25>5, and n=65 is much smaller than the population size, we can apply the above approximation.

The sampling distribution of the sample proportion, in this case, can be approximated as

p^∼N(p,p(1−p)n)∼N(0.35,0.35(1−0.35)65)∼N(0.35,0.0035).

Next, we determine the probability we wish to find. If more than 13 but fewer than 26 people bought something, then

1365<p^<26650.2<p^<0.4.

Hence, the probability we wish to find is P(0.2≤p^≤0.4). So, we convert p^ to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring:

P(0.2≤p^≤0.4)=P(0.2−0.350.0035<Z<0.4−0.350.0035)≈P(−2.54<Z<0.85)=P(Z<0.85)−P(Z<−2.54)=Φ(0.85)−Φ(−2.54)

Therefore,

P(0.2≤p^≤0.4)=Φ(0.85)−Φ(−2.54).
