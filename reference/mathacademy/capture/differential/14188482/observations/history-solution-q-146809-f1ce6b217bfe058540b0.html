Given a population in which a proportion p of the population has a particular characteristic, the sampling distribution of the sample proportion p^ can be approximated as

p^∼N(p,p(1−p)n),

where

  • n≤5%⋅N (the sample size is less than (or equal to) 5% of the population size),

  • np>5 (more than 5 sample elements have the characteristic), and

  • n(1−p)>5 (more than 5 sample elements do not have the characteristic).

Let's now examine each situation.

  • In situation I, we have n=52,5%⋅N=0.05⋅120=6<52.× Since the sample size is more than 5% of the population size, we cannot approximate the distribution of p^ using the given model.

  • In situation II, we have n=4≤5%⋅N=0.05⋅200=10✓np=4(0.7)=2.8≯5.× Since np≯5, we cannot approximate the distribution of p^ using the given model.

  • In situation III, we have n=20≤5%⋅N=0.05⋅500=25✓np=20(0.70)=14>5✓n(1−p)=20(1−0.70)=6>5.✓ Therefore, we can approximate the distribution of p^ using the given model.

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only."
