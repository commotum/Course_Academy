EXPLANATION

Given a population in which a proportion p of the population has a particular characteristic, the sampling distribution of the sample proportion p^ can be approximated as

p^∼N(p,p(1−p)n),

where the sample size n is much smaller than the population size, np>5, and n(1−p)>5.

In our case

  • the sample size is n=300, and

  • the population proportion is p=150000500000=0.3.

Now, since np=300(0.3)=90>5 and n(1−p)=300(1−0.3)=210>5, and the n=300 is much smaller than the population size, we can apply the above approximation.

The sampling distribution of the sample proportion, in this case, can be approximated as

p^∼N(p,p(1−p)n)∼N(0.3,0.3(1−0.3)300)∼N(0.3,0.0007).

Next, we determine the probability we wish to find. If between 72 and 90 of users in the sample have a paid account, then

72300≤p^≤903000.24≤p^≤0.3.

Hence, the probability we wish to find is P(0.24≤p^≤0.3). So, we convert p^ to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring:

P(0.24≤p^≤0.3)=P(0.24−0.30.0007≤Z≤0.3−0.30.0007)≈P(−2.27≤Z≤0)=P(Z≤0)−P(Z≤−2.27)=Φ(0)−Φ(−2.27)

From the table, we have

Φ(0)=0.5000,Φ(−2.27)=0.0116.

Therefore,

P(0.24≤p^≤0.3)=Φ(0)−Φ(−2.27)=0.5000−0.0116=0.4884.
