EXPLANATION

Two discrete random variables X and Y with a joint probability mass function f(x,y) are independent if and only if

f(x,y)=fX(x)⋅fY(y)

for all possible values x and y, where fX(x) and fY(y) are the marginal probability mass functions for X and Y, respectively.

With that in mind, let's examine our statements.

  • Statement I is true. From the table, we obtain that P(X=0,Y=−2)=f(0,−2)=14. On the other hand, P(X=0)⋅P(Y=−2)=fX(0)⋅fY(−2)=23⋅38=14.✓

  • Statement II is true. Checking all other possible products, we obtain the following: f(0,2)=512=23⋅58=fX(0)⋅fY(2)✓f(3,−2)=18=13⋅38=fX(3)⋅fY(−2)✓f(3,2)=524=13⋅58=fX(3)⋅fY(2)✓ So, P(X=x,Y=y)=P(X=x)⋅P(Y=y) for all possible x and y.

  • Statement III is true. Since statement II is true, our random variables X and Y must be independent.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I, II, and III."
