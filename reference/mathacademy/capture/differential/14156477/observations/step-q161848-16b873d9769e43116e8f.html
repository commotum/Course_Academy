Let f:U→R be continuously differentiable on an open set U⊆R such that f(a)=b and f′(a)≠0 for a∈U. Which of the following statements must be true?

  1. The function f is invertible in a neighborhood of a.
  2. The inverse f−1 is continuously differentiable in a neighborhood of b.
  3. The derivative of f−1 is given by (f−1)′(b)=f′(a).
a

b

c

d
None of the statements are true

EXPLANATION

The inverse function theorem for single-variable functions states the following:

If f:U→R is continuously differentiable on an open set U⊆R, f(a)=b and f′(a)≠0 for a∈U, then

  • f is invertible in some neighborhood of a,

  • f−1, the inverse of f, is continuously differentiable in a neighborhood of b, and

  • the derivative of f−1 is given by (f−1)′(b)=1f′(a)=1f′(f−1(b)).

With that in mind, let's examine our statements in turn.

  • Statement I is true. According to the theorem, the function f must be invertible in some neighborhood of a.

  • Statement II is true. Indeed, the inverse f−1 must be continuously differentiable in a neighborhood of b.

  • Statement III is false. According to the theorem, we must have (f−1)′(b)=1f′(a).

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and II only."
