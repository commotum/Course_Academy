EXPLANATION

We do not know the distribution of the population, nor do we know the population variance σ2. However, the sample size n=100≥30 is sufficiently large.

In cases like this, the random variable

X―−μS/n

can be approximated by the student's t-distribution Tn−1 with n−1 degrees of freedom, where

  • X― is the sample mean,

  • μ is the population mean,

  • S2 is the sample variance, and

  • n is the sample size.

Given a value α between 0 and 1, the corresponding [100(1−α)]% confidence interval for the population mean μ is given by

x―±tn−1,α/2⋅sn,

where P(T>tn−1,α/2)=α2, and T has a student's t-distribution with n−1 degrees of freedom.

In our case,

x―=3.1,n=100,s=0.48.

We are interested in finding a 90% confidence interval. So, we have

α=1−0.90=0.1⟹α2=0.05.

We also need to find the t-score value t99,0.05 such that P(T>t99,0.05)=0.05, where T has a student's t-distribution with n−1=100−1=99 degrees of freedom.

From the percentage points table of the t-distribution, we obtain that t99,0.05=1.660:
