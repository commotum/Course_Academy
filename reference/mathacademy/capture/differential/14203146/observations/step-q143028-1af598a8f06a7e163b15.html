EXPLANATION

We are told that the population is normally distributed, but we do not know the population variance σ.

In cases like this, the random variable

X―−μS/n

follows a student's t-distribution Tn−1 with n−1 degrees of freedom, where

  • X― is the sample mean,

  • μ is the population mean,

  • S2 is the sample variance, and

  • n is the sample size.

Given a value α between 0 and 1, the corresponding [100(1−α)]% confidence interval for the population mean μ of the original distribution is given by

x―±tn−1,α/2⋅sn,

where P(T>tn−1,α/2)=α2, and T has a student's t-distribution with n−1 degrees of freedom.

In our case,

x―=174ft,n=25,s=4ft.

We are interested in finding a 90% confidence interval. So, we have

α=1−0.9=0.1⟹α2=0.05.
