EXPLANATION

We must find the maximum value of f(x,y) subject to the constraint g(x,y)=x2+2y2−2=0.

Since f(x,y) is continuous and the constraint is closed and bounded, f(x,y) reaches a global maximum and a global minimum on the constraint.

Therefore, by the method of Lagrange multipliers, we must solve the following system: ∇f(x,y)=λ∇g(x,y),g(x,y)=0

We start by solving the first equation: ∇f(x,y)=λ∇g(x,y)⟨fx(x,y),fy(x,y)⟩=λ⟨gx(x,y),gy(x,y)⟩⟨2x,2y+4⟩=λ⟨2x,4y⟩

Therefore, we have the following:

{2x=2λx2y+4=4λy⇒{x(1−λ)=0y(2λ−1)=2

To solve this system, we break it up into several cases:

  • Assuming x=0 in the first equation of the system, the constraint condition x2+2y2−2=0 implies 2y2−2=0⇒y=±1. This gives the critical points (0,−1),(0,1).

  • Assuming λ=1 in the first equation of the system, the second equation of the system implies y(2(1)−1)=2⇒y=2. Then, the constraint condition x2+2y2−2=0 implies x2=−6. which gives a contradiction.

Therefore, f has possible extreme values at the points (0,−1),(0,1).

We evaluate f at these points. These are given in the following table:

(x,y) (0,−1) (0,1)
f(x,y) 1 9

Therefore, the function reaches a maximum value at the point (0,1) and its maximum value is 9.
