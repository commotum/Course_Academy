Let the random variable X be the number of defective tested bulbs.

Here, we have the following information:

  • there are n=6 tested bulbs

  • the probability of choosing a defective bulb is p=5%=0.05

So, X follows a binomial distribution, where X∼B(6,0.05).

The number of successes of a binomial random variable has the probability mass function

P(X=x)=(nx)px(1−p)n−x,

where n is the number of experiments, and p is the probability of success on each experiment.

Substituting the above information into the formula for probability mass function, we get

P(X=x)=(6x)(0.05)x(1−0.05)6−x=(6x)(0.05)x(0.95)6−x.

We want to find the probability that at least two bulbs are defective. This corresponds to P(X≥2).

However, computing

P(X≥2)=P(X=2)+P(X=3)+…+P(X=6)

will involve many computations, so let's express our answer in terms of the complement instead. By doing this, we get

P(X≥2)=1−P(X<2)=1−[P(X=0)+P(X=1)]=1−[(60)(0.05)0(0.95)6−0+(61)(0.05)1(0.95)6−1]=1−[1(0.05)0(0.95)6+6(0.05)1(0.95)5]=1−0.967226≈0.033

rounded to 3 decimal places.
