Which of the following random variables could be modeled as a binomial distribution?

  1. The number of students who got an A on a math exam in a class of 25 students
  2. The number of cards drawn until a red card is obtained
  3. The number of books published by a publishing house in a month
a
 I only
b
 II and III only
c
 I and III only
d
III only
e
 II only
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

A binomial distribution models the number of successes in a sequence of n independent experiments (where each outcome is either success or failure).

With that in mind, let's consider each of the given random variables.

  • Random variable I could be modeled as a binomial distribution. The number of students who got an A on a math exam can be interpreted as the number of successes in a sequence of 25 independent experiments.

  • Random variable II could not be modeled as a binomial distribution. Although drawing a red card can be interpreted as a "success," there is not a fixed number of trials in this experiment.

  • Random variable III could not be modeled as a binomial distribution. The number of books published cannot be interpreted as "success" or "failure."

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only."
