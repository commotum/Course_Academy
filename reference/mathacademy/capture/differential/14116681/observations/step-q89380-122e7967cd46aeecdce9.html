Incorrect
EXPLANATION

Let the random variable X be equal to the number of times that a 1 or a 2 is rolled.

Here, we have the following information:

  • there are n=9 rolls

  • the probability of getting a 1 or 2 on any roll of the die is p=25

So, X follows a binomial distribution, where X∼B(9,25). We want to find the probability of x=5 successes.

The number of successes of a binomial random variable has the probability mass function

P(X=x)=(nx)px(1−p)n−x,

where n is the number of experiments, and p is the probability of success on each experiment.

Substituting the above information into the formula for probability mass function, we get

P(X=5)=(95)(25)5(1−25)9−5=9!5!(9−5)!(25)5(35)4=126(25)5(35)4=0.167

rounded to 3 decimal places.
