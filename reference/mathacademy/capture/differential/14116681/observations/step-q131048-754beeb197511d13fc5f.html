A box of chocolates contains cherry creams and almond little cakes in the ratio of 3:2. Ten chocolates are selected at random, with replacement. What is the probability of getting at most 9 cherry creams? Round your answer to 3 decimal places.

a
 0.927
b
 0.846
c
 0.040
d
 0.994
e
 0.135
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

Let the random variable X be the number of selected cherry creams.

Here, we have the following information:

  • there are n=10 selected chocolates

  • since cherry creams and almond little cakes are in the ratio of 3:2, the probability of selecting a cherry cream from the box is p=35

So, X follows a binomial distribution, where X∼B(10,35).

The number of successes of a binomial random variable has the probability mass function

P(X=x)=(nx)px(1−p)n−x,

where n is the number of experiments, and p is the probability of success on each experiment.

Substituting the above information into the formula for probability mass function, we get

P(X=x)=(10x)(35)x(1−35)10−x=(10x)(35)x(25)10−x.

We want to find the probability of getting at most 9 cherry creams. This corresponds to P(X≤9).

However, computing

P(X≤9)=P(X=0)+P(X=1)+…+P(X=9)

will involve many computations, so let's express our answer in terms of the complement instead. By doing this, we get

P(X≤9)=1−P(X>9)=1−P(X=10)=1−(1010)(35)10(25)10−10=1−1(35)10(25)0=1−0.006047≈0.994

rounded to 3 decimal places.
