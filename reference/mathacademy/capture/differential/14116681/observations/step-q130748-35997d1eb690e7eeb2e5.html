70% of union members are in favor of a specific change to their conditions of employment. A random sample of 6 members is taken. What is the probability that at most 2 members are in favor of the changed conditions? Round your answer to 3 decimal places.

a
 0.989
b
 0.070

 0.173
d
 0.059
e
 0.272

Correct
EXPLANATION

Let the random variable X be the number of members in the sample in favor of the change.

Here, we have the following information:

  • there are n=6 members

  • since 70% of union members are in favor of the change, the probability of selecting a member in favor of the change is p=70%=0.7

So, X follows a binomial distribution, where X∼B(6,0.7).

The number of successes of a binomial random variable has the probability mass function

P(X=x)=(nx)px(1−p)n−x,

where n is the number of experiments, and p is the probability of success on each experiment.

Substituting the above information into the formula for probability mass function, we get

P(X=x)=(6x)(0.7)x(1−0.7)6−x=(6x)(0.7)x(0.3)6−x.

We want to find the probability that at most two members are in favor of the changed conditions. So, we compute

P(X≤2)=P(X=0)+P(X=1)+P(X=2)=(60)(0.7)0(0.3)6−0+(61)(0.7)1(0.3)6−1+(62)(0.7)2(0.3)6−2=1(0.7)0(0.3)6+6(0.7)1(0.3)5+15(0.7)2(0.3)4≈0.070

rounded to 3 decimal places.
