A sports shop sells table tennis balls in boxes of 6. The weight of a table tennis ball, in grams, is normally distributed with a mean of 2.72g and a standard deviation of 0.05g, while the weight of a box, in grams, is normally distributed with a mean of 12g and a standard deviation of 2g. If one box full of balls is randomly selected, what is the probability that it weighs less than 30g? You may assume that the weights are mutually independent.

EXPLANATION

Let Xi denote the weight of the ith ball, and let Y denote the weight of a box. Then, we have

Xi∼N(2.72,0.052),1≤i≤6, and Y∼N(12,22).

Let the random variable W denote the weight of a randomly selected box of balls. Then, since

W=X1+X2+⋯+X6+Y,

we have that W is normally distributed, where

E[W]=6⋅2.72+12=28.32Var[W]=6⋅0.052+22=4.015.

Therefore,

W∼N(28.32,4.015).

In order to find P(W<30), we convert W to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring: P(W<30)=P(Z<30−28.324.015)≈P(Z<0.84)=Φ(0.84)

Using the z-table, we see that

Φ(0.84)≈0.7995.

Therefore,

P(W<30)≈Φ(0.84)≈0.7995.
