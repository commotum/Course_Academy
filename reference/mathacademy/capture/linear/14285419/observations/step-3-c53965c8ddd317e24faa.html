Explanation

A critical point of f(x) is a point x=c in the domain of f such that either f′(c)=0 or f′(c) does not exist.

Previously, we found that

f′(x)=−6x2+12x+18.

We now proceed to find the critical points:

  • First, we compute the points where f′(c)=0. We set the derivative equal to zero, and solve for x: −6x2+12x+18=0x2−2x−3=0(x+1)(x−3)=0x=−1,3 Therefore, f(x) has stationary points at x=−1 and x=3.

  • Second, we note that f′(x) exists for all values of x in the domain of f(x). So f(x) has no non-stationary critical points.

Therefore, we conclude that the critical points of f(x) are x=x1=−1 and x=x2=3.

Finally,

x1⋅x2=−1⋅3=−3.
