Given a polynomial with integer coefficients, the rational roots theorem states that any rational roots of the polynomial must take the form

±factor of the constant termfactor of the leading coefficient.

For the given polynomial:

  • The constant term is −16, and its factors are ±1,±2,±4,±8 and ±16.

  • The leading coefficient is 4, and its factors are ±1,±2 and ±4.

First, we list out all the possibilities for the numerator and denominator:

±11,±12,±14,±21,±22,±24,±41,±42,±44,±81,±82,±84,±161,±162,±164.

Next, we reduce the fractions to lowest terms:

±1,±12,±14,±2,±1,±12,±4,±2,±1,±8,±4,±2,±16,±8,±4.

Notice that some potential roots are duplicates (shown above in red). Removing these duplicates, and listing those remaining in order from least to greatest in absolute value, we have

±14,±12±1,±2,±4,±8,±16.

This makes a total of 14 potential roots. (Remember that each ± sign accounts for 2 roots, and 2⋅7=14.)
