Explanation

The factor theorem states that (x−r) is a factor of a polynomial p(x) if and only if the root x=r is also a root of p(x).

Here, we consider the numbers 6, 9, and 10 as possible roots. Let's check each of these values in turn.

  • x=6 is not a root of p(x):

f(6)=(6)3+3(6)2−972=216+108−972=−648≠0

  • x=9 is a root of p(x):

f(9)=(9)3+3(9)2−972=729+243−972=972−972=0

  • x=10 is not a root of p(x):

f(10)=(10)3+3(10)2−972=1000+300−972=328≠0

Therefore, by the factor theorem, (x−9) is a factor of p(x).
