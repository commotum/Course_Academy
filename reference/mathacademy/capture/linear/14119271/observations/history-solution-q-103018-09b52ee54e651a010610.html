The division property of modular arithmetic states that

ka≡kb(modn)⟺a≡b(modn)

provided that k and n are coprime. In other words, we require gcd(k,n)=1.

In this case, the modulus n=10. Let's consider each value of k in turn.

  • If k=12, then gcd(k,n)=gcd(12,10)=2≠1. Therefore, our equivalence is not valid for k=12.

  • If k=3, then gcd(k,n)=gcd(3,10)=1. Therefore, our equivalence is valid for k=3.

  • If k=−5, then gcd(k,n)=gcd(−5,10)=5≠1. Therefore, our equivalence is not valid for k=−5.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II only."
