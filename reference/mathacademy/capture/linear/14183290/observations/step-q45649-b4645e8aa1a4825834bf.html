An economic study in Chile showed that its gross domestic product (GDP) increases fourfold each decade. If the GDP was $0.5 billion in 2010, how many decades will it take for the GDP to increase to $512 billion?

a
3
b
5
c
4.5
d

Correct
EXPLANATION

We know that the GDP increases fourfold each decade. So the GDP, which we'll call y(t), grows exponentially with a growth factor of 4.

The formula for exponential growth (in terms of a growth factor b) is

y=abt.

From the given information, we have the following:

  • a=0.5 billion dollars was the GDP in 2010.

  • b=4 is the growth factor.

  • t is the time, in decades, since 2010.

Therefore, the expression for y(t) is

y(t)=0.5⋅4t.

Now, to find the time it takes for the GDP to increase to $512 billion, we need to solve the equation y(t)=512 for t, as follows:

y(t)=5120.5⋅4t=5124t=5120.54t=10244t=45t=5

Hence, it will take 5 decades for the GDP to increase to $512 billion.
