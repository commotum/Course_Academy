EXPLANATION

The second-degree Taylor polynomial for f(x) about x=1 is P2(x)=f(1)+f′(1)(x−1)+f″(1)2(x−1)2.

From the graph, we observe:

  • f(1)<0. Therefore, the first term of the Taylor polynomial must be negative.

  • f′(1)<0, because the slope of the tangent at x=1 is negative. Therefore, the coefficient of the second term in the Taylor polynomial must be negative.

  • f″(1)>0, because the graph is concave up at x=1. Therefore, the coefficient of the third term in the Taylor polynomial must be positive.

The only option that meets the above criteria is P2(x)=−5−2(x−1)+6(x−1)2.
