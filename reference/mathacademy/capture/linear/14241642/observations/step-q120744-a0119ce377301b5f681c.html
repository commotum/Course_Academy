A group of randomly selected customers from a certain insurance company was asked about the type of vehicle driven and whether they received a speeding ticket in the last year. The results of the survey are summarized in the two-way frequency table below.

SUV Sport car Totals
Speeding ticket 80 5 85
No speeding ticket 20 5 25
Totals 100 10 110

There are 220 drivers in another insurance company. Assuming that the distribution of the results is the same for both companies, estimate the number of drivers in the second insurance company that drive SUV or received a speeding ticket, but not both.

50

EXPLANATION

To estimate the probability that a randomly selected driver drives the SUV or received a speeding ticket but not both, we

  • add up the numbers in the row "Speeding ticket" or the column "SUV", but not in both, and then

  • divide by the total number of data points in the table.

SUV Sport car Totals
Speeding ticket 80 5 85
No speeding ticket 20 5 25
Totals 100 10 110

Adding up these numbers and then dividing by the total number of data points in the table (110), we get

p=20+5110=522.

This represents the estimated probability that a randomly selected driver drives an SUV or received a speeding ticket but not both.

Finally, to find the estimated number of drivers in the second company that drive SUVs or received a speeding ticket, but not both, we multiply the total number of drivers in the company (220) by the probability above: n=220⋅p=220⋅522=50
