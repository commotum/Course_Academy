A group of randomly selected students from School A was asked about whether or not they use TV or the internet to access the local news. The results of the survey are summarized in the two-way frequency table below.

TV No TV Totals
Internet 90 15 105
No internet 30 15 45
Totals 120 30 150

There are 100 students in School B. Assuming that the distribution of the results is the same for both schools, estimate the number of School B students that access the local news either from TV or the internet, but not both.

a
 80
b
 70
c
 40
d
 30
e
 20

Correct
EXPLANATION

To estimate the probability that a randomly selected student gets the local news either from TV or the internet but not both, we

  • add up the numbers in the row "No internet" or the column "TV", but not in both, and then

  • divide by the total number of data points in the table.

TV No TV Totals
Internet 90 15 105
No internet 30 15 45
Totals 120 30 150

Adding up these numbers and then dividing by the total number of data points in the table (150), we get

p=30+15150=45150=0.3.

This represents the estimated probability that a randomly selected student gets the local news from TV or the internet but not both.

Finally, to find the estimated number of students of School B that get their local news either from TV or the internet but not both, we multiply the total number of students in the school (100) by the probability above: n=100⋅p=100⋅0.3=30
