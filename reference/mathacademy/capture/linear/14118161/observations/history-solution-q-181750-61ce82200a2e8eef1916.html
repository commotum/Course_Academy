Two sets A and B have the same cardinality if there exists a bijection between A and B.

Notice that the elements of B form an (infinite) geometric sequence with a common ratio r=5: r=5=102=5010=⋯

The nth term of our geometric sequence, denoted an, is given by an=2⋅5n−1.

Therefore, we can express B in set-builder notation as B={2⋅5n−1∣n∈N}.

Now, consider the function f:A→B that maps the underlying sets as follows:

A 1 2 3 4 ⋯ n ⋯
B 2 10 50 250 ⋯ 2⋅5n−1 ⋯

In general, for each n∈A, we have f(n)=2⋅5n−1∈B.

Notice that f is described in such a way that it is bijective. Indeed, it's

  • injective since f(n)≠f(m) whenever n≠m, and

  • surjective since each element in B (the second row) has a pre-image in A (the first row).

Therefore, |A|=|B|, i.e., the sets have the same cardinality.
