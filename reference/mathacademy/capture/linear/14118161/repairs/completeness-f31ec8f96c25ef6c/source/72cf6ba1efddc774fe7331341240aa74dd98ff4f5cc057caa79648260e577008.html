EXPLANATION

Two sets A and B have the same cardinality if there exists a bijection between A and B.

Notice that the elements of A form an (infinite) geometric sequence with a common ratio r=13: r=13=927=39=⋯

The nth term of our geometric sequence, denoted an, is given by an=27(13)n−1.

Therefore, we can express A in set-builder notation as A={27(13)n−1|n∈N}.

Now, consider the function f:B→A that maps the underlying sets as follows:

B 1 2 3 4 ⋯ n ⋯
A 27 9 3 1 ⋯ 27(13)n−1 ⋯

In general, for each n∈B, we have f(n)=27(13)n−1∈A.

Notice that f is described in such a way that it is bijective. Indeed, it's

  • injective since f(n)≠f(m) whenever n≠m, and

  • surjective since each element in A (the second row) has a pre-image in B (the first row).

Therefore, |A|=|B|, i.e., the sets have the same cardinality.
