John selected a pair of socks out of his drawer without looking in the drawer. He has 5 pairs of black dress socks, 4 pairs of brown dress socks, 10 pairs of black sports socks, and 3 pairs of white sport socks. What is the probability of selecting a pair of black socks or a pair of sports socks?

a
 911
b
 211
c
 511
d
 311
e
 811
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

Let us call B the event "a pair of black socks has been selected," and S the event "a pair of sports socks has been selected." Then the probability of selecting a pair of black socks or a pair of sports socks is P(B∪S), and we can apply the addition law:

P(B∪S)=P(B)+P(S)−P(B∩S)

First, let's find P(B), P(S), and P(B∩S).

  • There are 5+10=15 pairs of black socks among the 5+4+10+3=22 pairs of socks, so we have P(B)=1522.

  • There are 10+3=13 pairs of sports socks among the 22 pairs of socks, so we have P(S)=1322.

  • There are 10 pairs of black sports socks. So, we have P(B∩S)=1022.

Therefore, applying the addition law, we have

P(B∪S)=P(B)+P(S)−P(B∩S)=1522+1322−1022=1822=911.
