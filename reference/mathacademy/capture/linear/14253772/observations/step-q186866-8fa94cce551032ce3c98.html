EXPLANATION

Notice that the graph of y=−2f(x+1) can be obtained from the graph of y=f(x) by

  • translating it by 1 unit to the left, then

  • stretching it by a scale factor of 2 parallel to the y-axis, and finally

  • flipping it across the x-axis.

Since one of these transformations flips the graph across the x-axis, the combined transformation maps the global maximum of y=f(x) to the global minimum of y=−2f(x+1). So, we need to find the image of (−1,4), the global maximum of y=f(x), under the transformation.

To plot y=−2f(x+1), we apply the following steps:

  • First, we take the graph y=f(x) that passes through (−1,4).

  • Then, we shift the graph horizontally by 1 unit to the left and get the graph of y=f(x+1). After this transformation, the point (−1,4) will be mapped to the point
    (−1−1,4)=(−2,4).

  • Next, we stretch the result by a scale factor of 2 along the y-axis and get the graph of y=2f(x+1). After this transformation, the point (−2,4) will be mapped to the point
    (−2,2⋅4)=(−2,8).

  • Finally, we flip the result vertically across the x-axis and get the graph of y=−2f(x+1). After this transformation, the point (−2,8) will be mapped to the point (−2,−1⋅8)=(−2,−8).

Therefore, the global minimum of y=−2f(x+1) is (−2,−8).
