Method 1

To plot y=−f(x)−1, we apply the following steps:

  • First, take the graph y=f(x)+1.

  • Then, we shift the graph vertically by 1 unit down and get the graph of y=f(x). After this transformation, the point (−1,1) will be mapped to the point (−1,1−1)=(−1,0).

  • Next, we reflect the result across the x-axis and get the graph of y=−f(x). After this transformation, the point (−1,0) will be mapped to the point (−1,−(0))=(−1,0).

  • Finally, shift the result vertically by 1 unit down and obtain the graph of y=−f(x)−1. After this transformation, the point (−1,0) will be mapped to the point (−1,0−1)=(−1,−1).

Method 2

Since the point (−1,1) lies on the graph of y=f(x)+1, we have that

f(−1)+1=1⟹f(−1)=0.

Now consider the graph of y=−f(x)−1. Substituting x=−1, we get

y=−f(−1)−1=−0−1=−1.

Therefore, the point (−1,−1) must lie on y=−f(x)−1.
