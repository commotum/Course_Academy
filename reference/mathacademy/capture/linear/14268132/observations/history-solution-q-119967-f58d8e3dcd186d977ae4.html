First, let's write the function in a form that is convenient for transformations:

y=f(1−x)=f(−(x−1))

When performing graph transformations, we must remember the following rule:

  1. First, we perform the transformations inside the argument of f. We first carry out horizontal stretches and reflections, followed by horizontal translations.

  2. Then, we perform the transformations outside the argument of f. We first carry out vertical stretches and reflections, followed by vertical translations.

So, to plot the graph of y=f(−(x−1)), we apply the following steps:

  • Take the curve y=f(x).

  • Then, reflect the graph across the y-axis and get the graph of y=f(−x).

  • Finally, we shift the result horizontally by 1 unit to the right and obtain the graph of y=f(−(x−1)).

Therefore, the range of f(−(x−1)) is [1,5).

Note: Horizontal stretches and shifts do not change the range of the resulting function.
