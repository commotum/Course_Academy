EXPLANATION

By the converse of the perpendicular bisector theorem, a point equidistant from the endpoints of a segment lies on the perpendicular bisector of the segment.

Here, the points Y and Z are both equidistant from the endpoints of VW―. Therefore, Y and Z both lie on the perpendicular bisector of VW―.

Since only one line can be drawn through two given points, the line YZ⟷ is the perpendicular bisector of VW―.

In particular, we have that VX=WX. We can use this equation to solve for x:
