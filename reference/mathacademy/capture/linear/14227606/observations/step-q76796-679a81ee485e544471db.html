EXPLANATION

By the converse of the perpendicular bisector theorem, a point equidistant from the endpoints of a segment lies on the perpendicular bisector of the segment.

Here, the points P and N are both equidistant from the endpoints of KL―. Therefore, P and N both lie on the perpendicular bisector of KL―.

Since only one line can be drawn through two given points, the line PN⟷ is the perpendicular bisector of KL―.

In particular, we have that KM=LM. We can use this equation to solve for x:
