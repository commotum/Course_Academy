Let A denote the event of getting the same number on at least two of the dice. In this case, it is easier to find P(A′), where the complement A′ represents the event of getting different numbers after rolling the four dice.

We can compute the probability of the complement as P(A′)=nN,

where N is the total number of outcomes after rolling four dice, and n is the number of ways to assign a unique number from the set {1,2,3,4,5,6} to each dice.

To get a unique value for each of the 4 dice, we must choose 4 numbers out of 6, where the order matters. The number of ways to do this is

n=6P4=6!2!=360.

On the other hand, if the same number can be assigned to different dice, we have 6 ways to assign a number for the first die, 6 for the second, 6 for the third, and 6 for the fourth die. Multiplying these choices together, we get

N=6⋅6⋅6⋅6=1296.

Finally, the probability of the complement is P(A′)=nN=3601296≈0.28

rounded to 2 decimal places.

Therefore, we have

P(A)=1−P(A′)≈1−0.28=0.72

rounded to 2 decimal places.
