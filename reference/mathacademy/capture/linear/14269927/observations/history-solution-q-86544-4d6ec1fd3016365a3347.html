Let A denote the event that among 3 people at least two have the same birthday. In this case, it is easier to find P(A′), where A′ represents the event that all three people have their birthdays on different days.

We can compute the probability of the complement as P(A′)=nN,

where N is the total number of ways to assign a day of the week to each person, and n is the number of ways to assign a unique day of the week to each person.

To assign a unique day of the week to each of the 3 people, we must choose 3 days out of 7, where the order matters. The number of ways to do this is

n=7P3=7⋅6⋅5=210.

On the other hand, if the same day can be assigned to different persons, we have 7 ways to assign a day for the first person, 7 for the second and 7 for the third person. Multiplying these choices together, we get

N=7⋅7⋅7=343.

Finally, the probability of the complement is P(A¯)=nN=210343≈0.612

rounded to 3 decimal places.

Therefore, we have

P(A)=1−P(A′)≈1−0.612=0.388

rounded to 3 decimal places.
