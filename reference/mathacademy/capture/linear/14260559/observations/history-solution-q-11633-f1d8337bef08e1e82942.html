Let us call A the event "the student studies mathematics" and B the event "the student studies physics."

Note that there are 12 students who study both mathematics and physics. So, we draw two overlapping circles representing the events A and B and write the number 12 where the circles overlap.

  • The number of students who study mathematics only is 35−12=23, so we write the number 23 in the part of circle A that does not overlap with circle B.

  • The number of students who study physics only is 26−12=14, so we write the number 14 in the part of the circle B that does not overlap with circle A.

The total number of students who study mathematics or physics is 12+23+14=49, and there are 76 students in total, so the number of remaining students is 76−49=27.

To represent these 27 students who do not study mathematics nor physics, we write the number 27 outside of the circles A and B.
