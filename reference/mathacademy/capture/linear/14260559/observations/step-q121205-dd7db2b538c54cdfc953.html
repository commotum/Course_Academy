In a survey conducted among 100 people, it was found that 38% of them have been in Canada, 70% have been in France, and 11% have been in both countries. Draw a Venn diagram representing this sample space. If a person is selected at random among the 100 people, what is the probability that he has been in Canada but not in France? Round your answer to 2 decimal places.

a
 0.11
b
 0.14
c
 0.27
d
 0.59
e
 0.03
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

Clearly, 38 people have been in Canada, 70 people have been in France, and 11 people have been to both countries.

Let us call C the event "the person has been in Canada" and F the event "the person has been in France."

Note that there are 11 people who have been in both countries. We draw two overlapping circles representing the events C and F and write the number 11 where the circles overlap.

  • The number of people who have been in Canada only is 38−11=27, so we write the number 27 in the part of circle C that does not overlap with circle F.

  • The number of people who have been in France only is 70−11=59, so we write the number 59 in the part of circle F that does not overlap with circle C.

The total number of people who have been in Canada or France is 27+11+59=97, and there are 100 people in total, so the number of remaining people is 100−97=3.

To represent these 3 people who have not been in Canada or France, we write the number 3 outside of the circles C and F.
