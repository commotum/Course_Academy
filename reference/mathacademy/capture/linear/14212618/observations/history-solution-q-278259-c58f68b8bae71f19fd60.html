To find the vertical asymptotes of a rational function, we factor the numerator and denominator and cancel any common factors. Then, we set the denominator equal to zero and solve for x.

Factoring the numerator and denominator, we get f(x)=2(x+3)(x+1).

There are no common factors in the numerator and denominator, so we set the denominator equal to zero and solve for x: (x+3)(x+1)=0

This gives the two solutions x=−3 and x=−1.

Therefore, in ascending order, the equations of the vertical asymptotes are x=−3 and x=−1.
