EXPLANATION

First, let's factor the polynomial. We factor out a common factor −x, as follows: f(x)=−x3−2x2−4x=−x(x2+2x+4)

The equation is now fully factored. Note that the discriminant of x2+2x+4=0 is D=22−4(1)(4)=−12<0, which implies that the quadratic factor has no real roots. So by the zero-product rule, we deduce that f(x) has a single root at x=0.

We also find the y-intercept of the curve by substituting the value x=0 into the equation:

y=−03−2(0)2−4(0)=0

So, the curve:

  • has a y-intercept at (0,0),

  • passes through the x-axis at the single root x=0, and

  • has no more x-intercepts.
