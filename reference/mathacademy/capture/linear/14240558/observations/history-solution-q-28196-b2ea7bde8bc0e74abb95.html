The equation is already fully factored. Note that the discriminant of x2+3x+3=0 is

D=(3)2−4(1)(3)=−3<0,

which implies that the quadratic factor has no real roots. So by the zero-product rule, we deduce that the function f(x) has a single root at x=2.

We also find the y-intercept of the curve by plugging the value x=0 into the equation: y=13(2−0)(02+3(0)+3)=(2)(3)3=2.

So, the curve:

  • has a y-intercept at the point (0,2),

  • passes through the x-axis at the single root x=2, and

  • has no more x-intercepts.
