EXPLANATION

Notice that we are given a 3×3 matrix with the following eigenvalues:

  • λ=7 of algebraic multiplicity 2

  • λ=5 of algebraic multiplicity 1

As a result, we have one 1×1 Jordan block J1(5) corresponding to λ=5. However, there are two possibilities for the Jordan blocks corresponding to λ=7:

  • Case I. We may get the single 2×2 Jordan block J2(7). This happens when the generalized eigenvectors v1 and v2 corresponding to the eigenvalue λ=7 form the following chain: v2→A−7Iv1→A−7I0

  • Case II. We may get a composition of two 1×1 Jordan blocks, namely J1(7)⊕J1(7). This happens when the generalized eigenvectors v1 and v2 corresponding to the eigenvalue λ=7 form the following chains: v1→A−7I0v2→A−7I0

To figure out which of the cases we obtain in this situation, let's find the number of linearly independent eigenvectors corresponding to λ=7.

Computing A−7I, we get

A−7I=[92−2−616−2−29]−7[100010001]=[22−2−6−66−2−22].

Now, we reduce this matrix to row echelon form using Gaussian elimination, as follows:

A−7I=[22−2−6−66−2−22]R2:=R2+3R1∼[22−2000−2−22]R3:=R3+R1∼[22−2000000]

Since nullity(A−7I)=2, there will be two eigenvectors that correspond to the eigenvalue λ=7, say, v1 and v2. So, the generalized eigenvectors corresponding to the eigenvalue λ=7 form the following chains:

v1→A−7I0v2→A−7I0

Therefore, we have case II, and A has the following Jordan canonical form:

J1(7)⊕J1(7)⊕J1(5)=[700070005]

Note: The Jordan canonical form is unique up to the order of the Jordan blocks.
