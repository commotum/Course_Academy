EXPLANATION

According to the factor theorem, (ax−b) is a factor of the polynomial p(x) if and only if the root x=ba is also root of p(x).

Here, we are considering (5x+4). The root of this expression can be found by setting the expression equal to 0 and solving:

5x+4=05x=−4x=−45

According to the factor theorem, then, (5x+4) is a factor of p(x) if x=−45 is a root of p(x).

Now we will check if x=−45 is a root of the polynomials f, g and h.

  • Evaluating x=−45 at f(x), we get f(−45)=5(−45)3+4(−45)2=5(−64125)+4(1625)=−6425+6425=0. Consequently, by the factor theorem, we conclude that (5x+4) is indeed a factor of f(x).
  • Now, evaluating x=−45 at g(x), we get g(−45)=15(−45)2+25(−45)+4=15(1625)−20+4=485−16=−325≠0. So, by the factor theorem, (5x+4) is not a factor of g(x).
  • Finally, evaluating x=−45 at h(x), we get h(−45)=4(−45)3+5(−45)2=4(−64125)+5(1625)=−256125+165=144125≠0. So, by the factor theorem, (5x+4) is not a factor of g(x).

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only."
