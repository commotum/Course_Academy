EXPLANATION

Given a frequency table containing continuous data split into K classes, we can estimate the variance by treating all observations within each class as though they were equal to the midpoint of that class. This gives σn2≈1n∑i=1Kfi⋅(mi−x―)2, where

  • fi is the frequency of the ith class,

  • mi is the midpoint of the ith class,

  • n is the total number of observations, and

  • x― is the estimated mean, calculated using the class midpoints.

To find the variance, we add columns to our table for the midpoints of each interval, the squared deviations of the midpoints from the mean, and the products of the squared deviations and frequencies.



Range fi mi (mi−x―)2 fi(mi−x―)2
0≤x<10 8 5 72.25 578
10≤x<20 7 15 2.25 15.75
20≤x<30 5 25 132.25 661.25

Then, we divide the sum of all the values in the last column by 20 (the total number of observations): σn2≈578+15.75+661.2520=125520=62.75

Finally, the estimated standard deviation is the square root of the estimated variance: σn≈62.75≈7.9
