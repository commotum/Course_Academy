EXPLANATION

One event is more likely than another if it has a greater probability of occurring. If two events have the same probability, they are equally likely.

Let's consider each statement in turn.

  • To compute the experimental probability of a machine sorting a bin of parts in less than 30 seconds, we divide the number of times a machine sorted a bin of parts in less than 30 seconds (6+16=22) by the total number of times Sarah conducted the experiment (40). This gives P(sorts in <30 seconds)=2240.

    This probability is a relatively large number, closer to 1. Since 12<2240<1, this event is likely.

  • To compute the experimental probability of a machine sorting a bin of parts in more than 30 seconds, we divide the number of times a machine sorted a bin of parts in more than 30 seconds (10+5+3=18) by the total number of times Sarah conducted the experiment (40). This gives P(sorts in >30 seconds)=1840.

    This probability is a small number, closer to 0. Since 0<1840<12, this event is unlikely.

  • The experimental probability of a machine sorting a bin of parts in less than 30 seconds is greater than the experimental probability of a machine sorting a bin of parts in more than 30 seconds: P(sorts in <30 seconds)=2240>1840=P(sorts in >30 seconds)

    Therefore, a machine sorting a bin of parts in less than 30 seconds is more likely than a machine sorting a bin of parts in more than 30 seconds.
