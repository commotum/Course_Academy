Notice that the graph of y=f(x+3)−2 can be obtained from the graph of y=f(x) by

  • translating it by 3 units to the left, and then

  • translating it by 2 unit down.

Since these transformations do not flip the graph across the x-axis, the combined transformation maps the local maximum of y=f(x) to the local maximum of y=f(x+3)−2. So, we need to find the image of (−2,6), the local maximum of y=f(x), under the transformation.

To plot y=f(x+3)−2, we apply the following steps:

  • First, we take the graph y=f(x) that passes through (−2,6).

  • Then, we shift the graph horizontally by 3 units to the left and get the graph of y=f(x+3). After this transformation, the point (−2,6) will be mapped to the point
    (−2−3,6)=(−5,6).

  • Finally, we shift the result vertically by 2 units downward and get the graph of y=f(x+3)−2. After this transformation, the point (−5,6) will be mapped to the point (−5,6−2)=(−5,4).

Therefore, the local maximum of y=f(x+3)−2 is (−5,4).
