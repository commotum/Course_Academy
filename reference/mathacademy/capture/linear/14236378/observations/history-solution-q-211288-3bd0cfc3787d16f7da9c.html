Notice that the graph of y=12f(x−5) can be obtained from the graph of y=f(x) by

  • translating it by 5 units to the right, and then

  • stretching it by a scale factor of 12 parallel to the y-axis.

Since these transformations do not flip the graph across the x-axis, the combined transformation maps the global minimum of y=f(x) to the global minimum of y=12f(x−5). So, we need to find the image of (−2,−4), the global minimum of y=f(x), under the transformation.

To plot y=12f(x−5), we apply the following steps:

  • First, we take the graph y=f(x) that passes through (−2,−4).

  • Then, we shift the graph horizontally by 5 units to the right and get the graph of y=f(x−5). After this transformation, the point (−2,−4) will be mapped to the point
    (−2+5,−4)=(3,−4).

  • Finally, we stretch the result along the y-axis by a scale factor of 12 and get the graph of y=12f(x−5). After this transformation, the point (3,−4) will be mapped to the point (3,12⋅(−4))=(3,−2).

Therefore, the global minimum of y=12f(x−5) is (3,−2).
