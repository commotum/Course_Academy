The modes of a random variable X with probability density function f(x) are the values of x at the global maxima of f(x).

To find the global maxima of f(x), we find the critical points of f(x) and then apply the candidates test.

First, we find the critical points.

  • The piece of f(x) defined on −1≤x≤32 is f(x)=−96595(x4−2x2−2). Taking the derivative, we get f′(x)=−96595(4x3−4x)=−384595x(x2−1), and solving f′(x)=0, we get −384595x(x2−1)=0⟹x=0,±1. All of these critical points are within the interval [−1,32], so we keep them all.

  • The derivative doesn't exist at the endpoints x=±1. So, these are critical points as well, but −1 is included above.

Now, we evaluate f(x) at the critical points x=0,±1,32:

Finally, we compare all of the values above. The largest values are

Therefore, the modes of X are −1 and 1.
