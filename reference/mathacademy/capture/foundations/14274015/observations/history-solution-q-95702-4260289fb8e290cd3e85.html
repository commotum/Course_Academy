Given that A is an n×n symmetric matrix over R, the spectral theorem states the following:

  • The eigenvalues of A are all real.

  • Eigenvectors corresponding to different eigenvalues are orthogonal.

  • The dimension of the eigenspace for each eigenvalue equals the multiplicity of that eigenvalue as a root of the characteristic polynomial.

  • A is orthogonally diagonalizable.

With that in mind, let's examine our statements in turn.

  • Statement I is true. According to the theorem, eigenvectors corresponding to different eigenvalues must be orthogonal. But two eigenvectors that correspond to the same eigenvalue will always be parallel if the geometric multiplicity of the eigenvalue is 1.

  • Statement II is true. Indeed, the eigenvalues of a symmetric matrix are real. Also, since the roots of the characteristic polynomial have (algebraic) multiplicity all equal to 1, the eigenvalues are all distinct.

  • Statement III is false. According to the theorem, any symmetric matrix with real entries is orthogonally diagonalizable, but not necessarily orthogonal.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and II only."
