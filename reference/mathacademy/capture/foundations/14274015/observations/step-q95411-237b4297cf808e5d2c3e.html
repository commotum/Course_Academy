Given that A is a real symmetric matrix, which of the following statements are true?

  1. A must have all real eigenvalues
  2. A is diagonalizable
  3. If u and v are two eigenvectors that correspond to different eigenvalues, then u=αv for some scalar α

III only
b
II and III only

I only
d

I and III only

Incorrect
EXPLANATION

Given that A is an n×n symmetric matrix over R, the spectral theorem states the following:

  • The eigenvalues of A are all real.

  • Eigenvectors corresponding to different eigenvalues are orthogonal.

  • The dimension of the eigenspace for each eigenvalue equals the multiplicity of that eigenvalue as a root of the characteristic polynomial.

  • A is orthogonally diagonalizable.

With that in mind, let's examine our statements in turn.

  • Statement III is true. Indeed, the eigenvalues of a symmetric matrix are real.

  • Statement II is true. Indeed, any symmetric matrix with real entries is orthogonally diagonalizable, hence, is diagonalizable.

  • Statement I is false. According to the spectral theorem, eigenvectors corresponding to different eigenvalues must be orthogonal. Since the eigenvector are nonzero, we can't have u=αv for some scalar α.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and II only."
