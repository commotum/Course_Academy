EXPLANATION

The equation is a first-order linear equation with constant coefficients. To find the general solution, we must sum the complementary and particular solutions.

  • To find the complementary solution Ic, we solve the corresponding homogeneous equation, given by dIcdt+8Ic=0. Solving using an integration technique, such as separation of variables, we obtain the complementary solution Ic(t)=Ae−8t, where A is a constant of integration.

  • Next, we find the particular solution. Note that the right-hand side of the inhomogeneous equation is a polynomial of degree 0. So, we assume that the particular solution is also a polynomial of degree 0, i.e., Ip(t)=α. To find the value of α, we substitute Ip=α and dIpdt=(α)′=0, into the inhomogeneous equation: 0+8α=6⟹α=34 Therefore, the particular solution is Ip(t)=34.

Now, since the general solution is given by the sum of the complementary solution and the particular solution, the general solution in our case is

I(t)=Ic(t)+Ip(t)=Ae−8t+34.

Note the following:

  • The quantity Ic=Ae−8t is the transient current, since it goes to zero as t→∞.

  • The constant Ip=34 is the steady-state current. As t→∞, the current I approaches the value of the steady-state current.

Therefore, the steady-state current is 34 amperes.
