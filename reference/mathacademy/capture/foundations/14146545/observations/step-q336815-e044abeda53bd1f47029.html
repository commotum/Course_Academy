The diagram shows a series RL circuit with a source electromotive force (EMF) of 10sin⁡(3t)V, a resistor of resistance 6Ω, and an inductor of inductance 1H.

Kirchhoff's voltage law states that in a series circuit, the source voltage (EMF) equals the sum of the voltage drops.

In an RL circuit, there are two voltage drops:

  • The voltage drop across the resistor is proportional to the current, with a constant of proportionality R=6Ω. VR=6I

  • The voltage drop across the inductor is proportional to the rate of change of current, with a constant of proportionality L=1H. VL=1⋅dIdt=dIdt.

Therefore, by Kirchhoff's voltage law, the differential equation governing the amount of current I in the circuit is 10sin⁡(3t)=6I+dIdt which gives dIdt+6I=10sin⁡(3t).

This is a first-order linear equation with constant coefficients. So, to find the general solution, we must find the sum of the complementary and particular solutions.

  • To find the complementary solution Ic, we solve the corresponding homogeneous equation, given by dIcdt+6Ic=0. Solving using an integration technique, such as separation of variables, we obtain the complementary solution Ic(t)=Ae−6t, where A is a constant of integration.

  • Next, we find the particular solution. Note that the right-hand side of the inhomogeneous equation is sinusoidal with argument 3t. So, we assume that the particular solution is also sinusoidal with argument 3t, i.e., Ip(t)=αcos⁡(3t)+βsin⁡(3t). To find the values of α and β, we substitute Ip=αcos⁡(3t)+βsin⁡(3t),dIpdt=−3αsin⁡(3t)+3βcos⁡(3t), into the inhomogeneous equation and group like terms on the left-hand side: (−3αsin⁡(3t)+3βcos⁡(3t))+6(αcos⁡(3t)+βsin⁡(3t))=10sin⁡(3t)(−3α+6β)sin⁡(3t)+(6α+3β)cos⁡(3t)=10sin⁡(3t) Equating the coefficients, we get the following system of equations: {−3α+6β=10(equating the coefficients ofsin⁡(3t))6α+3β=0(equating the coefficients ofcos⁡(3t)) Solving this system gives α=−23, β=43. Therefore, the particular solution is Ip(t)=−23cos⁡(3t)+43sin⁡(3t).

Now, since the general solution is given by the sum of the complementary solution and the particular solution, the general solution in our case is I(t)=Ic(t)+Ip(t)=Ae−6t−23cos⁡(3t)+43sin⁡(3t).

Note the following:

  • The quantity Ic(t)=Ae−6t is the transient current, since it goes to zero as t→∞.

  • The function Ip(t)=−23cos⁡(3t)+43sin⁡(3t) is the steady-state current. It governs the current's long-term behavior.

Therefore, as t→∞, we have I(t)≈Ip(t)=−23cos⁡(3t)+43sin⁡(3t).
