EXPLANATION

The diagram shows a series RL circuit with a source electromotive force (EMF) of 4V, a resistor of resistance of 20Ω, and an inductor of inductance 2H.

Kirchhoff's voltage law states that in a series circuit, the source voltage (EMF) equals the sum of the voltage drops.

In an RL circuit, there are two voltage drops:

  • The voltage drop across the resistor is proportional to the current, with a constant of proportionality R=20Ω. VR=20I

  • The voltage drop across the inductor is proportional to the rate of change of current, with a constant of proportionality L=2H. VL=2⋅dIdt

Therefore, by Kirchhoff's voltage law, the differential equation governing the amount of current I in the circuit is

4=20I+2⋅dIdt

which gives dIdt+10I=2.

This is a first-order linear equation with constant coefficients. So, to find the general solution, we must find the sum of the complementary and particular solutions.

  • To find the complementary solution Ic, we solve the corresponding homogeneous equation, given by dIcdt+10Ic=0. Solving using an integration technique, such as separation of variables, we obtain the complementary solution Ic(t)=Ae−10t, where A is a constant of integration.

  • Next, we find the particular solution. Note that the right-hand side of the inhomogeneous equation is a polynomial of degree 0. So, we assume that the particular solution is also a polynomial of degree 0, i.e., Ip(t)=α. To find the value of α, we substitute Ip=α and dIpdt=(α)′=0 into the inhomogeneous equation: 0+10α=2⟹α=15 Therefore, the particular solution is Ip(t)=15.

Now, since the general solution is given by the sum of the complementary solution and the particular solution, the general solution in our case is

I(t)=Ic(t)+Ip(t)=Ae−10t+15.

We're told that the circuit starts with an initial current of 1A. So, we can apply the initial condition I(0)=1, and solve for A:

I(0)=1Ae−10(0)+15=1A=45

Therefore, we have the following expression for I(t):

I(t)=45e−10t+15=15(4e−10t+1)

Note the following:

  • The quantity Ic=45e−10t is the transient current, since it goes to zero as t→∞.

  • The constant Ip=15 is the steady-state current. As t→∞, the current I approaches the value of the steady-state current.
