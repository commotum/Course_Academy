The diagram shows a series RL circuit with a source electromotive force (EMF) of E(t)=5cos⁡(4t)V, a resistor with resistance 10Ω, and an inductor with an inductance of 2H.

Kirchhoff's voltage law states that in a series circuit, the source voltage (EMF) equals the sum of the voltage drops.

In an RL circuit, there are two voltage drops:

  • The voltage drop across the resistor is proportional to the current, with a constant of proportionality R=10Ω. VR=10I

  • The voltage drop across the inductor is proportional to the rate of change of current, with a constant of proportionality L=2H. VL=2dIdt

Therefore, by Kirchhoff's voltage law, the differential equation governing the amount of current I in the circuit is

5cos⁡(4t)=10I+2dIdt, which gives dIdt+5I=52cos⁡(4t).
