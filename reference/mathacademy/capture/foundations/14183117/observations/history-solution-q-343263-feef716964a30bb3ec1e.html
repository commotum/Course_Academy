Abel's identity says that if y1 and y2 are solutions of y″+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=0, then their Wronskian W(x) satisfies W′(x)=−P(x)W(x).

First, we rewrite our equation in standard form. Since x>0, we can divide both sides by 4x to get y″+x2−84xy′+cos⁡(x)4xy=0.

Now, the coefficient of y′ is P(x)=x2−84x.

Substituting this into Abel's identity gives W′(x)=(2x−x4)W(x).
