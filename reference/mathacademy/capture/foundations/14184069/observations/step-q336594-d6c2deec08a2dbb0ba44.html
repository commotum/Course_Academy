EXPLANATION

The given equation is a first-order linear equation with constant coefficients. To find the general solution, we must sum the complementary and particular solutions.

  • To find the complementary solution qc, we solve the corresponding homogeneous equation, given by dqcdt+6qc=0. Solving using an integration technique, such as separation of variables, we obtain the complementary solution qc(t)=Ae−6t, where A is a constant of integration.

  • Next, we find the particular solution. Note that the right-hand side of the inhomogeneous equation is a polynomial of degree 0. So, we assume that the particular solution is also a polynomial of degree 0, i.e., qp(t)=α. To find the value of α, we substitute qp=α and dqpdt=(α)′=0 into the inhomogeneous equation: 0+6α=12⟹α=112 Therefore, the particular solution is qp(t)=112.

Now, since the general solution is given by the sum of the complementary solution and the particular solution, the general solution in our case is q(t)=qc(t)+qp(t)=Ae−6t+112.

Note the following:

  • The quantity qc=Ae−6t is the transient charge, since it goes to zero as t→∞.

  • The constant qp=112 is the steady-state charge. As t→∞, the charge q approaches the value of the steady-state charge.

Therefore, the steady-state charge is 112 coulombs.
