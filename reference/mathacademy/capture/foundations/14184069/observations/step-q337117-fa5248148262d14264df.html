EXPLANATION

The current I, measured in amperes, is defined as the rate of flow of charge q, measured in coulombs. I=dqdt

Kirchhoff's voltage law states that in a series circuit, the source voltage (EMF) equals the sum of the voltage drops.

In an RC circuit, there are two voltage drops:

  • The voltage drop across the resistor is proportional to the current, with a constant of proportionality R=50Ω. So, VR=50I=50dqdt.

  • The voltage drop across the capacitor is related to its charge by q=CVC. Therefore, VC=q0.002=500q.

Therefore, by Kirchhoff's voltage law, the differential equation governing the amount of charge q on the capacitor is 8=50dqdt+500q, which gives dqdt+10q=425.

This is a first-order linear equation with constant coefficients. So, to find the general solution, we must find the sum of the complementary and particular solutions.

  • To find the complementary solution qc, we solve the corresponding homogeneous equation, given by dqcdt+10qc=0. Solving using an integration technique, such as separation of variables, we obtain the complementary solution qc(t)=Ae−10t, where A is a constant of integration.

  • Next, we find the particular solution. Note that the right-hand side of the inhomogeneous equation is a polynomial of degree 0. So, we assume that the particular solution is also a polynomial of degree 0, i.e., qp(t)=α. To find the value of α, we substitute qp=α and dqpdt=(α)′=0 into the inhomogeneous equation: 0+10α=425⟹α=2125 Therefore, the particular solution is qp(t)=2125.

Now, since the general solution is given by the sum of the complementary solution and the particular solution, the general solution in our case is q(t)=qc(t)+qp(t)=Ae−10t+2125.

We're told that the circuit starts with an initial charge of 1100C on the capacitor. So, we can apply the initial condition q(0)=1100, and solve for A: q(0)=1100Ae−10(0)+2125=1100A=−3500

Therefore, we have the following expression for q(t): q(t)=−3500e−10t+2125=1500(8−3e−10t)

Note the following:

  • The quantity qc=−3500e−10t is the transient charge, since it goes to zero as t→∞.

  • The constant qp=2125 is the steady-state charge. As t→∞, the charge q approaches the value of the steady-state charge.
