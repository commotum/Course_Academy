First, let's find the differential:

df(x,y,z)=(∂f∂x)dx+(∂f∂y)dy+(∂f∂z)dz=1xdx+1ydy+1zdz

At the point (1,1,1), the differential is

df(1,1,1)=1dx+1dy+1dz.

So, if we move to the point (1+Δx,1+Δy,1+Δz), then we can approximate the change in f as

Δf≈Δx+Δy+Δz.

Moving from (1,1,1) to (2,3,4), we have

Δx=2−1=1,Δy=3−1=2Δz=4−1=3.

So, we have

Δf≈Δx+Δy+Δz≈1+2+3≈6.

Therefore, the value of f(x,y,z) increases by approximately 6.
