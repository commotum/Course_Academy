Let y=eλx. Differentiating y with respect to x gives

y′=λeλx,y″=λ2eλx.

Substituting the above into our differential equation gives: λ2eλx−2λeλx+2eλx=0eλx(λ2−2λ+2)=0

The auxiliary equation is therefore

λ2−2λ+2=0.

The auxiliary equation can be solved by completing the square:

λ2−2λ+2=0(λ2−2λ+1)−1+2=0(λ−1)2+1=0(λ−1)2=−1λ−1=±iλ=1±i.

Therefore, the general solution to the differential equation is

y=ex(Pcos⁡x+Qsin⁡x).

Now let's find the constants P and Q.

Substituting y(0)=−2 into the general solution gives

P=−2.

So we have y=ex(−2cos⁡x+Qsin⁡x).

We now need to apply the condition y′(0)=4. We differentiate y to find y′:

y′=ex(−2cos⁡x+Qsin⁡x)+ex(2sin⁡x+Qcos⁡x)

Substituting y′(0)=−4 into the above gives

−2+Q=−4⟹Q=−2.

So we have P=−2 and Q=−2.

Therefore, the solution to the initial value problem is y=−2ex(cos⁡x+sin⁡x).
