EXPLANATION

Let y=eλx. Differentiating y with respect to x gives

y′=λeλx,y″=λ2eλx.

Substituting the above into our differential equation gives

λ2eλx+4λeλx+4eλx=0eλx(λ2+4λ+4)=0.

The auxiliary equation is therefore λ2+4λ+4=0.

Factoring the auxiliary equation gives

(λ+2)(λ+2)=0(λ+2)2=0

Therefore, λ=−2 is a repeated root.

So the general solution to the differential equation is y=Ae−2x+Bxe−2x.

Now let's find the constants A and B.

Substituting y(0)=1 into the general solution gives

A=1.

We now need to apply the condition y′(0)=3. First, we differentiate y to find y′:

y′=−2Ae−2x+−2Bxe−2x+Be−2x

Substituting y′(0)=3 into the above gives

−2A+B=3.

Using the fact that A=1 gives B=5.

Therefore, the solution to the initial value problem is y=e−2x+5xe−2x.
