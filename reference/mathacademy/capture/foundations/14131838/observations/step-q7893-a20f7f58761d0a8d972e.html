Solve the initial value problem y″+2y′+10y=0,y(π6)=1,  y′(π6)=2.

a
y=−e−x+π/6(cos⁡x−sin⁡3x)
b
y=ex−π/6(2cos⁡2x+sin⁡3x)
c
y=e−x+π/6(−cos⁡3x+sin⁡3x)
d
y=ex−π/6(cos⁡3x−2sin⁡3x)
e
y=−e−x+π/6(2cos⁡3x−sin⁡3x)
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

Let y=eλx. Differentiating y with respect to x gives

y′=λeλx,y″=λ2eλx.

Substituting the above into our differential equation gives λ2eλx+2λeλx+10eλx=0eλx(λ2+2λ+10)=0. The auxiliary equation is therefore

λ2+2λ+10=0.

The auxiliary equation can be solved by completing the square:

λ2+2λ+10=0(λ2+2λ+1)2−1+10=0(λ+1)2+9=0(λ+1)2=−9λ+1=±3iλ=−1±3i Therefore, the general solution to the differential equation is

y=e−x(Pcos⁡3x+Qsin⁡3x).

Now let's find the constants P and Q.

Substituting y(π6)=1 into the general solution gives

Qe−π/6=1Q=eπ/6.

We now need to apply the condition y′(π6)=2. First, we differentiate y to find y′:

y′=−e−x(Pcos⁡3x+Qsin⁡3x)+e−x(−3Psin⁡3x+3Qcos⁡3x)

Substituting y′(π6)=2 into the above gives

−Qe−π/6−3Pe−π/6=2−Q−3P=2eπ/6−eπ/6−3P=2eπ/6P=−eπ/6.

So we have Q=eπ/6 and P=−eπ/6.

Therefore, the solution to the initial value problem is y=e−x(−eπ/6cos⁡3x+eπ/6sin⁡3x)=e−x+π/6(−cos⁡3x+sin⁡3x).
