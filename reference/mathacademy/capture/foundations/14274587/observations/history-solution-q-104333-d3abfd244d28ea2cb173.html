The position of a point in cylindrical polar coordinates is written as (r,θ,z), where

  • r≥0 and θ∈[0,2π) are the usual plane polar coordinates, and

  • z∈(−∞,∞) is the usual Cartesian z-coordinate.

With that in mind, let's examine our equation:

z2=2r2

This equation requires that the square of the distance from any point on the surface to the xy-plane equals twice the square of the distance from that point to the z-axis. In other words, the tangent of the angle between any position vector on the surface and the z-axis must be equal to

rz=r2z2=±r22r2=±12.

Since the tangent of this angle can be a positive or negative number, the z-coordinate can also be positive or negative.
