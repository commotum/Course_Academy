EXPLANATION

We consider the cases λ=0 and λ≠0 separately, since they correspond to different solutions.

Case 1: First, we consider λ=0.

Setting λ=0 gives the equation y″=0. Integrating this equation gives y=Ax+B. Applying the conditions y′(0)=0 and y(3)=0 yields the trivial solution y≡0. Since the zero solution is not an eigenfunction, λ=0 does not correspond to an eigenvalue.

Case 2: Next, we consider λ>0.

To find the fundamental solutions, we set y=emx. The auxiliary equation is m2+36λ2=0. So, we have m=±6iλ. So, the fundamental solutions are y1(x)=cos⁡(6λx),y2(x)=sin⁡(6λx) and the general solution is y=Acos⁡(6λx)+Bsin⁡(6λx).

Differentiating gives y′=−6Aλsin⁡(6λx)+6Bλcos⁡(6λx).

Applying the boundary condition y′(0)=0 yields −6Aλsin⁡(0)+6Bλcos⁡(0)=0 which gives B=0. So, we have y=Acos⁡(6λx). Applying the boundary condition y(3)=0 yields Acos⁡(18λ)=0. For nontrivial solutions, we require 18λ=π2+nπ=(2n+1)π2 for n∈N. This gives the parameter values corresponding to the eigenvalues: λn=(2n+1)π36. The corresponding eigenfunctions are yn(x)=Bncos⁡(6λnx),Bn∈R.
