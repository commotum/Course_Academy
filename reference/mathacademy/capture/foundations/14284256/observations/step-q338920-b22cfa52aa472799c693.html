EXPLANATION

We consider the cases λ=0 and λ>0 separately, since they correspond to different solutions.

Case 1: First, we consider λ=0.

Setting λ=0 gives the equation y″=0. Integrating this equation gives y=Ax+B. Applying the conditions y(0)=y(π)=0 yields the trivial solution y≡0. Since the zero solution is not an eigenfunction, λ=0 does not correspond to an eigenvalue.

Case 2: Next, we consider λ>0.

To find the fundamental solutions, we set y=emx. The auxiliary equation is m2+16λ=0. So, we have m=±4iλ. So, the fundamental solutions are y1(x)=cos⁡(4xλ),y2(x)=sin⁡(4xλ) and the general solution is y=Acos⁡(4xλ)+Bsin⁡(4xλ).

Applying the boundary condition y(0)=0 yields Acos⁡(0)+Bsin⁡(0)=0 which gives A=0. So, we have y=Bsin⁡(4xλ).

Applying the boundary condition y(π)=0 yields Bsin⁡(4πλ)=0. For nontrivial solutions, we require 4πλ=nπ for n∈N. This gives the parameter values corresponding to the eigenvalues: λn=n216. The corresponding eigenfunctions are yn(x)=Bnsin⁡(4xλn),Bn∈R.
