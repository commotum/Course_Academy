EXPLANATION

We consider the cases λ=0 and λ≠0 separately, since they correspond to different solutions.

Case 1: First, we consider λ=0.

Setting λ=0 gives the equation y″=0. Integrating this equation gives y=Ax+B. Applying the conditions y′(0)=y′(2)=0 yields A=0. Thus, we have a nontrivial solution, so λ=0 is a parameter corresponding to the eigenvalue.

Case 2: Next, we consider λ>0.

To find the fundamental solutions, we set y=emx. The auxiliary equation is m2+49λ2=0. So, we have m=±7iλ. So, the fundamental solutions are y1(x)=cos⁡(7λx),y2(x)=sin⁡(7λx) and the general solution is y=Acos⁡(7λx)+Bsin⁡(7λx).

Differentiating gives y′=−7Aλsin⁡(7λx)+7Bλcos⁡(7λx).

Applying the boundary condition y′(0)=0 yields −7Aλsin⁡(0)+7Bλcos⁡(0)=0 which gives B=0. So, we have y=Acos⁡(7λx). Applying the boundary condition y′(2)=0 yields −7Aλsin⁡(14λ)=0.
