EXPLANATION

ln⁡(x+y+z)=ln⁡(2)−xyzln⁡(x+y+z)+xyz=ln⁡(2)

If we set f(x,y,z)=ln⁡(x+y+z)+xyz, our surface can be viewed as the level surface f(x,y,z)=ln⁡(2).

A normal vector to the level surface that passes through the point (1,0,1) can be found by computing ∇f(1,0,1).

So, we find the gradient and evaluate it at (1,0,1):

∇f(x,y,z)=fx(x,y,z)i+fy(x,y,z) j+fz(x,y,z) k=∂∂x(ln⁡(x+y+z)+xyz)i+∂∂y(ln⁡(x+y+z)+xyz)j+∂∂z(ln⁡(x+y+z)+xyz)k=(1x+y+z+yz) i+(1x+y+z+xz) j+(1x+y+z+xy) k∇f(1,0,1)=(11+0+1+(0)(1))i+(11+0+1+(1)(1)) j+(11+0+1+(1)(0))k=12i+32j+12k=⟨12,32,12⟩
