Newton's law of universal gravitation states that the gravitational force attracting two objects is proportional to the product of the two masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centers of mass: F=Gm1m2r2 where

  • F is the gravitational force between the objects, measured in Newtons (N),

  • G=(6.67×10−11)m3/(kg⋅s2) is the Newtonian constant of gravitation,

  • m1 and m2 are the masses of the objects, measured in kg, and

  • r is the distance between their centers of mass, measured in m.

First, let's rearrange the formula to make r the subject:

F=Gm1m2r2r2=Gm1m2Fr=Gm1m2F

Let m1 be the mass of the Sun and m2 be the mass of Neptune. Then, substituting in the known values, we have

r2=(6.67×10−11)⋅(1.99×1030)⋅(1.02×1026)6.69×1020=6.67⋅1.99⋅1.026.69×10−11⋅1030⋅10261020=(2.0237…)×1025=(20.237…)×1024.

Finally, taking the square root, we get

r=20.237…×1024=20.237…×1024=(4.498…)×1012.

Therefore, rounded to three significant figures, the distance between the Sun and Neptune is (4.50×1012)m.
