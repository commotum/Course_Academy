Let A be the set of all multiples of 4 that are greater than 0 and let B be the set of all multiples of 6 that are greater than 0. What is B∖A?

a
{4,8,16,20,28,…}
b
{12,24,36,48,60,…}
c
{18,30,42,54,66,…}
d
∅
e
{6,18,30,42,54,…}

Correct
EXPLANATION

The multiples of 4 that are greater than 0 are

A={4,8,12,16,20,24,28,32,36,…}.

Likewise, the multiples of 6 that are greater than 0 are

B={6,12,18,24,30,36,…}.

Now, B∖A is the difference of B and A, which means that we want to find the elements that are in B but not in A.

The elements of B that are also in A are 12,24,36, and so on (i.e. multiples of 12). So, we must remove these elements from B. Therefore, we have

B∖A=B−A={6,12,18,24,30,36,…}−{4,8,12,16,20,24,28,32,36,…}={6,18,30,42,54,…}.
