A company manufactures electronic components. The components are produced by three machines, A, B, and C. In a particular random sample, 31 of the components were produced by machine A, 34 by machine B, and 35 by machine C. On inspecting the sample for defective components, it was found that 2 defective components were produced by machine A, 1 by machine B, and 2 by machine C. Based on this data, what is the probability that a randomly selected component from the sample is defective?

EXPLANATION

Let S be the event that the chosen component is defective, and

  • let A be the event that the component was produced by machine A,

  • let B be the event that the component was produced by machine B,

  • let C be the event that the component was produced by machine C.

Then the disjoint union A⊔B⊔C covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability that a randomly selected component from the sample is defective, we use the law of total probability:

P(S)=P(S∩A)+P(S∩B)+P(S∩C)

First, notice that there is a total of

31+34+35=100

components in the sample.

  • To calculate P(S∩A), we take the number defective components produced by machine A (2) and divide by the total number of components produced (100). So, we have P(S∩A)=2100.

  • To calculate P(S∩B), we take the number defective components produced by machine B (1) and divide by the total number of components produced (100). So, we have P(S∩B)=1100.

  • To calculate P(S∩C), we take the number defective components produced by machine C (2) and divide by the total number of components produced (100). So, we have P(S∩C)=2100.

Finally, substituting the above information into the law of total probability, the probability that a randomly component is defective is

P(S)=P(S∩A)+P(S∩B)+P(S∩C)=2100+1100+2100=5100=120.
