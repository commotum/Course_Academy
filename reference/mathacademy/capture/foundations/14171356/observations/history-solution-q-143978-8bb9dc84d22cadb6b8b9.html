Let D be the event of selecting a defective bulb at random from one of the three boxes, and

  • let A be the event that the bulb is from box A,

  • let B be the event that the bulb is from box B

  • let C be the event that the bulb is from box C.

Then the disjoint union A⊔B⊔C covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability of selecting a defective bulb, we can use the law of total probability:

P(D)=P(D∩A)+P(D∩B)+P(D∩C)=P(D|A)P(A)+P(D|B)P(B)+P(D|C)P(C)

Let's go through the problem statement and translate each piece of information into mathematical notation.

  • Since a box is chosen at random, we have P(A)=P(B)=P(C)=13.

  • Since there are 2 defective bulbs out of 15 in box A and 1 defective bulb out of 10 in box B, we have P(D|A)=215,P(D|B)=110.

  • Since the probability that a randomly selected bulb is defective is 215, we have P(D)=215.

Substituting the above information into the law of total probability and solving for P(D|C), we get

P(D)=P(D|A)P(A)+P(D|B)P(B)+P(D|C)P(C)215=(215)(13)+(110)(13)+P(D|C)(13)215=245+130+13P(D|C)13P(D|C)=118P(D|C)=16

Therefore, the probability that a randomly selected bulb from box C is defective is 16.
