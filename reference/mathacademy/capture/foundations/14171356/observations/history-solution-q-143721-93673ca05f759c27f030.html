Let D be the event that a randomly selected tool is defective, and

  • let A be the event that the tool was produced by factory A,

  • let B be the event that the tool was produced by factory B,

  • let C be the event that the tool was produced by factory C.

Then the disjoint union A⊔B⊔C covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability that a randomly selected tool is defective, we can use the law of total probability:

P(D)=P(D∩A)+P(D∩B)+P(D∩C)=P(D|A)P(A)+P(D|B)P(B)+P(D|C)P(C)

Let's go through the problem statement and translate each piece of information into mathematical notation.

  • Since factory A produces 30% of the tools, factory B produces 34% of the tools, and factory C produces 36% of the tools, we have P(A)=0.3,P(B)=0.34,P(C)=0.36.

  • Since 2% of the tools produced by factory A, 5% of the tools produced by factory B, and 5% of the tools produced by factory C, are defective, we have P(D|A)=0.02,P(D|B)=0.05,P(D|C)=0.05.

Substituting the above information into the law of total probability, the probability that a randomly selected tool is defective is

P(D)=P(D|A)P(A)+P(D|B)P(B)+P(D|C)P(C)=(0.02)(0.3)+(0.05)(0.34)+(0.05)(0.36)=0.006+0.017+0.018=0.041.
