Let S be the event that the selected email is spam, and

  • let A1 be the event that the email was received in the first account,

  • let A2 be the event that the email was received in the second account,

  • let A3 be the event that the email was received in the third account.

Then the disjoint union A1⊔A2⊔A3 covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability that a randomly selected email is spam, we use the law of total probability:

P(S)=P(S∩A1)+P(S∩A2)+P(S∩A3)=P(S|A1)P(A1)+P(S|A2)P(A2)+P(S|A3)P(A3)

Let's go through the problem statement and translate each piece of information into mathematical notation.

  • Since Ann receives 40% of her emails in the first account, 35% in the second account, and 25% in the third account, we have P(A1)=0.4,P(A2)=0.35,P(A3)=0.25.

  • Since 10% of the emails received in the first account, 8% of the emails received in the first account, and 8% of the emails received in the third account are spam, we have P(S|A1)=0.1,P(S|A2)=0.08P(S|A3)=0.08.

P(S)=P(S|A1)P(A1)+P(S|A2)P(A2)+P(S|A3)P(A3)=(0.1)(0.4)+(0.08)(0.35)+(0.08)(0.25)=0.04+0.028+0.02=0.088.
