EXPLANATION

We wish to find the limits of integration such that our integral can be expressed as ∫ab∫v1(y)v2(y)∫u1(y,z)u2(y,z)dxdzdy+∫cd∫v3(y)v4(y)∫u3(y,z)u4(y,z)dxdzdy.

From the repeated integral, we see that the domain of integration R can be written as a type II region as follows:

R={(x,y,z):0≤z≤1,−z2−1≤y≤−z,0≤x≤2y+z}

Since all repeated integrals first integrate with respect to x, the x-limits remain the same as before:

∫01∫−z2−1−z∫02y+zdxdydz=∫ab∫v1(y)v2(y)∫02y+zdxdzdy+∫cd∫v3(y)v4(y)∫02y+zdxdzdy

Now, we consider the projection D of R onto the yz-plane shown below.

The region D is a type II region in the yz-plane:

D={(x,y):0≤z≤1,−z2−1≤y≤−z}.

To change the order of integration from dxdydz to dxdzdy, we split D into two type I regions.

Notice that the bottom and top boundaries of the left region, written in the form z=z(y), are

z=−y−1 and z=1.

Also, the bottom and top boundaries of the right region, written in the form z=z(y), are

z=0 and z=−y.

So, we can write D as a union of

D1={(y,z):−2≤y≤−1,−y−1≤z≤1}

and

D2={(y,z):−1≤y≤0,0≤z≤−y}.

Therefore, by swapping the order of integration, we obtain

∫−2−1∫−y−11∫02y+zdxdzdy+∫−10∫0−y∫02y+zdxdzdy.
