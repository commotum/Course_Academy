By changing the order of integration in the following repeated integrals so that they are integrated first with respect to x, then z, and then y, express the following sum as a single repeated integral.

∫01∫02z∫−yzf(x,y,z)dxdydz+∫13∫03−z∫−yzf(x,y,z)dxdydz

a
 ∫02∫y2/23−2y∫−yzf(x,y,z)dxdzdy
b
 ∫02∫y/23−y∫−yzf(x,y,z)dxdzdy
c
 ∫03∫y/23+y∫−yzf(x,y,z)dxdzdy
d
 ∫03∫2y3−y∫−yzf(x,y,z)dxdzdy
e
 ∫02∫2y2y+3∫−yzf(x,y,z)dxdzdy

Correct
EXPLANATION

We wish to find the limits of integration such that our integral can be expressed as ∫ab∫v1(y)v2(y)∫u1(y,z)u2(y,z)f(x,y,z)dxdzdy.

From the repeated integrals, we see that the domain of integration R=R1∪R2, where R1 and R2 are type II regions, is given by

R1={(x,y,z):0≤z≤1,0≤y≤2z,−y≤x≤z},R2={(x,y,z):1≤z≤3,0≤y≤3−z,−y≤x≤z}.

Since all repeated integrals first integrate with respect to x, the x-limits remain the same as before:

∫ab∫v1(y)v2(y)∫−yzf(x,y,z)dxdzdy

Now, we consider the projection D of R onto the yz-plane shown below.

The region D consists of two type II regions in the yz-plane:

D={(y,z):0≤z≤1,0≤y≤2z}∪{(y,z):1≤z≤3,0≤y≤3−z}.

To change the order of integration from dxdydz to dxdzdy, we write D as a type I region.

Notice that the bottom boundary of the region, written in the form z=z(y), is y=2z⟹z=y2 and the top boundary of the region is y=3−z⟹z=3−y.

Then, the type I representation of the region is

D={(y,z):0≤y≤2,y2≤z≤3−y},

as shown in the diagram below.

Therefore, by swapping the order of integration, we obtain

∫02∫y/23−y∫−yzf(x,y,z)dxdzdy.
