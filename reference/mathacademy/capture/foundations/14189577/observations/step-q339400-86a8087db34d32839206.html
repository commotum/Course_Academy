A fermentation vat initially holds 500L of liquid containing 8kg of dissolved nutrient. A feed solution containing 0.06kg/L of the nutrient is pumped into the vat at a rate of 10L/min, while the completely mixed liquid is drained out at a rate of 5L/min. Find, rounded to two decimal places, the amount of nutrient in the vat 8 minutes after pumping begins.

11.58kg

12.67kg

12.03kg

10.94kg

Correct
EXPLANATION

Let Q be the amount of nutrient in the vat at time t, in minutes. Then, the derivative dQdt describes how this amount changes with respect to time t. This is equal to the rate at which nutrient enters the tank minus the rate at which nutrient leaves the tank.

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′

Note that the units of dQdt,Qin′ and Qout′ are kg/min. Now, let's compute the required rates of change in steps.

Step 1: Calculating the rate at which nutrient enters the vat.

Note that a feed solution containing 0.06kg/L of the nutrient enters the vat at a rate of 10L/min. So, the rate at which the nutrient enters the vat is

Qin′=(0.06kgL)⋅(10Lmin)=(0.06kgL)⋅(10Lmin)=0.6kgmin.

Step 2: Calculating the rate at which nutrient exits the vat.

To find the rate at which nutrient leaves the vat, we must first determine the volume of liquid in the vat at time t.

The vat initially contains 500L of liquid; a solution enters at 10L/min and leaves at 5L/min. As a result, the volume of liquid in the vat after t minutes is 500+10t−5t=(500+5t)L.

Since Q is the amount of nutrient in the vat, the concentration of the mixed solution at time t is Q500+5tkg/L.

Therefore, as the completely mixed liquid leaves at a rate of 5L/min, nutrient leaves at a rate of

Qout′=(5Lmin)⋅(Q500+5tkgL)=(5Lmin)⋅(Q500+5tkgL)=5Q500+5tkgmin=Q100+tkgmin.

Step 3: Calculate the overall rate of change.

The rate of change equals the rate of entry minus the rate of exit. Therefore,

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′=0.6−Q100+t

which gives the equation dQdt+1100+tQ=0.6.

Step 4: Integrate the equation.

This is a first-order linear equation with variable coefficients. So we find the general solution using an integrating factor:

I(t)=e∫1/(100+t)dt=eln⁡|100+t|=100+t

Multiplying both sides of the differential equation by I(t)=100+t yields

(100+t)⋅(dQdt+1100+tQ)=(100+t)⋅0.6(100+t)dQdt+Q=0.6(100+t)ddt(Q(100+t))=0.6(100+t).

Now, we integrate with respect to t:

∫ddt(Q(100+t))dt=∫0.6(100+t)dtQ(100+t)=0.6⋅12(100+t)2+CQ(t)=0.3(100+t)+C100+t

Note that C is a constant of integration.

We're told that the vat initially contains 8kg of nutrient. So, we can apply the initial condition Q(0)=8, and solve for C:

Q(0)=80.3(100+0)+C100+0=830+C100=8C100=−22C=−2200

Finally, we obtain the following expression for Q(t):

Q(t)=0.3(100+t)−2200100+t.

Finally, the amount of nutrient in the vat 8 minutes after pumping begins is

Q(8)=0.3(100+8)−2200100+8=32.4−2200108≈12.03kg,

rounded to two decimal places.
