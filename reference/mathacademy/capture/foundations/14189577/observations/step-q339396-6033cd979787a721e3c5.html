EXPLANATION

Since Q is the amount of salt in the tank at time t, the derivative dQdt describes how this amount changes with respect to time t, in minutes. This is equal to the rate at which sweetener enters the tank minus the rate at which sweetener leaves the tank.

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′

Note that the units of dQdt,Qin′ and Qout′ are kg/min. Now, let's compute the required rates of change in steps.

Step 1: Calculating the rate at which sweetener enter the tank.

First, note that a solution containing 0.05kg/L of sweetener enters the tank at a rate of 24L/min. So, the rate at which the sweetener enters the tank is

Qin′=(0.05kgL)⋅(24Lmin)=(0.05kgL)⋅(24Lmin)=1.2kgmin.

Step 2: Calculating the rate at which sweetener exit the tank.

To find the rate at which sweetener leaves the tank, we must first determine the volume of liquid in the tank at time t.

The tank initially contains 240L of liquid; a solution enters at 24L/min and leaves at 32L/min. As a result, the amount of liquid in the tank after t minutes is 240+24t−32t=(240−8t)L.

Since Q is the amount of sweetener in the tank, the concentration of the mixed solution at time t is Q240−8tkg/L.

Therefore, as the completely mixed liquid leaves at a rate of 32L/min, sweetener leaves at a rate of

Qout′=(32Lmin)⋅(Q240−8tkgL)=(32Lmin)⋅(Q240−8tkgL)=32Q240−8tkgmin=4Q30−tkgmin.

Step 3: Calculate the overall rate of change.

The rate of change equals the rate of entry minus the rate of exit. Therefore,

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′=1.2−4Q30−t
