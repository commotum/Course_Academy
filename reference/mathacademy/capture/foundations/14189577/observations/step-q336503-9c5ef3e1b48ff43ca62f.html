A fertilizer tank for an irrigation system can hold up to 210gal of solution. Initially, it contains 90gal of fertilizer solution with 18lb of dissolved fertilizer. A more concentrated solution with concentration 0.5lb/gal flows into the tank at a rate of 18gal/min, while the completely mixed solution drains out at a rate of 12gal/min. Rounded to one decimal place, find the amount of fertilizer in the tank at the moment it begins to overflow.

EXPLANATION

Since Q is the amount of fertilizer in the tank at time t, the derivative dQdt describes how this amount changes with respect to time t, in minutes. This is equal to the rate at which fertilizer enters the tank minus the rate at which fertilizer leaves the tank.

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′

Note that the units of dQdt,Qin′ and Qout′ are lb/min. Now, let's compute the required rates of change in steps.

Step 1: Calculating the rate at which fertilizer enters the tank.

First, note that a solution containing 0.5lb/gal of fertilizer enters the tank at a rate of 18gal/min. So, the rate at which the fertilizer enters the tank is

Qin′=(0.5lbgal)⋅(18galmin)=(0.5lbgal)⋅(18galmin)=9lbmin.

Step 2: Calculating the rate at which fertilizer exits the tank.

To find the rate at which fertilizer leaves the tank, we must first determine the volume of solution in the tank at time t.

The tank initially contains 90gal of solution; solution enters at 18gal/min and mixed solution leaves at 12gal/min. As a result, the amount of solution in the tank after t minutes is V(t)=90+18t−12t=6(15+t)gal.

Note that V(t) is an increasing function, so the tank is filling.

Since Q is the amount of fertilizer in the tank, the concentration of the solution at time t is Q6(15+t)lb/gal.

Therefore, as the completely mixed solution leaves at a rate of 12gal/min, fertilizer leaves at a rate of

Qout′=(12galmin)⋅(Q6(15+t)lbgal)=(12galmin)⋅(Q6(15+t)lbgal)=12Q6(15+t)lbmin=2Q15+tlbmin.

Step 3: Calculate the overall rate of change.

The rate of change equals the rate of entry minus the rate of exit. Therefore,

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′=9−2Q15+t

which gives the equation dQdt+215+tQ=9.

Step 4: Integrate the equation.

This is a first-order linear equation with variable coefficients. So we find the general solution using an integrating factor:

Multiplying both sides of the differential equation by I(t)=(15+t)2 yields

∫ddt(Q(15+t)2)dt=∫9(15+t)2dtQ(15+t)2=9⋅13(15+t)3+CQ(t)=3(15+t)+C(15+t)2.

We're told that the tank initially contains 18lb of fertilizer. So, we can apply the initial condition Q(0)=18, and solve for C:

Q(0)=183(15+0)+C(15+0)2=1845+C(15)2=18C152=−27C=−27(15)2.

Q(t)=3(15+t)−27(15)2(15+t)2=3(15+t)−27(1515+t)2.

V(to)=21090+18to−12to=21090+6to=2106to=120to=20min.
