A mixing tank initially holds 400L of liquid containing 6kg of dissolved cleaning agent. A feed solution containing 0.08kg/L of the cleaning agent is pumped into the tank at a rate of 20L/min, while the completely mixed liquid is drained out at a rate of 20L/min. Find, rounded to two decimal places, the amount of cleaning agent in the tank 5 minutes after pumping begins.

a
12.18kg
b
11.75kg
c
11.32kg
d
10.94kg
e
12.63kg
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

Let Q be the amount of cleaning agent in the tank at time t, in minutes. Then, the derivative dQdt describes how this amount changes with respect to time t. This is equal to the rate at which cleaning agents enter the tank minus the rate at which cleaning agents leave the tank.

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′

Note that the units of dQdt,Qin′ and Qout′ are kg/min. Now, let's compute the required rates of change in steps.

Step 1: Calculating the rate at which cleaning agents enter the tank.

Note that a feed solution containing 0.08kg/L of the cleaning agent enters the tank at a rate of 20L/min. So, the rate at which the cleaning agent enters the tank is

Qin′=(0.08kgL)⋅(20Lmin)=(0.08kgL)⋅(20Lmin)=1.6kgmin.

Step 2: Calculating the rate at which cleaning agents exit the tank.

Next, note that the rate of flow in equals the rate of flow out, so the volume in the tank remains 400L. Then, since Q is the amount of cleaning agent in the tank, the concentration of the solution at time t is Q400kg/L.

Therefore, as the completely mixed liquid leaves at a rate of 20L/min, cleaning agent leaves at a rate of

Qout′=(20Lmin)⋅(Q400kgL)=(20Lmin)⋅(Q400kgL)=20Q400kgmin=Q20kgmin.

Step 3: Calculate the overall rate of change.

The rate of change equals the rate of entry minus the rate of exit. Therefore,

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′=1.6−Q20

which gives the equation

dQdt+120Q=1.6.

Step 4: Integrate the equation.

This is a first-order linear equation with constant coefficients. To find the general solution, we must sum the complementary and particular solutions.

  • To find the complementary solution Qc, we solve the corresponding homogeneous equation, given by dQcdt+120Qc=0. Solving using an integration technique, such as separation of variables, we obtain the complementary solution Qc(t)=Ae−t/20, where A is a constant of integration.

  • Next, we find the particular solution. Note that the right-hand side of the inhomogeneous equation is a polynomial of degree 0. So, we assume that the particular solution is also a polynomial of degree 0, i.e., Qp(t)=α. To find the value of α, we substitute Qp=α and dQpdt=(α)′=0 into the inhomogeneous equation: 0+120α=1.6⟹α=32. Therefore, the particular solution is Qp(t)=32.

Now, since the general solution is given by the sum of the complementary solution and the particular solution, the general solution in our case is

Q(t)=Qc(t)+Qp(t)=Ae−t/20+32.

We're told that the tank initially contains 6kg of dissolved cleaning agent. So, we can apply the initial condition Q(0)=6, and solve for A:

Q(0)=6Ae−0/20+32=6A=−26

Therefore, we obtain the following expression for Q(t):

Q(t)=32−26e−t/20

Finally, the amount of cleaning agent in the tank 5 minutes after pumping begins is

Q(5)=32−26e−5/20≈11.75kg,

rounded to two decimal places.
