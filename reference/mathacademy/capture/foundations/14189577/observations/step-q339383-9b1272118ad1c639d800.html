A pharmaceutical mixer initially holds 300L of liquid containing 1kg of dissolved medication. A solution containing 0.04kg/L of dissolved medication is pumped into the mixer at a rate of 15L/min, while the completely mixed liquid is drained out at a rate of 15L/min. Which expression gives the amount of medication Q(t), in kilograms, in the mixer t minutes after pumping begins?

a
Q(t)=12−11e−t/20
b
Q(t)=12−11et/20
c
Q(t)=1−12e−t/20
d
Q(t)=12−11e−t/15
e
Q(t)=12+11e−t/20
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

Let Q be the amount of medication in the mixer at time t, in minutes. Then, the derivative dQdt describes how this amount changes with respect to time t. This is equal to the rate at which medication enters the mixer minus the rate at which medication leaves the mixer.

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′

Note that the units of dQdt,Qin′ and Qout′ are kg/min. Now, let's compute the required rates of change in steps.

Step 1: Calculating the rate at which additives enter the mixer.

Note that a feed solution containing 0.04kg/L of the medication enters the mixer at a rate of 15L/min. So, the rate at which the medication enters the mixer is

Qin′=(0.04kgL)⋅(15Lmin)=(0.04kgL)⋅(15Lmin)=0.6kgmin.

Step 2: Calculating the rate at which additives exit the mixer.

Next, note that the rate of flow in equals the rate of flow out, so the volume in the mixer remains 300L. Then, since Q is the amount of medication in the mixer, the concentration of the solution at time t is Q300kg/L.

Therefore, as the completely mixed liquid leaves at a rate of 15L/min, medication leaves at a rate of

Qout′=(15Lmin)⋅(Q300kgL)=(15Lmin)⋅(Q300kgL)=15Q300kgmin=Q20kgmin.

Step 3: Calculate the overall rate of change.

The rate of change equals the rate of entry minus the rate of exit. Therefore,

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′=0.6−Q20

which gives the equation

dQdt+120Q=0.6.

Step 4: Integrate the equation.

This is a first-order linear equation with constant coefficients. To find the general solution, we must sum the complementary and particular solutions.

  • To find the complementary solution Qc, we solve the corresponding homogeneous equation, given by dQcdt+120Qc=0. Solving using an integration technique, such as separation of variables, we obtain the complementary solution Qc(t)=Ae−t/20, where A is a constant of integration.

  • Next, we find the particular solution. Note that the right-hand side of the inhomogeneous equation is a polynomial of degree 0. So, we assume that the particular solution is also a polynomial of degree 0, i.e., Qp(t)=α. To find the value of α, we substitute Qp=α and dQpdt=(α)′=0 into the inhomogeneous equation: 0+120α=0.6⟹α=12. Therefore, the particular solution is Qp(t)=12.

Now, since the general solution is given by the sum of the complementary solution and the particular solution, the general solution in our case is

Q(t)=Qc(t)+Qp(t)=Ae−t/20+12.

We're told that the mixer initially contains 1kg of dissolved medication. So, we can apply the initial condition Q(0)=1, and solve for A:

Q(0)=1Ae−0/20+12=1A=−11

Therefore, we obtain the following expression for Q(t):

Q(t)=12−11e−t/20.
