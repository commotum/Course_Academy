A generalized eigenvector vn of rank n corresponding to the eigenvalue λ of a matrix A is a nonzero vector such that vn→A−λIvn−1→A−λI…→A−λIv1→A−λI0, where vn−1,vn−2,…,v1≠0. An eigenvector is simply a generalized eigenvector of rank 1.

With that in mind, let's examine our statements in turn.

  • Statement I is false. Since w→A−6I0, i.e. A−6I maps w to 0 in 1 step, the vector w is a generalized eigenvector of rank 1, not 3.

  • Statement II is true. Indeed, since x→A+7Iy→A+7Iz→A+7I0, i.e. A+7I maps x to 0 in 3 steps, the vector x is a generalzed eigenvector of rank 3 corresponding to the eigenvalue λ=−7 of A.

  • Statement III is true. Indeed, since y→A+7Iz→A+7I0, i.e. A+7I maps y to 0 in 2 steps, the vector y is a generalized eigenvector of rank 2 corresponding to the eigenvalue λ=−7 of A.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II and III only."
