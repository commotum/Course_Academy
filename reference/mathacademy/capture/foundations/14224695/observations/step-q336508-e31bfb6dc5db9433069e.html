A wastewater treatment tank at a chemical plant can hold up to 180gal of liquid. Initially, it contains 120gal of wastewater with 40lb of dissolved contaminant. A more concentrated wastewater stream with concentration 1.25lb/gal flows into the tank at a rate of 12gal/min, while the completely mixed solution drains out at a rate of 8gal/min. Rounded to one decimal place, find the amount of contaminant in the tank at the moment it begins to overflow.

EXPLANATION

Since Q is the amount of contaminant in the tank at time t, the derivative dQdt describes how this amount changes with respect to time t, in minutes. This is equal to the rate at which contaminant enters the tank minus the rate at which contaminant leaves the tank.

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′

Note that the units of dQdt,Qin′ and Qout′ are lb/min. Now, let's compute the required rates of change in steps.

Step 1: Calculating the rate at which contaminant enters the tank.

First, note that a solution containing 1.25lb/gal of contaminant enters the tank at a rate of 12gal/min. So, the rate at which the contaminant enters the tank is

Qin′=(1.25lbgal)⋅(12galmin)=(1.25lbgal)⋅(12galmin)=15lbmin.

Step 2: Calculating the rate at which contaminant exits the tank.

To find the rate at which contaminant leaves the tank, we must first determine the volume of liquid in the tank at time t.

The tank initially contains 120gal of liquid; solution enters at 12gal/min and mixed solution leaves at 8gal/min. As a result, the amount of liquid in the tank after t minutes is V(t)=120+12t−8t=4(30+t)gal.

Note that V(t) is an increasing function, so the tank is filling.

Since Q is the amount of contaminant in the tank, the concentration of the solution at time t is Q4(30+t)lb/gal.

Therefore, as the completely mixed solution leaves at a rate of 8gal/min, contaminant leaves at a rate of

Qout′=(8galmin)⋅(Q4(30+t)lbgal)=(8galmin)⋅(Q4(30+t)lbgal)=8Q4(30+t)lbmin=2Q30+tlbmin.

Step 3: Calculate the overall rate of change.

The rate of change equals the rate of entry minus the rate of exit. Therefore,

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′=15−2Q30+t

which gives the equation dQdt+230+tQ=15.

Step 4: Integrate the equation.

This is a first-order linear equation with variable coefficients. So we find the general solution using an integrating factor:

I(t)=e∫2/(30+t)dt=e2ln⁡|30+t|=eln⁡(30+t)2=(30+t)2.

Multiplying both sides of the differential equation by I(t)=(30+t)2 yields

(30+t)2⋅(dQdt+230+tQ)=(30+t)2⋅15(30+t)2dQdt+2(30+t)Q=15(30+t)2ddt(Q(30+t)2)=15(30+t)2.

Now, we integrate with respect to t:

∫ddt(Q(30+t)2)dt=∫15(30+t)2dtQ(30+t)2=15⋅13(30+t)3+CQ(t)=5(30+t)+C(30+t)2.

We're told that the tank initially contains 40lb of contaminant. So, we can apply the initial condition Q(0)=40, and solve for C:

Q(0)=405(30+0)+C(30+0)2=40150+C(30)2=40C302=−110C=−110(30)2.

Therefore, we obtain the following expression for Q(t):

Q(t)=5(30+t)−110(30)2(30+t)2=5(30+t)−110(3030+t)2.

Now, let to be the time when the tank begins to overflow. The tank has a capacity of 180gal, so we can use the condition V(to)=180 to solve for to:

V(to)=180120+12to−8to=180120+4to=1804to=60to=15min.

Finally, rounded to one decimal place, the amount of contaminant in the tank at the moment it begins to overflow is

Q(15)=5(30+15)−110(3030+15)2=225−110(3045)2≈176.1lb.
