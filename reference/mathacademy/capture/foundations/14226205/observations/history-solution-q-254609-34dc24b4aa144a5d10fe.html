Since the cyclist encounters potholes continuously in time, independently, and at a constant average rate, we can model the time until the next pothole as an exponential distribution.

Let X be the time, in minutes, until the cyclist encounters the next pothole. Since the cyclist encounters, on average, 4 potholes per hour (or 1 pothole every 15 minutes), the rate is λ=115 potholes per minute, and we can write X∼Exp(115).

Recall that exponential random variables have the memoryless property. Therefore, given two positive real numbers s,t, we have that P(X>s+t|X>s)=P(X>t)=e−λt.

We can compute the complementary conditional probability as follows: P(X≤s+t|X>s)=1−P(X>s+t|X>s)=1−e−λt

In our case, we have λ=115, s=30, and t=10.

Substituting these values into the above formula, we get P(X≤30+10|X>30)=P(X≤10)=1−e−λt=1−e−(1/15)⋅(10)=1−e−2/3≈0.487.
