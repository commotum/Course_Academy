Given a second-order linear inhomogeneous differential equation, the variation of parameters method requires that we seek a particular solution of the form yp(x)=u1(x)y1(x)+u2(x)y2(x) that satisfies the constraint yp′=u1y1′+u2y2′. Here, y1 and y2 are linearly independent solutions of the corresponding homogeneous equation, and u1 and u2 are functions to be determined.

In this case, we're told that the fundamental solutions are

y1=ex,y2=e−4x.

Substituting the fundamental solutions into our expression for yp, we get

yp=u1ex+u2e−4x.

Substituting the fundamental solutions into our constraint equation for yp′, we get

yp′=u1(ex)′+u2(e−4x)′.

Next, we substitute y=yp and y′=yp′ into the differential equation, as follows:

y″+3y′−4y=f(x)(yp′)′+3yp′−4yp=f(x)

Using our expressions for yp and yp′, we have: (u1(ex)′+u2(e−4x)′)′+3(u1(ex)′+u2(e−4x)′)−4(u1ex+u2e−4x)=f(x)(u1ex−4u2e−4x)′+3u1ex−12u2e−4x−4u1ex−4u2e−4x=f(x)u1′ex+u1ex−4u2′e−4x+16u2e−4x+3u1ex−12u2e−4x−4u1ex−4u2e−4x=f(x)

Next, we group all terms proportional to u1 and u2:

u1(ex+3ex−4ex)⏟0+u2(16e−4x−12e−4x−4e−4x)⏟0+u1′ex−4u2′e−4x=f(x)

Note that the expressions in parentheses are both zero. Therefore, the equation reduces to

u1′ex−4u2′e−4x=f(x).
