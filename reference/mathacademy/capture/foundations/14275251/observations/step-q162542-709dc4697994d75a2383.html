EXPLANATION

If S is an oriented surface with unit normal vector n, and F is continuous vector field, then the flux of F across S measured with respect to n is given by ∬SF⋅dS=∬SF⋅ndS.

Notice that only the x-component of F=⟨8x2(y2+z2),0,0⟩ is non-zero. Since the lateral side of S is parallel to the x-axis, the flux of F through S will be non-zero only at the front and back sides.


Let's consider the flux of F through these two sides.

  • Let S1 denote the front surface. On this surface, we have x=1 for y2+z2≤4. The outward-pointing unit normal vector to S1 is n1=⟨1,0,0⟩. Using the fact that x=1 on S1, we have F⋅n1=⟨8x2(y2+z2),0,0⟩⋅⟨1,0,0⟩=⟨8(12)(y2+z2),0,0⟩⋅⟨1,0,0⟩=⟨8(y2+z2),0,0⟩⋅⟨1,0,0⟩=8(y2+z2). Therefore, the flux of F across S1 is given by ∬S1F⋅dS=∬S1F⋅n1dS=∬S18(y2+z2)dS=8∬D(y2+z2)dA. Applying a change of variables (y,z)→(r,θ),dA=rdrdθ, we have 8∬D(y2+z2)dA=8∬Dr2⋅rdrdθ=8∫02π∫02r3drdθ=8∫02πdθ∫02r3dr=8⋅2π⋅[14r4]02=16π⋅4=64π.

  • Let S2 denote the back surface. On this surface, we have x=0 for y2+z2≤4. Therefore, for everywhere on S2, we have F(0,y,z)=⟨8(02)(y2+z2),0,0⟩=⟨0,0,0⟩. Therefore, ∬S2F⋅ndS=∬S20⋅ndS=∬S20dS=0.

Finally, ∬SF⋅dS=∬SF⋅ndS=∬S1F⋅ndS+∬S2F⋅ndS=64π+0=64π.
