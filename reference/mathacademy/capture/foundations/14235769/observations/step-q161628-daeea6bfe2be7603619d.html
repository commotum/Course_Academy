If (r,θ,z) are the cylindrical polar coordinates of a point, then its Cartesian coordinates (x,y,z) can be found by using the formulas

x=rcos⁡θ,y=rsin⁡θ,z=z.

First, let's sketch the projection of R onto the xy-plane:

The region R is a right vertical cylinder of radius 2 whose axis passes through the point (2,0) in the xy-plane, and is

  • bounded below by z=y−4=rsin⁡θ−4, and

  • bounded above by z=3.

With that in mind, let's write the equation (x−2)2+y2=4 in polar coordinates: (x−2)2+y2=4(rcos⁡θ−2)2+(rsin⁡θ)2=4r2cos2⁡θ−4rcos⁡θ+4+r2sin2⁡θ=4r2(cos2⁡θ+sin2⁡θ)−4rcos⁡θ=0r2−4rcos⁡θ=0r(r−4cos⁡θ)=0
