Assuming y=eλx and differentiating y with respect to x gives y′=λeλx,y″=λ2eλx.

Substituting the above into our differential equation gives λ2eλx+eλx=0eλx(λ2+1)=0.

The auxiliary equation is therefore λ2+1=0.

Solving the auxiliary equation, we get λ2+1=0λ2=−1λ=±i.

So, λ=±i are the purely imaginary roots of the auxiliary equation.

Therefore, any linear combination of the functions Re(eix)=cos⁡x and Im(eix)=sin⁡x is a solution to the original differential equation.

Among the given options, only sin⁡2x is not a solution.
