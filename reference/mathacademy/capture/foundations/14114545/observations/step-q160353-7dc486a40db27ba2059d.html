EXPLANATION

We're told that T has no critical points in the interior of Δ. So, to map the entire region Δ onto the xy-plane, we transform each side of Δ separately:

  • Consider the image of the side S1, where we have v=0 and 0≤u≤1. Substituting v=0 into our transformation equations, we obtain (x,y)=T(u,v)=(v,u(1+v2))=(0,u(1+02))=(0,u),0≤u≤1. This is a vertical line segment traversed from the point (0,0) to the point (0,1) in the xy-plane.

  • Consider the image of the side S2. Here, we have u=1 and 0≤v≤1. Substituting u=1 into our transformation equations, we obtain (x,y)=T(u,v)=(v,u(1+v2))=(v,1(1+v2))=(v,1+v2),0≤v≤1. This is an upward-opening parabola traversed from the point (0,1) to the point (1,2) in the xy-plane.

  • Consider the image of the side S3. Here, we have v=1 and 1≥u≥0. Substituting v=1 into our transformation equations, we obtain (x,y)=T(u,v)=(v,u(1+v2))=(1,u(1+12))=(1,2u),1≥u≥0. This is a vertical line segment traversed from the point (1,2) to the point (1,0) in the xy-plane.

  • Consider the image of the side S4. Here, we have u=0 and 1≥v≥0. Substituting u=0 into our transformation equations, we obtain (x,y)=T(u,v)=(v,u(1+v2))=(v,0(1+v2))=(v,0),1≥v≥0. This is a horizontal line segment traversed from the point (1,0) to the point (0,0) in the xy-plane.
