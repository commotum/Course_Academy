EXPLANATION

First, we need to find the complementary function yc(x), which is the solution to the homogeneous equation

y″+2y′+26y=0.

Writing down and solving the auxiliary equation gives

λ2+2λ+26=0(λ2+2λ+1)−1+26=0(λ+1)2+25=0(λ+1)2=−25λ+1=±5iλ=−1±5i.

So λ=−1±5i, and therefore the complementary function yc(x) is yc(x)=e−x(Pcos⁡5x+Qsin⁡5x).

We now need to find the particular integral, yp(x).

The right-hand side of the inhomogeneous equation is a polynomial of degree 2. Therefore, we assume that the particular integral is also a polynomial of degree 2, i.e.

yp(x)=αx2+βx+γ where α, β, and γ are constants that are to be determined.

Calculating the first and second derivatives of yp gives

yp′=2αx+β,yp″=2α.

To find the values of α, β, and γ, we substitute the derivatives into the differential equation. This gives

2α+2(2αx+β)+26(αx2+βx+γ)=13x2+15x−24.

We then group the terms on the left-hand side, as follows:

26αx2+(26β+4α)x+(2α+2β+26γ)=13x2+15x−24

Equating the coefficients, we get the following system of equations:

{26α=13(equating the coefficients ofx2)26β+4α=15(equating the coefficients ofx)2α+2β+26γ=−24(equating the constants)

Solving this system gives α=12,β=12,γ=−1.

Therefore, the particular solution yp(x) is

yp(x)=x22+x2−1.

The general solution is given by

y(x)=yc(x)+yp(x),

and therefore, the general solution in our case is y=e−x(Pcos⁡5x+Qsin⁡5x)+x22+x2−1.

Now let's find the constants P and Q.

Substituting y(0)=2 into the general solution gives

P−1=2. Thus, P=3.

We now need to apply the condition y′(0)=52. First, we differentiate y to find y′:

y′=e−x[(5Q−P)cos⁡5x−(5P+Q)sin⁡5x]+x+12

Substituting y′(0)=52 into the above gives

5Q−P+12=52.

So we have P=3 and Q=1.

Therefore, the solution to the initial value problem is y=e−x(3cos⁡5x+sin⁡5x)+x22+x2−1.
