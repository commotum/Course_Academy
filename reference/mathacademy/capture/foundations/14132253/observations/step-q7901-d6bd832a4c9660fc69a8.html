EXPLANATION

First, we need to find the complementary function yc(x), which is the solution to the homogeneous equation

y″+6y′+9y=0.

Writing down and solving the auxiliary equation gives

λ2+6λ+9=0(λ+3)2=0.

So λ=−3 is a repeated root, and therefore the complementary function yc(x) is yc(x)=Ae−3x+Bxe−3x.

We now need to find the particular integral yp(x).

The right-hand side of the inhomogeneous equation is a polynomial of degree 0. Therefore, we assume that the particular integral is also a polynomial of degree 0, i.e.

yp(x)=α where α is a constant that is to be determined.

Calculating the first and second derivatives of yp gives

yp′=0,yp″=0.

To find the value of α, we substitute the derivatives into the differential equation:

0+0+9α=3⟹α=13

Therefore,

yp(x)=13.

The general solution is given by

y(x)=yc(x)+yp(x),

and therefore, the general solution in our case is y=Ae−3x+Bxe−3x+13.

Now let's find the constants A and B.

Substituting y(0)=43 into the general solution gives

A+13=43.

Thus, A=1.

We now need to apply the condition y′(0)=5. First, we differentiate y to find y′:

y′=(B−3A)e−3x−3Bxe−3x

Substituting y′(0)=5 into the above gives

B−3A=5.

So we have A=1 and B=8.

Therefore, the solution to the initial value problem is y=e−3x+8xe−3x+13.
