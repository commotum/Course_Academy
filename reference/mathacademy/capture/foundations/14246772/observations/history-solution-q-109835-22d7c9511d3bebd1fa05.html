For the given matrix D with eigenvalue λ=−e, a generalized eigenvector of rank 4 is a vector v such that

(D+eI)4v=0and(D+eI)3v≠0.

Notice the following:

  • We are given that Null(D+eI)3 has dimension 3. This means that the solutions to (D+eI)3v=0 form a subspace of dimension 3.

  • We are given that Null(D+eI)4 has dimension 4. This means that the solutions to (D+eI)4v=0 form a subspace of dimension 4.

Now, since Null(D+eI)3 is a subspace of dimension 2 in Null(D+eI)4, which is a subspace of dimension 4, our basis can contain only 1 vector that lies in Null(D+eI)4 but does not lie in Null(D+eI)3.

Therefore, there will be nullity(D+eI)4−nullity(D+eI)3=4−3=1 generalized eigenvector of rank 4 corresponding to the eigenvalue λ=−e.
