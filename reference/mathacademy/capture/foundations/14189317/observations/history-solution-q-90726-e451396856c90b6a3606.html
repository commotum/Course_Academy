Assuming y=eλx and differentiating y with respect to x gives

y′=λeλx,y″=λ2eλx.

Substituting the above into our differential equation gives

λ2eλx+6λeλx+8eλx=0eλx(λ2+6λ+8)=0.

The auxiliary equation is therefore λ2+6λ+8=0.

Factoring the auxiliary equation results in

(λ+2)(λ+4)=0,

and so the solutions of the auxiliary equation are λ=−2 or λ=−4.

Therefore, any linear combination of the functions e−2x and e−4x is a solution to the original differential equation.

Among the given options, only e−6x is not a solution.
