Let S be the event that a chosen email is spam, and

  • let B1 be the event that the email was received in the first account,

  • let B2 be the event that the email was received in the second account,

  • let B3 be the event that the email was received in the third account.

Then the disjoint union B1⊔B2⊔B3 covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability that a randomly selected email is spam, we use the law of total probability:

P(S)=P(S∩B1)+P(S∩B2)+P(S∩B3)

First, notice that Alice received a total of

16+10+14=40

emails in all three accounts.

  • To calculate P(S∩B1), we take the number of emails in the first account that are spam (5) and divide by the total number of emails received (40). So, we have P(S∩B1)=540.

  • To calculate P(S∩B2), we take the number of emails in the second account that are spam (2) and divide by the total number of emails received (40). So, we have P(S∩B2)=240.

  • To calculate P(S∩B3), we take the number of emails in the third account that are spam (3) and divide by the total number of emails received (40). So, we have P(S∩B3)=340.

Finally, substituting the above information into the law of total probability, the probability that a randomly selected email is spam is

P(S)=P(S∩B1)+P(S∩B2)+P(S∩B3)=540+240+340=1040=14.
