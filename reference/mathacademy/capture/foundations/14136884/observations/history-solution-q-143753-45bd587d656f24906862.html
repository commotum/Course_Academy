Let C be the event that the client paid with a credit card, and

  • let R be the event that the client bought Regular gasoline,

  • let Q be the event that the client bought Premium gasoline,

  • let S be the event that the client bought Supreme gasoline.

Then the disjoint union R⊔Q⊔S covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability that a randomly selected client paid with a credit card, we can use the law of total probability:

P(C)=P(C∩R)+P(C∩Q)+P(C∩S)=P(C|R)P(R)+P(C|Q)P(Q)+P(C|S)P(S)

Let's go through the problem statement and translate each piece of information into mathematical notation.

  • Since 40% of the clients buy Regular gasoline, 45% of the clients buy Premium gasoline, and 15% of the clients buy Supreme gasoline, we have P(R)=0.4,P(Q)=0.45,P(S)=0.15.

  • Since 65% of the clients that buy Regular gasoline, 50% of the clients that buy Premium gasoline, and 60% of the clients that buy Supreme gasoline, paid with a credit card, we have P(C|R)=0.65,P(C|Q)=0.5,P(C|S)=0.6.

Substituting the above information into the law of total probability, the probability that a randomly selected client paid with a credit card is

P(C)=P(C|R)P(R)+P(C|Q)P(Q)+P(C|S)P(S)=(0.65)(0.4)+(0.5)(0.45)+(0.6)(0.15)=0.26+0.225+0.009=0.575.
