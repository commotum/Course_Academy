Let R be the event of selecting a red marble at random from one of the three bags, and

  • let A be the event that the marble is from bag 1,

  • let B be the event that the marble is from bag 2,

  • let C be the event that the marble is from bag 3.

Then the disjoint union A⊔B⊔C covers the entire sample space.

To calculate the probability of selecting a red marble at random from one of the three bags, we can use the law of total probability:

P(R)=P(R∩A)+P(R∩B)+P(R∩C)=P(R|A)P(A)+P(R|B)P(B)+P(R|C)P(C)

Let's go through the problem statement and translate each piece of information into mathematical notation.

  • Since a bag is selected at random, we have P(A)=P(B)=P(C)=13.

  • Since there are 12 red marbles out of 20 in bag 1, 14 red marbles out of 20 in bag 2, and 10 red marbles out of 20 in bag 3, we have P(R|A)=1220,P(R|B)=1420,P(R|C)=1020.

Substituting the above information into the law of total probability, the probability of selecting a red marble at random from one of the three bags is

P(R)=P(R|A)P(A)+P(R|B)P(B)+P(R|C)P(C)=(1220)(13)+(1420)(13)+(1020)(13)=1260+1460+1060=3660=35.
