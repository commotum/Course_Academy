A gas station has a rectangular gasoline storage tank stored underground. The tank is 15 feet long and 10 feet wide. A worker lowers a dip stick into the tank and notices that the depth of the gasoline is decreasing by 2 feet every hour. How fast is the volume of gasoline decreasing?

a
300feet3/hour
b
400feet3/hour
c
440feet3/hour
d
360feet3/hour
e
240feet3/hour
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

We define the following variables:

  • let L and W be the length and width of the tank, respectively, and

  • let h and V be the height and volume of the gasoline in the tank, respectively.

Note that h and V change with time, while L and W are constants.

We are given that L=15ft,W=10ft,dhdt=−2ft/hour.

We would like to find dVdt.

The relation between the two time-dependent variables, V and h, is given by the formula for the volume of the gasoline in the tank: V=LWh

We differentiate both sides of V=LWh with respect to t, treating both L and W as constants, and get

ddtV=ddt(LWh)dVdt=LWdhdt.

Finally, we can plug in the known values and get dVdt=(15)(10)dhdtdVdt=150⋅(−2)dVdt=−300.

Therefore, the gasoline level is decreasing at a rate of 300ft3/hour.
