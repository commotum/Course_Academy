By changing the order of integration in the following repeated integrals so that they are integrated first with respect to y, then z, and then x, express the following sum as a single repeated integral.

∫02∫0z/2∫xz+1f(x,y,z)dydxdz+∫23∫03−z∫xz+1f(x,y,z)dydxdz

a
 ∫01∫2x−x+3∫xz+1f(x,y,z)dydzdx.
b
 ∫02∫x2−x∫xz+1f(x,y,z)dydzdx.
c
∫01∫2x−x2+3∫xz+1f(x,y,z)dydzdx.
d
 ∫02∫x/2−x2+3∫xz+1f(x,y,z)dydzdx.
e
 ∫01∫x2−x+3∫xz+1f(x,y,z)dydzdx.
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

We wish to find the limits of integration such that our integral can be expressed as ∫ab∫v1(x)v2(x)∫u1(x,z)u2(x,z)f(x,y,z)dydzdx.

From the repeated integrals, we see that the domain of integration R=R1∪R2, where R1 and R2 are type III regions, is given by

R1={(x,y,z):0≤z≤2,0≤x≤z2,x≤y≤z+1},R2={(x,y,z):2≤z≤3,0≤x≤3−z,x≤y≤z+1}.

Since all repeated integrals first integrate with respect to y, the y-limits remain the same as before:

∫ab∫v1(x)v2(x)∫xz+1f(x,y,z)dydzdx

Now, we consider the projection D of R onto the xz-plane shown below.

The region D consists of two type II regions in the xz-plane:

D={(x,z):0≤z≤2,0≤x≤z2}∪{(x,z):2≤z≤3,0≤x≤3−z}.

To change the order of integration from dydxdz to dydzdx, we write D as a type I region.

Notice that the bottom boundary of the region, written in the form z=z(x), is x=z2⟹z=2x and the top boundary of the region is x=3−z⟹z=−x2+3.

Then, the type I representation of the region is

D={(x,z):0≤x≤1,2x≤z≤−x2+3},

as shown in the diagram below.

Therefore, by swapping the order of integration, we obtain

∫01∫2x−x2+3∫xz+1f(x,y,z)dydzdx.
