EXPLANATION

We wish to find the limits of integration such that our integral can be expressed as ∫ab∫v1(x)v2(x)∫u1(x,z)u2(x,z)dydzdx+∫cd∫v3(x)v4(x)∫u3(x,z)u4(x,z)dydzdx.

From the repeated integral, we see that the domain of integration R can be written as a type III region as follows:

R={(x,y,z):0≤z≤1,z≤x≤ez,z2−1≤y≤x+z}

Since all repeated integrals first integrate with respect to y, the y-limits remain the same as before:

∫01∫zez∫z2−1x+zdydxdz=∫ab∫v1(x)v2(x)∫z2−1x+zdydzdx+∫cd∫v3(x)v4(x)∫z2−1x+zdydzdx

Now, we consider the projection D of R onto the xz-plane shown below.

The region D is a type II region in the xz-plane:

D={(x,z):0≤z≤1,z≤x≤ez}.

To change the order of integration from dydxdz to dydzdx, we split D into two type I regions.

Notice that the bottom and top boundaries of the left region, written in the form z=z(x), are

z=0 and z=x2.

Also, the bottom and top boundaries of the right region, written in the form z=z(x), are

z=ln⁡x and z=1.

So, we can write D as a union of

D1={(x,z):0≤x≤1,0≤z≤x2}

and

D2={(x,z):1≤x≤e,ln⁡x≤z≤1}.

Therefore, by swapping the order of integration, we obtain

∫01∫0x2∫z2−1x+zdydzdx+∫1e∫ln⁡x1∫z2−1x+zdydzdx.
