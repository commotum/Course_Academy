∫−20∫(x2−4)/2(x+2)3/4∫xy2dzdydx=∫p0∫q0∫xy2dzdxdy+∫02∫r0∫xy2dzdxdy

EXPLANATION

We wish to find the limits of integration such that our integral can be expressed as ∫ab∫v1(y)v2(y)∫u1(x,y)u2(x,y)dzdxdy+∫cd∫v3(y)v4(y)∫u3(x,y)u4(x,y)dzdxdy.

From the repeated integral, we see that the domain of integration R can be written as a type I region as follows:

R={(x,y,z):−2≤x≤0,12(x2−4)≤y≤14(x+2)3,x≤z≤y2}

Since all repeated integrals first integrate with respect to z, the z-limits remain the same as before:

∫−20∫(x2−4)/2(x+2)3/4∫xy2dzdydx=∫ab∫v1(y)v2(y)∫xy2dzdxdy+∫cd∫v3(y)v4(y)∫xy2dzdxdy

Now, we consider the projection D of R onto the xy-plane shown below.

The region D is a type I region in the xz-plane:

D={(x,y):−2≤x≤0,12(x2−4)≤y≤14(x+2)3}.

To change the order of integration from dzdydx to dzdxdy, we split D into two type II regions.

Notice that the bottom and top boundaries of the lower region, written in the form x=x(y), are

x=−2y+4 and x=0.

Also, the bottom and top boundaries of the upper region, written in the form x=x(y), are

x=4y3−2 and x=0.

So, we can write D as a union of

D1={(x,y):−2≤y≤0,−2y+4≤x≤0}

and

D2={(x,y):0≤y≤2,4y3−2≤x≤0}.

Therefore, by swapping the order of integration, we obtain

∫−20∫−2y+40∫xy2dzdxdy+∫02∫4y3−20∫xy2dzdxdy.
