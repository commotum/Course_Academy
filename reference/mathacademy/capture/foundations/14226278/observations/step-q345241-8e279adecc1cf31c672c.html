A series RCL circuit has a source electromotive force (EMF) of 15V, a resistor of resistance 14Ω, a capacitor of capacitance 124F, and an inductor of inductance 2H. Find, in amperes, the amount of current in the circuit after 0.3 seconds, given that the initial charge on the capacitor is 14C and the initial current is 2A.

Hint: The voltage drop VC across the capacitor is related to its charge by q=CVC, where C is the capacitance measured in Farads.

EXPLANATION

The diagram for our circuit is shown below.

The current I, measured in amperes, is defined as the rate of flow of charge q, measured in coulombs. I=dqdt

Kirchhoff's voltage law states that in a series circuit, the source voltage (EMF) equals the sum of the voltage drops.

In an RCL circuit, there are three voltage drops:

  • The voltage drop across the resistor is proportional to the current, with a constant of proportionality R=14Ω. So, VR=14I.

  • The voltage drop across the capacitor is related to its charge by q=CVC. Therefore, VC=qC=q124=24q.

  • The voltage drop across the inductor is proportional to the rate of change of current, with a constant of proportionality L=2H. Hence, VL=2dIdt.

Therefore, by Kirchhoff's voltage law, these values are related by the equation

15=14I+24q+2dIdt.

Differentiating with respect to t, the differential equation governing the current in the circuit is

ddt(15)=ddt(14I+24q+2dIdt)0=14dIdt+24dqdt+2d2Idt2,

which, when substituting in I=dqdt, gives

d2Idt2+7⋅dIdt+12I=0.

The given equation is a second-order linear homogeneous equation with constant coefficients. To find the general solution, we must take a linear combination of two linearly independent solutions.

Assuming the solutions take the form I=eλt, we obtain two solutions, namely, I=e−3t and I=e−4t. So, the general solution in our case is

I(t)=Ae−3t+Be−4t.

We're told that the circuit starts with an initial charge of 14C on the capacitor and an initial current of 2A. Now, solving the Kirchhoff's law equation for dIdt, we have dIdt=152−7I−12q.

So, we can apply the initial conditions I(0)=2 and I′(0)=152−7I(0)−12q(0)=152−7⋅2−12⋅14=−192, and solve for A and B.

  • Substituting I(0)=2 into our general solution gives 2=Ae−0+Be−02=A+B.

  • Differentiating I, we get I′=−3Ae−3t−4Be−4t Substituting in I′(0)=−192 gives −192=−3Ae−0−4Be−0−192=−3A−4B.

Putting the two equations in A and B together gives the following system:

Solving this system gives A=−32 and B=72. Therefore, the equation for the current (in amperes) in the circuit is

I(t)=−32e−3t+72e−4t.

Finally, the current in the circuit after 0.3 seconds, in amperes, is I(0.3)=−32e−0.9+72e−1.2≈0.444, rounded to three decimal places.
