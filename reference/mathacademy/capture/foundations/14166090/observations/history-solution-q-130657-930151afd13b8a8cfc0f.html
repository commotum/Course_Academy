A geometric random variable X with probability of success p has the following probability mass function:

f(x)=(1−p)x−1p

Let X be the number of picked candies until a lemon-flavored candy is obtained. Since the chance to get a lemon-flavored candy is 6%, the number of picked candies until a lemon-flavored candy is obtained can be modeled as a geometric random variable with a probability of success

p=6%=6100=0.06.

So, X∼Geom(0.06). In this case, X has the following probability mass function:

f(x)=(1−0.06)x−1(0.06)=(0.94)x−1(0.06)

Therefore, the probability that the 9th attempt is the first lemon-flavored candy is

P(X=9)=f(9)=(0.94)9−1(0.06)=(0.94)8(0.06)≈0.037

rounded to 3 decimal places.
