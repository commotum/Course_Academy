Recall that curlF denotes the curl of the vector field F.

To understand intuitively whether a vector field has curl at a point P, imagine that our vector field F represents the velocity field of a stream of flowing water. We place a paddlewheel at P with the axis of rotation pointing in the z-direction, and we note whether the paddlewheel rotates or not.

  • If the paddlewheel is forced to rotate due to the action of the water, then the vector field has curl, i.e., curlF≠0.

  • If the paddlewheel does not rotate due to the action of the water, then the velocity field has zero curl, i.e., curlF=0.

If curlF=0 everywhere in R3, we say that F is irrotational.

With that in mind, let's check each statement:

  • Statement II is true, whereas statement I is false. If a paddlewheel were placed at any point P in the manner described above, it would rotate clockwise. Therefore, curlF≠0.

  • Statement III is true. Using our right hand, if we wrap our fingers in the direction of rotation (clockwise), our thumb will point into the screen. Therefore, curlF also points into the screen, in the direction of decreasing z.

  • Statement IV is false. Since curlF≠0, the vector field F is not irrotational.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II and III only."
