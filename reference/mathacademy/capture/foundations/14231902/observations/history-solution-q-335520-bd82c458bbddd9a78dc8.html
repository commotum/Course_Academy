A shift of coordinates is a translation of the phase space and does not alter the structure of the system’s equilibrium set. Each equilibrium point of the original system is translated to a corresponding equilibrium in the shifted coordinates.

Thus, although one of the equilibria is moved to the origin, the total number of equilibrium points remains the same.

Therefore, the correct answer is "The shifted system still has exactly three equilibrium points."
