We can parameterize C as follows:

r(t)=[x(t)y(t)]=[5cos⁡t5sin⁡t],t∈[0,2π)

To calculate the outward-pointing normal vector of a closed, positively oriented curve, we follow three steps:

Step 1: We compute the tangent vector r′(t).

Differentiating the expression for r(t), we get

r′(t)=[x′(t)y′(t)]=[−5sin⁡t5cos⁡t].

Step 2: Since C is oriented counterclockwise, we rotate r′(t) by 90∘ clockwise to obtain an outward-pointing normal vector.

The rotation matrix that corresponds to a 90∘ clockwise rotation is

[01−10].

Rotating the vector r′(t) by 90∘ clockwise, we get

[01−10][−5sin⁡t5cos⁡t]=[5cos⁡t5sin⁡t].

Step 3: Finally, we normalize the vector found in step 2.

Note that the magnitude of the vector found in step 2 equals the magnitude of r′(t) (since they differ only by a rotation).
