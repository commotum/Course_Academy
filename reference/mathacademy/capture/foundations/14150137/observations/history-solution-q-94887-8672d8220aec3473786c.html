The rate of change of f(x,y,z) with respect to t along the curve r(t) is given by ddt[f(r(t))]. To calculate this, we can use the chain rule.

The chain rule in vector form states that

ddt[f(r(t))]=∇f(r(t))⋅r′(t).

First, we need to compute ∇f(x,y): ∇f(x,y)=⟨∂∂x(xyz),∂∂y(xyz),∂∂z(xyz)⟩=⟨yz,xz,xy⟩

Next, we substitute x=2t,y=t and z=3t−1 into the above to find ∇f(r(t)): ∇f(r(t))=⟨t(3t−1),2t(3t−1),2tt⟩=⟨(3t−1)t,6t2−2t,2tt⟩

Now, we calculate the derivative of r(t), which is r′(t)=⟨ddt(2t),ddt(t),ddt(3t−1)⟩=⟨2,12t,3⟩.

Applying the chain rule, we get

ddt[f(r(t))]=∇f(r(t))⋅r′(t)=⟨(3t−1)t,6t2−2t,2tt⟩⋅⟨2,12t,3⟩=(3t−1)t⋅2+(6t2−2t)⋅12t+2tt⋅3=6tt−2t+3t2−tt+6tt=12tt−2t+3t2−tt.

Therefore, the rate of change of f along the curve r(t) at t=1 is ddt[f(r(1))]=12(1)1−21+3(1)2−11=12.
