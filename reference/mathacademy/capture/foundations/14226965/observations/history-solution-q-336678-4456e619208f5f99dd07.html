Let Q be the amount of sugar in the vat at time t. Then, the derivative dQdt describes how this amount changes with respect to time t, in minutes. This is equal to the rate at which sugar enters the vat minus the rate at which sugar leaves the vat.

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′

Note that the units of dQdt,Qin′ and Qout′ are kg/min. Now, let's compute the required rates of change in steps.

Step 1: Calculating the rate at which sugar enters the vat.

Note that pure water (i.e., water containing 0kg/L of sugar) enters the vat at a rate of 14L/min. So, the rate at which the sugar enters the vat is

Qin′=(0kgL)⋅(14Lmin)=(0kgL)⋅(14Lmin)=0kgmin.

Step 2: Calculating the rate at which sugar exits the vat.

To find the rate at which sugar leaves the vat, we first determine the volume of solution in the vat at time t.

The vat initially contains 500L of solution; water enters at 14L/min and drains out at 10L/min. As a result, the amount of solution in the vat after t minutes is 500+14t−10t=(500+4t)L.

Since Q is the amount of sugar in the vat, the concentration of the solution at time t is Q500+4tkgL.

Therefore, as the completely mixed sugar-water solution leaves at a rate of 10L/min, sugar leaves at a rate of

Qout′=(10Lmin)⋅(Q500+4tkgL)=(10Lmin)⋅(Q500+4tkgL)=10Q500+4tkgmin=5Q2(125+t)kgmin.

Step 3: Calculate the overall rate of change.

The rate of change equals the rate of entry minus the rate of exit. Therefore,

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′=0−5Q2(125+t) which gives the equation dQdt=−52(125+t)Q.

Step 4: Integrate the equation.

Separating variables and integrating both sides with respect to t, we get

where A=±eC is a constant of integration.

We're told that the vat initially contains 15kg of sugar. So, we can apply the initial condition Q(0)=15, and solve for A:

Q(0)=15A(125+0)5/2=15A=15⋅1255/2

Therefore, we obtain the following expression for Q(t):

Q(t)=15(125125+t)5/2
