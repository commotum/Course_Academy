Let [T]B and [T]C be the matrices of a linear transformation T relative to the bases B and C, respectively. If PB→C is the change-of-coordinates matrix from basis B to basis C, which of the following statements are true?

  1. [T]C=PB→C⋅[T]B⋅PB→C−1
  2. [T]B=PB→C⋅[T]C⋅PB→C
  3. [T]B=PB→C−1⋅[T]C⋅PB→C−1
a
I and III only
b
II only
c

EXPLANATION

The connection between the matrices of our linear transformation T relative to the bases B and C is given by the following diagram:

Here, moving along an arrow means pre-multiplying by the corresponding matrix. Algebraically, we can write this as

[T]C=PB→C⋅[T]B⋅PB→C−1,

where PB→C is the change-of-coordinates matrix from B to C.

Solving this equation for [T]B, we obtain the alternative formulation

[T]B=PB→C−1⋅[T]C⋅PB→C.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only."
