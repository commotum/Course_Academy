A gambling room has three roulette tables, table 1, table 2, and table 3. Data of all bets of a particular evening are collected.

  • 30% of bets were played at table 1, and 5% of those bets were won.
  • 35% of bets were played at table 2, and 6% of those bets were won.
  • 35% of bets were played at table 3, and 7% of those bets were won.

If 10% of bets were played in the first hour, and 4% of bets made in the first hour were won, what is the probability that a randomly chosen winning bet was played in the first hour? Round your answer to 3 decimal places.

EXPLANATION

Let F be the event that a bet is played in the first hour, W be the event that a bet is won, and T1,T2,T3 be the events that a bet is played at table 1, 2, or 3, respectively.

Let's break down the information given in the problem statement:

  • From the bullet points, we have the following information: P(T1)=0.3P(W|T1)=0.05P(T2)=0.35P(W|T2)=0.06P(T3)=0.35P(W|T3)=0.07 The disjoint union T1⊔T2⊔T3 covers the entire sample space, and we have P(T1)+P(T2)+P(T3)=0.3+0.35+0.35=1.

  • Since 10% of bets in the entire evening are played in the first hour, we have P(F)=0.1.

  • Since 4% of bets made in the first hour are won, we have P(W|F)=0.04.

We want to find the probability that a randomly chosen winning bet is played in the first hour, which is represented as P(F|W).

Using Bayes' theorem combined with the law of total probability, we have

P(F|W)=P(W|F)P(F)P(W)=P(W|F)P(F)P(W|T1)P(T1)+P(W|T2)P(T2)+P(W|T3)P(T3)=(0.04)(0.1)(0.05)(0.3)+(0.06)(0.35)+(0.07)(0.35)=0.0040.0605≈0.066,

rounded to 3 decimal places.
