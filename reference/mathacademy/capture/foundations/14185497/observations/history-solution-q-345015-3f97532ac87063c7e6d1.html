The diagram shows a series RCL circuit with a source electromotive force (EMF) of 8V, a resistor of resistance 14Ω, a capacitor of capacitance 0.1F, and an inductor of inductance 4H.

The current I, measured in amperes, is defined as the rate of flow of charge q, measured in coulombs. I=dqdt

Kirchhoff's voltage law states that in a series circuit, the source voltage (EMF) equals the sum of the voltage drops. In an RCL circuit, there are three voltage drops:

  • The voltage drop across the resistor is proportional to the current, with a constant of proportionality R=14Ω. So, VR=14I=14dqdt.

  • The voltage drop across the capacitor is related to its charge by q=CVC. Therefore, VC=qC=q0.1=10q.

  • The voltage drop across the inductor is proportional to the rate of change of current, with a constant of proportionality L=4H. Hence, VL=4dIdt=4d2qdt2.

Therefore, by Kirchhoff's voltage law, the differential equation governing the amount of charge q on the capacitor is 4d2qdt2+14dqdt+10q=8.

The steady-state charge is the value that the charge q(t) approaches as t→∞. In a stable RCL circuit with a constant electromotive force, the transient response (the part of the solution that depends on the initial conditions) eventually decays to zero, leaving a constant charge.

When the system reaches steady state, the charge q is constant. Consequently, the first and second derivatives of the charge with respect to time are zero: dqdt=0andd2qdt2=0.

Substituting these steady-state conditions into the differential equation gives 4(0)+14(0)+10q=8, which simplifies to 10q=8⟹q=810=45.

Physically, this represents the state where the capacitor is fully charged, the inductor acts as a short circuit, and the current has ceased to flow.

Therefore, the steady-state charge is 45 coulombs.
