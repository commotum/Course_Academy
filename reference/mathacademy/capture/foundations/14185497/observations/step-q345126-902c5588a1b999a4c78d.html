A series RCL circuit has a source electromotive force (EMF) of 12V, a resistance of 32Ω, a capacitance of 116F, and an inductance of L=8H. Find the steady-state charge for this circuit.

Hint: The voltage drop VC across the capacitor is related to its charge by q=CVC, where C is the capacitance measured in Farads.

EXPLANATION

The diagram for our circuit is shown below.

The current I, measured in amperes, is defined as the rate of flow of charge q, measured in coulombs. I=dqdt

Kirchhoff's voltage law states that in a series circuit, the source voltage (EMF) equals the sum of the voltage drops. In an RCL circuit, there are three voltage drops:

  • The voltage drop across the resistor is proportional to the current, with a constant of proportionality R=32Ω. So, VR=32I=32dqdt.

  • The voltage drop across the capacitor is related to its charge by q=CVC. Therefore, VC=qC=q(1/16)=16q.

  • The voltage drop across the inductor is proportional to the rate of change of current, with a constant of proportionality L=8H. Hence, VL=8dIdt=8ddt(dqdt)=8d2qdt2.

Therefore, by Kirchhoff's voltage law, the differential equation governing the amount of charge q on the capacitor is 8d2qdt2+32dqdt+16q=12.

The steady-state charge is the value that the charge q(t) approaches as t→∞. In a stable RCL circuit with a constant electromotive force, the transient response eventually decays to zero, leaving a constant charge.

When the system reaches steady state, the charge q is constant. Consequently, the first and second derivatives of the charge with respect to time are zero: dqdt=0andd2qdt2=0.

Substituting these steady-state conditions into the differential equation gives 8(0)+32(0)+16q=12, which simplifies to 16q=12⟹q=1216=34.

Physically, this represents the state where the capacitor is fully charged and the current has ceased to flow.

Therefore, the steady-state charge is 34 coulombs.
