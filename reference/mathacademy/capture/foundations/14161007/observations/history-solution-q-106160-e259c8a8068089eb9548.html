Let F be the event that a randomly selected person speaks a foreign language, and let B be the event that a randomly selected person has a bachelor's degree. Then, we require P(B|F), which we can compute using Bayes' theorem.

P(B|F)=P(F|B)P(B)P(F).

From the problem statement, we have the following:

  • P(F|B)=0.9, since 90% of all people who have a bachelor's degree speak a foreign language.

  • P(B)=0.31, since 31% of the adult population has a bachelor's degree.

  • P(F)=0.63, since 63% of the adult population speak a foreign language.

Therefore, using Bayes' theorem, we have

P(B|F)=P(F|B)P(B)P(F)=(0.9)(0.31)0.63≈44%
