A laboratory test for a particular disease is conducted on some patients. Of the tested patients, 97% of those infected and 1% of those not infected tested positive for the disease. Only 1% of the entire population is infected with the disease. What is the probability that a randomly chosen patient that tested positive is infected? Round your answer to 2 decimal places.

a
0.23
b
0.17
c
0.25
d
0.49
e
0.03

Incorrect
EXPLANATION

Let D be the event that a randomly chosen patient is infected with the disease, and let T be the event they tested positive. Then, we require P(D|T), which we can compute using Bayes' theorem.

P(D|T)=P(T|D)P(D)P(T)=P(T|D)P(D)P(T|D)P(D)+P(T|D′)P(D′)

From the problem statement, we have the following:

  • Since the probability of an infected person testing positive is 97%, and the probability of a non-infected person testing positive is 1%, we have P(T|D)=0.97,P(T|D′)=0.01.

  • Since only 1% of the population has the disease, we have P(D)=0.01,P(D′)=1−0.01=0.99.

Therefore, using Bayes' theorem, we have

P(D|T)=P(T|D)P(D)P(T|D)P(D)+P(T|D′)P(D′)=(0.97)(0.01)(0.97)(0.01)+(0.01)(0.99)≈0.49 rounded to 2 decimal places.
