Suppose X and Y are discrete random variables. Let the joint CDF of X and Y be F(x,y), where

F(9,10)=0.8,F(9,4)=0.3,F(6,10)=0.4,F(6,4)=0.2,

and the supports of X and Y, respectively, are

SX={3,6,9,12},SY={2,4,6,8,10,12}.

Find P(6<X≤9,4<Y≤10).

a
0.8
b
0.5
c
0.4
d
0.2
e
0.3
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

Note the following:

  • For a given joint PMF f(x,y), we can think of each point where f(x,y)≠0 as a point mass in the plane, where the "probability mass" at each point equals the probability associated with that point.

  • The joint CDF F(x,y)=P(X≤x,Y≤y) is the total "probability mass" that lies in the infinite rectangular region that has its top-right corner at (x,y) and whose sides are parallel to the coordinate axis.

Given the supports of X and Y, the joint support S is a subset of

SX×SY={3,6,9,12}×{2,4,6,8,10,12}.

Let's sketch the points in SX×SY, which contains the joint support.

Let's sketch the region corresponding to the probability we wish to find.

Geometrically, this region can be obtained as follows:

  1. Take the infinite rectangle that has its top-right corner at (9,10).

  2. Subtract the infinite rectangles that have their top-right corners at (9,4) and (6,10).

  3. Finally, we need to add back the infinite rectangle that has its top-right corner at (6,4). This is because we subtracted this region twice in step 2.

Therefore, we have

P(6<X≤9,4<Y≤10)=P(X≤9,Y≤10)⏞top-right corner at (9,10)=−P(X≤9,Y≤4)⏞top-right corner at (9,4)−P(X≤6,Y≤10)⏞top-right corner at (6,10)=+P(X≤6,Y≤4)⏞top-right corner at (6,4)=F(9,10)−F(9,4)−F(6,10)+F(6,4)=0.8−0.3−0.4+0.2=0.3.
