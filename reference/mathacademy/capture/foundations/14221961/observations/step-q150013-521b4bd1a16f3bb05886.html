EXPLANATION

Remember that ∫CF⋅dr gives the total work done by a force F in moving a particle along C:

  • If the force works with the motion of the particle (i.e., the force helps to push the particle along the path), then ∫CF⋅dr>0.

  • If the force works against the motion of the particle (i.e., the force impedes the motion of the particle as it moves along the path), then ∫CF⋅dr<0.

With that in mind, let's check each statement.

  • Statement I is true while statement II is false. It's clear from the picture below that F assists the motion of the particle as it moves from A to B.

    So, ∫CF⋅dr>0.

  • Statement III is true. It's clear from the picture below that F works against the motion of the particle as it moves from B to A.

    So, ∫−CF⋅dr<0.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and III only."
