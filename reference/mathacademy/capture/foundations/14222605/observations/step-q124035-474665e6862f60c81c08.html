EXPLANATION

Let C be a piecewise-smooth curve given by the vector function r(t) for t∈[a,b]. Let f be a differentiable function of two or three variables such that ∇f is continuous on C. The fundamental theorem for line integrals states that ∫C∇f⋅dr=f(r(b))−f(r(a)).

We start by parametrizing the curve C. A suitable parametrization is r(t)=⟨cos⁡t,t⟩,t∈[0,π].

Then, the position vectors of the endpoints of the curve are given by r(0)=⟨1,0⟩,r(π)=⟨−1,π⟩.

Applying the fundamental theorem for line integrals, we have ∫C∇f⋅dr=f(r(π))−f(r(0))=f(−1,π)−f(1,0)=(π2−(−1))−(02−1)=(π2+1)+1=π2+2.
