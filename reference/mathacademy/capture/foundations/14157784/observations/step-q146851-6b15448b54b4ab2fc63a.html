Suppose X and Y are discrete random variables. Let the joint CDF of X and Y be F(x,y), where

F(5,3)=0.6,F(5,1)=0.23,F(2,3)=0.32,F(2,1)=0.05,

and the supports of X and Y, respectively, are

SX={1,2,3,4,5,6},SY={1,2,3,4}.

Find P(2<X≤5,2≤Y<4).

a
0.28
b
0.05
c
0.23
d
0.1
e
0.32
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

Note the following:

  • For a given joint PMF f(x,y), we can think of each point where f(x,y)≠0 as a point mass in the plane, where the "probability mass" at each point equals the probability associated with that point.

  • The joint CDF F(x,y)=P(X≤x,Y≤y) is the total "probability mass" that lies in the infinite rectangular region that has its top-right corner at (x,y) and whose sides are parallel to the coordinate axis.

Given the supports of X and Y, the joint support S is a subset of

SX×SY={1,2,3,4,5,6}×{1,2,3,4}.

Let's plot the points in SX×SY, which contains the joint support, and sketch the region corresponding to the probability we wish to find.

Notice that this region encompasses the same possible support points as the rectangular region with its top-right corner at (5,3) and bottom-left corner at (2,1) below, where the top and right boundaries are inclusive and the bottom and left boundaries are exclusive.

Geometrically, this region can be obtained as follows:

  1. Take the infinite rectangle that has its top-right corner at (5,3).

  2. Subtract the infinite rectangles that have their top-right corners at (5,1) and (2,3).

  3. Finally, we need to add back the infinite rectangle that has its top-right corner at (2,1). This is because we subtracted this region twice in step 2.

Therefore, we have

P(2<X≤5,2≤Y<4)=P(X≤5,Y≤3)⏞top-right corner at (5,3)=−P(X≤5,Y≤1)⏞top-right corner at (5,1)−P(X≤2,Y≤3)⏞top-right corner at (2,3)=+P(X≤2,Y≤1)⏞top-right corner at (2,1)=F(5,3)−F(5,1)−F(2,3)+F(2,1)=0.6−0.23−0.32+0.05=0.1.
