The general solution to the equation of forced harmonic motion without damping is the sum of the complementary function and a particular solution.

The complementary solution yc represents simple harmonic motion and can be written as yc=Acos⁡ωt+Bsin⁡ωt, where A and B are arbitrary constants of integration.

In our case, ω2=36⟹ω=6.

Thus, we have yc=Acos⁡6t+Bsin⁡6t.

We now need to find the particular integral yp(t).

The external force is a linear polynomial. Therefore, we assume that the particular integral is also a linear polynomial, i.e. yp=α+βt, where α,β are constants to be determined.

Substituting yp into the differential equation and equating coefficients gives α=0,β=12.

Therefore, the particular solution yp(t) is yp(t)=t2,

and the general solution is y=Acos⁡6t+Bsin⁡6t+t2.

Since the particle is initially at the origin with velocity 1cm/s, the initial conditions are y(0)=0 and y′(0)=1.

Solving with these conditions for A and B gives A=0,B=112

So our solution is y(t)=sin⁡6t12+t2.

To find the position of the particle after 2 seconds, we substitute into the equation t=2. We get y(2)=sin⁡6(2)12+(2)2=(sin⁡1212+1)cm.
