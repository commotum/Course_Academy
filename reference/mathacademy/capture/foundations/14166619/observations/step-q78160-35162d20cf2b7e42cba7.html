Suppose that a matrix A has characteristic polynomial p(λ)=λ(λ+1)3(λ−2)4, the rank of (A−2I)2 equals 7, and the rank of (A−2I)3 equals 6. If a basis of R8 consists of linearly independent generalized eigenvectors of A, how many of these are generalized eigenvectors of rank 3 corresponding to the eigenvalue λ=2?

a
2
b
5
c
3
d
1
e
8
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

For the given matrix A with eigenvalue λ=2, a generalized eigenvector of rank 3 is a vector v such that

(A−2I)3v=0and(A−2I)2v≠0.

Notice that A must be an 8×8 matrix.

  • Since the rank of (A−2I)2 equals 7, then by the rank-nullity theorem, we have that nullity(A−2I)2=8−7=1. So, the solutions to (A−2I)2v=0 form a subspace Null(A−2I)2 of dimension 1 in R8.

  • Since the rank of (A−2I)3 equals 6, then by the rank-nullity theorem, we have that nullity(A−2I)3=8−6=2. So, the solutions to (A−2I)3v=0 form a subspace Null(A−2I)3 of dimension 2 in R8.

Since Null(A−2I)2 is a subspace of dimension 1 in Null(A−2I)3, which is a subspace of dimension 2, our basis can contain only 1 vector that lies in Null(A−2I)3 but does not lie in Null(A−2I)2.

Therefore, there will be nullity(A−2I)3−nullity(A−2I)2=2−1=1 generalized eigenvector of rank 3 corresponding to the eigenvalue λ=2.
