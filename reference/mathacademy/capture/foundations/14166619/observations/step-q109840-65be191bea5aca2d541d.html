EXPLANATION

For the given matrix S with eigenvalue λ=3, a generalized eigenvector of rank 4 is a vector v such that

(S−3I)4v=0and(S−3I)3v≠0.

Notice the following:

  • We are given that Null(S−3I)3 has dimension 3. This means that the solutions to (S−3I)3v=0 form a subspace of dimension 3.

  • We are given that Null(S−3I)4 has dimension 4. This means that the solutions to (S−3I)4v=0 form a subspace of dimension 4.

Now, since Null(S−3I)3 is a subspace of dimension 3 in Null(S−3I)4, which is a subspace of dimension 4, our basis can contain only 1 vector that lies in Null(S−3I)4 but does not lie in Null(S−3I)3.

Therefore, there will be nullity(S−3I)4−nullity(S−3I)3=4−3=1 generalized eigenvector of rank 4 corresponding to the eigenvalue λ=3.
