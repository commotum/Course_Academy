Let's start by picking a point on the plane. To do that, we simply substitute some concrete values for x and y into the equation and solve for z. Let's choose x=1 and y=−3 (though it doesn't really matter what values we choose). Solving for z, we get

2x+y−2z=−72(1)+(−3)+7=2z6=2zz=3.

So, we get the point Q(1,−3,3) which lies on the plane.

Computing PQ→, we get

PQ→=⟨1,−3,3⟩−⟨−2,−8,−1⟩=⟨3,5,4⟩.

We also need a normal vector to the plane. For any Cartesian equation of a plane ax+by+cz=d, the normal vector is n=⟨a,b,c⟩. So, for the plane Π, we have n=⟨2,1,−2⟩.
