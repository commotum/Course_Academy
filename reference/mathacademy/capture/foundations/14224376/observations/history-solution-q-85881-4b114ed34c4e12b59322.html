A geometric random variable X with probability of success p has the following probability mass function:

f(x)=(1−p)x−1p

Let X be the number of times the coin is tossed until it comes up heads. Since the chance of getting a head in every toss is 50%, the number of tosses until it comes up heads can be modeled as a geometric random variable with a probability of success

p=50%=50100=0.5.

So, X∼Geom(0.5). In this case, X has the following probability mass function:

f(x)=(1−0.5)x−1(0.5)=(0.5)x−1(0.5)

Therefore, the probability that head appears in the 3rd toss is

P(X=3)=f(3)=(0.5)3−1(0.5)=(0.5)2(0.5)≈0.125

rounded to 3 decimal places.
