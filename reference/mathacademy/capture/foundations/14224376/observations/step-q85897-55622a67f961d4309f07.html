In a town, blood tests are being carried out on all inhabitants to determine whether they have caught a virus. On average, 30% of the inhabitants test positive for the virus. What is the probability that the first positive test result will be made on the 3rd test or later?

a
0.49
b
0.38
c
0.62
d
0.34
e
0.59
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

A geometric random variable X with probability of success p has the following probability mass function: f(x)=(1−p)x−1p

Let X be the number of inhabitants checked until the first infected patient is found. Since the chance of a single patient being infected is 30%, the number of inhabitants checked until the first infected patient is found can be modeled as a geometric random variable with a probability of success p=30%=30100=0.3.

So, X∼Geom(0.3). In this case, X has the following probability mass function: f(x)=(1−0.3)x−1(0.3)=(0.7)x−1(0.3)

To compute P(X≥3), we will use the complement: P(X≥3)=1−P(X<3)

Computing the probability of the complement, we get P(X<3)=P(X∈{1,2})=f(1)+f(2)=(0.7)1−1(0.3)+(0.7)2−1(0.3)=(0.3)+(0.7)(0.3)=[1+0.7](0.3)=0.51.

Therefore, the probability of finding the first positive patient on the 3rd test or later is P(X≥3)=1−P(X<3)=1−0.51=0.49.
