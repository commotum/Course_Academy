A geometric random variable X with probability of success p has the following probability mass function:

f(x)=(1−p)x−1p

Let X be the number of games played until they lose their first game. Since the chance of losing each game is 20%, the number of games played before the team loses can be modeled as a geometric random variable with a probability of success

p=20%=20100=0.2.

So, X∼Geom(0.2). In this case, X has the following probability mass function:

f(x)=(1−0.2)x−1(0.2)=(0.8)x−1(0.2)

To compute P(X>4), we will use the complement:

P(X>4)=1−P(X≤4)

Computing the probability of the complement, we get

P(X≤4)=P(X∈{1,2,3,4})=f(1)+f(2)+f(3)+f(4)=(0.8)1−1(0.2)+(0.8)2−1(0.2)+(0.8)3−1(0.2)+(0.8)4−1(0.2)=(0.2)+(0.8)(0.2)+(0.8)2(0.2)+(0.8)3(0.2)=[1+0.8+(0.8)2+(0.8)3](0.2)=0.5904.

Therefore, the probability that the team will play at least 4 games before their first loss

P(X>4)=1−P(X≤4)=1−0.5904=0.4096.
