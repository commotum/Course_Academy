The general solution to the equation of forced harmonic motion with light damping is the sum of the complementary function and a particular solution.

The complementary solution yc is the solution to the homogeneous equation y″+6y′+10y=0.

Writing down and solving the auxiliary equation gives λ2+6λ+10=0(λ+3)2+1=0(λ+3)2=−1λ+3=±iλ=−3±i.

Thus, we have yc=e−3t(Pcos⁡t+Qsin⁡t).

We now need to find the particular integral yp(t).

The external force is a linear polynomial. Therefore, we assume that the particular integral is also a linear polynomial, i.e. yp=α+βt, where α, β are constants to be determined.

Substituting yp into the differential equation and equating coefficients gives α=−3, β=5.

Therefore, the particular solution yp(t) is yp(t)=−3+5t,

and the general solution is y=e−3t(Pcos⁡t+Qsin⁡t)+5t−3.

Since the particle is initially at the origin with zero velocity, the initial conditions are y(0)=0 and y′(0)=0.

Solving with these conditions for P and Q gives P=3,Q=4.

So our solution is y(t)=e−3t(3cos⁡t+4sin⁡t)+5t−3.
