The position y(t), measured in centimeters, of a particle oscillating with forced harmonic motion without damping is governed by the equation

y″+49y=−24cos⁡t,

where t>0 is the time, in seconds. Given that the particle is initially at the origin with zero velocity, find the position of the particle after π2 seconds.

a
y(π2)=2cm
b
y(π2)=12cm
c
y(π2)=0cm
d
y(π2)=π2cm
e
y(π2)=1cm
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

The general solution to the equation of forced harmonic motion without damping is the sum of the complementary function and a particular solution.

The complementary solution yc represents simple harmonic motion and can be written as yc=Acos⁡ωt+Bsin⁡ωt, where A and B are arbitrary constants of integration.

In our case, ω2=49⟹ω=7.

Thus, we have yc=Acos⁡7t+Bsin⁡7t.

We now need to find the particular integral yp(t).

The external force is −24cos⁡t. Therefore, we assume that the particular integral is yp=αcos⁡t+βsin⁡t, where α,β are constants to be determined.

Substituting yp into the differential equation and equating coefficients gives α=−12,β=0.

Therefore, the particular solution yp(t) is yp(t)=−12cos⁡t,

and the general solution is y=Acos⁡7t+Bsin⁡7t−12cos⁡t.

Since the particle is initially at the origin with zero velocity, the initial conditions are y(0)=0 and y′(0)=0.

Solving with these conditions for A and B gives A=12,B=0.

So our solution is y(t)=12cos⁡7t−12cos⁡t.

To find the position of the particle after π2 seconds, we substitute into the equation t=π2. We get y(π2)=12cos⁡(7π2)−12cos⁡(π2)=0cm.
