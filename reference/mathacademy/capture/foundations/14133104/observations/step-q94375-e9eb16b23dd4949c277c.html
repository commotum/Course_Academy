The position y(t), measured in meters, of a particle oscillating with forced harmonic motion with light damping is governed by the equation y″+2y′+2y=10et,

where t>0 is the time, in seconds. Given that the particle is initially at the origin with velocity 4ms, find the particle's position after π3 seconds.

a
y(π3)=(3−eπ/3)m
b
y(π3)=(−1+2eπ/3)m
c
y(π3)=(−e−π/3+2eπ/3)m
d
y(π3)=(−2e−π/3+3eπ/3)m
e
y(π3)=(2e−π/3−4eπ/3)m
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

The general solution to the equation of forced harmonic motion with light damping is the sum of the complementary function and a particular solution.

The complementary solution yc is the solution to the homogeneous equation

y″+2y′+2y=0.

Writing down and solving the auxiliary equation gives: λ2+2λ+2=0(λ+1)2+1=0(λ+1)2=−1λ+1=±iλ=−1±i.

Thus, we have yc=e−t(Pcos⁡t+Qsin⁡t). where P and Q are arbitrary constants of integration.

We now need to find the particular integral yp(t).

The external force is an exponential force. The constant factor in the exponent is 1, which is not a root of the auxiliary equation. Therefore, we assume a particular exponential solution of the form yp=αet, where α is a constant to be determined.

Substituting yp into the differential equation and equating coefficients gives α=2.

Therefore, the particular solution yp(t) is

yp(t)=2et, and the general solution is

y=e−t(Pcos⁡t+Qsin⁡t)+2et.

Since the particle is initially at the origin with velocity 4ms, the initial conditions are y(0)=0 and y′(0)=4.

Solving with these conditions for P and Q gives P=−2,Q=0.

So our solution is y(t)=−2e−tcos⁡t+2et.

To find the position of the particle after π3 seconds, we substitute into the equation t=π3. We get y(π3)=−2e−π/3cos⁡(π3)+2eπ/3=(−e−π/3+2eπ/3)m.
