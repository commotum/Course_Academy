The general solution to the equation of forced harmonic motion without damping is the sum of the complementary function and a particular solution.

The complementary solution yc represents simple harmonic motion and can be written as

yc=Acos⁡ωt+Bsin⁡ωt,

where A and B are arbitrary constants of integration.

In our case, ω2=4⟹ω=2.

Thus, we have

yc=Acos⁡2t+Bsin⁡2t.

We now need to find the particular integral yp(t).

The external force is a polynomial of degree 2. Therefore, we assume that the particular integral is also a polynomial of degree 2, i.e.,

yp=αt2+βt+γ where α, β and γ are constants that are to be determined.

Substituting yp into the differential equation and equating coefficients gives α=2,β=0,γ=−1.

Therefore, the particular solution yp(t) is

yp(t)=2t2−1,

and the general solution is

y=Acos⁡2t+Bsin⁡2t+2t2−1.

Since the particle is initially at the origin with zero velocity, the initial conditions are y(0)=0 and y′(0)=0.

Solving with these conditions for A and B gives

A=1,B=0.

So our solution is

y(t)=cos⁡2t+2t2−1.

To find the position of the particle after one second, we substitute into the equation t=1. We get

y(1)=cos⁡2(1)+2(1)2−1=(cos⁡2+1)m.
