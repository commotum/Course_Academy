The position of a point in cylindrical polar coordinates is written as (r,θ,z), where

  • r≥0 and θ∈[0,2π) are the usual plane polar coordinates, and

  • z∈(−∞,∞) is the usual Cartesian z-coordinate.

The picture shows a half-plane that passes through the z-axis.

We note the following:

  • If we project the vector OP→ onto the xy-plane for every point P on our surface, the resulting vector makes an angle of 3π4 radians with the positive x-axis. Therefore, we must have θ=3π4 for every point on the surface.

  • On the other hand, r and z can vary arbitrarily in their respective domains.

Therefore, the equation of the surface in cylindrical polar coordinates is θ=3π4.
