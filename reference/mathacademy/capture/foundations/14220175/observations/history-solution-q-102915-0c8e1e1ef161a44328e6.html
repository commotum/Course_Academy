The position of a point in cylindrical polar coordinates is written as (r,θ,z), where

  • r≥0 and θ∈[0,2π) are the usual plane polar coordinates, and

  • z∈(−∞,∞) is the usual Cartesian z-coordinate.

The picture shows a circular cylinder of radius 2 whose axis of symmetry is the z-axis.

We note the following:

  • Since the distance from any point on our surface to the z-axis equals 2, we must have r=2 for every point on the surface.

  • On the other hand, θ and z can vary arbitrarily in their respective domains.

Therefore, the equation of the surface in cylindrical polar coordinates is r=2.
