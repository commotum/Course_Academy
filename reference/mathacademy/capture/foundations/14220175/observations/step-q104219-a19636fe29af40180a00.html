What surface is defined by the equation θ=3π8 in cylindrical polar coordinates?

a
A half-plane that passes through the z-axis
b
A circular cylinder
c
A plane that is parallel to the xy-plane
d
A half-cone
e
A cone
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

The position of a point in cylindrical polar coordinates is written as (r,θ,z), where

  • r≥0 and θ∈[0,2π) are the usual plane polar coordinates, and

  • z∈(−∞,∞) is the usual Cartesian z-coordinate.

With that in mind, let's examine our equation:

θ=3π8

This equation requires that if we project the vector OP→ onto the xy-plane for every point P on our surface, the resulting vector makes an angle of 3π8 radians with the positive x-axis. So, we must have θ=3π8 for every point on the surface. On the other hand, r and z can vary arbitrarily in their respective domains.

Therefore, our surface is a half-plane that passes through the z-axis, shown below.
