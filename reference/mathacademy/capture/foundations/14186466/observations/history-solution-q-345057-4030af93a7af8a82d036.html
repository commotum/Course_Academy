The current I, measured in amperes, is defined as the rate of flow of charge q, measured in coulombs. I=dqdt

Kirchhoff's voltage law states that in a series circuit, the source voltage (EMF) equals the sum of the voltage drops.

In an RCL circuit, there are three voltage drops:

  • The voltage drop across the resistor is proportional to the current, with a constant of proportionality R=32Ω. So, VR=32I.

  • The voltage drop across the capacitor is related to its charge by q=CVC. Therefore, VC=qC=q0.005=200q.

  • The voltage drop across the inductor is proportional to the rate of change of current, with a constant of proportionality L=8H. Hence, VL=8dIdt.

Therefore, by Kirchhoff's voltage law, these values are related by the equation

10=32I+200q+8dIdt.

Differentiating with respect to t, the differential equation governing the current in the circuit is

ddt(10)=ddt(32I+200q+8dIdt)0=32dIdt+200dqdt+8d2Idt2,

which, when substituting in I=dqdt, gives

d2Idt2+4dIdt+25I=0.
