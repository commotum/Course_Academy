A 10500mL-capacity reactor initially holds 7500mL of a chemical solution containing 98g of reactant. Pure solvent begins to flow into the reactor at a rate of 90mL/s, while the completely mixed solution flows out at a rate of 60mL/s. Find the amount of reactant in the reactor at the moment it begins to overflow.

a
60g
b
55g
c
52g
d
48g
e
50g

Incorrect
EXPLANATION

Let Q be the amount of reactant in the reactor at time t. Then, the derivative dQdt describes how this amount changes with respect to time t, in seconds. This is equal to the rate at which reactant enters the reactor minus the rate at which reactant leaves the reactor.

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′

Note that the units of dQdt,Qin′ and Qout′ are g/s. Now, let's compute the required rates of change in steps.

Step 1: Calculating the rate at which reactant enters the reactor.

First, note that pure solvent (i.e., a solution containing 0g/mL of reactant) enters the reactor at a rate of 90mL/s. So, the rate at which the reactant enters the reactor is Qin′=(0gmL)⋅(90mLs)=(0gmL)⋅(90mLs)=0gs.

Step 2: Calculating the rate at which reactant exits the reactor.

To find the rate at which reactant leaves the reactor, we must first determine the volume of solution in the reactor at time t.

The reactor initially contains 7500mL of solution; pure solvent enters at 90mL/s, and mixed solution leaves at 60mL/s. As a result, the amount of solution in the reactor after t seconds is V(t)=7500+90t−60t=30(250+t)mL.

Note that V(t) is an increasing function, so the reactor is filling.

Since Q is the amount of reactant in the reactor, the concentration of the solution at time t is Q30(250+t)gmL.

Therefore, as the completely mixed solution leaves at a rate of 60mL/s, reactant leaves at a rate of Qout′=(60mLs)⋅(Q30(250+t)gmL)=(60mLs)⋅(Q30(250+t)gmL)=2Q250+tgs.

Step 3: Calculate the overall rate of change.

The rate of change equals the rate of entry minus the rate of exit. Therefore,

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′=0−2Q250+t

which gives the equation dQdt=−2250+tQ.

Step 4: Integrate the equation

Separating variables and integrating both sides with respect to t, we get

where A=±eC is a constant of integration.

We're told that the reactor initially contains 98g of reactant. So, we can apply the initial condition Q(0)=98, and solve for A:

Q(0)=98A(250+0)2=98A=98(250)2

Therefore, we obtain the following expression for Q(t):

Q(t)=98(250)2(250+t)2=98(250250+t)2

Now, let to be the time when the reactor begins to overflow. The reactor has a capacity of 10500mL, so we can use the condition V(to)=10500 to solve for to:

V(to)=1050030(250+to)=10500250+to=350to=100s

Q(100)=98(250250+100)2=98(250350)2=98(57)2=50g.
