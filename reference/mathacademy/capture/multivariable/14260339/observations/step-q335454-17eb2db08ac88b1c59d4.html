Which statement correctly describes an unstable equilibrium point?

a
Some solutions starting arbitrarily close to the equilibrium eventually leave a fixed neighborhood of it.
b
Solutions that start anywhere in the state space eventually remain in some fixed neighborhood of the equilibrium.
c
Solutions that start sufficiently close to the equilibrium remain close and converge to the equilibrium as t→∞.
d
Solutions that start close to the equilibrium remain close to it for all future times, but may not converge to it.
e
Solutions approach the equilibrium only if the system is linear and all eigenvalues are real.
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

Let's recall the definitions of stability:

  • An equilibrium point x∗ is called stable if for any neighborhood O of x∗, there exists a neighborhood O1⊆O such that every orbit that begins inside O1 at some point in time t=t0 remains inside O for all t>t0.

    In plain words, this means that solutions that start sufficiently close to the equilibrium remain close, but may not converge to the equilibrium.

  • An equilibrium point x∗ is called asymptotically stable if it is stable and there exists a neighborhood O of x∗ such that every orbit that begins inside O approaches x∗ as t→∞.

    In plain words, this means that solutions that start sufficiently close to the equilibrium remain close and converge to the equilibrium as t→∞.

  • An equilibrium point x∗ is called unstable if it is not stable.

    In plain words, this means that solutions eventually move away from the equilibrium for some initial conditions arbitrarily close to it.

Therefore, the correct answer is the following:

Some solutions starting arbitrarily close to the equilibrium eventually leave a fixed neighborhood of it.
