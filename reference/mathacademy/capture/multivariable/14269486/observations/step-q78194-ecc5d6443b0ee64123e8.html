EXPLANATION

Recall that a linear congruence ax≡b(modn) has a unique solution modulo n if and only if gcd(a,n)=1.

In the given congruence, we have a=17 and n=50, where gcd(a,n)=gcd(17,50)=1. So, our congruence does indeed have a unique solution.

To find the solution, we need to multiply both sides of the equation by the multiplicative inverse of 17(mod50). To find the inverse of 17(mod50), use the extended Euclidean algorithm.

First, we apply the forward reduction:

50=17⋅2+16↙↙17=16⋅1+1

Solving for the rightmost terms in the equations above, we get

16=50−17⋅21=17−16

Then, we back-substitute:

1=17−16=17−(50−17⋅2)=17⋅3−50

We can write this result in modulo 50, as follows:

17⋅3−50≡1(mod50)17⋅3≡1(mod50)

So, 17⋅3≡1(mod50), which means that 3 is the multiplicative inverse of 17 (modulo 50).

Now, multiplying both sides of our original congruence by 3, we get the following:

17x≡9(mod50)3⋅17x≡3⋅9(mod50)(3⋅17)x≡27(mod50)1⋅x≡27(mod50)x≡27(mod50)

Therefore, the solution is x≡27(mod50).
