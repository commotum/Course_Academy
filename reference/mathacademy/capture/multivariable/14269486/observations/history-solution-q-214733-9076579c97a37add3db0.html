Since the modulus n=4 is fairly small, we can solve the congruence by trial and error.

Substituting x=0,1,2,3 into the congruence, we obtain the following:

3⋅0≡0≢3(mod4)×3⋅1≡3(mod4)✓3⋅2≡6≡1⋅4+2≡2≢3(mod4)×3⋅3≡9≡2⋅4+1≡1≢3(mod4)×

Therefore, the solution is x≡1(mod4).
