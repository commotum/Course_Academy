EXPLANATION

Infinite discontinuities coincide with the vertical asymptotes of y=f(x). The only possible way that y=f(x) can have vertical asymptotes is if −3x+3 grows without bound within its respective subdomain.

We compute the asymptotes of −3x+3. Setting the denominator equal to zero, we get x+3=0⟹x=−3. Although both branches of the function are defined for x≠−3, we have that limx→(−3)−(−3x+3)=∞,limx→(−3)+(−3x+3)=−∞, and therefore we conclude that x=−3 is indeed an infinite discontinuity.
