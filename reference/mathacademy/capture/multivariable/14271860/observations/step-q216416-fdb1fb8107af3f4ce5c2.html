If n and m are integers and n is a multiple of 6, then n⋅m is a multiple of 6.

Which of the following statements are true regarding the implication above?

  1. n being a multiple of 6 is a necessary condition for n⋅m being a multiple of 6
  2. n⋅m being a multiple of 6 is a necessary condition for n being a multiple of 6
  3. n being a multiple of 6 is a sufficient condition for n⋅m being a multiple of 6
  4. n⋅m being a multiple of 6 is a sufficient condition for n being a multiple of 6
a
II and III only
b
III and IV only
c
I and IV only
d
I and II only
e
I, II, III, and IV
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

Given that the implication S⇒N is true, we have the following:

  • S being true guarantees that N must also be true. Therefore, S is a sufficient condition for N.

  • For S to be true, it is necessary that N is true. Therefore, N is a necessary condition for S. However, note that N being true does not guarantee that S is true.

These ideas can be summarized as follows:

Sufficient⇒Necessary.

In our case,

(n is a multiple of 6)⏟sufficient⇒(n⋅m is a multiple of 6)⏟necessary

is a true statement. Therefore:

  • Statement II is true. n⋅m being a multiple of 6 is a necessary condition for n being a multiple of 6.

  • Statement III is true. n being a multiple of 6 is a sufficient condition for n⋅m being a multiple of 6.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II and III only."
