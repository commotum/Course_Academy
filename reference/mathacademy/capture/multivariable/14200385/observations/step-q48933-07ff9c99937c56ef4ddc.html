  1. h(x) has a relative maximum at x=−2
  2. The graph of y=h(x) is concave down on the interval (−2,1)
  3. h(x) has a relative minimum at x=3

EXPLANATION

Let's examine each of the statements separately.

  • Statement I is true. The graph y=h′(x) intersects the x-axis at x=−2 meaning that h′(x)=0. Moreover, h′(x) changes its sign by going from positive to negative. Thus, there is a relative maximum at this point.

  • Statement II is false. The slope of the tangent line to y=h′(x) changes its sign somewhere near x=0. Hence, the graph y=h(x) has an inflection point inside (−2,1).

  • Statement III is false. We have that h′(3)=0 but h′(x) does not change its sign around this point. Thus, there is no extremum.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only".
