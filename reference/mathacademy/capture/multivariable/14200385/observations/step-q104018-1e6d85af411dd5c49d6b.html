The graph of y=f′(x), the derivative of y=f(x), is defined on the interval [−4,4] and shown above. Find all the inflection points for y=f(x) on (−4,4).

a
x=−1 only
b
x=−1 and x=2
c
x=1 and x=1
d
x=1 and x=2
e
x=2 only
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

First, note that f′(x) exists at every point of the interval x∈[−4,4].

We need to investigate the behavior of f″(x). We recall the following:

  • When f′(x) is increasing, we have f″(x)>0.

  • When f′(x) is decreasing, we have f″(x)<0.

The function f(x) has an inflection point at x=a, if the following two conditions are satisfied:

  • f″(x)=0 or f″(x) is undefined, and

  • f″(x) changes its sign (from negative to positive or vice versa) at that point.

We summarize the information from the given graph in the table below:

x (−4,−1) −1 (−1,1) 1 (1,2) 2 (2,4)
f′ ↗ const. ↗ 0 ↘
f″ + not def. 0 not def. + 0 −

From the table, we conclude that f″(x) changes its sign only at the point x=2. Therefore, x=2 is the only point of inflection of y=f(x).

Watch out! The second derivative f″(x) does not change the sign when it passes through x=−1 or x=1. Hence, neither x=−1 nor x=1 are points of inflection.
