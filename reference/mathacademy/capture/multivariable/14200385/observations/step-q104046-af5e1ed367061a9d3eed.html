The graph y=f′(x), the derivative of y=f(x), is shown above. Of the following, which best describes the function y=f(x) on the interval [−4,5]?

a
f(x) has two relative maximum and one inflection point
b
f(x) has a relative minimum and two inflection points
c
f(x) has two relative extrema and three inflection points
d
f(x) has two relative minimum and one inflection point
e
f(x) has a relative maximum and two inflection points
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

First, notice that y=f′(x) intersects the x-axis (where we have f′(x)=0) twice, at the points A, B and C shown below.

An extremum of y=f(x) occurs when the graph y=f′(x) changes its sign. Hence, we have a relative minimum at A, since the derivative goes from negative to positive, and a relative maximum at B, since the derivative goes from positive to negative.

Now, an inflection point may occur where the second derivative y=f″(x) is zero (the tangent line to y=f′(x) is horizontal) or y=f″(x) does not exist (a rapid change in the slope). These are the points C, D, E, and F as shown on the graph below.

We obtain an inflection point if the slope of the tangent line to y=f′(x) changes its sign. Thus, we have inflection points only at C,D, and E. Indeed, at points C and E the slope goes from positive to negative, while at D the slope goes from negative to positive.

Therefore, we get two relative extrema and three inflection points on [−2,5] for y=f(x).
