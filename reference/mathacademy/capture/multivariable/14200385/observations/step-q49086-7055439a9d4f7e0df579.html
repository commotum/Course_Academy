The graph y=g′(x), the derivative of y=g(x), is shown above. Of the following, which best describes the function y=g(x) on the interval [−4,4]?

a
g(x) has two relative minima and two inflection points
b
g(x) has one relative maximum, one relative minimum, and one inflection point
c
g(x) has one relative maximum and two inflection points
d
g(x) has one relative minimum and one inflection point
e
g(x) has one relative maximum, one relative minimum, and two inflection points
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

First, notice that y=g′(x) intersects the x-axis (where we have g′(x)=0) twice, at the points A and B as shown below.

An extremum of y=g(x) occurs when the graph y=g′(x) changes its sign. Hence, we have an extremum at B only. This is a relative maximum since the derivative goes from positive to negative.

Now, an inflection point may occur where the second derivative y=g″(x) is zero (the tangent line to y=g′(x) is horizontal) or y=g″(x) does not exist (a rapid change in the slope). These are points the A, C and D on the graph.

We obtain an inflection point if the slope of the tangent line to y=g′(x) changes its sign. Thus, we have an inflection point at A, where the slope goes from negative to positive, and an inflection point at C, where the slope goes from positive to negative.

Therefore, we get one relative maximum and two inflection points on [−4,4] for y=g(x).
