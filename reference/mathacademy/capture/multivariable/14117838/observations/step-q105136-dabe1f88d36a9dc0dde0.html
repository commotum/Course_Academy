Let V=C[−π,π], the vector space of continuous functions over [−π,π], and let the inner product on V be defined as ⟨x(t),y(t)⟩=∫−ππx(t)y(t)dt.

Now consider the following elements of V:

f1(t)=cos⁡t,f2(t)=sin⁡t,g(t)=5cos⁡t−4sin⁡t+1

Find the orthogonal projection of g(t) onto S=Span{f1(t),f2(t)} given that f1(t)⊥f2(t) with respect to the inner product, and that the orthogonal projection of g(t) onto Span{f1(t)} equals 5cos⁡t.

a
5cos⁡t−4sin⁡t
b
cos⁡t−sin⁡t
c
5cos⁡t−4sin⁡t+1
d
5cos⁡t+4sin⁡t+1
e
4cos⁡t+5sin⁡t+1
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

Since the set {f1(t),f2(t)} is orthogonal, the orthogonal projection of g(t) onto the subspace S is given by

projSg(t)=projf1(t)g(t)+projf2(t)g(t).

We are given that projf1(t)g(t)=5cos⁡t, so let's now find the orthogonal projection of g(t) onto Span{f2(t)}. This can be computed as

projf2(t)g(t)=⟨g(t),f2(t)⟩⟨f2(t),f2(t)⟩f2(t).

In our case, we have

⟨g(t),f2(t)⟩=∫−ππg(t)f2(t)dt=∫−ππ(5cos⁡t−4sin⁡t+1)⋅sin⁡tdt=5∫−ππcos⁡tsin⁡tdt−4∫−ππsin2⁡tdt+∫−ππsin⁡tdt

Notice that sin⁡t is an odd function, while cos⁡t is even. Hence, their product cos⁡tsin⁡t must be an odd function. Now, since we have an integral over the interval [−π,π], which is symmetric about the origin, we can conclude that

∫−ππcos⁡tsin⁡tdt=∫−ππsin⁡tdt=0.
So, we have ⟨g(t),f2(t)⟩=−4∫−ππsin2⁡tdt.

Therefore,

projf2(t)g(t)=−4∫−ππsin2⁡tdt⟨f2(t),f2(t)⟩⋅sin⁡t=−4∫−ππsin2⁡tdt∫−ππsin2⁡tdt⋅sin⁡t=−4sin⁡t.

Finally,

projSg(t)=projf1(t)g(t)+projf2(t)g(t)=5cos⁡t−4sin⁡t.
