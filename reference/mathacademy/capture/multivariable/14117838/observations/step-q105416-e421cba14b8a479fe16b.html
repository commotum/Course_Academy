Let V=C[−π,π], the vector space of continuous functions over [−π,π], and let the inner product on V be defined as ⟨x(t),y(t)⟩=∫−ππx(t)y(t)dt.

Now consider the following elements of V:

f1(t)=sin⁡t,f2(t)=cos⁡t,g(t)=sin2⁡t−cos⁡t

Find the orthogonal projection of g(t) onto S=Span{f1(t),f2(t)} given that f1(t)⊥f2(t) with respect to the inner product, and that the orthogonal projection of g(t) onto Span{f2(t)} equals −cos⁡t.

a
−cos⁡t+2sin⁡t
b
−cos⁡t−sin⁡t
c
−cos⁡t−2sin⁡t
d
−cos⁡t
e
−cos⁡t+sin⁡t

Correct
EXPLANATION

Since the set {f1(t),f2(t)} is orthogonal, the orthogonal projection of g(t) onto the subspace S is given by

projSg(t)=projf1(t)g(t)+projf2(t)g(t).

We are given that projf2(t)g(t)=−cos⁡t, so let's now find the orthogonal projection of g(t) onto Span{f1(t)}. This can be computed as

projf1(t)g(t)=⟨g(t),f1(t)⟩⟨f1(t),f1(t)⟩f1(t).

In our case, we have

⟨g(t),f1(t)⟩=∫−ππg(t)f1(t)dt=∫−ππ(sin2⁡t−cos⁡t)⋅sin⁡tdt=∫−ππsin⁡t(sin2⁡t−cos⁡t)dt

Notice that sin⁡t is an odd function, while sin2⁡t−cos⁡t is even. Hence, their product sin⁡t(sin2⁡t−cos⁡t) must be an odd function. Now, since we have an integral over the interval [−π,π], which is symmetric about the origin, we can conclude that

∫−ππsin⁡t(sin2⁡t−cos⁡t)dt=0.

Therefore,

projf1(t)g(t)=0⟨f1(t),f1(t)⟩⋅sin⁡t=0.

Finally,

projSg(t)=projf1(t)g(t)+projf2(t)g(t)=0−cos⁡t=−cos⁡t.
