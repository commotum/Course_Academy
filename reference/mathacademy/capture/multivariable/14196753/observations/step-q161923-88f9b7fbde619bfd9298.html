EXPLANATION

We will use the change of variables formula in the form

∭Rf(x,y,z)dV=∭Δf(ρcos⁡θsin⁡ϕ,ρsin⁡θsin⁡ϕ,ρcos⁡ϕ)ρ2sin⁡ϕdρdθdϕ.

If (ρ,θ,ϕ) are the spherical coordinates of a point, then its Cartesian coordinates (x,y,z) can be found by using the following formulas:

x=ρcos⁡θsin⁡ϕ,y=ρsin⁡θsin⁡ϕ,z=ρcos⁡ϕ

Notice that the region R represents one-half of the sphere of radius 3 centered at (−3,0,0).

With that in mind, let's now write the x2+y2+z2=−6x in spherical polar coordinates:

x2+y2+z2=−6xρ2=−6ρcos⁡θsin⁡ϕρ(ρ+6cos⁡θsin⁡ϕ)=0

Since ρ cannot be identical to zero on the entire sphere, we must have ρ=−6cos⁡θsin⁡ϕ.

As a result, we obtain that in spherical polar coordinates, our region can be expressed as

Δ={(ρ,θ,ϕ):0≤ρ≤−6cos⁡θsin⁡ϕ,π≤θ≤3π2,0≤ϕ≤π}.

Next, we substitute the spherical polar coordinates into the function f(x,y,z)=xx2+y2:

f(x,y,z)=f(ρcos⁡θsin⁡ϕ,ρsin⁡θsin⁡ϕ,ρcos⁡ϕ)=ρcos⁡θsin⁡ϕ(ρcos⁡θsin⁡ϕ)2+(ρsin⁡θsin⁡ϕ)2=ρcos⁡θsin⁡ϕρ2sin2⁡ϕ(cos2⁡θ+sin2⁡θ)=cos⁡θρsin⁡ϕ

Therefore, using the change of variables formula, we obtain

∭Rxx2+y2dV=∭Δcos⁡θρsin⁡ϕ⋅ρ2sin⁡ϕdρdθdϕ=∫0π∫π3π/2∫0−6cos⁡θsin⁡ϕρcos⁡θdρdθdϕ.
