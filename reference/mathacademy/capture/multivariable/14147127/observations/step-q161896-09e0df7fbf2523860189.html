Consider the vector field F=⟨z,0,2⟩ and the closed surface S that represents the boundary of the solid shown above. Let D be the projection of S onto the xy-plane. The flux of F across S can be expressed in terms of a double integral as ∬SF⋅dS=∬DAAAAAAAdA. Which of the following could be the missing expression?

a
xx2+y2−x
b
xyx2+y2+2
c
xx2+y2+y
d
yx2+y2−y
e
yx2+y2−2

Incorrect
EXPLANATION

Since we have a closed surface, the positive orientation is outward-pointing by convention. So,

  • the upper surface S1:z=g(x,y)=1−x2+y2 is oriented upward, and

  • the lower surface S2:z=h(x,y)=0 is oriented downward.

Now, we write down the flux across each of the surfaces separately.

  • Consider the upper cone S1.

    We compute the partial derivatives of z=g(x,y): ∂g∂x=∂∂x(1−x2+y2)=−xx2+y2∂g∂y=∂∂y(1−x2+y2)=−yx2+y2

    If we denote F=⟨P,Q,R⟩, then P=z=1−x2+y2,Q=0,R=2.

    As a result, since the positive orientation is upward, we have ∬S1F⋅dS=∬D(−P∂g∂x−Q∂g∂y+R)dA=∬D−(1−x2+y2)⋅(−xx2+y2)−0⋅(−yx2+y2)+2dA=∬Dxx2+y2−x+2dA.

  • Consider the lower plane S2.

    Since the positive orientation is downward, the unit normal vector to the plane is n=⟨0,0,−1⟩. As a result, we have ∬S2F⋅dS=∬S2F⋅ndS=∬D⟨z,0,2⟩⋅⟨0,0,−1⟩dA=∬D−2dA.

Therefore, the flux of F across the entire surface is ∬SF⋅dS=∬S1F⋅dS+∬S2F⋅dS=∬Dxx2+y2−x+2dA+∬D−2dA=∬Dxx2+y2−xdA.
