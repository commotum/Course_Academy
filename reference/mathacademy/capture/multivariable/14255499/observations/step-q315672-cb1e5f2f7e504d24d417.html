EXPLANATION

First, let's draw a picture of a cylinder. Let r be the radius, and h the height.

We wish to minimize the manufacturing cost. We can express the manufacturing cost C in terms of r and h as follows: C=(2πrh)⋅4+(2πr2)⋅1.5=8πrh+3πr2.

We know that the volume is fixed at 6πft3, so we also have the constraint condition 6π=πr2h.

Now, by making h the subject of the constraint equation, we have that h=6r2.

Plugging the above into our expression for C, we obtain C(r)=8πr(6r2)+3πr2=48πr+3πr2.

We want to minimize C, so we need to differentiate C and set the derivative equal to zero. The derivative is C′(r)=−48πr2+6πr.

Setting the derivative equal to zero and solving for r gives a stationary point, as follows: C′(r)=0−48πr2+6πr=06πr=48πr26πr3=48πr3=8r=83r=2.

Using a calculator, we can verify that if r<2 then C′(r)<0, and if r>2 then C′(r)>0, which means that the stationary point r=2 is indeed a minimum of C(r).

Therefore, the tank with the smallest manufacturing cost has radius r=2ft.
