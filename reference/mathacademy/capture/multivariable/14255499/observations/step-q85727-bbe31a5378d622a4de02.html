We need to create a cylindrical tin with a lid using 96πin2 of sheet metal. What is the largest possible volume of the cylinder?

a
144πin3
b
216πin3
c
48πin3
d
128πin3
e
108πin3
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

First, let r be the radius and h be the height of the cylinder.

We wish to maximize the volume

V=πr2h.

We know that the surface area is fixed at 96πin2, so we also have the constraint equation

2πrh+2πr2=96π.

Now, by making h the subject of the constraint equation, we have that

2πrh=96π−2πr2h=96π−2πr22πrh=48−r2r.

Plugging the above into our expression for V, we obtain

V(r)=πr2×48−r2rV(r)=(48r−r3)π.

We want to maximize V, so we need to differentiate V and set the derivative equal to zero. The derivative is V′(r)=(48−3r2)π.

Setting the derivative equal to zero and solving for r gives a stationary point, as follows:

V′(r)=0(48−3r2)π=048=3r2r2=16r=4in

Note that, in the last step, we only considered the positive root because the radius r must be positive.

Finally, we confirm that r=4 is a maximum using the second derivative test. The second derivative V″(r) is

V″(r)=−6πr,

and we can see that V″(4)<0. So, the stationary point r=4 is indeed a maximum of V(r).

So the cylinder with the largest volume has a radius of r=4in.

Finally, to find the largest volume, we substitute r=4in into the equation for the volume V:

V(4)=(48(4)−(4)3)π=(192−64)π=128π

Therefore, the largest possible volume of the cylinder is V=128πin3.
