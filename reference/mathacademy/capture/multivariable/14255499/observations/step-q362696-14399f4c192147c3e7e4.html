EXPLANATION

First, let r be the radius and h be the height of the cylinder.

We wish to maximize the volume

V=πr2h.

We know that the surface area is fixed at 108πin2, so we also have the constraint equation

2πrh+πr2=108π.

Now, by making h the subject of the constraint equation, we have that

2πrh=108π−πr2h=54r−r2.

Plugging the above into our expression for V, we obtain

V(r)=πr2(54r−r2)V(r)=(54r−r32)π.

We want to maximize V, so we need to differentiate V and set the derivative equal to zero. The derivative is V′(r)=(54−3r22)π.

Setting the derivative equal to zero and solving for r gives a stationary point, as follows:

V′(r)=0(54−3r22)π=054=3r22r2=36r=6in

Note that, in the last step, we only considered the positive root because the radius r must be positive.

Finally, we confirm that r=6 is a maximum using the second derivative test. The second derivative V″(r) is

V″(r)=−6πr2=−3πr,

and we can see that V″(6)<0. So, the stationary point r=6 is indeed a maximum of V(r).

So the cylinder with the largest volume has a radius of r=6in.

Finally, to find the largest volume Vmax, we substitute r=6in into the equation for the volume V:

Vmax=V(6)=(54(6)−12(6)3)π=(324−2162)π=(324−108)π=216πin3
