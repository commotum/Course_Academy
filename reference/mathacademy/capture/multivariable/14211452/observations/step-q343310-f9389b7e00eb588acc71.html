A worker is coating a surface with epoxy using a thickness gauge, which fixes the epoxy height at 5in. If the length is increasing at a rate of 3in/s and the width is increasing at a rate of 2in/s, how fast is the surface area of the epoxy layer increasing when its length is 12in and its width is 7in?
The surface area is increasing at a rate of
70
in2/s.

Incorrect
EXPLANATION

Let x,y,z be the dimensions of the solid, and let S be its surface area. We are given that x=12in,y=7in,z=5in,dxdt=3in/s,dydt=2in/s.

We would like to find dSdt.

We want to find the rate of change of S knowing the rate of change of x and y. So, we start with the formula for the surface area of the rectangular prism: S=2xz+2xy+2yz

Since z=5in is not changing, we get S=10x+2xy+10y.

Now, we differentiate with respect to time using the chain rule, and we get

dSdt=ddt(10x+2xy+10y)dSdt=(10+2x)(dydt)+(10+2y)(dxdt).

We can now plug in the known values into the above expression. This gives dSdt=(10+2⋅12)(2)+(10+2⋅7)(3)=(34)(2)+(24)(3)=68+72=140in2/s.

Therefore, the surface area of the rectangular prism is increasing at a rate of 140in2/s.
