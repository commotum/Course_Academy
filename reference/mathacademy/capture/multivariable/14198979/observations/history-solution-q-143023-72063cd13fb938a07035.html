We do not know the distribution of the population. However, the sample size n=180≥30 is sufficiently large. Therefore, according to the central limit theorem, we may use the following approximation: X―−μσ/n∼N(0,1),

where

  • X― is the sample mean,

  • μ is the population mean,

  • σ2 is the population variance, and

  • n is the sample size.

Given a value α between 0 and 1, the corresponding [100(1−α)]% confidence interval for the population mean μ of the original distribution is given by

x―±zα/2⋅σn,

where P(Z>zα/2)=α2 and Z∼N(0,1).

In our case,

x―=255,n=180,σ=36.

We are interested in finding a 95% confidence interval. So, we have

α=1−0.95=0.05⟹α2=0.025.

We also need to find the z-score value z0.025 such that P(Z>z0.025)=0.025, where Z∼N(0,1).
