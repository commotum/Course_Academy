Consider a function f(t) for which the Laplace transform is L{f(t)}=F(s). How can we obtain the graph of L{e−7tf(t)} from the graph of F(s)?

a
Shift the graph of F(s) by 7 units to the right.
b
Shift the graph of F(s) by 7 units to the left.
c
The Laplace transform is unchanged.
d
Scale the graph of F(s) by a scale factor of 7 vertically.
e
Scale the graph of F(s) by a scale factor of 7 horizontally.
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

The first shifting theorem states that if L{f(t)}=F(s), then L{eatf(t)}=F(s−a).

This implies that multiplying a function f(t) by eat shifts the corresponding Laplace transform horizontally along the s-axis.

  • If a>0, the graph of F(s) shifts to the right.

  • If a<0, the graph of F(s) shifts to the left.

In our case, to get the graph of L{e−7tf(t)}, we must shift the graph of F(s) by 7 units to the left.
