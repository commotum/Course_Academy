To prove a statement using induction, we use the following framework:

  • Step 1: Base Case

  • Step 2: Inductive Hypothesis

  • Step 3: Inductive Step

  • Step 4: Conclusion

In this case, the general statement P(n) we're required to prove the following:

[1012]n=[102n−12n]

We start by proving the base case, P(1). To do this, we substitute n=1 into both the left-hand side and right-hand side.

In our case, this gives the following:

Base Case: We first prove that our statement is true for the base case n=1. [1012]1=[1021−121]

Proving that the base case is true is usually straightforward. We simply calculate the left-hand and right-hand sides independently and show they're equal.

Next, we write down our inductive hypothesis. We assume that P(k) is true for some n=k≥1. To do this, we simply replace n with k in our original statement P(n).

In our case, this gives the following:

Inductive Hypothesis: We assume that for some n=k≥1, we have [1012]k=[102k−12k].

Then, we move onto the inductive step: We must show that P(k)⇒P(k+1). We start by writing down P(k+1).

Inductive Step: We're required to prove that [1012]k+1=[102k+1−12k+1].

The inductive step is where most of the work lies. The idea is to rewrite the left-hand side of this equation using the inductive hypothesis and apply algebraic techniques to show that this equals the right-hand side.

Once we're shown that P(k)⇒P(k+1), the proof is complete, and we state our conclusion.

Conclusion: Therefore, [1012]n=[102n−12n] for all n∈N.
