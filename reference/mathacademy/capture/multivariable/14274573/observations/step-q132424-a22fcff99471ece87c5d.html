EXPLANATION

In the given congruence, we have a=20 and n=24, and so gcd(a,n)=gcd(20,24)=4. Since b=16 is divisible by 4, our congruence has 4 solutions.

First, we use the division property of congruences:

20x≡16(mod24)4⋅5x≡4⋅4(mod4⋅6)4⋅5x≡4⋅4(mod4⋅6)5x≡4(mod6)

Notice that in the new congruence, we have a1=5 and n1=6, where gcd(a1,n1)=gcd(5,6)=1. So, our congruence has a unique solution modulo 6.

To find the solution, we need to multiply both sides of the equation by the multiplicative inverse of 5(mod6). To find the inverse of 5(mod6), we compute the exponents of 5(mod6) until we get a result of 1(mod6):

52≡5⋅5≡25≡4⋅6+1≡1(mod6)

So, 5⋅5≡1(mod6), which means that 5 is its own multiplicative inverse (modulo 6).

Now, multiplying both sides of our congruence by 5, we get the following:

5x≡4(mod6)5⋅5x≡5⋅4(mod6)(5⋅5)x≡20(mod6)x≡20(mod6)x≡3⋅6+2(mod6)x≡2(mod6)

So, we get the solution x≡2(mod6).

But our original congruence was modulo 24 (not modulo 6). To find all the solutions modulo 24, we repeatedly add 6 modulo 24 until we reach the number 2 again:

x1≡2(mod24)x2≡2+6≡8(mod24)x3≡8+6≡14(mod24)x4≡14+6≡20(mod24)x5≡20+6≡26≡24+2≡2≡x1(mod24)

Therefore, the solutions are x≡2,8,14,20(mod24).

Finally, 2+8+14+20≡44≡24+20≡20(mod24).
