s(cos⁡t−sin⁡t)es(sin⁡t+cos⁡t)

(cos⁡t−sin⁡t)es(sin⁡t+cos⁡t)

(cos⁡t+sin⁡t)es(sin⁡t+cos⁡t)

Correct
EXPLANATION

The chain rule for a multivariable function f(x,y) with two independent variables s and t, where x=x(s,t) and y=y(s,t), can be expressed as follows: ∂f∂t=∂f∂x⋅∂x∂t+∂f∂y⋅∂y∂t

First, we find the partial derivatives of f with respect to x and y: ∂f∂x=ex+y,∂f∂y=ex+y

Writing the above results in terms of s and t, we get ∂f∂x=essin⁡t+scos⁡t,∂f∂y=essin⁡t+scos⁡t.

Next, we take the derivatives of x and y with respect to t, and we get ∂x∂t=scos⁡t,∂y∂t=−ssin⁡t.

Finally, substituting all of our derivatives into the multivariable chain rule, we get ∂f∂t=∂f∂x⋅∂x∂t+∂f∂y⋅∂y∂t=(essin⁡t+scos⁡t)(scos⁡t)+(essin⁡t+scos⁡t)(−ssin⁡t)=(scos⁡t−ssin⁡t)essin⁡t+scos⁡t=s(cos⁡t−sin⁡t)es(sin⁡t+cos⁡t).
