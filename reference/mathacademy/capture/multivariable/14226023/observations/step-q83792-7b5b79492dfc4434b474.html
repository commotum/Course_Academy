EXPLANATION

The area bounded by a polar curve is given by A=12∫θ1θ2r2(θ)dθ.

Since r(θ)=2cos⁡(7θ), we obtain

A=12∫θ1θ2(2cos⁡(7θ))2dθ=∫θ1θ2cos2⁡(7θ)dθ.

We now proceed to find the integration limits.

The curve bounding the region crosses itself at the origin. Therefore, we want to find the values of θ for which r=0, or 2cos⁡(7θ)=0.

Notice that the first petal lies between the first and fourth quadrants. So it's convenient to assume that θ can be negative.

We now solve the above equation for 7θ∈[−π,π):

7θ=−π2,π2⟹θ=−π14,π14

Therefore, the first petal is traversed for θ∈[−π14,π14]. Notice that this interval lies between the first and fourth quadrants, the same as our petal.

Therefore, our expression for the area is given by
