EXPLANATION

The area bounded by a polar curve is given by A=12∫θ1θ2r2(θ)dθ.

Since r(θ)=1+cos⁡(4θ+5π6), we obtain

A=12∫θ1θ2(1+cos⁡(4θ+5π6))2dθ

We now proceed to find the integration limits.

The curve bounding the region crosses itself at the origin. Therefore, we want to find the values of θ for which r=0, or 1+cos⁡(4θ+5π6)=0cos⁡(4θ+5π6)=−1.

From the diagram, the first petal extends from the fourth quadrant to the first quadrant. Since the petal begins in the fourth quadrant, it is convenient to allow negative values of θ when determining the interval.

The solutions for 4θ+5π6 closest to 0 on either side are 4θ+5π6=−π,π⟹4θ=−11π6,π6⟹θ=−11π24,π24.

Therefore, the first petal is traversed for

θ∈[−11π24,π24].

Notice that this interval lies between the first and fourth quadrants, the same as our petal.

Therefore, our expression for the area is given by

12∫−11π/24π/24(1+cos⁡(4θ+5π6))2dθ.
