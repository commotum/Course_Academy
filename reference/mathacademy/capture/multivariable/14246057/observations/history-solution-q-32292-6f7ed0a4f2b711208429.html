A vector-valued function is continuous at a point if and only if each of its components is continuous at that point.

Let's now consider the functions one-by-one.

  • Function f(t) is not continuous at t=0 since its first component cot⁡t is not continuous at t=0. Indeed, the left and right limits are not equal: limt→+0cot⁡t=+∞limt→−0cot⁡t=−∞ Hence, limt→0cot⁡t doesn’t exist.

  • Function g(t) is not continuous at t=0 since its first component 2t is not continuous at t=0.

  • Function h(t) is continuous at t=0 because each of its components 2t, cos⁡t and t4 are continuous at t=0.

Therefore, the answer is:
