Let X represent the annual income (in dollars) of a randomly selected employee. Then we have X∼N(50000,150002).

We want to find P(46700<X<56150). First, we convert X to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring:

P(46700<X<56150)=P(46700−5000015000<Z<56150−5000015000)=P(−0.22<Z<0.41)

Now, we express the required probability in terms of Φ(z):

P(−0.22<Z<0.41)=P(Z<0.41)−P(Z≤−0.22)=P(Z≤0.41)−P(Z≤−0.22)=Φ(0.41)−Φ(−0.22)

From the z-table, we see that

Φ(0.41)=0.6591Φ(−0.22)=0.4129.

So, we have

P(−0.22<Z<0.41)=Φ(0.41)−Φ(−0.22)=0.6591−0.4129=0.2462≈0.25

Therefore, the probability that a randomly selected employee's income is more than $46700 but less than $56150 is approximately 0.25.
