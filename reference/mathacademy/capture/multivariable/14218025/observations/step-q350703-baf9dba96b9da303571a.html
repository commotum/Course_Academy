The standard Maclaurin expansion of ex is ex=1+x+x22!+x33!+⋯=1+x+x22+x36+⋯.

The standard Maclaurin expansion for 11−x is 11−x=1+x+x2+x3+⋯.

Replacing x with 2x, we get 11−2x=1+(2x)+(2x)2+(2x)3+⋯=1+2x+4x2+8x3+⋯.

To find the expansion of ex1−2x, we multiply the expansions of ex and 11−2x, as follows: ex1−2x=(1+x+x22+x36+⋯)(1+2x+4x2+8x3+⋯)

Let's work out each term individually:

  • There is a single constant term, given by 1⋅1=1.

  • There are two x terms, and their sum is given by 1⋅(2x)+x⋅1=2x+x=3x.

  • There are three x2 terms, and their sum is given by 1⋅(4x2)+x⋅(2x)+(x22)⋅1=4x2+2x2+x22=6x2+x22=12x22+x22=13x22.

So, the first three nonzero terms of the expansion are ex1−2x=1+3x+13x22+796x3+⋯.
