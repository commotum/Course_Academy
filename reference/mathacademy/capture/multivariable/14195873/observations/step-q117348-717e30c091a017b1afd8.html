We wish to construct a cylindrical can with a base and a lid. Our cylinder must have an exact volume of 2000πcm3. What is the radius of the cylinder that gives the smallest surface area?

a
10cm
b
5cm
c
13cm
d
20cm
e
26cm

Correct
EXPLANATION

First, let's draw a picture of a cylinder. Let r be the radius, and h the height.

We wish to minimize the surface area

S=2πrh+2πr2.

We know that the volume is fixed at 2000πcm3, so we also have the constraint condition

2000π=πr2h.

Now, by making h the subject of the constraint equation, we have that

h=2000r2.

Plugging the above into our expression for S, we obtain

S(r)=2πr(2000r2)+2πr2=4000πr+2πr2.

We want to minimize S, so we need to differentiate S and set the derivative equal to zero. The derivative is

S′(r)=−4000πr2+4πr.

Setting the derivative equal to zero and solving for r gives a stationary point, as follows:

S′(r)=0−4000πr2+4πr=04πr=4000πr24πr3=4000πr3=1000r=10003r=10cm

Using a calculator, we can verify that if r<10 then S′(r)<0, and if r>10 then S′(r)>0, which means that the stationary point r=10 is indeed a minimum of S(r).

Therefore, the cylinder with the smallest surface area has radius r=10cm.
