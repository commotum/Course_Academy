A manufacturer wishes to build a cylindrical container with a base but no lid. The container must have an exact volume of 274πin3. To make the container, the manufacturer uses two materials:

  • a plastic sheet that costs $6 per in2 for the lateral surface,

  • a plastic sheet that costs $1.50 per in2 for the base.

What is the radius r of the container that gives the smallest manufacturing cost? Do not round the answer.

EXPLANATION

First, let's draw a picture of a cylinder. Let r be the radius, and h the height.

We wish to minimize the manufacturing cost. We can express the manufacturing cost C in terms of r and h as follows: C=2πrh⋅6+πr2⋅1.50=12πrh+1.5πr2

We know that the volume is fixed at 274πin3, so we also have the constraint condition 274π=πr2h.

Now, by making h the subject of the constraint equation, we have that h=274r2.

Plugging the above into our expression for C, we obtain C(r)=12πr(274r2)+1.5πr2=81πr+1.5πr2.

We want to minimize C, so we need to differentiate C and set the derivative equal to zero. The derivative is C′(r)=−81πr2+3πr.

Setting the derivative equal to zero and solving for r gives a stationary point, as follows: C′(r)=0−81πr2+3πr=03πr=81πr23πr3=81πr3=27r=273=3

Using a calculator, we can verify that if r<3 then C′(r)<0, and if r>3 then C′(r)>0, which means that the stationary point r=3 is indeed a minimum of C(r).

Therefore, the container with the smallest manufacturing cost has radius r=3in.
