EXPLANATION

First, let's draw a picture of a cylinder. Let r be the radius, and h the height.

We wish to minimize the surface area S=2πrh+2πr2.

We know that the volume is fixed at 128πft3, so we also have the constraint condition 128π=πr2h.

Now, by making h the subject of the constraint equation, we have that h=128r2.

Plugging the above into our expression for S, we obtain

S(r)=2πr(128r2)+2πr2=256πr+2πr2.

We want to minimize S, so we need to differentiate S and set the derivative equal to zero. The derivative is S′(r)=−256πr2+4πr.

Setting the derivative equal to zero and solving for r gives a stationary point, as follows: S′(r)=0−256πr2+4πr=04πr=256πr24πr3=256πr3=64r=643=4ft

Using a calculator, we can verify that if r<4 then S′(r)<0, and if r>4 then S′(r)>0, which means that the stationary point r=4 is indeed a minimum of S(r).

Finally, to find the smallest surface area, we substitute r=4ft into the equation for the surface area S:

S(4)=256π4+2π(4)2=301.592…≈302ft2, rounded to the nearest integer.

Therefore, the smallest possible surface area of the cylinder is S=302ft2.
