We wish to maximize the volume

V=πr2h.

We know that the surface area is fixed at 64πcm2, so we also have the constraint equation

2πrh+πr2=64π.

Now, by making h the subject of the constraint equation, we have that

2πrh=64π−πr2h=64π−πr22πrh=64−r22r.

Plugging the above into our expression for V, we obtain

V(r)=πr2×64−r22rV(r)=(64r−r3)π2.

We want to maximize V, so we need to differentiate V and set the derivative equal to zero. The derivative is V′(r)=(64−3r2)π2.

Setting the derivative equal to zero and solving for r gives a stationary point, as follows:

V′(r)=0(64−3r2)π2=064=3r2r2=643r=833cm

Note that, in the last step, we only considered the positive root because the radius r must be positive.

Finally, we confirm that r=833 is a maximum using the second derivative test. The second derivative V″(r) is

V″(r)=−6πr2=−3πr,

and we can see that V″(833)<0. So, the stationary point r=833 is indeed a maximum of V(r).

Therefore, the cylinder with the largest volume has a radius of r=833cm.
