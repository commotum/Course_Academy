We wish to construct a cylindrical can with a base and a lid. Our cylinder must have an exact volume of 686πmm3. What is the smallest surface we can get?

a
306πmm2
b
324πmm2
c
424πmm2
d
286πmm2
e
294πmm2
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

First, let's draw a picture of a cylinder. Let r be the radius, and h the height.

We wish to minimize the surface area

S=2πrh+2πr2.

We know that the volume is fixed at 686πmm3, so we also have the constraint condition

686π=πr2h.

Now, by making h the subject of the constraint equation, we have that

h=686r2.

Plugging the above into our expression for S, we obtain

S(r)=2πr(686r2)+2πr2=1372πr+2πr2.

We want to minimize S, so we need to differentiate S and set the derivative equal to zero. The derivative is

S′(r)=−1372πr2+4πr.

Setting the derivative equal to zero and solving for r gives a stationary point, as follows:

S′(r)=0−1372πr2+4πr=04πr=1372πr24πr3=1372πr3=343r=3433=7mm

Using a calculator, we can verify that if r<7 then S′(r)<0, and if r>7 then S′(r)>0, which means that the stationary point r=7 is indeed a minimum of S(r).

Therefore, the cylinder with the smallest surface area has radius r=7mm.

Finally, to find the smallest surface area, we substitute r=7mm into the equation for the surface area S:

S(7)=1372π7+2π(7)2=294πmm2.

We conclude that the smallest possible surface area of the cylinder is S=294πmm2.
