First, let's draw a picture of a cylinder. Let r be the radius, and h the height.

We wish to minimize the surface area

S=2πrh+πr2.

We know that the volume is fixed at 64πmm3, so we also have the constraint condition

64π=πr2h.

Now, by making h the subject of the constraint equation, we have that

h=64r2.

Plugging the above into our expression for S, we obtain

S(r)=2πr(64r2)+πr2=128πr+πr2.

We want to minimize S, so we need to differentiate S and set the derivative equal to zero. The derivative is

S′(r)=−128πr2+2πr.

Setting the derivative equal to zero and solving for r gives a stationary point, as follows:

S′(r)=0−128πr2+2πr=02πr=128πr22πr3=128πr3=64r=643r=4mm

Using a calculator, we can verify that if r<4 then S′(r)<0, and if r>4 then S′(r)>0, which means that the stationary point r=4 is indeed a minimum of S(r).

So the cylinder with the smallest surface area has radius r=4mm.

Finally, to find the smallest surface area, we substitute r=4mm into the equation for the surface area S:

S(4)=128π4+π(4)2=32π+16π=48πmm2

Therefore, the smallest possible surface area of the cylinder is S=48πmm2.
