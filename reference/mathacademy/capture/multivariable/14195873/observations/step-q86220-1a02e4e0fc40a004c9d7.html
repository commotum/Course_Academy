A manufacturer wishes to build a cylindrical container with a base but no lid. The container must have an exact volume of 72πft3. To make the container, the manufacturer uses two materials:

  • a plastic sheet that costs $1.5 per ft2 for the lateral surface,

  • a plastic sheet that costs $0.5 per ft2 for the base.

What is the radius of the container that gives the smallest manufacturing cost?

a
2ft
b
7ft
c
9ft
d
3ft
e
6ft
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

First, let's draw a picture of a cylinder. Let r be the radius, and h the height.

We wish to minimize the manufacturing cost. We can express the manufacturing cost C in terms of r and h as follows:

C=2πrh⋅1.5+πr2⋅0.5=3πrh+0.5πr2.

We know that the volume is fixed at 72πft3, so we also have the constraint condition

72π=πr2h.

Now, by making h the subject of the constant equation, we have that

h=72r2.

Plugging the above into our expression for C, we obtain

C(r)=3πr(72r2)+0.5πr2=216πr+0.5πr2.

We want to minimize C, so we need to differentiate C and set the derivative equal to zero. The derivative is C′(r)=−216πr2+πr.

Setting the derivative equal to zero and solving for r gives a stationary point, as follows:

C′(r)=0−216πr2+πr=0πr=216πr2πr3=216πr3=216r=2163r=6ft

Using a calculator, we can verify that if r<6 then C′(r)<0, and if r>6 then C′(r)>0, which means that the stationary point r=6 is indeed a minimum of C(r).

Therefore, the container with the smallest manufacturing cost has radius r=6ft.
