Explanation

Previously, we found that the third-degree Taylor polynomial of f(x) about x=0 is

P3(x)=2−12x+14x2−16x3

and the Maclaurin series for cos⁡2x is

cos⁡(2x)=1−2x2+23x4−⋯.

To find the expansion of f(x)cos⁡(2x), we multiply the expansions of f(x) and cos⁡(2x), as follows:

f(x)cos⁡(2x)=(2−12x+14x2−16x3)(1−2x2+23x4−⋯)

Let's work out each term individually:

  • There is a constant term, given by 2⋅1=2.

  • There is a single x term, given by −12x⋅1=−12x.

  • There are two x2 terms and their sum is given by 2⋅(−2x2)+14x2⋅1=−4x2+14x2,=−154x2.

All other terms contain powers of x greater than 2.

Therefore, the second-degree Taylor polynomial of f(x)cos⁡(2x) about x=0 is

P2(x)=2−12x−154x2.
