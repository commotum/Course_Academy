The lengths of metal rods produced by a certain factory are normally distributed with a mean of 100 centimeters and a standard deviation of 4 centimeters. What is the probability that a randomly selected rod is longer or equal to 96.8cm but shorter than 102.4cm? Round your answer to two decimal places.

EXPLANATION

Let X represent the rod length (in centimeters). Then we have X∼N(100,42).

We want to find P(96.8≤X<102.4). First, we convert X to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring:

P(96.8≤X<102.4)=P(96.8−1004≤Z<102.4−1004)=P(−0.80≤Z<0.60)

Now, we express the required probability in terms of Φ(z):

P(−0.80≤Z<0.60)=P(Z<0.60)−P(Z<−0.80)=P(Z≤0.60)−P(Z≤−0.80)=Φ(0.60)−Φ(−0.80)

From the z-table, we see that

Φ(0.60)=0.7257Φ(−0.80)=0.2119.

So, we have

P(−0.80<Z<0.60)=Φ(0.60)−Φ(−0.80)=0.7257−0.2119=0.5138≈0.51

Therefore, the probability that a randomly selected rod is longer or equal to 96.8cm but shorter than 102.4cm is approximately 0.51.
