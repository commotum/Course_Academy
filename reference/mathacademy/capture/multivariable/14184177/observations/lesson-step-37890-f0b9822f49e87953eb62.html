Interpreting Confidence Intervals
?

It's important to be able to interpret confidence intervals correctly.

Imagine we conduct several independent random samples from a population. The mean of the sample will be different for each sample. Some values of might lie close to the population mean and some could lie further away.

Suppose we construct a confidence interval for each sample and plot them on the same diagram. We might get a picture similar to the one below.

In the diagram above, we see that nine out of ten of our confidence intervals contain while one of them (the red one) does not contain the population mean

The idea is that if we construct many confidence intervals, one for each sample, then approximately of these confidence intervals will contain the population mean
