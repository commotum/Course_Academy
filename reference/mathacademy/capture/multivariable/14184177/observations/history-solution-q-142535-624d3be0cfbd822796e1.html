We are told that the population is normally distributed. Therefore, the distribution of sample mean is also normal, and we have that

X―−μσ/n∼N(0,1),

where

  • X― is the sample mean,

  • μ is the population mean,

  • σ2 is the population variance, and

  • n is the sample size.

Given a value α between 0 and 1, the corresponding [100(1−α)]% confidence interval for the population mean μ of the original distribution is given by

x―±zα/2⋅σn,

where P(Z>zα/2)=α2 and Z∼N(0,1).

In our case,

x―=100g,n=10,σ=12g.

We are interested in finding a 99% confidence interval. So, we have

α=1−0.99=0.01⟹α2=0.005.

We also need to find the z-score value z0.005 such that P(Z>z0.005)=0.005, where Z∼N(0,1).

From the percentage points table of the normal distribution, we obtain that z0.005=2.576:

zp 1.282 1.645 1.960 2.326 2.576
