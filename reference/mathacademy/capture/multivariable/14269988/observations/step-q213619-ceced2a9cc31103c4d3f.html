EXPLANATION

In the given congruence, we have a=20 and n=28, and so gcd(a,n)=gcd(20,28)=4. Since b=8 is divisible by 4, our congruence has 4 solutions.

First, we use the division property of congruences:

20x≡8(mod28)4⋅5x≡4⋅2(mod4⋅7)4⋅5x≡4⋅2(mod4⋅7)5x≡2(mod7)

Notice that in the new congruence, we have a1=5 and n1=7, where gcd(a1,n1)=gcd(5,7)=1. So, our congruence has a unique solution modulo 7.

To find the solution, we need to multiply both sides of the equation by the multiplicative inverse of 5(mod7). To find the inverse of 5(mod7), we compute the exponents of 5(mod7) until we get a result of 1(mod7):

52≡25≡3⋅7+4≡4(mod7)53≡4⋅5≡20≡2⋅7+6≡6(mod7)54≡6⋅5≡30≡4⋅7+2≡2(mod7)55≡2⋅5≡10≡1⋅7+3≡3(mod7)56≡3⋅5≡15≡2⋅7+1≡1(mod7)

So, 3⋅5≡1(mod7), which means that 3 is the multiplicative inverse of 5 (modulo 7).

Now, multiplying both sides of our congruence by 3, we get the following:

5x≡2(mod7)3⋅5x≡3⋅2(mod7)(3⋅5)x≡6(mod7)x≡6(mod7)

So, we get the solution x≡6(mod7).

But our original congruence was modulo 28 (not modulo 7). To find all the solutions modulo 28, we repeatedly add 7 modulo 28 until we reach the number 6 again:

x1≡6(mod28)x2≡6+7≡13(mod28)x3≡13+7≡20(mod28)x4≡20+7≡27(mod28)x5≡27+7≡34≡28+6≡6≡x1(mod28)

Therefore, the solutions are x1,2,3,4≡6,13,20,27(mod28).
