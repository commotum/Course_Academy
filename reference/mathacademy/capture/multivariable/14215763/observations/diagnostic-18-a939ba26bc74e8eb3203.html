To apply the ratio test, we define an=(x+5)nn(n+1). Then, we calculate the ratio of two successive terms and take the limit as n→∞:

L=limn→∞|an+1an|=limn→∞|(x+5)n+1(n+1)(n+2)⋅n(n+1)(x+5)n|=limn→∞|n(x+5)n+2|=|x+5|limn→∞nn+2=|x+5|limn→∞(1(1+2n))=|x+5|⋅1=|x+5|

The series converges if L<1. Therefore,

|x+5|<1−1<x+5<1−6<x<−4.

So, the radius of convergence is R=1, and the series converges for any x that lies in the open interval (−6,−4).

The ratio test gives no conclusion about the convergence of the series when L=1. So, when |x+5|=1, we do not know if the series converges. This means that the endpoints x=−6 and x=−4 need to be investigated separately using another test for convergence.

  • When x=−6 we have ∑n=1∞(x+5)nn(n+1)=∑n=1∞(−6+5)nn(n+1)=∑n=1∞(−1)nn2+n which is convergent by the alternating series test.

  • When x=−4 we have ∑n=1∞(x+5)nn(n+1)=∑n=1∞(−4+5)nn(n+1)=∑n=1∞1nn2+n=∑n=1∞1n2+n Comparing the above series with ∑n=1∞1n2, we see that the series is convergent by the comparison test.

So the interval of convergence is I=[−6,−4].
