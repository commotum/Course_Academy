First, let's recall the following facts about the "and" $(\land)$ and "or" $(\lor)$ logical connectives:

  • The statement $A \land B$ is true if both $A$ and $B$ are true.

  • The statement $A \lor B$ is true if either $A$ is true, or $B$ is true, or both $A$ and $B$ are true.

In our case, $P$ is true, $Q$ is true, and $R$ is true. Therefore, $Q \land R$ is true, and the result is true: \begin{align*} P \land (Q\land R) &\equiv\text{true} \land \overbrace{(\textrm{true} \land \textrm{true})}^{\textrm{true}} \\[5pt] &\equiv \textrm{true} \land \textrm{true} \\[3pt] &\equiv\textrm{true} \end{align*}
