First, let's recall the following facts about the "and" $(\land)$ and "or" $(\lor)$ logical connectives:

  • The statement $A \land B$ is true if both $A$ and $B$ are true.

  • The statement $A \lor B$ is true if either $A$ is true, or $B$ is true, or both $A$ and $B$ are true.

In our case, $P$ is true, $Q$ is false, and $R$ is false. Therefore, $Q \lor R$ is false, and the result is false: \begin{align*} P \land ( Q\lor R ) &\equiv\text{true} \land \overbrace{(\textrm{false} \lor \textrm{false})}^{\textrm{false}}\\[5pt] &\equiv \textrm{true} \land \textrm{false} \\[3pt] &\equiv \textrm{false} \end{align*}
