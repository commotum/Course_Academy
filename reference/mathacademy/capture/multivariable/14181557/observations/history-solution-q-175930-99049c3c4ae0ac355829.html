First, let's recall the following facts about the "and" $(\land)$ and "or" $(\lor)$ logical connectives:

  • The statement $A \land B$ is true if both $A$ and $B$ are true.

  • The statement $A \lor B$ is true if either $A$ is true, or $B$ is true, or both $A$ and $B$ are true.

In our case, $P$ is true, $Q$ is true, and $R$ is false. Therefore, $P \lor Q$ is true, but the result is false: \begin{align*} (P \lor Q)\land R &\equiv \overbrace{(\textrm{true} \lor \textrm{true})}^{\textrm{true}} \land \text{false} \\[5pt] &\equiv \textrm{true} \land \textrm{false} \\[3pt] &\equiv \textrm{false} \end{align*}
