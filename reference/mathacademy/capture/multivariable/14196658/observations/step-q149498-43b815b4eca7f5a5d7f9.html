Let f(x) be positive and strictly increasing for x∈[5,10], and differentiable for x∈(5,10). Suppose we approximate the area under the curve y=g(x)=x3⋅f(x) over the interval [5,10] using a right Riemann sum. Given that g(x)>0, which of the following statements must be true?

  1. f′(x)<0
  2. g′(x)<0
  3. The Riemann sum overestimates the true area.
a
III only
b
II only
c
I only
d
I and II only
e
II and III only

Correct
EXPLANATION

We're given that g(x)=x3⋅f(x)>0. Now, recall the following:

  • If g(x) is strictly increasing, the right Riemann sum gives an overestimate of the area under the curve y=g(x).

  • If g(x) is strictly decreasing, the right Riemann sum gives an underestimate of the area under the curve y=g(x).

Let's now consider each statement in turn:

  • Statement I is false. Since f(x) is strictly increasing, we have f′(x)>0.

  • Statement II is false. Differentiating g(x)=x3⋅f(x), we get g′(x)=(x3)′⋅f(x)+x3⋅f′(x)=3x2⋅f(x)+x3⋅f′(x). Now, since f(x) is positive and strictly increasing over the interval (5,10), we have 3x2>0,f(x)>0,x3>0,f′(x)>0. Hence, we obtain g′(x)=3x2⏟>0⋅f(x)⏟>0+x3⏟>0⋅f′(x)⏟>0=3x2⋅f(x)⏟>0+x3⋅f′(x)⏟>0>0.

  • Statement III is true. Since g′(x)>0, the function g(x) is strictly increasing. Therefore, the right Riemann sum gives an overestimate of the area under the curve y=x3⋅f(x).

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only."
