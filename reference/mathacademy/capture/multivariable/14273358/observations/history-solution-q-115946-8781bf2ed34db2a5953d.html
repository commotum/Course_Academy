Recall that critical points of y=f(x) occur when f′(x)=0 or f′(x) does not exist. In our case, there are two critical points:

x=−1 and x=1

Also, we have the following:

  • If f′(x)>0 then f(x) is increasing.

  • If f′(x)<0 then f(x) is decreasing.

We can now summarize the information from the graph in the table below.

Let's now sketch the graph of y=f(x):

  • First, we draw the critical points. Consider the vertical lines x=−1 and x=1.

    • We choose an arbitrary point on the vertical line x=1.

    • Since f(x) is increasing ↗ on (−1,1), the point at x=−1 must be lower than the point at x=1.

  • Next, we consider the behavior of y=f(x) in the neighborhood of the critical points:

    • Since f(x) is decreasing ↘ on (−∞,−1) and increasing ↗ on (−1,1), the graph of f(x) goes up to the left and to the right of x=−1.

    • Since f(x) is increasing ↗ on (−1,1) and decreasing ↘ on (1,∞), the graph of f(x) goes down to the left and to the right of x=1.

    • Furthermore, since f′(x) is increasing to the left of x=0, we must have f″(x)>0, and, since it is decreasing to the right of x=0, we must have f″(x)<0. So, x=0 is an inflection point at which the curve y=f(x) changes from concave up to concave down.
