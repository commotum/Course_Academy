Recall that critical points of y=f(x) occur when f′(x)=0 or f′(x) does not exist. In our case, there are two critical points:

x=−2 and x=2

Also, we have the following:

  • If f′(x)>0 then f(x) is increasing.

  • If f′(x)<0 then f(x) is decreasing.

We can now summarize the information from the graph in the table below.

Let's now sketch the graph of y=f(x):

  • First, we draw the critical points. We choose an arbitrary point on the vertical lines x=−2 and x=2. Since f(x) is decreasing ↘ on (−2,2), the point at x=−2 must be higher than the point at x=2, as shown below.

  • Next, we consider the behavior of y=f(x) in the neighborhood of the critical points:

    • Since f(x) is increasing ↗ on (−∞,−2) and is decreasing ↘ on (−2,2), the graph of y=f(x) goes down to the left and to the right of x=−2.

    • Since f(x) is decreasing ↘ on (−2,2) and is increasing ↗ on (2,∞), the graph of y=f(x) goes up to the left and to the right of x=2.
