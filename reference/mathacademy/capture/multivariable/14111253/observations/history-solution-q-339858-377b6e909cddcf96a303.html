For an initial value problem y′=f(x,y),y(a)=c, the trapezoidal method with step size Δx is given by Δy=12(y′+ynew′)⋅Δx, with ynew′=f(xnew,ynew) evaluated at the next x and y values.

First, note that we are given y(0) and asked to find y(2). The distance from x=0 to x=2 is 2, and since we are asked to use two steps of equal size, our step size must be Δx=2−02=1.

Now, let's proceed with the trapezoidal method. In general, Δy is given by

Δy=12(y′+ynew′)⋅Δx=12((x+y)+(xnew+ynew))⋅1=12(x+xnew+y)+12ynew.

We substitute this into ynew=y+Δy:

ynew=y+Δyynew=y+12(x+xnew+y)+12ynewynew=12(x+xnew+3y)+12ynew

Then, we solve for the general formula for ynew:

12ynew=12(x+xnew+3y)ynew=x+xnew+3y

We now proceed to calculate our approximations of y(1) and y(2).

Step 1.

From the initial conditions, we have x=0,y=0.

Therefore, the new x value is xnew=x+Δx=0+1=1.

Hence, the new y value is

ynew=x+xnew+3y=0+1+3⋅0=1.

Step 2.

From the previous step, we have x=1,y=1.

Therefore, the new x value is xnew=x+Δx=1+1=2.

Hence, the new y value is

ynew=x+xnew+3y=1+2+3⋅1=6.

Therefore, we conclude that y(1)≈1.00,y(2)≈6.00.
