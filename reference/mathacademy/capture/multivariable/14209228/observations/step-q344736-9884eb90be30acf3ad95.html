A small, circular puddle evaporates on a floor. If the puddle’s area is decreasing at a rate of 32πcm2/min, calculate the rate of change of its circumference at the moment when the radius is 8cm. Do not round the answer.
The circumference of the puddle is decreasing at a rate of
4π
cm/min.

Correct
EXPLANATION

In this situation, we want to find dCdt at the moment when r=8, and we are also told that dAdt=−32π.

To start, let's set up an equation relating the area A and the circumference C of the circle. Since C=2πr and A=πr2, the relationship between A and C is

A=πr2=π(C2π)2=C24π.

Differentiating this relationship with respect to t using implicit differentiation, we get

ddt(A)=ddt(C24π)dAdt=dCdt⋅ddC(C24π)dAdt=dCdt⋅C2π.

When r=8cm, C=2π(8)=16π cm. Hence, substituting dAdt=−32π and C=16π, and solving for dCdt. We get

−32π=dCdt⋅16π2π−32π=dCdt⋅8dCdt=−4π.

We conclude that the circumference is decreasing at a rate of 4π cm/min.
