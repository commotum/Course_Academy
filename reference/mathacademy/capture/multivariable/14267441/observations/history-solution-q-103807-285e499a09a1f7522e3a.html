We have a vertical tangent when

dydx=y′(θ)x′(θ)

is infinite. This means that

x′(θ)=0andy′(θ)≠0.

To find the vertical tangents, we proceed as follows:

Step 1: Compute the derivatives y′(θ) and x′(θ).

We're given that

x′(θ)=−e−θ(cos⁡θ+sin⁡θ),y′(θ)=e−θ(cos⁡θ−sin⁡θ).

Step 2: Find the values of θ where x′(θ)=0:

By writing down and solving the equation, we get

−e−θ(cos⁡θ+sin⁡θ)=0cos⁡θ+sin⁡θ=0sin⁡θ=−cos⁡θtan⁡θ=−1θ=3π4+πn

where n is any integer. However, among the solutions above, only one belongs to the required domain, namely

θ1=3π4.

Step 3: Check to see whether our candidate has y′(θ)=0.

We substitute our candidate solution into y′(θ) and check that y′(θ)≠0. If y′(θ)=0, further investigation is necessary.

  • Substituting θ1=3π4, we get y′(3π4)=e−π/4(cos⁡(3π4)−sin⁡(3π4))=−e−π/42≠0.✓

Therefore, our curve has a vertical tangent at θ=3π4.
