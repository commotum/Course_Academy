The system can be rewritten in matrix form as x′=\boldsymbolf(x) where x=[x(t)y(t)] and \boldsymbolf(x)=[x2+y−1ln⁡(x+3)−y−(ln⁡3−1)].

We need to compute the Jacobian matrix (or the total derivative) of \boldsymbolf(x).

In our case, we have the following:

\boldsymbolf′(x)=[∂f1∂x∂f1∂y∂f2∂x∂f2∂y]

First, we compute the partial derivatives:

∂f1∂x=∂∂x(x2+y−1)=2x∂f1∂y=∂∂y(x2+y−1)=1∂f2∂x=∂∂x(ln⁡(x+3)−y−(ln⁡3−1))=1x+3∂f2∂y=∂∂y(ln⁡(x+3)−y−(ln⁡3−1))=−1

Thus, we have

\boldsymbolf′(x)=[2x11x+3−1].

Now, we evaluate the Jacobian at the equilibrium x∗=[01] to get

\boldsymbolf′(x∗)=[0113−1].

Notice that the matrix is invertible (its determinant is not zero), so the system is almost linear at x∗.

Finally, since we have two real eigenvalues with opposite signs, our equilibrium is an unstable saddle.
