EXPLANATION

Let's denote f1(x,y)=3x(1−2x)−xy and f2(x,y)=−3y+4xy.

Now, we compute the partial derivatives:

∂f1∂x=∂∂x(3x(1−2x)−xy)=3−12x−y,∂f1∂y=∂∂y(3x(1−2x)−xy)=−x∂f2∂x=∂∂x(−3y+4xy)=4y,∂f2∂y=∂∂y(−3y+4xy)=−3+4x

Thus, the Jacobian matrix is J=[3−12x−y−x4y−3+4x].

Next, we evaluate the Jacobian at the equilibrium x∗=[0.50] to get

J(x∗)=[3−12(0.5)−0−0.54(0)−3+4(0.5)]=[−3−0.50−1].

The eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix are λ1=−3 and λ2=−1. Since the eigenvalues are real and both negative, our equilibrium is an asymptotically stable sink.
