EXPLANATION

Since f(x,y) is continuous on the closed, bounded region D, the function attains a global maximum and minimum on D.

Step 1. Find the critical points inside the region D.

To determine the critical points, let's first sketch the region:

Since the point (0,−3) is in the interior of D, we include it in the list of candidates for extrema.

Step 2. Find the points on the boundary that could give rise to extreme values.

In this case, we are given the points on the boundary of our region that could potentially be global extrema for f. These are

(−2,−4),(−1,−1),(1,−1),(2,−4).

Step 3. Evaluate f(x,y) at each of the candidate points, and compare their values.

Evaluating f(x,y) at each of the points mentioned above, we get the following:

f(0,−3)=2(0)(−3)+6(0)−3=−3f(−2,−4)=2(−2)(−4)+6(−2)−3=1f(−1,−1)=2(−1)(−1)+6(−1)−3=−7f(1,−1)=2(1)(−1)+6(1)−3=1f(2,−4)=2(2)(−4)+6(2)−3=−7

Therefore, on the closed region D, we have that

  • f attains the global minimum of −7 at the points (−1,−1) and (2,−4),

  • f attains the global maximum of 1 at the points (−2,−4) and (1,−1).
