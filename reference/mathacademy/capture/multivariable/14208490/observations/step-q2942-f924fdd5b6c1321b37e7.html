The length of one leg of a right triangle increases at the rate of 3 m/s, while the length of the second leg is decreasing at the rate of 2 m/s. How fast is the length of the hypotenuse changing when the length of the increasing leg is 12 m and the length of the decreasing leg is 16 m?

15 m/s

EXPLANATION

We start by drawing a diagram of the problem.

In the diagram,

  • x is the length of the increasing side,

  • y is the length of the decreasing side,

  • z is the length of the hypotenuse.

As shown in the diagram, x, y, and z form a right triangle with z as the hypotenuse. We wish to find the rate of change of z, which is dzdt.

We know that x=12m and y=16m. We can find z using the Pythagorean Theorem, as follows:

x2+y2=z2122+162=z2400=z2z=20m

We also know that:

  • dxdt=3m/s when x=12m and y=16m,

  • dydt=−2m/s when x=12m and y=16m.

Finally, we differentiate both sides of x2+y2=z2 with respect to t and then substitute in the known values, as follows:

ddt(x2+y2)=ddt(z2)2xdxdt+2ydydt=2zdzdt2(12)(3)+2(16)(−2)=2(20)dzdt8=40dzdtdzdt=840dzdt=15

Therefore, the length of the hypotenuse is changing at a rate of 15m/s.
