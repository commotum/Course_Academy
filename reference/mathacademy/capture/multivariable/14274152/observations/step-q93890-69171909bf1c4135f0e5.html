EXPLANATION

We have the linear Diophantine equation

ax+by=c where a=3, b=11, and c=7. We're given that the equation has solutions.

Notice that our equation can't be solved for either of the two variables using integer coefficients. So, let's find the linear representation of gcd(3,11) using the extended Euclidean algorithm. First, we apply the forward reduction:

11=3⋅3+2↙↙3=2⋅1+1

Solving for the remainders (the rightmost terms) in the equations above, we get

2=11−91=3−2⋅1.

Then, we back-substitute:

1=3−2⋅1=3−(11−9)=3⋅4+11⋅(−1).

As a result, we obtain that d=gcd(a,b)=1 and

3⋅4+11⋅(−1)=1.

Multiplying this equation by 7, we get

3⋅4+11⋅(−1)=13⋅4⋅7+11⋅(−1)⋅7=73⋅28+11⋅(−7)=7,

which means that (x,y)=(28,−7) is a solution to our Diophantine equation.

Now, the general solution is

(28+bdt,−7−adt)=(28+11t,−7−3t),

where t=0,±1,±2,….

The smallest possible positive value of x=28+11t is attained when t=−2. Therefore,

x∼=28+11⋅(−2)=6.
