EXPLANATION

Suppose that X1,X2,…,Xn is a random sample of size n from a population with a population mean μ and population standard deviation σ. The central limit theorem (or CLT) states that for sufficiently large n, the distribution of the sample mean X― can be approximated as X―∼N(μ,σ2n).

In our case, we have n=121,μ=300,σ=55.

Therefore, by the CLT, we have that the distribution of the sample mean can be approximated as X―∼N(μ,σ2n)∼N(300,552121)∼N(300,25).

We want to find P(X―<295). So, we convert X― to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring: P(X―<295)=P(Z<295−30025)=P(Z<−55)=P(Z<−1)=Φ(−1)

From the table, we have Φ(−1)=0.1587.

Therefore, P(X―<295)=Φ(−1)=0.1587.
