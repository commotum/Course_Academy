Let us consider the squared distance S between (4,0) and a general point P(x,y): S=(x−4)2+(y−0)2

We require that the point P lies on the curve y=6x. Substituting this into the above gives

S=(x−4)2+(y−0)2=(x−4)2+(6x)2=x2−8x+16+6x=x2−2x+16.

To find the minimum distance, we take the first derivative of S(x) and equate it to zero. This gives the following: S′(x)=2x−2=02x=2x=22x=1

We can see that S″(x)=2>0. Therefore, x=1 is a minimum of S.

Since S gives the squared distance between the two points, the actual distance d between the two points when x=1 is given by d=S(1).

Computing this quantity, we get

d=S(1)=12−2(1)+16=1−2+16=15.
