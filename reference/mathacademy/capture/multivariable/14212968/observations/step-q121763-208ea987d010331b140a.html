EXPLANATION

In the figure, y is the kite's height, and x is the horizontal distance between Greta and the kite. The quantity y=150 inches does not change with time because the kite height remains the same.

We wish to calculate dθdt. To do this, let's start by setting up a relation between x and y. Relative to the angle θ, the sides x and y are the adjacent and opposite, respectively. So, we have

tan⁡θ=yxtan⁡θ=150xx=150cot⁡θ.

Now, we differentiate both sides of the equation with respect to t using implicit differentiation, and we get

ddt(x)=ddt(150cot⁡θ)dxdt=(−150csc2⁡θ)dθdt.

From the given information, we have dxdt=−10. Substituting this into the above, we reach

−10=(−150csc2⁡θ)dθdt115=(csc2⁡θ)dθdtdθdt=sin2⁡θ15.

Therefore, the rate at which the angle between the kite string and the horizontal is increasing is sin2⁡θ15 radians per second.
