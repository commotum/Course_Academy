A 13ft ladder leans against the side of a building, forming an angle θ with the ground. Given that the foot of the ladder F is being pulled away from the building at the rate of 0.5ft/s, at what rate is θ decreasing at the moment when the top of the ladder T is 12ft above the ground?

a
112rad/s
b
14rad/s
c
124rad/s
d
113rad/s
e
18rad/s

Correct
EXPLANATION

In the figure, x is the horizontal distance from the foot of the ladder to the wall of the building, y is the vertical distance between the top of the ladder and the ground, and z is the length of the ladder. The quantity z=13 feet does not change with time because the ladder's length doesn't change.

We wish to calculate dθdt when y=12 feet. To do this, let's start by setting up a relation between x and y. Relative to the angle θ, the sides x and z are the adjacent and hypotenuse, respectively. So, we have

cos⁡θ=xzcos⁡θ=x13x=13cos⁡θ.

Now, we differentiate both sides of the equation with respect to t using implicit differentiation, and we get

ddt(x)=ddt(13cos⁡θ)dxdt=(−13sin⁡θ)dθdt.

From the given information, we have dxdt=0.5. Substituting this into the above, we reach

0.5=(−13sin⁡θ)dθdt−126=sin⁡θdθdtdθdt=−126csc⁡θ.

Relative to the angle θ, the sides y and z are the opposite and hypotenuse, respectively. So, we have

sin⁡θ=yzsin⁡θ=1213csc⁡θ=1312.

Therefore,

dθdt=−126⋅1312=−124.

So, the angle θ is decreasing at a rate of 124 radians per second.
