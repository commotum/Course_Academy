The region enclosed by the curves y=x3 and y=x in the first quadrant is rotated 2π radians about the line y=2. Which of the following gives the volume of the resulting solid?

a
π∫01([2−x3]2−[2−x]2)dx
b
π∫01(x−x3)dx
c
π∫−11([2−x]2−[2−x3]2)dx
d
π∫01([2−x3]2−x2)dx
e
π∫−11(x3−x)dx
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

First, we want to determine the limits of integration. We find the intersections of the curves by solving the following equation:

x3=xx3−x=0x(x2−1)=0x=−1,0,1 Hence, our limits of integration are from x=0 to x=1.

Now, let's draw our vertical arrow through the given region. The arrow should be pointing away from the axis of rotation.

The vertical arrow

  • enters the region through the inner curve y=x, and

  • leaves through the outer curve y=x3.

Next, we can find the volume of our solid as the difference between the volumes of the solids generated by the outer and inner curves.

  • Step I. Figure out the volume generated by the outer curve y=x3.

The corresponding volume is given by Vouter=π∫01[(upper)−(lower)]2dx=π∫01[(2)−(x3)]2dx=π∫01[2−x3]2dx.

  • Step II. Figure out the volume generated by the inner curve y=x.

The corresponding volume is given by Vinner=π∫01[(upper)−(lower)]2dx=π∫01[(2)−(x)]2dx=π∫01[2−x]2dx.

  • Step III. Find the difference.

Finally, we can write the volume of the original solid as V=Vouter−Vinner=π∫01[2−x3]2dx−π∫01[2−x]2dx=π∫01([2−x3]2−[2−x]2)dx.
