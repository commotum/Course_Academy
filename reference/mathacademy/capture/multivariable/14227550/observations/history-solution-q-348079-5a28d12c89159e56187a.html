We have a horizontal tangent when dydx=y′(θ)x′(θ)=0 which is equivalent to y′(θ)=0andx′(θ)≠0.

To find the horizontal tangents, we proceed as follows:

Step 1: Compute the derivatives y′(θ) and x′(θ).

We're given that x′(θ)=−sin⁡θ−2sin⁡θcos⁡θ,y′(θ)=2cos2⁡θ+cos⁡θ−1.

Step 2: Find the values of θ where y′(θ)=0:

By writing down and solving the equation, we get 2cos2⁡θ+cos⁡θ−1=0(2cos⁡θ−1)(cos⁡θ+1)=0.

Therefore, by the zero-product property, we have to solve two equations: 2cos⁡θ−1=0,cos⁡θ+1=0

Let's solve each equation separately.

  • First, we solve the equation 2cos⁡θ−1=0,0≤θ<π. The solution to this equation is θ1=π3.

  • Then, we solve the equation cos⁡θ+1=0,0≤θ<π. There are no solutions to this equation for 0≤θ<π.

Step 3: Check to see whether any candidates have x′(θ)=0.

We substitute our candidate solutions into x′(θ) and check that x′(θ)≠0. If x′(θ)=0, further investigation is necessary.

  • Substituting θ1=π3, we get x′(π3)=−sin⁡(π3)−2sin⁡(π3)cos⁡(π3)=−32−2⋅32⋅12=−32−32=−3≠0.✓ So, θ1=π3 is a valid solution.

Therefore, our curve has a horizontal tangent at θ=π3.
