EXPLANATION

We have a horizontal tangent when

dydx=y′(θ)x′(θ)=0

which is equivalent to

y′(θ)=0andx′(θ)≠0.

To find the horizontal tangents, we proceed as follows:

Step 1: Compute the derivatives y′(θ) and x′(θ).

We're given that x′(θ)=2eθ(cos⁡θ−sin⁡θ),y′(θ)=2eθ(cos⁡θ+sin⁡θ).

Step 2: Find the values of θ where y′(θ)=0.

By writing down and solving the equation, we get 2eθ(sin⁡θ+cos⁡θ)=0sin⁡θ+cos⁡θ=0sin⁡θ=−cos⁡θtan⁡θ=−1θ=−π4+πn where n is any integer. However, among the solutions above, only one belongs to the required domain, namely θ1=3π4.

Step 3: Check to see whether our candidate has x′(θ)=0.

We substitute our candidate solution into x′(θ) and check that x′(θ)≠0. If x′(θ)=0, further investigation is necessary.

  • Substituting θ1=3π4, we get x′(3π4)=2e(3π/4)(cos⁡(3π4)−sin⁡(3π4))=−22e(3π/4)≠0.✓ Therefore, θ1=3π4 is a valid solution.

Therefore, our curve has a horizontal tangent at θ=3π4.
