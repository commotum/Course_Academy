The local truncation error (LTE) is the error made in a single step of a numerical method, when the step begins from an exact value. The LTE of a numerical method at step n from x with exact value yexact(x) to xnew=x+Δx is τn=yexact(xnew)−ynew=yexact(xnew)−(yexact(x)+Δy).

We find the LTE at each step, rounding to three decimal places in each case:

  • After the first step, we have ynew=0.970. So, the LTE of the first step is τ1=yexact(0.25)−0.970=cos⁡(0.25)−0.970≈−0.001.

  • After the second step, we have ynew=0.880. So, the LTE of the second step is τ2=yexact(0.5)−0.880=cos⁡(0.5)−0.880≈−0.002.

  • After the third step, we have ynew=0.735. So, the LTE of the third step is τ3=yexact(0.75)−0.735=cos⁡(0.75)−0.735≈−0.003.

Therefore, the completed table is as given below.

n x y τn
0 0 1.000 0
1 0.25 0.970 −0.001
2 0.5 0.880 −0.002
