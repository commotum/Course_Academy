An initial value problem has the exact solution yexact(x)=e0.3x. A numerical method is used to approximate the solution to the initial value problem, resulting in the table below, where y denotes the approximate solution at each step, and τn is the local truncation error at each step. At each step, the exact value is used to approximate the new value.

Calculate the local truncation error at each step. Round your answers to three decimal places.

EXPLANATION

The local truncation error (LTE) is the error made in a single step of a numerical method, when the step begins from an exact value. The LTE of a numerical method at step n from x with exact value yexact(x) to xnew=x+Δx is τn=yexact(xnew)−ynew=yexact(xnew)−(yexact(x)+Δy).

We find the LTE at each step, rounding to three decimal places in each case:

  • After the first step, we have xnew=0.19 and ynew=1.052. So, the LTE of the first step is τ1=yexact(0.19)−1.052=e0.3⋅0.19−1.052≈0.007.

  • After the second step, we have xnew=0.38 and ynew=1.123. So, the LTE of the second step is τ2=yexact(0.38)−1.123=e0.3⋅0.38−1.123≈−0.002.

  • After the third step, we have xnew=0.57 and ynew=1.181. So, the LTE of the third step is τ3=yexact(0.57)−1.181=e0.3⋅0.57−1.181≈0.005.
