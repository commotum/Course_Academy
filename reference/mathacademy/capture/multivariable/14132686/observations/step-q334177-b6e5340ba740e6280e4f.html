Consider the following initial value problem: y′=2x+4,y(0)=0 Suppose we wish to approximate the solution using the following numerical method: Δy=13(x2+y′)⋅Δxynew=y+Δy Given that the exact solution is yexact(x)=x2+4x, compute the local truncation error, τ6, for the step starting at x=1 with step size Δx=0.2. Round your answer to three decimal places.
τ6≈
0.773

Correct
EXPLANATION

The local truncation error (LTE) is the error made in a single step of a numerical method, when the step begins from an exact value. The LTE of a numerical method at step n from x with exact value yexact(x) to xnew=x+Δx is τn=yexact(xnew)−ynew=yexact(xnew)−(yexact(x)+Δy).

We're given that the exact solution is yexact(x)=x2+4x. So, the y-value at the start of the step x=1 is yexact(1)=5.

Now, let's find the y-increment given by the numerical method.

First, the slope at the start of the step is yexact′(1)=2(1)+4=2+4=6.

So, the increment at this step is

Δy=13(x2+yexact′(1))⋅Δx=13(1+6)⋅0.2≈0.4667.

Next, we compute the exact value at xnew:

yexact(xnew)=yexact(x+Δx)=yexact(1+0.2)=yexact(1.2)=(1.2)2+4(1.2)=6.24

Finally, we calculate the LTE for the step starting at x=1:

τ6=yexact(1.2)−(yexact(1)+Δy)=6.24−(5+0.4667)≈0.773
