The volume of a cone is increasing at a rate of 34πm3/s. The diameter of the cone is always one third of its height. Find the rate of the cone's height increase when the height is 6m. Do not round the answer.

EXPLANATION

Let's call h the height of the cone and r the radius of its base. Both of these quantities, as well as the volume v, change with time. In particular, we know that dvdt=34πm3/s, and we need to find dhdt when h=6m.

We need to find a relation between h and the variable for which we know the rate of change, which is v. This is given by the formula for the volume of a cone: v=13πr2h

Before we differentiate the equation with respect to time, note that r also appears in the formula. So, we need to simplify the formula by writing r in terms of h.

We're told that the diameter is one third of the height. So, we have d=13h2r=13hr=16h.

Therefore, we can write the volume formula as v=13π(16h)2h=1108πh3.

Now that we have an equation relating v and h, we differentiate with respect to time using the chain rule. This gives dvdt=136πh2(dhdt).

Finally, substituting the known values and solving for dhdt, we get 34π=136π(6)2(dhdt)dhdt=34.

Thus, the cone's height is increasing at a rate of 34 m/s.
