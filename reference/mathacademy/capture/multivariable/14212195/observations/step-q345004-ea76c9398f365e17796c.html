Water is leaking out of a cone-shaped container so that the volume of the water decreases at a rate of 12in3/min. The height of the container is three times its base radius. Find the rate of decrease of the radius of the water's surface when the radius is 6in. Do not round the answer.

EXPLANATION

Let's call h the height of the conical volume of water and r be the radius of its surface. Both of these quantities, as well as the water's volume v, change with time. In particular, we know that dvdt=−12in3/min, and we want to find drdt when r=6in.

We need to find a relation between v and the variable for which we want the rate of change, which is r. This is given by the formula for the volume of a cone: v=13πr2h

Before we differentiate the equation with respect to time, note that h also appears in the formula. So, we need to simplify the formula by writing h in terms of r.

We're told that the height is always three times its base radius. So, we have h=3r.

Therefore, we can write the volume formula as v=13πr2h=13πr2(3r)=πr3.

Now that we have an equation relating v and r, we differentiate with respect to time using the chain rule: ddtv=ddt(πr3)=3πr2drdt

Finally, substituting the known values and solving for drdt, we get −12=3π(6)2drdt−12=108πdrdtdrdt=−19πin/min.

Therefore, the radius of the water's surface decreases at a rate of 19πin/min.
