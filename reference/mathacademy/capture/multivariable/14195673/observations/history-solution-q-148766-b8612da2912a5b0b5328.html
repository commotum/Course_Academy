We're given that g(x)=x⋅f(x)>0. Now, recall the following:

  • If g(x) is strictly increasing, the left Riemann sum gives an underestimate of the area under the curve y=g(x).

  • If g(x) is strictly decreasing, the left Riemann sum gives an overestimate of the area under the curve y=g(x).

Let's now consider each statement in turn:

  • Statement I is true. Since f(x) is strictly increasing, we have f′(x)>0.

  • Statement II is true. Differentiating g(x)=x⋅f(x), we get g′(x)=(x)′⋅f(x)+x⋅f′(x)=f(x)+x⋅f′(x). Now, since f(x) is positive and strictly increasing over the interval (1,4), we have x>0,f(x)>0,f′(x)>0. Hence, we obtain g′(x)=f(x)⏟>0+x⏟>0⋅f′(x)⏟>0=f(x)⏟>0+x⋅f′(x)⏟>0>0.

  • Statement III is true. Since g′(x)>0, the function g(x) is strictly increasing. Therefore, the left Riemann sum gives an underestimate of the area under the curve y=x⋅f(x).

Therefore, the correct answer is "I, II, and III."
