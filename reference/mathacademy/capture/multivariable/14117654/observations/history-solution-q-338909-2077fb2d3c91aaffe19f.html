We consider the cases λ=0 and λ≠0 separately, since they correspond to different solutions.

Case 1: First, we consider λ=0.

Setting λ=0 gives the equation y″=0. Integrating this equation gives y=Ax+B. Applying the conditions y′(0)=y′(π)=0 yields A=0. Thus, we have a nontrivial solution, so λ=0 is a parameter value corresponding to the eigenvalue.

Case 2: Next, we consider λ>0.

To find the fundamental solutions, we set y=emx. The auxiliary equation is m2+16λ2=0. So, we have m=±4iλ. So, the fundamental solutions are y1(x)=cos⁡(4λx),y2(x)=sin⁡(4λx) and the general solution is y=Acos⁡(4λx)+Bsin⁡(4λx).

Differentiating gives y′=−4Aλsin⁡(4λx)+4Bλcos⁡(4λx).

Applying the boundary condition y′(0)=0 yields −4Aλsin⁡(0)+4Bλcos⁡(0)=0 which gives B=0. So, we have y=Acos⁡(4λx). Applying the boundary condition y′(π)=0 yields −4Aλsin⁡(4λπ)=0. For nontrivial solutions, we require 4λπ=nπ for n∈N. This gives the parameter values corresponding to the eigenvalues: λn=n4. The corresponding eigenfunctions are yn(x)=Bncos⁡(4λnx),Bn∈R.
