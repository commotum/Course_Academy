In a plumbing store, the length of a water pipe is normally distributed with a mean of 2m and a standard deviation of 0.05m. If Carl bought 25 pipes, what is the probability that the total length of the pipes he purchased is more than 49.8m and less 50.2m? It can be assumed that the lengths of pipes are mutually independent.

EXPLANATION

Let Xi denote the length in the ith pipe. Then, we have

Xi∼N(2,0.052),1≤i≤25.

Let the random variable L denote the total length of 25 randomly selected pipes. Then, since

L=X1+X2+⋯+X25,

we have that L is normally distributed, where

E[L]=25⋅2=50,Var[L]=25⋅0.052=0.0625.

Therefore,

L∼N(50,0.0625).

In order to find P(49.8<L<50.2), we convert L to a standard normal random variable by z-scoring: P(49.8<L<50.2)=P(49.8−500.0625<Z<50.2−500.0625)≈P(−0.8<Z<0.8)=1−2P(Z>0.8)=1−2[1−P(Z<0.8)]=2P(Z<0.8)−1=2Φ(0.8)−1

Using the z-table, we see that

Φ(0.8)≈0.7881.

Therefore,

P(49.8<L<50.2)≈2Φ(0.8)−1≈2⋅0.7881−1≈0.5762.
