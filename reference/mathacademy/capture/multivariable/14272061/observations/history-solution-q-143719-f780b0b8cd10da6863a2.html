When a polar curve is differentiable at a point θ0, there is a horizontal tangent at θ0 if

dydx=y′(θ0)x′(θ0)=0

which is equivalent to

y′(θ0)=0andx′(θ0)≠0.

However, if the curve is continuous at θ0 yet the derivative y′(x) takes the indeterminate form 00 at this point, we say that the curve has a horizontal tangent at this point if

limθ→θ0dydx=0.

In this case, we're told that x′(3π2)=0.

With that in mind, let's examine our statements in turn.

  • Statement I is false. Substituting θ=3π2 into the expression for y′(θ), we get y′(3π2)=sin⁡(2⋅3π2)cos⁡(2⋅3π2)=sin⁡(3π)cos⁡(3π)=0.

  • Statement II is false. Computing the limit as θ→3π2, we obtain limθ→3π/2dydx=limθ→3π/2y′(θ)x′(θ)=limθ→3π/2(sin⁡(2θ)cos⁡(2θ)cos2⁡θ(cos2⁡θ−3sin2⁡θ))=limθ→3π/2(2sin⁡(θ)cos⁡(θ)cos⁡(2θ)cos2⁡θ(cos2⁡θ−3sin2⁡θ))=limθ→3π/2(2sin⁡(θ)cos⁡(θ)cos⁡(2θ)cos2⁡θ(cos2⁡θ−3sin2⁡θ))=limθ→3π/2(2sin⁡(θ)cos⁡(2θ)cos⁡θ(cos2⁡θ−3sin2⁡θ))=2sin⁡(3π2)cos⁡(2⋅3π2)cos⁡(3π2)(cos2⁡(3π2)−3sin2⁡(3π2))=2⋅(−1)⋅(−1)0⋅(0−3⋅1)=20≠0.

  • Statement III is false. Since the limit in part II does not equal to 0, there curve does not have a horizontal tangent at θ=3π2. In fact, it is a vertical tangent.

Therefore, the correct answer is "None of the statements are true."
