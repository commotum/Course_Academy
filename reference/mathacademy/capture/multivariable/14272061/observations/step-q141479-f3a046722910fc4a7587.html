  1. x′(0)=0
  2. limθ→0dydx=0
  3. The curve has a horizontal tangent at θ=0

EXPLANATION

When a polar curve is differentiable at a point θ0, there is a horizontal tangent at θ0 if

dydx=y′(θ0)x′(θ0)=0

which is equivalent to

y′(θ0)=0andx′(θ0)≠0.

However, if the curve is continuous at θ0 yet the derivative y′(x) takes the indeterminate form 00 at this point, we say that the curve has a horizontal tangent at this point if

limθ→θ0dydx=0.

In this case, we're told that y′(0)=0.

With that in mind, let's examine our statements in turn.

  • Statement I is true. Substituting θ=0 into the expression for x′(θ), we get x′(0)=2sin⁡(0)cos2⁡(0)−sin3⁡(0)=2⋅0⋅1−0=0.

  • Statement II is true. Computing the limit, we obtain limθ→0dydx=limθ→0y′(θ)x′(θ)=limθ→0(3sin2⁡θcos⁡θ2sin⁡θcos2⁡θ−sin3⁡θ)=limθ→0(sin⁡θ⋅3sin⁡θcos⁡θsin⁡θ⋅(2cos2⁡θ−sin2⁡θ))=limθ→0(sin⁡θ⋅3sin⁡θcos⁡θsin⁡θ⋅(2cos2⁡θ−sin2⁡θ))=limθ→0(3sin⁡θcos⁡θ2cos2⁡θ−sin2⁡θ)=3sin⁡(0)cos⁡(0)2cos2⁡(0)−sin2⁡(0)=3⋅0⋅12⋅1−0=02=0.

  • Statement III is true. Since the limit in part II equals 0, there curve has a horizontal tangent at θ=0.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I, II, and III."
