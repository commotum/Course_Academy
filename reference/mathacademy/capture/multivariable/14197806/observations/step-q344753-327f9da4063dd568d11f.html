Since g is a continuous function on [−3,4], we can apply the Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT) in each subinterval of [−3,4].

We will go through each subinterval of [−3,4] and check if the IVT guarantees a solution to the equation g(x)=1 on this interval.

  • On the subinterval [−3,−2], we have g(−3)=−4 and g(−2)=3. Since 1∈[−4,3], the IVT does guarantee a solution to g(x)=1 on this subinterval.

  • On the subinterval [−2,−1], we have g(−2)=3 and g(−1)=0. Since 1∈[0,3], the IVT does guarantee a solution to g(x)=1 on this subinterval.

  • On the subinterval [−1,1], we have g(−1)=0 and g(1)=−2. Since 1∉[−2,0], the IVT does not guarantee a solution to g(x)=1 on this subinterval.

  • On the subinterval [1,3], we have g(1)=−2 and g(3)=−5. Since 1∉[−5,−2], the IVT does not guarantee a solution to g(x)=1 on this subinterval.

  • On the subinterval [3,4], we have g(3)=−5 and g(4)=−1. Since 1∉[−5,−1], the IVT does not guarantee a solution to g(x)=1 on this subinterval.

The IVT guarantees solutions to g(x)=1 on the subintervals [−3,−2] and [−2,−1].

Therefore, g(x)=1 has at least 2 solutions.
