At each point P on a particular curve, the derivative y′(x) is inversely proportional to the square of the slope of the line through P and the origin. The derivative at the point (−2,3) equals 4. What differential equation describes this curve?

a
y′(x)=9x2y2
b
y′(x)=3x2y2
c
y′(x)=9xy
d
y′(x)=3x2y2
e
y′(x)=3y2x2
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

Let's highlight the important words in the given statement:

At each point P on a particular curve, the derivative y′(x) is inversely proportional to the square of the slope of the line through P and the origin.

We note the following:

  • The slope of the line through P(x,y) and the origin is given by yx.

  • Since the derivative is inversely proportional to the square of the slope of the line through P and the origin, we have y′(x)=k⋅[(yx)2]−1=k⋅(xy)2 where k is a constant.

So, our differential equation is

y′(x)=kx2y2.

In addition, we're told that y′(x)=4 when at the point (−2,3). We can use this to determine the value of the coefficient k:

4=k(−2)232⟹k=9

Finally, the differential equation describing the curve is y′(x)=9x2y2.
