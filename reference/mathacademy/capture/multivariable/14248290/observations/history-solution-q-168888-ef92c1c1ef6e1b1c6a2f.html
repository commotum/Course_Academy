A function f(x,y) has a local minimum point at (a,b) if f(x,y)≥f(a,b) for all points (x,y) in a neighborhood of (a,b). In other words, we can take a neighborhood of (x,y)-values such that the point (x,y,f(x,y)) is the lowest point on the graph in the neighborhood. Among the given options, such neighborhoods exist for the point C. Hence, C is a local minimum.
