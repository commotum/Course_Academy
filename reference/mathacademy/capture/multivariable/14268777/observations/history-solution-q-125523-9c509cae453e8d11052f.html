First, since we are rotating about a vertical line, we need to solve the equation of our second curve for x:

(x+2)2−7y2=8⟹x=7y2+8−2

Note that we take the positive root since x≥0.

Now, we want to determine the limits of integration. We find the intersections of the curves by solving the following equation:

y2=7y2+8−2y2+2=7y2+8(y2+2)2=7y2+8y4+4y2+4=7y2+8y4−3y2−4=0(y2−4)(y2+1)=0(y−2)(y+2)(y2+1)=0 Hence, our limits of integration are from y=−2 to y=2.

Now, let's draw a horizontal arrow through the given region. The arrow should be pointing away from the axis of rotation.

The horizontal arrow

  • enters the region through the inner curve x=y2, and

  • leaves through the outer curve x=7y2+8−2.

Next, we can find the volume of our solid as the difference between the volumes of the solids generated by the outer and inner curves.

  • Step I. Figure out the volume generated by the outer curve x=7y2+8−2.

The corresponding volume is given by Vouter=π∫−22[(right)−(left)]2dy=π∫−22[(7y2+8−2)−(−2)]2dy=π∫−22[7y2+8]2dy.

  • Step II. Figure out the volume generated by the inner curve x=y2.

The corresponding volume is given by Vinner=π∫−22[(right)−(left)]2dy=π∫−22[(y2)−(−2)]2dy=π∫−22[y2+2]2dy.

  • Step III. Find the difference.

Finally, we can write the volume of the original solid as V=Vouter−Vinner=π∫−22[7y2+8]2dy−π∫−22[y2+2]2dy=π∫−22(7y2+8−(y2+2)2)dy.
