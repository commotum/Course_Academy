Harry shines a flashlight on a 10in tall cat approaching him. If he is holding the flashlight 30in above the ground and the animal's shadow is changing at a rate of 11in/s, how fast is the cat approaching Harry?

22in/s

EXPLANATION

Let's draw a figure according to the given data.

In the figure, x is the distance between Harry and the cat, while y is the length of the cat's shadow. Both x and y change with time. In particular, we know that the velocity of the cat's shadow dydt=−11in/s.

Notice that the velocity of the cat's shadow is negative because the cat is walking toward Harry, and therefore the distance between Harry and the cat decreases with time.

Since we want to calculate dxdt, we need to find a relation between x and y. Since the two triangles are similar, the ratios between the corresponding sides are equal, and we have x+y30=y1010x+10y=30y10x=20yx=2y.

We differentiate both sides of the above equation with respect to t using the chain rule, and we get ddt(x)=ddt(2y)dxdt=2⋅dydt.

Finally, substituting dydt=−11in/s into the above gives dxdt=2(−11)=−22in/s.

Therefore, the speed of the cat is 22in/s.
