EXPLANATION

We say that an implication has a vacuous proof if the antecedent is always false: P⏞Always false⇒Q

It does not matter whether the consequent is true or false.

With that in mind, let's examine our antecedents in turn.

  • Predicate I, defined for x∈R, is always true. ×

  • Predicate II, defined for x∈R, is not always false. ×

  • Statement III is always false. ✓

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only."
