EXPLANATION

To prove a statement using induction, we use the following framework:

  • Step1: Base Case

  • Step 2: Inductive Hypothesis

  • Step 3: Inductive Step

  • Step 4: Conclusion

In this case, the general statement P(n) we're required to prove is the following:

1+3n≤4n

We start by proving the so-called base case, P(1). To do this, we substitute n=1 into both the left-hand side and right-hand side.

In our case, this gives the following:

Base Case: We first prove that our statement is true for the base case n=1. 1+3(1)≤41

Proving that the base case is true is usually straightforward. We simply calculate the left-hand and right-hand sides independently and show they're equal.

Next, we write down our inductive hypothesis. We assume that P(k) is true for some n=k≥1. To do this, we simply replace n with k in our original statement P(n).

In our case, this gives the following:

Inductive Hypothesis: We assume that for some n=k≥1, we have 1+3k≤4k

Then, we move on to the inductive step: We must show that P(k)⇒P(k+1). We start by writing down P(k+1).

Inductive Step: We're required to prove that 1+3(k+1)≤4k+1.

The inductive step is where most of the work lies. The idea is to rewrite the left-hand side of this inequality using the inductive hypothesis and apply algebraic techniques to show that this is less than or equal to the right-hand side.

Once we've shown that P(k)⇒P(k+1), the proof is complete, and we state our conclusion.

Conclusion: Therefore, 1+3n≤4n for all n≥1.
