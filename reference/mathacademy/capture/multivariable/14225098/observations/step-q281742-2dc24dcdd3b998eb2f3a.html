If the polar curve r=kcos⁡θ, where k is a constant, passes through the point A with Cartesian coordinates (−3,3), then k=
−4
.

Correct
EXPLANATION

First, we find the polar coordinates of the point A. The distance from A to the origin is

r=x2+y2=(−3)2+(3)2=23.

Now, since A lies in the second quadrant, we get

θ=π−arctan⁡|yx|=π−arctan⁡|3−3|=π−π6=5π6.

So, the polar coordinates of A are (r,θ)=(23,5π6).

To find the value of k, we substitute the polar coordinates of the point A into the polar equation of the curve:

23=kcos⁡θ23=kcos⁡(5π6)23=k(−32)k=−4
