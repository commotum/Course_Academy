EXPLANATION

First, if we denote g(x)=f′(x) then f″(x)=g′(x). So, we need to sketch the graph of y=g′(x) given the graph of y=g(x), as usual.

Notice the following:

  • At the critical points, where the tangent line to y=g(x) is parallel to the x-axis, we have g′(x)=0.

  • When g(x) is increasing, we have g′(x)>0.

  • When g(x) is decreasing, we have g′(x)<0.

We don't know the exact value of g′(0), but from the graph of y=g(x) we find that the slope of the tangent to g′(x) at x≈0 is negative.
