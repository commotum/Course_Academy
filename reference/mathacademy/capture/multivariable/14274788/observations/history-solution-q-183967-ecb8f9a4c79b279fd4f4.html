The principle of induction states the following: Suppose we have a sequence of mathematical statements P(1), P(2), P(3),…, where

  • P(1) is true, and

  • P(k)⇒P(k+1) for every k∈N.

Then, P(n) is true for every n∈N.

In this case, the general statement P(n) we're required to prove is the following:

P(n):an=5n−2

We start by proving the base case. To do this, we substitute n=1 into both the left-hand side and right-hand side.

Base Case: We first prove our statement is true for the base case n=1. For the left-hand side, we have a1=3, and for the right-hand side, we have 5(1)−2=3. Thus, our statement is true for n=1.

Next, we state our inductive hypothesis. Here, we assume that the given statement is true for some n=k≥1.

Inductive Hypothesis: We assume that for some n=k≥1, we have ak=5k−2.

We must show that P(k)⇒P(k+1). Let's start by writing down P(k+1). This is the first part of the inductive step.

Inductive Step: We're required to prove that ak+1=5(k+1)−2=5k+3.(∗)

The idea is to rewrite the left-hand side of (∗) using the recurrence relation and inductive hypothesis to show that this equals the right-hand side. We do this as follows:

Using the recurrence relation, we have ak+1=ak+5. By the inductive hypothesis ak+5=(5k−2)+5=5k−2+5=5k+3, as required.

Since we've shown that the left-hand side of (∗) equals the right-hand side, our proof is complete. So, let's write down our conclusion.

Conclusion: Therefore, by the principle of induction, an=5n−2 is true for all n∈N.
