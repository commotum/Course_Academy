If S is an oriented surface with unit normal vector n, and F is continuous vector field, then the flux of F across S measured with respect to n is given by

∬SF⋅dS=∬SF⋅ndS.

Notice that only the z-component of F=⟨0,0,x3+y2+z⟩ is non-zero. Since the lateral sides of S are parallel to the z-axis, the flux of F through S will be non-zero only at the top and bottom sides.

Let's consider the flux of F through these two sides.

  • Let S1 denote the top surface. On this surface, we have z=1 for 0≤x≤1 and 0≤y≤1. The outward-pointing unit normal vector to S1 is n1=⟨0,0,1⟩. Using the fact that z=1 on S1, we have F⋅n1=⟨0,0,x3+y2+z⟩⋅⟨0,0,1⟩=⟨0,0,x3+y2+1⟩⋅⟨0,0,1⟩=x3+y2+1. Therefore, we can calculate the flux of F across S1 as follows: ∬S1F⋅dS=∬S1F⋅n1dS=∬S1x3+y2+1dS=∬Dx3+y2+1dA=∫01∫01x3+y2+1dxdy=∫01[14x4+xy2+x]x=0x=1dy=∫01y2+54dy=[13y3+54y]01=1912.

  • Let S2 denote the bottom surface. On this surface, we have z=0 for 0≤x≤1 and 0≤y≤1. The outward-pointing unit normal vector to S2 is n2=⟨0,0,−1⟩. Using the fact that z=0 on S2, we have F⋅n2=⟨0,0,x3+y2+z⟩⋅⟨0,0,−1⟩=⟨0,0,x3+y2⟩⋅⟨0,0,−1⟩=−(x3+y2). Therefore, we can calculate the flux of F across S2 as follows: ∬S2F⋅dS=∬S2F⋅n2dS=∬S2−(x3+y2)dS=−∬Dx3+y2dA=−∫01∫01x3+y2dxdy=−∫01[14x4+xy2]x=0x=1dy=−∫01y2+14dy=−[13y3+14y]01=−712.

Finally,

∬SF⋅dS=∬SF⋅ndS=∬S1F⋅ndS+∬S2F⋅ndS=1912+(−712)=1.
