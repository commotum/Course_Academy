EXPLANATION

Let S be an oriented piecewise-smooth surface bounded by a simple, closed, piecewise-smooth boundary C with positive orientation. If a vector field F has continuous partial derivatives for all its components in an open region in space that contains S, then Stokes' theorem states that

∮CF⋅dr=∬ScurlF⋅dS.

There are many surfaces whose boundary is the curve C representing the intersection of the cylinder x2+y2=1 and the paraboloid z=(x−1)2+(y+1)2. One such surface would be the plane S with the equation

z=g(x,y)=(x−1)2+(y+1)2.

We first compute the partial derivatives of the graph z=g(x,y):

∂g∂x=∂∂x((x−1)2+(y+1)2)=2(x−1)∂g∂y=∂∂y((x−1)2+(y+1)2)=2(y+1)

Next, we substitute the equation z=g(x,y) into the expression for curlF:

curlF|z=g(x,y)=⟨x+y,−x+y,−2z⟩|z=(x−1)2+(y+1)2=⟨x+y⏟P,−x+y⏟Q,−2(x−1)2−2(y+1)2⏟R⟩

So, we have

∬ScurlF⋅dS=∬D(−P∂g∂x−Q∂g∂y+R)dA=∬D(−(x+y)⋅2(x−1)−(−x+y)⋅2(y+1)−2((x−1)2+(y+1)2))dA=∬D2((x+y)(1−x)+(x−y)(y+1)−(x−1)2−(y+1)2)dA=∬D4(−x2−y2+2x−y−1)dA,

where D is region enclosed by the projection of C onto the xy-plane.

Notice that the projection of C onto the xy-plane is the circle x2+y2=1 with radius 1 centered at the origin. Therefore, using the change of variables to polar coordinates

x=rcos⁡θ,y=rsin⁡θ,dA=rdrdθ,

we obtain
