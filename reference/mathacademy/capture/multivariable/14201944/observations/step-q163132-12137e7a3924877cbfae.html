For the function f:R2→R, defined as

f(x,y)=cos⁡(3x−y),

the quadratic Taylor polynomial of f about a=[00] is given by

f(x)≈∗+[∗0][∗y]+12[∗y]T[∗3∗−1][xy].

Find the sum of the six missing expressions.

a
2x−5
b
3x−8
c
2x+4
d
3x+6
e
2x+2
Submit
Correct
EXPLANATION

Suppose that the function $\mathbf{f}:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ is defened by

\[ f:\begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\x_2 \\ \vdots \\ x_n \end{bmatrix} \longrightarrow z \]

and is twice differentiable at $\mathbf x = \mathbf a.$ Then, the quadratic Taylor polynomial of $f$ about $\mathbf a$ is given by

\begin{align*} f(\mathbf x) &\approx f(\mathbf a) + \boldsymbol{f}'(\mathbf a) (\mathbf x - \mathbf a) + \dfrac12(\mathbf x - \mathbf a)^T\boldsymbol{f}''(\mathbf a)(\mathbf x - \mathbf a), \end{align*}

where

\[ \boldsymbol{f}'(\mathbf a) = \left.\begin{bmatrix} \dfrac{\partial f }{\partial x_1} & \dfrac{\partial f }{\partial x_2} & \cdots & \dfrac{\partial f }{\partial x_n} \end{bmatrix}\right|_{\mathbf x = \mathbf a} \]

and

\[ \boldsymbol{f}''(\mathbf a) = \left. \begin{bmatrix} \dfrac{\partial^2 f }{\partial x_1^2} & \dfrac{\partial^2 f }{\partial x_1\partial x_2} & \cdots & \dfrac{\partial^2 f }{\partial x_1 \partial x_n} \\[5pt] \dfrac{\partial^2 f }{\partial x_2\partial x_1 } & \dfrac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_2^2} & \cdots & \dfrac{\partial^2 f }{\partial x_2 \partial x_n} \\[5pt] \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\[5pt] \dfrac{\partial^2 f }{\partial x_n\partial x_1 } & \dfrac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_n\partial x_2} & \cdots & \dfrac{\partial^2 f }{\partial x_n^2} \end{bmatrix} \right._{\mathbf x = \mathbf a} \]

In this case, we have the following:

\begin{align*} \boldsymbol{f}'(\mathbf x) &= \begin{bmatrix} \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x} & \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y} \end{bmatrix}, &\qquad \boldsymbol{f}''(\mathbf x) &= \begin{bmatrix} \dfrac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} & \dfrac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x \partial y} \\ \dfrac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y \partial x} & \dfrac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2} \end{bmatrix} \end{align*}

Now, we compute the terms of our Taylor polynomial:

  • Calculating the value of the function, we have \begin{align*} f(0,0) &= 1. \end{align*}

  • Calculating the first derivatives, we have the following: \begin{align*} \boldsymbol{f}'(\mathbf x) &= \begin{bmatrix} -3\sin(3x-y) & \sin(3x-y) \end{bmatrix} \\[5pt] \boldsymbol{f}'(0,0) &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \end{align*}

  • Calculating the second derivative, we have the following: \begin{align*} \boldsymbol{f}''(\mathbf x) &= \begin{bmatrix} -9\cos(3x-y) & 3\cos(3x-y) \\ 3\cos(3x-y) & -\cos(3x-y) \end{bmatrix} \\[5pt] \boldsymbol{f}''(0,0) &= \begin{bmatrix} -9 & 3 \\ 3 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \end{align*}

Therefore, the second-degree Taylor polynomial is given by

\[ f(\mathbf x) \approx \bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}1} + \begin{bmatrix} \bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}0} & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}x} \\ y \end{bmatrix} + \dfrac12 \begin{bmatrix} \bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}x} \\ y \end{bmatrix}^T \begin{bmatrix} \bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}-9} & 3 \\ \bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}3} & -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} \]

Finally, the sum of the missing expressions is

\[ 1 + 0+ x + x+ (-9) + 3 = 2x-5. \]
