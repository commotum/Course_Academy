To determine whether f is even or odd, we compare f(−x) and f(x): f(−x)=sin⁡(2(−x))=sin⁡(−2x)=−sin⁡(2x)=−f(x). Since f(−x)=−f(x), we have that f is odd.

For Fourier series on [−π,π], the cosine coefficients are given by an=1π∫−ππf(x)cos⁡(nx)dx, and the constant coefficient is a0=1π∫−ππf(x)dx.

Here, f(x) is odd and cos⁡(nx) is even, so f(x)cos⁡(nx) is odd. Also, f(x) itself is odd.

Now, the integral of an odd function over [−π,π] is 0. Thus, an=0 for all n≥1, and a0=0.

Therefore, the function f(x) is odd and the cosine terms in its Fourier series must be zero.
