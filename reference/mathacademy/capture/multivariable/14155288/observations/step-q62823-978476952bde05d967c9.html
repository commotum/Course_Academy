Air is being pumped into a rubber speed boat. The rate at which the pressure increases in the boat is given by the function

r(t)=2t+4,

where r(t) is measured in PSI per second and t>0 is the time in seconds since the pumping started.

The boat has a slow puncture which releases air at a rate of 1 PSI every second.

If the boat had a pressure of 10 PSI in the beginning, how much pressure does it have after 20 seconds?

a
460 PSI
b
450 PSI
c
100 PSI
d
470 PSI
e
110 PSI

EXPLANATION

The pressure increases at a rate of 2t+4 PSI per second and is decreasing at a rate of 1 PSI per second. Therefore, the rate of change of pressure (call it R(t)) is

R(t)=r(t)−1=2t+4−1=2t+3

To find the total pressure after t seconds (call it T(t)), we apply the second fundamental theorem of calculus:

T(t)=T(0)+∫0tR(x)dx=10+∫0t(2x+3)dx

To ensure that T is a function of t only, we use a different variable (x) for the integration.

To find out the quantity of pressure after 20 seconds, we substitute t=20 into the above and evaluate.

T(20)=10+∫020(2x+3)dx=10+[x2+3x]|020=10+[(202+3(20))]=10+460=470
