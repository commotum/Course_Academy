EXPLANATION

Recall that critical points of y=f(x) occur when f′(x)=0 or f′(x) does not exist. In our case, there is one critical point:

x=−1.5

Also, we have the following:

  • If f′(x)>0 then f(x) is increasing.

  • If f′(x)<0 then f(x) is decreasing.

We can now summarize the information from the graph in the table below.

  • First, we draw the critical point. We choose an arbitrary point on the vertical line x=−1.5, as shown below.

  • Next, we consider the behavior of y=f(x) in the neighborhood of the critical points:

    • Since f(x) is increasing ↗ on (−∞,−1.5) and is increasing ↗ on (−1.5,+∞), the graph of y=f(x) goes down to the left and up to the right of x=−1.5.

    • Furthermore, since f′(x) is decreasing to the left of x=−1.5, we must have f″(x)<0, and, since it is increasing to the right of x=−1.5, we must have f″(x)>0. So, x=−1.5 is an inflection point at which the curve y=f(x) changes from concave down to concave up.
