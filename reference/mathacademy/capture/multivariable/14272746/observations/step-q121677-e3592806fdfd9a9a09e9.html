A liquid is flowing out of a conical-shaped tube at a constant rate of 108πin3/h. The larger section of the tube is 60in in radius and the length of the tube is 120in. How fast is the liquid level dropping when there is 144πin3 of liquid remaining in the tube?

3in/h
b
92in/h
c
14in/h
d
4in/h

74in/h

EXPLANATION

First, let's consider the dimensions of the tube. Let's call H=120in the height of the funnel, and R=60in its radius at the top. These quantities are fixed and do not change with time.

Now, let's consider the liquid level. We call h the liquid level's height and r the radius of the cone formed by the liquid. Both of these quantities, and the water volume v, change with time. In particular, we know that

dvdt=−108πin3/h,

and we want to find dhdt when v=144πin3.

We need to find a relation between h and the variable for which we know the rate of change, which is v. This is given by the formula for the volume of a cone:

v=13πr2h

Before we differentiate the equation with respect to time, note that r also appears in the formula. So, we need to simplify the formula by writing r in terms of h.

Note that the cones formed by the funnel and the water must be similar. Therefore, we must have

rh=RHrh=60120r=12h.

Therefore, we can write the volume formula as

v=13πr2h=13π(12h)2h=112πh3.

Now that we have an equation relating v and h, we differentiate with respect to time using the chain rule:

ddtv=ddt(112πh3)dvdt=14πh2dhdt.

We want to find the rate at which the level of water in the funnel is changing when v=144πin3. We can compute the value of h at this moment as follows:

144π=112πh3h3=1728h=12in.

Finally, we plug in the known values into the formula for dvdt, and we get

−108π=14π(12)2dhdt−108=36dhdtdhdt=−3in/h.

Therefore, the liquid level is decreasing at a rate of 3in/h.
