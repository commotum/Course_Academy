A chemical is being poured into a conical vat at a constant rate of 18πm3/h. The vat has a radius of 10m at the wider end and a height of 10m. Find the rate of the liquid level increase at the moment when there is 9πm3 of liquid in the vat. Do not round the answer.
The liquid level is increasing at a rate of
2
m/h.

EXPLANATION

First, let's consider the dimensions of the vat. Let's call H=10m the height of the vat, and R=10m its radius at the top. These quantities are fixed and do not change with time.

Now, let's consider the liquid level. We call h the liquid level's height and r the radius of the cone formed by the liquid. Both of these quantities, and the liquid volume v, change with time. In particular, we know that

dvdt=18πm3/h,

and we want to find dhdt when v=9πm3.

We need to find a relation between h and the variable for which we know the rate of change, which is v. This is given by the formula for the volume of a cone:

v=13πr2h

Before we differentiate the equation with respect to time, note that r also appears in the formula. So, we need to simplify the formula by writing r in terms of h.

Note that the cones formed by the vat and the liquid must be similar. Therefore, we must have

rh=RHrh=1010r=h.

Therefore, we can write the volume formula as

v=13πr2h=13π(h)2h=13πh3.

Now that we have an equation relating v and h, we differentiate with respect to time using the chain rule:

ddtv=ddt(13πh3)dvdt=πh2dhdt

We want to find the rate at which the level of liquid in the vat is changing when v=9πm3. We can compute the value of h at this moment as follows:

9π=13πh3h3=27h=3m

Finally, we plug in the known values into the formula for dvdt, and we get

18π=π(3)2dhdt18=9dhdtdhdt=2m/h.

Therefore, the liquid level is increasing at a rate of 2m/h.
