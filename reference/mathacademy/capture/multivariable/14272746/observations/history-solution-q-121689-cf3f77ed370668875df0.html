First, let's consider the dimensions of the cup. Let's call H=75cm the height of the conical cup, and R=15cm its radius at the top. These quantities are fixed and do not change with time.

Now, let's consider the water level. We call h the height of the water and r the radius of the cone formed by the water. Both of these quantities, and the water volume v, change with time. In particular, we know that

dhdt=1h2cm/s,

and we want to know dvdt.

We need to find a relation between v and the variable for which we know the rate of change, which is h. This is given by the formula for the volume of a cone:

v=13πr2h

Before we differentiate the equation with respect to time, note that r also appears in the formula. So, we need to simplify the formula by writing r in terms of h.

Note that the cones formed by the cup and the water must be similar. Therefore, we have

rh=RHrh=1575r=h5.

Therefore, we can write the volume formula as

v=13π(h5)2hv=175πh3.

Now that we have an equation relating v and h, we differentiate with respect to time using the chain rule:

ddtv=ddt(175πh3)dvdt=125πh2dhdtdvdt=125πh2(1h2)dvdt=125π

Therefore, the volume of water is increasing at a constant rate of π25cm3/s.
