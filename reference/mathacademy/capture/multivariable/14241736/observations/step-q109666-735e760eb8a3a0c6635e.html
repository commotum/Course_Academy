Suppose that a matrix A has characteristic polynomial p(λ)=(λ+1)3(λ−3)2(λ+2)4, and for the eigenvalue λ=−1, there are exactly 2 eigenvectors. If a basis of R9 consists of linearly independent generalized eigenvectors of A, how many of these generalized eigenvectors of rank greater than 1 correspond to the eigenvalue λ=−1?

EXPLANATION

The number of generalized eigenvectors of rank greater than 1 that correspond to an eigenvalue λ is equal to the difference between the algebraic multiplicity and the geometric multiplicity of λ.

In the characteristic polynomial p(λ)=(λ+1)3(λ−3)2(λ+2)4, we see that the root λ=−1 has multiplicity 3. So the algebraic multiplicity of the eigenvalue λ=−1 is 3.

However, there are only 2 eigenvectors corresponding to the eigenvalue λ=−1, so the geometric multiplicity of the eigenvalue λ=−1 is 2.

Therefore, the number of generalized eigenvectors of rank greater than 1 corresponding to λ=−1 is 3−2=1.
