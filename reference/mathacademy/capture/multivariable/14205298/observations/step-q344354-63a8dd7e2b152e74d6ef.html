In a physics experiment, the net charge on a sensor (in mC) after t seconds is given by the function Q(t). The values of Q(t) for different values of t are given in the table below:

t 1.0 1.5 2.0 2.5 3.0 3.5
Q(t) 4 3 2 1 −1 −2

Approximate the sensor’s charge instantaneous rate of change at t=2.5 using a central difference approximation with step size h=0.5.

The sensor’s charge at t=2.5 is decreasing at approximately
3
mC per second.

Correct
EXPLANATION

We use the central difference approximation Q′(t)≈Q(t+h)−Q(t−h)2h, at time t=2.5 with step size h=0.5. This gives Q′(2.5)≈Q(2.5+0.5)−Q(2.5−0.5)2(0.5)=Q(3.0)−Q(2.0)1.0=−1−21.0=−31.0=−3. Therefore, the sensor’s charge at t=2.5 is decreasing at approximately 3mC per second.
