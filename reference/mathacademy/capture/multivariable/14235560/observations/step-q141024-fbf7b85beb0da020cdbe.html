A study conducted on 3400 teenagers by the transportation department of a particular city reveals that teenagers' preference for the type of home-to-school transportation changes with time. The following table shows the results of this study.

How many teenagers would eventually go to school on electric scooters if the listed probabilities remained constant over many years?

EXPLANATION

From the table, we get the corresponding stochastic matrix

P=[0.20.200.40.40.40.40.40.6].

Notice that the matrix P2 contains only positive entries. So, the matrix P is regular. As a result, after many years, the distribution will eventually converge to the steady-state vector of P, and this convergence does not depend on the initial state.

Recall that a steady-state vector of a stochastic matrix P is a stochastic vector q such that

Pq=q⟹(P−I)q=0.

Computing P−I, we get

P−I=[0.20.200.40.40.40.40.40.6]−[100010001]=[−0.80.200.4−0.60.40.40.4−0.4].

The matrix equation (P−I)q=0 is equivalent to the system of linear equations with the augmented matrix M, which we reduce to row echelon form using Gaussian elimination as follows:

M=[−0.80.2000.4−0.60.400.40.4−0.40]R1:=5R1R2:=5R2R3:=52R3∼[−41002−32011−10]R1↔R3∼[11−102−320−4100]R2:=R2+(−2)R1R3:=R3+4R1∼[11−100−54005−40]R3:=R2+R3∼[11−100−5400000]

The reduced matrix above has two pivot columns (the 1st and 2nd). Thus, x3 is a free variable. From the second equation, we obtain x2=45x3. Substituting this into the first equation, we obtain x1+(45x3)−x3=0⟹x1=15x3. Hence, the general solution is

[15x345x3x3],x3∈R.

Since the steady-state vector must be stochastic, its entries should add up to 1. Therefore, we have

15x3+45x3+x3=12x3=1x3=12.

Therefore,

q=[11041012].

So, after many years, 12 of the teenagers will go to school in electric scooters. Therefore, the total number of teenagers in the city that will go to school in electric scooters will be

12⋅3400=1700.
