A study shows how the sports preferences of young people in a certain community vary from year to year. The following table shows the results of this study.

From:
Baseball Basketball Football

Football 0.1 0.2 0.8

For example, 10% of people who used to prefer baseball now prefer football.

Given that 1260 young people were interviewed for the study and that the probabilities listed remain constant for many years, how many of those people will eventually prefer baseball?

EXPLANATION

From the table, we get the corresponding stochastic matrix

P=[0.80.200.10.60.20.10.20.8].

Notice that the matrix P2 contains only positive entries. So, the matrix P is regular. As a result, after many years, the distribution will eventually converge to the steady-state vector of P, and this convergence does not depend on the initial state.

Recall that a steady-state vector of a stochastic matrix P is a stochastic vector q such that

Pq=q⟹(P−I)q=0.

Computing P−I, we get

P−I=[0.80.200.10.60.20.10.20.8]−[100010001]=[−0.20.200.1−0.40.20.10.2−0.2].

The matrix equation (P−I)q=0 is equivalent to the system of linear equations with the augmented matrix M, which we reduce to row echelon form using Gaussian elimination as follows:

M=[−0.20.200.1−0.40.20.10.2−0.2]R1:=−5R1R2:=10R2R3:=10R3∼[1−1001−42012−20]R2:=R2+(−1)R1R3:=R3+(−1)R1∼[1−1000−32003−20]R3:=R3+R2∼[1−1000−3200000]

The reduced matrix above has two pivot columns (the 1st and 2nd). Thus, x3 is a free variable. From the second equation, we obtain x2=23x3. Substituting this into the first equation, we obtain

x1−23x3+0x3=0⟹x1=23x3.

Hence, the general solution is [23x323x3x3],x3∈R.

Since the steady-state vector must be stochastic, its entries should add up to 1. Therefore, we have

23x3+23x3+x3=173x3=1x3=37.

Therefore,

q=[272737].

So, after many years, 27 of the people will prefer baseball. Therefore, the number of people who will prefer baseball is 27⋅1260=360people.
