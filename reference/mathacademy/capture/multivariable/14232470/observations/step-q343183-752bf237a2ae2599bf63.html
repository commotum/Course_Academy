A firefighter F is lowering a rescue basket B from a helicopter. The basket stays 40ft above the ground while it moves horizontally toward the firefighter at a speed of 12ft/s. Find, in radians per second as a function of θ, the rate at which the angle θ between the cable and the horizontal is increasing.

EXPLANATION

In the figure, y is the basket's height, and x is the horizontal distance between the firefighter and the basket. The quantity y=40 feet does not change with time because the basket height remains the same.

We wish to calculate dθdt. To do this, let's start by setting up a relation between x and y. Relative to the angle θ, the sides x and y are the adjacent and opposite, respectively. So, we have

tan⁡θ=yxtan⁡θ=40xx=40cot⁡θ.

Now, we differentiate both sides of the equation with respect to t using implicit differentiation, and we get

ddt(x)=ddt(40cot⁡θ)dxdt=(−40csc2⁡θ)dθdt.

From the given information, we have dxdt=−12ft/s (negative because the horizontal distance is decreasing). Substituting this into the above, we reach

−12=(−40csc2⁡θ)dθdt1240=(csc2⁡θ)dθdtdθdt=1240sin2⁡θdθdt=310sin2⁡θ.

Therefore, the angle is increasing at a rate of 310sin2⁡θrad/s.
