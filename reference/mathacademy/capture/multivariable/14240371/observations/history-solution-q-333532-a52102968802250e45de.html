The system can be rewritten in matrix form as x′=\boldsymbolf(x) where x=[x(t)y(t)] and \boldsymbolf(x)=[1πsin⁡(πx)+y1−x2].

We need to compute the Jacobian matrix (or the total derivative) of \boldsymbolf(x).

In our case, we have the following:

\boldsymbolf′(x)=[∂f1∂x∂f1∂y∂f2∂x∂f2∂y]

First, we compute the partial derivatives:

∂f1∂x=∂∂x(1πsin⁡(πx)+y)=cos⁡(πx)∂f1∂y=∂∂y(1πsin⁡(πx)+y)=1∂f2∂x=∂∂x(1−x2)=−2x∂f2∂y=∂∂y(1−x2)=0

Thus, we have

\boldsymbolf′(x)=[cos⁡(πx)1−2x0].

Next, we evaluate the Jacobian at the equilibrium x∗=[10] to get

\boldsymbolf′(x∗)=[cos⁡(π(1))1−2(1)0]=[−11−20].

Notice that the matrix is invertible (its determinant is not zero), so the system is almost linear at x∗.

To find the eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix, we write down the characteristic equation:

Solving the equation, we get the eigenvalues λ1,2=−12±72i.

Finally, since we have two complex eigenvalues with negative real parts, our equilibrium is an asymptotically stable spiral sink.
