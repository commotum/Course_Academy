A coffee producer company estimates that the profit of its coffee production, in dollars, is given by P(x)=−0.002x2+3.9x−3, where x are the tens of kilograms of coffee produced daily. Given that the company can produce at most 10000 kilograms per day, how many kilograms should the company produce per day to maximize its daily profit?

a
3900
b
9750
c
375
d
10000
e
4000

Incorrect
EXPLANATION

Since x represents the number of tens of kilograms of coffee produced daily, and since the company produces at most 10000 kilograms per day, the permitted domain for x is given by 0≤x≤1000. We need to find the critical points of P(x) for 0<x<1000. Taking the derivative, we get

P′(x)=−0.004x+3.9,

and solving P′(x)=0 gives the following critical point:

P′(x)=0−0.004x+3.9=0x=975

Notice that x=975 lies in the interval 0<x<1000. All that's left now is to confirm that x=975 does indeed give a maximum value of P(x). To do this, we compute the second derivative:

P″(x)=−0.004<0.

Since the second derivative is negative, the point x=975 is indeed a local maximum of P(x). And since P(x) is quadratic, we conclude that x=975 is a global maximum of P(x).

Therefore, the company should produce 975⋅10=9750 kilograms of coffee per day to maximize its daily profit.
