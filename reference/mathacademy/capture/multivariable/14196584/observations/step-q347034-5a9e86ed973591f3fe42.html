A private art studio can enroll between 8 and 14 students in a painting workshop. The fee that each student pays is $72 if exactly 8 students enroll, and if more than 8 students are enrolled, the fee drops by $4 for each additional student. Each student pays the same fee.

How many students must the workshop have to maximize its revenue?

EXPLANATION

First, note that if there are 8+x students, then the fee is 72−4x. So, the total revenue is given by R(x)=(8+x)⏟number of students×(72−4x)⏟fee=(8+x)(72−4x)=576−32x+72x−4x2=576+40x−4x2.

Since the workshop can only have between 8 and 14 students, the permitted domain for x is given by 8≤x+8≤14⇒0≤x≤6.

Therefore, x=0 and x=6 are both candidates for the global maxima of R(x).

We need to find the critical points of R(x) for 0<x<6. Taking the derivative, we get R′(x)=40−8x, and solving R′(x)=0 gives the following critical point: R′(x)=040−8x=0x=5

Notice that x=5 lies in the domain 0<x<6. All that's left now is to confirm that x=5 gives a maximum. To do this, we compute the second derivative: R″(x)=−8<0

Since the second derivative is negative, the point x=5 is indeed a local maximum of R(x). And since R(x) is quadratic, we conclude that x=5 is a global maximum of R(x).

Therefore, the workshop must have 8+5=13 students enrolled to maximize its revenue.
