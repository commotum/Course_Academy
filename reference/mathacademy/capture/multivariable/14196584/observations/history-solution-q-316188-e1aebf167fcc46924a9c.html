To solve this problem, we apply the candidates test.

First, note that for selling x ride passes per day, the park has a revenue (in dollars) of R(x)=15x.

The cost (in dollars) of operating the park is made up of a fixed cost and a variable cost: C(x)=700⏟fixed cost+2x⏟variable cost

Therefore, the profit function is given by P(x)=R(x)−C(x)=15x−(700+2x)=13x−700.

The permitted domain is 0≤x≤500. Therefore, x=0 and x=500 are both candidates for the global maximum.

Next, we take the derivative of the profit function: P′(x)=13, which is constant and positive. Since P′(x)>0 for all x, the profit function is increasing throughout the domain.

Thus, the maximum profit occurs at the endpoint x=500. Let’s confirm by computing values at both endpoints: P(0)=13(0)−700=−700,P(500)=13(500)−700=6500−700=5800✓

Therefore, the number of ride passes that maximizes the park’s profit is 500.
