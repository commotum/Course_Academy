First, note that if there are 12+x kids, then the price per ticket is 80−4x. So, the total revenue is given by R(x)=(12+x)⏟number of kids×(80−4x)⏟price per ticket=(12+x)(80−4x)=960−48x+80x−4x2=960+32x−4x2.

Since there can only be between 12 and 20 kids, the permitted domain for x is given by 12≤x+12≤200≤x≤8.

Therefore, x=0 and x=8 are both candidates for the global maxima of R(x).

We need to find the critical points of R(x) for 0<x<8. Taking the derivative, we get R′(x)=32−8x,

and solving R′(x)=0 gives the following critical point:

R′(x)=032−8x=08x=32x=4

Notice that x=4 lies in the interval 0<x<8. All that's left now is to confirm that x=4 does indeed give a maximum value of R(x). To do this, we compute the second derivative:

R″(x)=−8<0

Since the second derivative is negative, the point x=4 is indeed a local maximum of R(x). And since R(x) is quadratic, we conclude that x=4 is a global maximum of R(x).

Therefore, the theme park should admit 12+4=16 kids to maximize its revenue.
