A factory spends C(x)=0.002375x2+0.3x+1000 dollars to manufacture x units of a certain product. The factory sells the product for 30−x100 dollars per unit. Given that the factory can produce at most 2000 units per year, how many units should the factory produce per year to maximize its annual profit?
The optimal number of production is
1200
.

Correct
EXPLANATION

To solve this problem, we apply the candidates test.

First, note that for selling x units of the product per year, the factory has an annual revenue (income) of R(x)=(30−x100)⋅x=30x−0.01x2 dollars.

Therefore, the profit function is given by

P(x)=R(x)−C(x)=30x−0.01x2−(0.002375x2+0.3x+1000)=−1000+29.7x−0.012375x2.

We note that the permitted domain is 0≤x≤2000.

We need to find the critical points of P(x) for 0<x<2000. Taking the derivative, we get

P′(x)=29.7−0.02475x.

Solving P′(x)=0 gives the following critical point:

P′(x)=029.7−0.02475x=0x=1200

Notice that x=1200 lies in the interval 0<x<2000. All that's left now is to confirm that x=1200 does indeed give a maximum value of P(x). To do this, we compute the second derivative:

P″(x)=−0.02475<0.

Since the second derivative is negative, the point x=1200 is indeed a maximum of P(x).

Our candidates are x=0, x=1200, and x=2000. Computing P(x) at each of these values, we get the following:

P(0)=−1000P(1200)=−1000+29.7(1200)−0.012375(1200)2=16820✓P(2000)=−1000+29.7(2000)−0.012375(2000)2=9500

Therefore, we conclude that the factory will have a maximum annual profit when it produces 1200 units per year.
