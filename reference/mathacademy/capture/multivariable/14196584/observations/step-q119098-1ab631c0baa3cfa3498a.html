A coffee shop can produce up to 500 cups of coffee per day. When the price per cup of coffee is $3, the coffee shop sells all of them, and, for each dollar increase in the price, 50 cups remain unsold. If the cost for producing a cup of coffee is $0.40, what price per cup should be set to obtain the maximum profit?

a
$1.65
b
$6.7
c
$6.3
d
$1.85
e
$3
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

To solve this problem, we apply the candidates test.

First, note that if there are x hundreds of unsold cups of coffee, the coffee shop sells (5−x) hundreds of cups of coffee, and the price per cup is (3+2x) dollars. So, the total revenue (in hundreds of dollars) is given by

R(x)=(5−x)⏟sold cups of coffee×(3+2x)⏟price per cup=15+10x−3x−2x2=15+7x−2x2.

On the other hand, for selling (5−x) hundreds of cups of coffee, the coffee shop has a cost (in hundreds of dollars) of

C(x)=0.4(5−x)=2−0.4x.

Therefore, the profit function is given by

P(x)=R(x)−C(x)P(x)=(15+7x−2x2)−(2−0.4x)=13+7.4x−2x2.

We note that the permitted domain is 0≤x≤5.

We need to find the critical points of P(x) for 0<x<5. Taking the derivative, we get

P′(x)=7.4−4x,

and solving P′(x)=0 gives the following critical point:

P′(x)=07.4−4x=04x=7.4x=1.85

Notice that x=1.85 lies in the interval 0<x<5. All that's left now is to confirm that x=1.85 does indeed give a maximum value of P(x). To do this, we compute the second derivative:

P″(x)=−4<0

Since the second derivative is negative, the point x=1.85 is indeed a local maximum of P(x). And since P(x) is quadratic, we conclude that x=1.85 is a global maximum of P(x).

Therefore, we conclude that the coffee shop will have a maximum profit level when there are 185 unsold cups of coffee.

We know that the price per cup is (3+2x) dollars. If there are 1.85 hundreds of unsold cups of coffee, then the price per cup is

3+2(1.85)=$6.7.

Therefore, the coffee shop should sell each cup of coffee for $6.7.
