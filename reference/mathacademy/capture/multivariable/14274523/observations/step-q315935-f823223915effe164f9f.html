First, let r be the radius of the circular sector and let θ be the angle (in radians) subtended by it. We want to maximize the area A=12r2θ. We know that the perimeter is fixed at 40cm, so we also have the constraint condition 40=2r+θr. Now, by making θ the subject of the constraint equation, we find that θ=40−2rr=40r−2. Plugging the above into our expression for A, we obtain A(r)=12r2(40r−2)=20r−r2.

We want to maximize A, so we need to differentiate A and set the derivative equal to zero. The derivative is A′(r)=20−2r. Setting A′(r)=0 and solving for r gives A′(r)=020−2r=0−2r=−20r=(−20)(−2)r=10. Finally, we confirm that r=10 is a maximum using the second derivative test. The second derivative gives A″(r)=−2⟹A″(10)=−2<0. Since A″(10) is negative, we conclude that r=10 is indeed a maximum of A(r).

Therefore, the circular sector with the largest area will be that in which the radius r is equal to 10cm.
