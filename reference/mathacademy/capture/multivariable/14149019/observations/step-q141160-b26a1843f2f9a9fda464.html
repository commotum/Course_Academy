Consider the standard Hermitian inner product ⟨⋅,⋅⟩ over C. Which of the following statements are true?

  1. ⟨ia,b⟩=i⟨a,b⟩ for any a,b∈Cn
  2. ⟨a,α―b⟩=⟨αa,b⟩ for any a,b∈Cn and any α∈C
  3. ⟨ia,a⟩∈R for any a∈Cn

EXPLANATION

Recall that the standard Hermitian inner product over C satisfies the following properties:

  • It is linear in the first argument. For any a,b,c∈Cn and any α∈C, we have

    ⟨a+b,c⟩=⟨a,c⟩+⟨b,c⟩ and ⟨αa,b⟩=α⟨a,b⟩.

  • It is antilinear in the second argument. For any a,b,c∈Cn and any α∈C, we have

    ⟨a,b+c⟩=⟨a,b⟩+⟨a,c⟩ and ⟨a,αb⟩=α―⟨a,b⟩.

  • It has conjugate symmetry. For any a,b∈Cn, we have

    ⟨a,b⟩=⟨b,a⟩―.

  • It is positive-definite. For any a∈Cn, we have

    ⟨a,a⟩ is a non-negative real number, and ⟨a,a⟩=0 if and only if a=0.

With that in mind, let's examine our statements.

  • Statement I is true. Indeed, the inner product in Cn is linear in the first argument. So, a constant multiple from the first argument can be factored out.

  • Statement II is true. By the antilinearity of a complex inner product in the second argument and linearity in the first argument, we have ⟨a,α―b⟩=(α―)―⟨a,b⟩=α⟨a,b⟩=⟨αa,b⟩.

  • Statement III is false. Notice that ⟨a,a⟩∈R for any a∈Cn. Therefore, if a≠0, then ⟨ia,a⟩=i⟨a,a⟩∉R.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and II only."
