A piece of cardboard measures 8cm×6cm. A tray of height ycm is formed by cutting a y×ycm2 square from each of the corners and folding up the sides, as shown in the diagram. Determine the height of the box that gives the maximum possible volume. Round your answer to two decimal places.

a
2.42cm
b
1.13cm
c
1.76cm
d
0.85cm
e
2.73cm
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

Step 1: We need to find the values of x,y, and z such that the volume

V=xyz is the greatest possible value.

Step 2: Notice that the width x and the depth z of the tray are given by

x=8−2y,z=6−2y.

Since x>0 and z>0, we must have that 8−2y>0 and 6−2y>0, hence y<3. Therefore, the range of validity for y is 0<y<3.

Step 3: Substituting the above into the expression for V, we get

V=xyz=(8−2y)(y)(6−2y)=4y3−28y2+48y.

Step 4: We solve the equation V′(y)=0. Taking the first derivative, we have V′(y)=12y2−56y+48,

and solving for the stationary points, we get

V′(y)=012y2−56y+48=03y2−14y+12=0y=7±133.

We discard 7+133 since we already know that 0<y<3.

Step 5: We confirm that 7−133 is a maximum using the second derivative test. The second derivative is V″(y)=24y−56.

Since V″(7−133) is negative, we conclude that 7−133 is indeed a maximum of V(y).

Therefore, the stationary point y=7−133≈1.13 gives the maximum possible volume.
