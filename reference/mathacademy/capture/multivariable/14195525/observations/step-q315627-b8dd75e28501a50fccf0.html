A box with no lid and a square base has a manufacturing cost that depends on two materials. The material for the base costs $5 per square meter, and the material for the lateral faces costs $2 per square meter. The box must have a volume of 10m3.

What is the value of x for the box that has the smallest production cost? Do not round the answer.

EXPLANATION

Step 1: We need to find the value of x such that the manufacturing cost C=5x2+2⋅4xy=5x2+8xy is the smallest possible value.

Step 2: The volume must be 10m3. So, we have the constraint equation V=x⋅x⋅y10=x2y.

Step 3: We make y the subject of the constraint equation: x2y=10y=10x2

Then, we substitute the above into our expression for C, and get C=5x2+8xy=5x2+8x⋅10x2=5x2+80x.

Step 4: We solve the equation C′(x)=0. Taking the derivative, we have C′(x)=10x−80x2, and solving for the stationary points, we get C′(x)=010x−80x2=010x3=80x3=8x=2.

Step 5: We confirm that x=2 is a minimum using the second derivative test. The second derivative is C″(x)=10+160x3⟹C″(2)=10+20=30>0.

Since C″(2) is positive, we conclude that x=2 is indeed a minimum of C(x).

Therefore, the production cost is the smallest when x=2 units.
