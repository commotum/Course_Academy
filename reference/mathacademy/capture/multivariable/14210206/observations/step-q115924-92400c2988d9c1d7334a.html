The sides (in meters) of a square are decreasing. At a particular moment, the rate of decrease of the area of the square is numerically equal to ten times the rate of decrease of the sides. What is the length of the sides of the square at that moment?

a
2 m
b
10 m
c
5 m
d
2.5 m
e
12 m
Submit
Incorrect
EXPLANATION

Let x and A be the length of the sides and the area of the square, respectively. We want to find

dAdt.

The area A is related to side length x by the formula

A=x2.

Differentiating this relationship with respect to t using implicit differentiation, we get

ddt(A)=ddt(x2)dAdt=dxdt⋅ddx(x2)dAdt=dxdt⋅2x

The given information tells us that dAdt=10⋅dxdt. So, substituting dAdt in the above gives

10⋅dxdt=2x⋅dxdt10⋅dxdt=2x⋅dxdt10=2xx=5.

Therefore, at that particular moment we have x=5 m.
