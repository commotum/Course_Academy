Question 2 ?
The flux of G through the duct surface is
−128
.
Submit
Correct
Explanation

If S is an oriented surface with unit normal vector n, and G is a continuous vector field, then the flux of G across S measured with respect to n is given by

∬SG⋅dS=∬SG⋅ndS.

Notice that only the z-component of G=⟨0,0,−xy−2z2⟩ is non-zero. Since the lateral sides of S are parallel to the z-axis, the flux of G through S will be non-zero only at the top and bottom sides.

Let's consider the flux of G through these two sides.

  • Let S1 denote the top surface. On this surface, we have z=4 for 0≤x≤2 and 0≤y≤2. The outward-pointing unit normal vector to S1 is n1=⟨0,0,1⟩. Using the fact that z=4 on S1, we have G⋅n1=⟨0,0,−xy−2z2⟩⋅⟨0,0,1⟩=⟨0,0,−xy−32⟩⋅⟨0,0,1⟩=−xy−32. Therefore, we can calculate the flux of G across S1 as follows: ∬S1G⋅dS1=∬S1G⋅n1dS=∬S1(−xy−32)dS=−∬D(xy+32)dA=−∫02∫02(xy+32)dxdy=−∫02[12x2y+32x]x=0x=2dy=−∫02(2y+64)dy=−[y2+64y]y=0y=2=−132.

  • Let S2 denote the bottom surface. On this surface, we have z=0 for 0≤x≤2 and 0≤y≤2. The outward-pointing unit normal vector to S2 is n2=⟨0,0,−1⟩. Using the fact that z=0 on S2, we have G⋅n2=⟨0,0,−xy−2z2⟩⋅⟨0,0,−1⟩=⟨0,0,−xy⟩⋅⟨0,0,−1⟩=xy. Therefore, we can calculate the flux of G across S2 as follows: ∬S2G⋅dS2=∬S2G⋅n2dS=∬S2xydS=∬DxydA=∫02∫02xydxdy=∫02xdx⋅∫02ydy=[12x2]x=0x=2⋅[12y2]y=0y=2=2⋅2=4

Finally,

∬SG⋅dS=∬SG⋅ndS=∬S1G⋅ndS+∬S2G⋅ndS=−132+4=−128.

Recall that, if F=Pi+Qj is a vector field with simply connected domain D⊆R2, then the following are equivalent:

  • F is a source-free vector field.

  • ∇⋅F=∂P∂x+∂Q∂y=0.
