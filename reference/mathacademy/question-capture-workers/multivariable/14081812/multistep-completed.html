Let F(x,y)=P(x,y)i+Q(x,y)j be a vector field with domain D=R2, where P and Q have continuous first-order partial derivatives everywhere on D. Then, we have the following theorem:

F is conservative if and only if ∂P∂y=∂Q∂x everywhere on D.

For our vector field F, the domain is D=R2, and we have P=ay3−1,Q=3xy2+1.

Computing the partial derivatives, we get ∂P∂y=3ay2,∂Q∂x=3y2.

It's clear that the partial derivatives exist and are continuous on R2. Equating the partial derivatives, we get 3ay2=3y23a=3a=1.

Therefore, we conclude that a=1.
