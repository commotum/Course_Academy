  1. F1(t)=∫01x+tdx,t>0
  2. F2(t)=∫01xtx2+t2dx,t>0
  3. F3(t)=∫011x−tdx,t>1
a
I and III only
b
None
c
II and III only
d
I, II, and III
e
I and II only

Incorrect
EXPLANATION

Suppose f(x,t) is continuous on R=[a,b]×[c,d] and ∂f∂t exists and is continuous on R. Then, the function F(t)=∫abf(x,t)dx is differentiable on (c,d), and F′(t)=ddt∫abf(x,t)dx=∫ab∂f∂t(x,t)dx. This is known as differentiating under the integral sign.

With that in mind, let's check each function:

  • For the first function, we have f1(x,t)=x+t. We fix t0>0 and choose [c,d]⊂(0,∞) such that c<t0<d, so R=[0,1]×[c,d].

    Note that f1 is continuous on R. Computing the partial derivative, we have ∂f1∂t=12x+t, which exists and is continuous on R (since x+t≥c>0 on R). Thus, differentiating under the integral sign is valid at t0. And since our choice of t0 was arbitrary, we can compute F1′(t) for all real t>0.

  • For the second function, we have f2(x,t)=xtx2+t2. We fix t0>0 and choose [c,d]⊂(0,∞) such that c<t0<d, so R=[0,1]×[c,d].

    Note that f2 is continuous on R (since the denominator never vanishes on R). Computing the partial derivative, we have ∂f2∂t=x(x2+t2)−2xt2(x2+t2)2, which exists and is continuous on R. Thus, differentiating under the integral sign is valid at t0. And since our choice of t0 was arbitrary, we can compute F2′(t) for all real t>0.

  • For the third function, we have f3(x,t)=1x−t. We fix t0>1 and choose [c,d]⊂(1,∞) such that c<t0<d, so R=[0,1]×[c,d].

    Note that for t>1 and x∈[0,1], the denominator x−t never vanishes on R, so f3 is continuous on R. Computing the partial derivative, we have ∂f3∂t=1(x−t)2, which exists and is continuous on R. Thus, differentiating under the integral sign is valid at t0. And since our choice of t0 was arbitrary, we can compute F3′(t) for all real t>1.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I, II, and III."
