Correct
EXPLANATION

If a function f(t) is piecewise continuous on [0,∞) and has exponential order a, then its Laplace transform F(s)=L{f(t)} exists for every real s>a, and F is infinitely differentiable in this region of convergence.

The smoothness property states that, for each integer n≥0 and s>a, we have F(n)(s)=(−1)nL{tnf(t)}.

Let's consider each statement in turn.

  • Statement I is false. Smoothness has no bearing on whether the Laplace transform vanishes.
    A function of exponential order always has a well-defined Laplace transform for s>a, and there is no reason for that transform to be identically zero.

  • Statement II is true. The smoothness theorem states that for f(t) of exponential order, the Laplace transform F(s)=L{f}(s) is infinitely differentiable at every point in its region of convergence, and we have F(n)(s)=(−1)nL{tnf(t)}(s).

  • Statement III is true. The Laplace transform F(s) of a function f(t) is always infinitely differentiable in its region of convergence, even if f(t) is non-smooth.

Therefore, the answer is "II and III only."
