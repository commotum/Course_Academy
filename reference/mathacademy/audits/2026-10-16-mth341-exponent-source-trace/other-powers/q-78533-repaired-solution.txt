Computing ${R}^{2},$ we have

$$
\begin{aligned}{R}^{2} & =R\cdot R \\  & =\begin{bmatrix}-4 & 2 \\ -8 & 4\end{bmatrix}\cdot \begin{bmatrix}-4 & 2 \\ -8 & 4\end{bmatrix} \\  & =\begin{bmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix} \\  & =O.\end{aligned}
$$

Notice that, since ${R}^{2}=O,$ the matrix $R$ is a nilpotent matrix of index $2.$ 

We can use this fact to compute ${R}^{5}$ quickly, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}{R}^{5} & ={R}^{2}\cdot {R}^{3} \\  & =O\cdot {R}^{3} \\  & =O\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, 

$$
{R}^{3}=\begin{bmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}.
$$
