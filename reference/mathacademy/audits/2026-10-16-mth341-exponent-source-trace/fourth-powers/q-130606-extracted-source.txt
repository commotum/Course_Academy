Problem:
If $C=[\begin{aligned}6 & 4 \\ -9 & -6\end{aligned}]$, then ${C}^{4}=$

Source worked solution:
Computing ${C}^{2},$ we have

$$
\begin{aligned}{C}^{2} & =C\cdot C \\  & =[\begin{aligned}6 & 4 \\ -9 & -6\end{aligned}]\cdot [\begin{aligned}6 & 4 \\ -9 & -6\end{aligned}] \\  & =[\begin{aligned}0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{aligned}] \\  & =O.\end{aligned}
$$

Notice that, since ${C}^{2}=O,$ the matrix $C$ is a nilpotent matrix of index $2.$ 

We can use this fact to compute ${C}^{4}$ quickly, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}{C}^{2} & ={C}^{2}\cdot {C}^{2} \\  & =O\cdot O \\  & =O\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, 

$$
{C}^{4}=[\begin{aligned}0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{aligned}].
$$
