Problem:
If $K=[\begin{aligned}9 & 27 \\ -3 & -9\end{aligned}]$, then ${K}^{4}=$

Source worked solution:
Computing ${K}^{2},$ we have

$$
\begin{aligned}{K}^{2} & =K\cdot K \\  & =[\begin{aligned}9 & 27 \\ -3 & -9\end{aligned}]\cdot [\begin{aligned}9 & 27 \\ -3 & -9\end{aligned}] \\  & =[\begin{aligned}0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{aligned}] \\  & =O.\end{aligned}
$$

Notice that, since ${K}^{2}=O,$ the matrix $K$ is a nilpotent matrix of index $2.$ 

We can use this fact to compute ${K}^{4}$ quickly, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}{K}^{2} & ={K}^{2}\cdot {K}^{2} \\  & =O\cdot O \\  & =O\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, 

$$
{K}^{4}=[\begin{aligned}0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{aligned}].
$$
