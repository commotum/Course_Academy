# Failure-Point Audit

Use this reference as a field guide, not a blacklist. No single feature proves that a passage was generated by an LLM. Prioritize substantive failures over stylistic coincidences.

## Strong failure points

### Generic claims and superficial analysis

- The sentence could describe almost any subject.
- A familiar prose template appears to be searching for content.
- The paragraph labels a relationship without naming its mechanism or direction.

Fix by stating the exact claim, evidence, relevance, and limitation. Delete the sentence if those cannot be recovered.

### Unsupported significance

Watch for `pivotal`, `crucial`, `key`, `testament`, `underscores`, `highlights`, `broader significance`, and similar language.

Fix by naming what changed, for whom, by how much, or what now depends on it. Do not replace one importance word with another.

### Promotional or brochure tone

Watch for unearned evaluations such as `vibrant`, `renowned`, `rich`, `remarkable`, `groundbreaking`, `profound`, `robust`, and `intuitive`.

Keep evaluation only when it is attributed, technically defined, evidenced, or appropriate to a persuasive genre. Otherwise state observable features.

### Vague attribution and false consensus

Watch for `experts believe`, `observers note`, `critics argue`, `research suggests`, `widely regarded`, `many`, and passive suggestions without a source.

Name the person, study, committee, publication, date, test conditions, and scope supported by the evidence. Preserve disagreements and limits.

### Fabrication and citation mismatch

Check whether each source exists, was actually inspected, supports the adjacent claim, supports its strength, and is cited at the useful page or section. Treat a source summary and the writer's inference as different things.

### Speculation from missing information

An unsuccessful search supports only the statement that the reviewed sources did not explain the point. Do not invent a probable motive or causal story. Label a hypothesis and give its evidence, or stop at the boundary.

### Drafting-process leakage

Remove assistant greetings, `Here is...` preambles, offers to do more, prompt instructions, knowledge-cutoff disclaimers, placeholders, internal citation tokens, copied chat text, tracking parameters, malformed markup, unused references, and broken links.

## Moderate failure points

These matter when they accumulate or displace concrete content.

### Ceremonial verbs

Examples: `serves as`, `stands as`, `represents a`, `boasts`, `utilizes`, `authored`, `commenced`, `facilitated`.

Prefer the accurate direct verb. Do not simplify away a meaningful distinction.

### Dense abstraction

Examples: `landscape`, `interplay`, `innovation`, `engagement`, `impact`, `tapestry`, `ecosystem`, `foster`, `enhance`, `showcase`, `delve`.

Ask which people, institutions, rules, actions, measurements, or causal relations the abstraction denotes.

### Self-summarizing participial endings

Examples: `highlighting its importance`, `reflecting a broader shift`, `reinforcing its reputation`, `solidifying its position`, `contributing to ongoing efforts`.

Delete the ending or replace it with a real consequence. Keep an `-ing` clause that communicates a genuine simultaneous action, method, or result.

### Formulaic rhetoric

- automatic groups of three;
- `not only X, but also Y` without a misconception to correct;
- `more than just X` used only to dramatize;
- balanced `despite challenges` conclusions;
- canned future-outlook language.

Use the number and shape of claims the evidence requires. End with the strongest supported result, implication, decision, or unresolved question.

### Generic structure

Watch for automatic overview, background, key features, significance, challenges, future outlook, and conclusion sections. Rebuild around the questions and decisions specific to the genre.

### Elegant variation

Cycling from `company` to `organization`, `enterprise`, `firm`, and `entity` can create ambiguity. Repeat the precise noun when the referent has not changed.

### Universal professional voice

Check whether a lab report, email, encyclopedia entry, critique, discussion post, and product description have all been pushed toward the same polished explanatory register. Restore genre-specific priorities and the author's existing voice.

### Decorative formatting

Remove headings, bold mini-labels, lists, tables, rules, and dashes that do not improve scanning, comparison, hierarchy, or meaning.

## Weak indicators

Do not treat these as problems by themselves:

- one em dash or curly quotation mark;
- good grammar or polished prose;
- an occasional transition or formal term;
- one three-item list;
- one word such as `crucial` or `delve`;
- headings, bullets, or a table with a clear function;
- a mixture of formal and casual phrasing;
- a document without citations when the genre does not require them.

## Final read

Read the revision aloud. Locate sentences that are smooth but empty, ceremonial, oddly symmetrical, or unlike competent writing in this genre. Then explain, without relying on the prose itself, why each section exists, which evidence matters, and where uncertainty remains. If that explanation is impossible, the underlying editorial judgment still needs work.
