# Create Course Map

Turn supported course information into a map that describes the course's purpose, scope, progression, and intended learning outcomes.

## Resources

Read the bundled [Guide to Creating a Course Map](references/Guide_to_Creating_a_Course_Map.md) before drafting. Use [Course Map Template](assets/Course_Map_Template.md) as the output starting point; adapt a copy, leaving the bundled asset intact. These are the supplied source documents, and this skill does not depend on their original Downloads location.

## Workflow

1. Establish the target course and available evidence. For an existing course, use the supplied research brief and course materials, preserving terminology, scope, emphasis, and intended achievement level. For a new course, establish its intended scope and distinguish proposed choices from existing requirements.
2. Resolve gaps that would materially change the map. If research is needed, the companion [gather-course-information skill](../gather-course-information/SKILL.md) produces the source-backed brief. Do not repeat adequate research. If evidence supports only part of a map, draft that part and identify the missing support rather than inventing details.
3. Adapt the template following the guide. Use one `#` course title and these three `##` sections:
   - `Course Description`: what the course is and why it is offered.
   - `Course Overview`: what students study and how the major areas connect, in coherent prose. Describe a teaching sequence only when established.
   - `Course Outcomes`: identifiable course-level capabilities, expressed as `-` bullets with optional `###` categories.
4. Preserve the outcomes introduction exactly:

   > Upon successful completion of this course, students will have mastered the following:

   Preserve the supported level in the individual outcomes; this introduction does not justify upgrading introductory exposure to advanced proficiency.
5. Let substantive learning expectations determine category and bullet counts. Categories may have unequal numbers of outcomes or be omitted in favor of a flat list. Paragraph counts are also flexible. The template's repeated placeholders impose no numerical requirements.
6. Keep published outcomes distinguishable from synthesized wording in the research brief or accompanying working notes. Ground any synthesis in evidence of the intended capability and level; a topic name alone does not establish proficiency. Retain source and limitation records even when the finished map omits citations.

## Deliver and Check

Write to the user's requested destination when supplied; otherwise return the completed Markdown. For multiple courses, produce a separate map for each.

Check that the description, overview, and outcomes describe the same scope; that no unsupported prerequisites, applications, sequence, or capabilities were added; and that no substantive expectation was lost merely to fit the template. Remove all template placeholders, empty categories, and redundant wording.

The finished map must not contain a `Course Content` section or a numbered outline of units, sections, and topics. Report material unresolved limitations alongside the map without adding extra sections to its required structure.
