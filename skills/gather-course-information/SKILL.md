# Gather Course Information

Establish what an existing university course covers, its intended level, and what students are expected to learn. Produce a separate, traceable research brief for each course.

## Resource

Read the bundled [Guide to Gathering Course Information](references/Guide_to_Gathering_Course_Information.md) before researching. Follow its source-evaluation guidance and use the research-brief template in section 6. The guide is the supplied source document, and this skill does not depend on its original Downloads location.

## Workflow

1. Resolve the university and complete course code from the request or supplied materials. Ask for missing identity information when it cannot be inferred reliably. Record any specified term, campus, delivery mode, section, and variant. Unless a particular offering is requested, research the general course using the current official catalog and the closest relevant syllabus; make that scope explicit.
2. Browse official university sources to verify the title, code, catalog year, credits, description, and formal prerequisites or restrictions. Preserve qualifications such as “introduction to” and “selected topics.” Clearly label paraphrases and any direct quotations.
3. Find matching syllabi, departmental outcomes, instructor course pages, schedules, and supporting documents. The guide provides search patterns and fallbacks. Read the underlying material, not only search snippets; inspect relevant PDF pages when tables, schedules, or scans need visual review. If browsing or a document is unavailable, record the limitation and use accessible supplied evidence without claiming live verification.
4. Evaluate both authority and applicability. Record each source's title, verified URL or supplied local path, host, document type, term/date, access date, relevant pages, course identifiers, and complete or partial access. Label older, undated, archived, reposted, honors, and section-specific material. Confirm course renumberings or equivalence through university evidence.
5. Extract source-linked notes for purpose and scope, published course-level outcomes, coverage and methods, documented progression, and prerequisites and applications. Preserve distinctions between course and program outcomes, required and optional coverage, formal prerequisites and recommended background, and confirmed and provisional schedules.
6. Resolve source disagreements according to the intended offering; retain unresolved differences as limitations. Do not combine every topic across years or variants, transfer expectations between neighboring courses, infer a teaching sequence, or assume that an entire textbook is taught. If published outcomes are not located, say so and keep coverage notes available for later synthesis.
7. Prepare the brief using the guide's section 6 structure. Attach a source identifier to every substantive note or coherent group of notes, with page or section references where useful. Keep source identifiers, URLs, and limitations in this record even if a later presentation omits citations. Lists of topics and schedule notes are appropriate here.

## Completion and Handoff

Use the requested output destination when supplied; otherwise return the brief in Markdown. Replace the brief template's placeholders, mark missing information explicitly, and let the evidence determine the number of sources and entries.

Stop searching when further work is unlikely to resolve a meaningful gap or change the map's scope. There is no source-count quota. Check that the brief identifies the correct course and that its substantive notes are traceable to applicable evidence. State which map sections can be supported and what remains missing; a broad catalog description alone does not support detailed outcomes.

For inaccessible LMS materials, note the access limitation and any relevant published department or instructor contact when useful; gathering information does not authorize contacting anyone.

When the user also requests a finished course map, continue with the companion [create-course-map skill](../create-course-map/SKILL.md), passing the brief and accessible materials. Its bundled guide and template are the course-map resources referenced in the research guide. Otherwise, the completed research brief is the deliverable.
