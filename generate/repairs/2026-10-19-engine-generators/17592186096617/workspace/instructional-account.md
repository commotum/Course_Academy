# Instructional specification

## Evidence and learner operation
The target is differentiating a product containing a primitive trigonometric function. Tutorial 2622 says the derivative is `u'v+uv'`, and notes that computing a product first is often impossible or tedious. Canonical example e-3634 differentiates `x cos x` by naming factors, differentiating each, substituting, then simplifying. Prerequisite examples e-134/e-136 establish polynomial/radical derivatives; e-3631/e-3632 establish sine/cosine/tangent derivatives. Exponential factors are explicitly supported by q-310188, q-71004, q-104297, q-1931 and q-319350. Neighboring e-3636 adds point evaluation and is excluded.

Before selecting parameters, the learner must identify two factors, distinguish a factor from its derivative, compute both derivatives, form the two product-rule terms, and select the complete derivative or supply the missing term. This is not multiplication of derivatives. Completion questions retain their one math blank and supplied complementary term. Radios retain five complete mathematical choices. Worked solutions retain identification, rule, substituted derivatives and final sum. There are no diagrams (source images is empty).

## Fixed backbone and allowed variation
Exactly two factors and one application of the product rule are fixed because those are the assessed operation. Trig arguments remain x to avoid adding the chain rule. Polynomial degree ranges from 1..4 and coefficients have nonzero magnitude 1..6; degree 1 is explicitly taught by e-3634, and degrees 2..4 retain a single power-rule derivative with small integer arithmetic; radical exponent remains 1/2 to preserve one elementary derivative and comparable workload. Exponential base is 2..7, excluding 1 because it collapses a factor's derivative. Constant offsets are 1..6; signs of scalar coefficients may vary. No evaluation, domains requiring case splits, quotient rule or higher derivatives are added. Derivatives are interpreted where factors are differentiable (x>0 for radicals, cos x !=0 for tangent).

Answer-bearing role selection precedes incidental values: completion selects which of u'v or uv' is missing; polynomial radio profiles select sin, cos or tan before coefficients/degree. These are conservative same-method extensions. Other source profiles keep their primitive trig identity because their distinct captured error banks use specific trig expressions; changing those banks would require extra inferred distractors. Coefficients and powers remain genuine differentiation parameters, not cosmetic labels. Radio option order is shuffled independently. Blank order follows the actual rule terms and is mathematical information. Both missing-term assignments must occur within each completion profile, and all three primitive trig derivatives must occur within the polynomial radio profiles. Original parameter replay forces the source trig and missing term.

Every dependent expression, key, complementary term and solution is derived from the same factors. Controlled missing-term and trig counterfactuals hold numeric data constant; coefficient and degree boundaries are also tested. Changing option order must preserve the answer value. A reliable sign/position/template shortcut unsupported by the derivative evidence is a failure. Recognizing cos'=-sin or tan'=sec² is the intended prerequisite, not an incidental shortcut; exp'=exp sometimes permits copying a factor and is explicitly retained as a source simplification. Finite source-specific banks do not alone demonstrate mastery of all product derivatives.

Delivery checks compare role and profile coverage and simple position/sign cues before and after shared normalization, difficulty and inventory deduplication. Learner-history selection cannot be exercised without live state; read-only selection inspection establishes whether any structural cue is preferred within the easy band, and the limitation is reported.
