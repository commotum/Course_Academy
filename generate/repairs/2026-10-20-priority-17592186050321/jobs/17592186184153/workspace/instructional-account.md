## Evidence and learner operation

The lesson Introduction (tutorial 701, UUID 207bbd12-158a-58be-b000-82cace6dcdde) states that “the integral of the sum of functions is equal to the sum of the integrals of each function” and combines the two arbitrary constants into C. Canonical example e-371 integrates x²+1 by splitting terms, rewriting 1=x⁰, increasing each exponent by one, dividing by that new exponent, and combining the results. Prerequisites e-364, e-367, e-368 establish monomial, radical, and reciprocal antiderivatives. The later lesson passage tutorial 703 introduces nontrivial constant factors; this generator does not migrate that additional workload into the present knowledge point.

Before sampling numbers the learner must identify the two summands and their signs, rewrite radicals/reciprocals as powers if necessary, apply the power rule separately (exponent never -1), and recombine with one arbitrary constant. In the derivative blank the learner constructs the nonconstant part of the antiderivative; +C is supplied outside the blank. For radios the learner distinguishes an antiderivative of BOTH terms from a complete bank of five expressions.

## Backbone, slots and roles

Exactly two terms keeps the source sum-rule workload. Nonconstant coefficients have magnitude one to avoid the next knowledge point's constant-factor workload. One constant summand preserves the complete moderate examples. Exponents exclude -1 to avoid logarithms. Variables x,y,t are interchangeable notation, not counted as mathematical diversity. The derivative-function name stays y with independent variable t, retaining the source response fragment. Five radio choices and one field are the observed response contract. All choices carry C in the supported radio profiles; missing-C detection is therefore not an incidental answer cue. The source blank has external C.

Choose profile first; choose sign of each summand and exponent case before magnitude, variable, term order, and option order. Eligible answer-bearing roles are the signed constant, the signed power, the integrated coefficient/exponent pair, and the correct option's position. No option index is fixed. Neither term is always negative or always first. There is no qualifying label or subset-count decision in this skill. Changing sign is a conservative application of the lesson's explicit ± rule; reordering terms follows commutativity and changes no method. Varying integer/rational exponents is supported by the prerequisites. These are declared extensions, not claims of additional observed banks.

P and B: nonconstant exponent n in {2,3,4,5,6}; coefficient s in {-1,1}; constant k in {-6,...,-1,1,...,6}. For B variable=t; for P x/y/t. R: reciprocal square-root exponent n in {3,5,7}, p=-n/2, s in {-1,1}, signed constant k from the same set. This preserves negative exponent simplification and excludes the logarithmic case and more difficult roots. Restricting R to positive variable makes real radical and rational-power identities unambiguous; prompt states variable>0. P/B are real polynomial identities. No condition excludes a useful cancellation of arithmetic factors.

Let N=n+1 and a=s/(p+1) for R. Complete profile P, represented as coefficient/exponent pairs:

1. k*v+s*v^N (omit divisor)
2. k*v²+(s/2)*v^N (both coefficients/powers wrong)
3. k+(s/N)*v^n (fail power increments)
4. k*v+(s/N)*v^N (correct)
5. k*v-(2s/n)*v^N (sign and coefficient error)

Profile R, q=1-n/2 (<0):

1. (a/2)*v^q+(k-1)*v (half reciprocal coefficient, decrement constant)
2. s*v^(-n/2)-k*v (unintegrated reciprocal and reverse constant sign)
3. (a/2)*v^q-v (half coefficient, constant coefficient -1)
4. (a/2)*v^(-q)+k*v (wrong reciprocal direction/coefficient)
5. a*v^q+k*v (correct)

At n=3,s=1,k=4 these exactly replay all five q-71169 values. At n=3,s=-1,k=5 profile P exactly replays q-71167. These are observed algebraic patterns; interpretations of compound mistakes are inferred. Reject exact coincident options (R k=-1 makes options 1 and 3 different unless k=0, already excluded); validate distinctness and unique antiderivative independently. Applying identical term order to ALL bank expressions prevents answer-only ordering cues.
