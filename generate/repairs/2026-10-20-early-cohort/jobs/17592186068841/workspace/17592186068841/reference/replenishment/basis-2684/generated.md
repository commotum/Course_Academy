Find the general solution to the differential equation $y'-6y=0$ for $y\ne0.$

1. $y=K e^{- 6 x}$
2. $y=K e^{6}$
3. $y=K + e^{6 x}$
4. $y=K e^{6 x}$
5. $y=K + 6 x$

To find the general solution, we first separate the variables:

$$
\begin{aligned}y'-6y=0 \\ \frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}-6y & =0 \\ \frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} & =6y \\ \frac{1}{y}\cdot \frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} & =6\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we integrate both sides with respect to $x:$

$$
\begin{aligned}\int \frac{1}{y}\cdot \frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}\,\text{d}x & =\int (6)\,\text{d}x \\ \int \frac{1}{y}\,\text{d}y & =\int (6)\,\text{d}x \\ \ln|y| & =6 x+C\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the general solution to the differential equation (in implicit form) is

$$
\ln|y|=6 x+C.
$$

We're required to write the solution in the form $y=y(x).$ By doing this, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}\ln|y| & =6 x+C \\ |y| & =e^{6 x+C} \\ |y| & =e^{6 x}\cdot e^C \\ |y| & =K e^{6 x}\end{aligned}
$$

where $K=e^C$ and $K>0.$

We can drop the absolute value bars by allowing $K$ to be either positive or negative. Therefore, the most general solution is

$$
y=K e^{6 x}.
$$
