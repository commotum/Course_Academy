Review topic

A disinfectant reservoir in a water treatment facility can hold up to 172gal of liquid. Initially, it contains 52gal of disinfectant solution with 52lb of dissolved disinfectant. A more concentrated solution with concentration 1.5lb/gal flows into the reservoir at a rate of 12gal/min, while the completely mixed solution drains out at a rate of 8gal/min. Rounded to one decimal place, find the amount of disinfectant in the reservoir at the moment it begins to overflow.

Solution:

Answer · correct value:

255.6lb

Since Q is the amount of disinfectant in the reservoir at time t, the derivative

dQdt

describes how this amount changes with respect to time t, in minutes. This is equal to the rate at which disinfectant enters the reservoir minus the rate at which disinfectant leaves the reservoir.

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′

Note that the units of dQdt,Qin′ and Qout′ are lb/min. Now, let's compute the required rates of change in steps.

Step 1: Calculating the rate at which disinfectant enters the reservoir.

First, note that a solution containing 1.5lb/gal of disinfectant enters the reservoir at a rate of 12gal/min. So, the rate at which the disinfectant enters the reservoir is

Qin′=(1.5lbgal)⋅(12galmin)=(1.5lbgal)⋅(12galmin)=18lbmin.

Step 2: Calculating the rate at which disinfectant exits the reservoir.

To find the rate at which disinfectant leaves the reservoir, we must first determine the volume of solution in the reservoir at time t.

The reservoir initially contains 52gal of solution; solution enters at 12gal/min and mixed solution leaves at 8gal/min. As a result, the amount of solution in the reservoir after t minutes is

V(t)=52+12t−8t=4(13+t)gal.

Note that V(t) is an increasing function, so the reservoir is filling.

Since Q is the amount of disinfectant in the reservoir, the concentration of the solution at time t is

Q4(13+t)lb/gal.

Therefore, as the completely mixed solution leaves at a rate of 8gal/min, disinfectant leaves at a rate of

Qout′=(8galmin)⋅(Q4(13+t)lbgal)=(8galmin)⋅(Q4(13+t)lbgal)=8Q4(13+t)lbmin=2Q13+tlbmin.

Step 3: Calculate the overall rate of change.

The rate of change equals the rate of entry minus the rate of exit. Therefore,

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′=18−2Q13+t

which gives the equation

dQdt+213+tQ=18.

Step 4: Integrate the equation.

This is a first-order linear equation with variable coefficients. So we find the general solution using an integrating factor:

I(t)=e∫2/(13+t)dt=e2ln|13+t|=eln(13+t)2=(13+t)2.

Multiplying both sides of the differential equation by I(t)=(13+t)2 yields

(13+t)2⋅(dQdt+213+tQ)=(13+t)2⋅18(13+t)2dQdt+2(13+t)Q=18(13+t)2ddt(Q(13+t)2)=18(13+t)2.

Now, we integrate with respect to t:

∫ddt(Q(13+t)2)dt=∫18(13+t)2dtQ(13+t)2=18⋅13(13+t)3+CQ(t)=6(13+t)+C(13+t)2.

We're told that the reservoir initially contains 52lb of disinfectant. So, we can apply the initial condition Q(0)=52, and solve for C:

Q(0)=526(13+0)+C(13+0)2=5278+C(13)2=52C132=−26C=−26(13)2.

Therefore, we obtain the following expression for Q(t):

Q(t)=6(13+t)−26(13)2(13+t)2=6(13+t)−26(1313+t)2.

Now, let to be the time when the reservoir begins to overflow. The reservoir has a capacity of 172gal, so we can use the condition V(to)=172 to solve for to:

V(to)=17252+12to−8to=17252+4to=1724to=120to=30min.

Finally, rounded to one decimal place, the amount of disinfectant in the reservoir at the moment it begins to overflow is

Q(30)=6(13+30)−26(1313+30)2=258−26(1343)2≈255.6lb.
