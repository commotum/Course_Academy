Review topic

A dye vat initially holds 600L of liquid containing 7kg of dissolved dye. A feed solution containing 0.03kg/L of the dye is pumped into the vat at a rate of 10L/min, while the completely mixed liquid is drained out at a rate of 10L/min. Which expression gives the amount of dye Q(t), in kilograms, in the vat t minutes after pumping begins?

CorrectPreview only
Solution:

Answer · correct value:

Q(t)=18−11e−t/60

Let Q be the amount of dye in the vat at time t, in minutes. Then, the derivative

dQdt

describes how this amount changes with respect to time t. This is equal to the rate at which dye enters the vat minus the rate at which dye leaves the vat.

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′

Note that the units of dQdt,Qin′ and Qout′ are kg/min. Now, let's compute the required rates of change in steps.

Step 1: Calculating the rate at which dye enters the vat.

Note that a feed solution containing 0.03kg/L of the dye enters the vat at a rate of 10L/min. So, the rate at which the dye enters the vat is

Qin′=(0.03kgL)⋅(10Lmin)=(0.03kgL)⋅(10Lmin)=0.3kgmin.

Step 2: Calculating the rate at which dye exits the vat.

Next, note that the rate of flow in equals the rate of flow out, so the volume in the vat remains 600L. Then, since Q is the amount of dye in the vat, the concentration of the solution at time t is

Q600kg/L.

Therefore, as the completely mixed liquid leaves at a rate of 10L/min, dye leaves at a rate of

Qout′=(10Lmin)⋅(Q600kgL)=(10Lmin)⋅(Q600kgL)=10Q600kgmin=Q60kgmin.

Step 3: Calculate the overall rate of change.

The rate of change equals the rate of entry minus the rate of exit. Therefore,

dQdt=Qin′−Qout′=0.3−Q60

which gives the equation

dQdt+160Q=0.3.

Step 4: Integrate the equation.

This is a first-order linear equation with constant coefficients. To find the general solution, we must sum the complementary and particular solutions.

  • To find the complementary solution Qc, we solve the corresponding homogeneous equation, given by

dQcdt+160Qc=0.

Solving using an integration technique, such as separation of variables, we obtain the complementary solution

Qc(t)=Ae−t/60,

where A is a constant of integration.

  • Next, we find the particular solution. Note that the right-hand side of the inhomogeneous equation is a polynomial of degree 0. So, we assume that the particular solution is also a polynomial of degree 0, i.e.,

Qp(t)=α.

To find the value of α, we substitute Qp=α and dQpdt=(α)′=0 into the inhomogeneous equation:

0+160α=0.3⟹α=18.

Therefore, the particular solution is Qp(t)=18.

Now, since the general solution is given by the sum of the complementary solution and the particular solution, the general solution in our case is

Q(t)=Qc(t)+Qp(t)=Ae−t/60+18.

We're told that the vat initially contains 7kg of dissolved dye. So, we can apply the initial condition Q(0)=7, and solve for A:

Q(0)=7Ae−0/60+18=7A=−11

Therefore, we obtain the following expression for Q(t):

Q(t)=18−11e−t/60.
