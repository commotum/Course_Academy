A rinse tank initially holds 240L of water containing 12kg of dissolved dye. A solution containing 0.05kg/L of dissolved dye is pumped into the tank at a rate of 16L/min, while the completely mixed water is drained out at a rate of 24L/min. If the amount of dye Q(t), in kilograms, in the tank t minutes after pumping begins is given by

Q(t)=α(1−t30)+β(1−t30)3

where α and β are constants, what is the value of α−β?

Solution:

Answer · correct value:

12

Let Q be the amount of dissolved substance in the tank at time t, in minutes. The amount entering minus the amount leaving gives dQdt=Qin′−Qout′. The units of these rates are kg/min.

Step 1: Calculating the rate at which substance enters the tank. Qin′=(0.05kgL)⋅(16Lmin)=(0.05kgL)⋅(16Lmin)=0.8kg/min.

Step 2: Calculating the rate at which substance exits the tank. The volume is V(t)=240+16t−24t=(240−8t)L. The concentration is Q240−8tkg/L. Hence, Qout′=(24Lmin)⋅(Q240−8tkgL)=(24Lmin)⋅(Q240−8tkgL)=24Q240−8tkg/min=3Q30−tkg/min.

Step 3: Calculate the overall rate of change. dQdt=Qin′−Qout′=0.8−3Q30−t. Thus, dQdt+330−tQ=0.8.

Step 4: Integrate the equation. This is a first-order linear equation with variable coefficients. So we find the general solution using an integrating factor: I(t)=e∫3/(30−t)dt=e−3ln⁡|30−t|=eln⁡(30−t)−3=(30−t)−3. Multiplying both sides by I(t)=(30−t)−3 yields (30−t)−3⋅(dQdt+330−tQ)=(30−t)−3⋅0.8(30−t)−3dQdt+3(30−t)−4Q=0.8(30−t)−3ddt(Q(30−t)−3)=0.8(30−t)−3. Now, we integrate with respect to t: ∫ddt(Q(30−t)−3)dt=∫0.8(30−t)−3dtQ(30−t)−3=0.8⋅12(30−t)−2+CQ(t)=0.4(30−t)+C(30−t)3. Note that C is a constant of integration. The initial condition is Q(0)=12. Thus, 0.4(30−0)+C(30−0)3=1212+C(30)3=12C(30)3=0C=0. Finally, we obtain the following expression for Q(t): Q(t)=12−2t5. Writing this in the supplied normalized form gives Q(t)=12(1−t30). Finally, we have α=12,β=0, and α−β=12−(0)=12.
