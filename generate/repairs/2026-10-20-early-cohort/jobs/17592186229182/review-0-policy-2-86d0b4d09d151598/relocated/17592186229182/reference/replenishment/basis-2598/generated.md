An aquarium initially holds $1200L$ of water containing $18kg$ of dissolved minerals.
A solution containing $0.04kg/L$ of dissolved minerals is pumped into the aquarium at a rate of $24L/min$, while the completely mixed water is drained out at a rate of $36L/min$. If the amount of minerals $Q(t)$, in kilograms, in the tank $t$ minutes after pumping begins is given by
$Q(t) = α(1 - \frac{t}{100}) + β(1 - \frac{t}{100})^{3}$
where $α$ and $β$ are constants, what is the value of $α + β$?

> A scientific calculator is required to answer this question.

- $48$
- $30$
- $18$ [correct]
- $66$
- $78$

At $t=0,$ the supplied model gives $Q(0)=\alpha(1-0/100)+\beta(1-0/100)^{3}=\alpha+\beta.$ The initial amount is $18\,\text{kg},$ so $\alpha+\beta=18.$

q-339396 | authored-operations | :question.difficulty/moderate | eligible=True

A fruit syrup tank initially holds $1200\,\text{L}$ of liquid containing $10\,\text{kg}$ of dissolved sweetener.
A solution containing $0.04\,\text{kg/L}$ of dissolved sweetener is pumped into the tank at a rate of $36\,\text{L/min},$ while the completely mixed liquid is drained out at a rate of $48\,\text{L/min}.$ If the amount of sweetener $Q(t),$ in kilograms, in the tank $t$ minutes after pumping begins is given by

$$
Q(t)=\alpha \,(1-\frac{t}{100})+\beta {(1-\frac{t}{100})}^{4}
$$

where $\alpha $ and $\beta $ are constants, what is the value of $\alpha -\beta ?$

- $10$
- $48$
- $86$ [correct]
- $96$
- $58$

Let $Q$ be the amount of dissolved substance in the tank at time $t,$ in minutes. The amount entering minus the amount leaving gives
$$\frac{dQ}{dt}=Q'_{\text{in}}-Q'_{\text{out}}.$$
The units of these rates are $\text{kg/min}.$

**Step 1**: Calculating the rate at which substance enters the tank.
$$\begin{aligned}Q'_{\text{in}}&=(0.04\,\frac{\text{kg}}{\text{L}})\cdot(36\,\frac{\text{L}}{\text{min}})\\
&=(0.04\,\frac{\text{kg}}{\cancel{\text{L}}})\cdot(36\,\frac{\cancel{\text{L}}}{\text{min}})\\&=1.44\,\text{kg/min}.\end{aligned}$$

**Step 2**: Calculating the rate at which substance exits the tank.
The volume is
$$V(t)=1200+36t-48t=(1200-12t)\,\text{L}.$$
The concentration is $\frac{Q}{1200-12t}\,\text{kg/L}.$ Hence,
$$\begin{aligned}Q'_{\text{out}}&=(48\,\frac{\text{L}}{\text{min}})\cdot(\frac{Q}{1200-12t}\,\frac{\text{kg}}{\text{L}})\\
&=(48\,\frac{\cancel{\text{L}}}{\text{min}})\cdot(\frac{Q}{1200-12t}\,\frac{\text{kg}}{\cancel{\text{L}}})\\
&=\frac{48Q}{1200-12t}\,\text{kg/min}=\frac{4Q}{100-t}\,\text{kg/min}.\end{aligned}$$

**Step 3**: Calculate the overall rate of change.
$$\begin{aligned}\frac{dQ}{dt}&=Q'_{\text{in}}-Q'_{\text{out}}\\&=1.44-\frac{4Q}{100-t}.\end{aligned}$$
Thus,
$$\frac{dQ}{dt}+\frac{4}{100-t}Q=1.44.$$

**Step 4**: Integrate the equation.
This is a first-order linear equation with variable coefficients. So we find the general solution using an integrating factor:
$$\begin{aligned}I(t)&=e^{\int 4/(100-t)\,dt}\\&=e^{-4\ln|100-t|}\\&=e^{\ln (100-t)^{-4}}\\&=(100-t)^{-4}.\end{aligned}$$
Multiplying both sides by $I(t)=(100-t)^{-4}$ yields
$$\begin{aligned}(100-t)^{-4}\cdot(\frac{dQ}{dt}+\frac{4}{100-t}Q)&=(100-t)^{-4}\cdot 1.44\\
(100-t)^{-4}\frac{dQ}{dt}+4(100-t)^{-5}Q&=1.44(100-t)^{-4}\\\frac{d}{dt}(Q(100-t)^{-4})&=1.44(100-t)^{-4}.\end{aligned}$$
Now, we integrate with respect to $t:$
$$\begin{aligned}\int\frac{d}{dt}(Q(100-t)^{-4})\,dt&=\int 1.44(100-t)^{-4}\,dt\\Q(100-t)^{-4}&=1.44\cdot\frac{1}{3}(100-t)^{-3}+C\\Q(t)&=0.48(100-t)+C(100-t)^{4}.\end{aligned}$$
Note that $C$ is a constant of integration.
The initial condition is $Q(0)=10.$ Thus,
$$\begin{aligned}0.48(100-0)+C(100-0)^{4}&=10\\
48+C(100)^{4}&=10\\C(100)^{4}&=-38\\C&=- \frac{19}{50000000}.\end{aligned}$$
Finally, we obtain the following expression for $Q(t):$
$$Q(t)=- \frac{12 t}{25} - \frac{19 \left(100 - t\right)^{4}}{50000000} + 48.$$
Writing this in the supplied normalized form gives
$$Q(t)=48(1-\frac{t}{100})-38(1-\frac{t}{100})^{4}.$$
Finally, we have $\alpha=48,\beta=-38,$ and
$$\alpha-\beta=48-(-38)=86.$$
