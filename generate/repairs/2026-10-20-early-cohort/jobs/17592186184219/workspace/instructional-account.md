# Instructional account (before repair)

The learner reads the column count in an m by n matrix, distinguishes the supplied dimension from the requested rank or nullity, substitutes in rank(A)+nullity(A)=n, and subtracts the supplied integer. The response is one of five distinct nonnegative integer radio choices. The worked solution states the theorem, identifies n, substitutes the given dimension, isolates the target and evaluates the subtraction. This is easy one-step arithmetic; moderate and hard are unsupported. No row reduction, basis extraction or invertibility inference is added.

Tutorial 0728c510-110b-5bde-93ea-9050a87a6ecc says that pivot and non-pivot columns together give the total number of columns, and explicitly states rank(A)+nullity(A)=n for a matrix that need not be square. The canonical example 9615d8af-b005-5449-be4b-a7487375c98f uses an 8 by 6 matrix with rank 5 to obtain nullity 1. Prerequisite topics are The Rank of a Matrix and The Dimension of the Null Space of a Matrix. Their contextual diagrams are not used in these questions.

Source a05d1874-f484-5b03-a7ba-b2697b84a74b asks rank from nullity; 245a8171-aeb5-5caf-950d-44cbb3c0aece asks nullity from rank. All 14 current questions have this backbone and five-choice contract. Their full observed prompts, banks, correct designations and solutions are retained in runtime-source.json and independently compared in checks. The answer-bearing assignments are the requested rank/nullity role and the correct integer's position within a recognizable numerical bank. Neither is mathematically fixed. The former determines which dimension is supplied; the latter must depend on column count minus that supplied dimension, not on the distractor shape. The old construction translated each bank relative to its original correct value, freezing this latter assignment (e.g. e9ab814e-2358-5160-95c5-d43671e5affe always put the answer second-largest in a consecutive bank). This is a design defect, not a constraint in the lesson.

For new variants choose a target and observed bank shape, then choose any of its five values as the answer before filling compatible n, k and m. Translate the shape within 0..12, independently of that choice. Choose n at least the largest offered value; choose rows so every offered value is feasible as the target dimension. Thus feasibility cannot eliminate all but one choice. Set k=n-c. These constraints depend on the bank, not the chosen answer. The finite bounds keep subtraction comparable to the source (whose n is 4..10); extending to 2..12, endpoints zero and all five answer roles is a conservative same-method extension. Exact replay remains available with explicit source parameters and profile. Explicit non-source parameter paths may translate the chosen bank to the computed answer; all feasible answer roles remain eligible.

Five options and the solution sequence are fixed to preserve the observed response contract and workload. A is fixed because renaming it adds no instructional value. The relation, integer domain and rank<=min(m,n) are mathematical constraints. The choice offsets have no established misconception interpretation; their shapes are observed but changing the correct role is authored. No known source arithmetic errors were found.
