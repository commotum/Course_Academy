Find the general nonzero real solution to the differential equation

(x−6)dydx+y=(x−6)2y3/4.

Work on an interval where x−6>0. Choose constants and intervals on which all displayed real expressions are defined. Take y>0.

Answer · correct value:

y=((x−6)9/4+C9(x−6)1/4)4

First, we rearrange the equation, as follows:

(x−6)dydx+y=(x−6)2y3/4dydx+y(x−6)=(x−6)y3/4

Notice that our equation is an instance of the Bernoulli differential equation

dydx+p(x)y=q(x)yn.

In this case, we have

p(x)=(x−6)−1,q(x)=(x−6),n=34.

Now, we divide the differential equation by y3/4, and we get

y−3/4dydx+(x−6)−1y1/4=(x−6).(∗)

Then, we make the substitution

z=y1−n=y1/4.

Differentiating z(x) using the chain rule, we get

dzdx=14y−3/4dydx⟹y−3/4dydx=4dzdx.

Therefore, equation (∗) can be written as a linear equation in z(x) as follows:

4dzdx+(x−6)−1z=(x−6)

This differential equation is linear, so we can use the method of integrating factors. But first, we write the above equation in standard form:

dzdx+14(x−6)−1z=(x−6)4

The above equation is now in the standard form

dzdx+P(x)z=Q(x),

with

P(x)=14⋅(x−6),Q(x)=(x−6)4.

We then compute an integrating factor:

I(x)=e∫P(x)dx=e∫(14⋅(x−6))dx=e(1/4)ln(x−6)=(x−6)1/4

Multiplying both sides of the standard form equation by I(x)=(x−6)1/4 yields

(x−6)1/4⋅(dzdx+14(x−6)−1z)=(x−6)1/4⋅((x−6)4)(x−6)1/4⋅dzdx+14(x−6)−3/4z=(x−6)5/44ddx(z(x−6)1/4)=(x−6)5/44.

Now, we integrate with respect to x:

∫ddx(z(x−6)1/4)dx=∫(x−6)5/44dxz(x−6)1/4=19(x−6)9/4+C1z=(x−6)9/4+C9(x−6)1/4,

where C=9C1.

Finally, we determine y(x) by writing z=y1/4. This results in

y1/4=(x−6)9/4+C9(x−6)1/4⇒y=((x−6)9/4+C9(x−6)1/4)4.

These are the nonzero real solutions on the specified intervals; exclude denominator zeros and restrict constants and intervals to the real branches used above. Since z=y1/4>0, retain only intervals where the displayed expression for z is positive.
