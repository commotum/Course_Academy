Find the general nonzero real solution to the differential equation

dydx−y=y. Take y>0. For even powers and powers of 2/3 in the choices, retain only intervals where the base is positive. Choose constants and intervals on which the displayed expressions are real and finite.

Solution:

Answer · correct value:

y=(C⋅e(x+1)2−1)2

This is a Bernoulli equation with

p(x)=−1,q(x)=1,n=12.

Divide by y12 and set z=y12. The chain rule gives

dzdx=12⋅ydydx.

Hence the linear equation in standard form is

dzdx+(−12)z=12.

An integrating factor is

I(x)=e∫−12dx=e−(x+1)2.

Multiplying through and using the product rule gives

ddx(e−(x+1)2z)=e−(x+1)22.

Integrating with respect to x, we obtain

e−(x+1)2z=−e−(x+1)2+C.

Thus

z=C⋅e(x+1)2−1.

Finally, substitute z=y12 and solve for y:

y=(C⋅e(x+1)2−1)2.

Here C is an arbitrary real constant; exclude singularities and restrict to the stated real intervals. The displayed expression for z must be positive because z=y12>0.
