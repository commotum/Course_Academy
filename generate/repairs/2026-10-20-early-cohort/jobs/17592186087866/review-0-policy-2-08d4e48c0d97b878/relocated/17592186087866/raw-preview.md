Write the following first-order linear ODE in standard form.

$$
2\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}-3\operatorname{cos}⁡x=1-4y\operatorname{sin}⁡x
$$

- [ ] $\frac{dy}{dx} + (4\cos x)y = 1 + 3\sin x$
- [ ] $\frac{dy}{dx} + (4\sin x)y = 1 + 3\cos x$
- [ ] $\frac{dy}{dx} + \frac{1 + 3\cos x}{2} = (2\sin x)y$
- [ ] $\frac{dy}{dx} + (2\sin x)y = 1 + \frac{3}{2}\cos x$
- [ ] $\frac{dy}{dx} + (2\sin x)y = \frac{1 + 3\cos x}{2}$

- [ ] $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}+(4\operatorname{sin}⁡x)y=1+3\operatorname{cos}⁡x$
- [ ] $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}+(2\operatorname{sin}⁡x)y=1+\frac{3}{2}\operatorname{cos}⁡x$
- [ ] $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}+(4\operatorname{cos}⁡x)y=1+3\operatorname{sin}⁡x$
- [x] $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}+(2\operatorname{sin}⁡x)y=\frac{1+3\operatorname{cos}⁡x}{2}$
- [ ] $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}+\frac{1+3\operatorname{cos}⁡x}{2}=(2\operatorname{sin}⁡x)y$

A first-order linear ODE is in standard form if it is written as

$$
\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}+P(x)y=Q(x).
$$

Converting the given equation to standard form, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}2\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}-3\operatorname{cos}⁡x & =1-4y\operatorname{sin}⁡x \\ 2\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}+4y\operatorname{sin}⁡x & =1+3\operatorname{cos}⁡x \\ \frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}+{{(2\operatorname{sin}⁡x)}_{⏟}}_{P(x)}y & ={{\frac{1+3\operatorname{cos}⁡x}{2}}_{⏟}}_{Q(x)}.\end{aligned}
$$

Now, the equation is written in standard form with $P(x)=2\operatorname{sin}⁡x$ and $Q(x)=\frac{1+3\operatorname{cos}⁡x}{2}.$
