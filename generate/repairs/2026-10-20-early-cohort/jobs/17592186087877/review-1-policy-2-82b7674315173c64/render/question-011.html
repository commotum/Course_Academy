Consider the following first-order linear ODE for the function y=y(x):

dydx+(2)⋅y=2e−2x

Write the equation in the form ddx(yI(x))=I(x)Q(x), where I(x) is an integrating factor.

CorrectPreview only
Solution:

Answer · correct value:

ddx(y⋅(e2x))=(e2x)⋅(2e−2x)

This equation is in the standard form

dydx+P(x)y=Q(x)

with P(x)=2 and Q(x)=2e−2x. We proceed to find an integrating factor I(x) using the definition:

I(x)=e∫P(x)dx=e∫(2)dx=e2x

Now, we multiply our differential equation through by I(x)=e2x as follows:

(e2x)⋅(dydx+(2)⋅y)=(e2x)⋅(2e−2x)(e2x)⋅dydx+(2)⋅(e2x)⋅y=(e2x)⋅(2e−2x)

Finally, we rewrite the left hand side as ddx(y⋅I(x)):

ddx(y⋅(e2x))=(e2x)⋅(2e−2x)
