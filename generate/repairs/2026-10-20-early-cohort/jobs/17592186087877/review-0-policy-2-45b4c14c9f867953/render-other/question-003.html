Consider the following first-order linear ODE for the function y=y(x):

(x2)⋅dydx+(x)⋅y=x2cos⁡(x),x>0

Write the equation in the form ddx(yI(x))=I(x)Q(x), where I(x) is an integrating factor.

Solution:

Answer · correct value:

ddx(y⋅(x))=(x)⋅(cos⁡(x))

First, we write the equation in standard form. This gives

dydx+(1x)⋅y=cos⁡(x).

This equation is in the standard form

dydx+P(x)y=Q(x)

with P(x)=1x and Q(x)=cos⁡(x). We proceed to find an integrating factor I(x) using the definition:

I(x)=e∫P(x)dx=e∫(1x)dx=eln⁡(x)=x

Now, we multiply our differential equation through by I(x)=x as follows:

(x)⋅(dydx+(1x)⋅y)=(x)⋅(cos⁡(x))(x)⋅dydx+(1x)⋅(x)⋅y=xcos⁡(x)

Finally, we rewrite the left hand side as ddx(y⋅I(x)):

ddx(y⋅(x))=xcos⁡(x).
