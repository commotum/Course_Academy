Review topic

An engineer observed that the growth of the number of solar panels installed at a facility is directly proportional to the difference between the facility’s maximum number of solar panels and the current number installed. Let P(t) be the number of solar panels, measured in panels, and let t≥0 be the time in years. If the maximum number of solar panels the facility can support is 156 panels, which differential equation could be used to model the number of solar panels installed? Assume that k>0 is a constant of proportionality.

Choose an answer
CorrectPreview only
Solution:

Answer · correct value:

dPdt=k(156−P)

The rate at which the number of solar panels P(t) installed changes is proportional to the difference between the maximum number of solar panels the facility can support and the current number of solar panels. Therefore, we have the differential equation

dPdt=k(a−P),

where a is the maximum number of solar panels the facility can support, and k>0 is a constant of proportionality.

In our case, a=156 panels, and we have

dPdt=k(156−P).
