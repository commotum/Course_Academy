Review topic

An ecologist found that the growth of the number of frogs in a wetland is directly proportional to the difference between the wetland’s maximum frog population and the current population. Let F(t) be the frog population, measured in number of frogs, and let t≥0 be the time in years. If the maximum frog population the wetland can support is 232 frogs, which differential equation could be used to model the frog population? Assume that k>0 is a constant of proportionality.

Choose an answer
CorrectPreview only
Solution:

Answer · correct value:

dFdt=k(232−F)

The rate at which the frog population F(t) of the wetland changes is proportional to the difference between the maximum frog population the wetland can support and the current frog population. Therefore, we have the differential equation

dFdt=k(a−F),

where a is the maximum frog population the wetland can support, and k>0 is a constant of proportionality.

In our case, a=232 frogs, and we have

dFdt=k(232−F).
