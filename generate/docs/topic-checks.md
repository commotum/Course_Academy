## Substitution and antiderivatives

When the target requires choosing a substitution and completing an integral, easy practice retains both requirements. A supplied-u task under another KP does not establish the same objective. Simplify mathematical instances or arithmetic, or use source-compatible complete-answer choices; do not stop at a transformed integral. Inspect the displayed prompt and every choice, not support metadata.

Calibrate the work of recognizing the inner function, matching its derivative, handling constant factors, changing bounds and evaluating endpoints where those steps belong to this target. Keep derivative notation familiar; do not introduce identities by CAS printing. For inverse-trig integration, small perfect-square normalizations often control incidental work. For exponential substitution, vary supported inner functions and differential relationships rather than only the base. These are design examples, not mandatory parameter restrictions.

Judge difficulty after useful simplification. Cancellation, zero results and zero endpoint contributions remain legitimate when they express the target skill. Exercise supported function/endpoint cases when their differences matter to that skill; do not require a sign, endpoint-count or rank quota in every small batch. Investigate an invariant outcome only when a learner-observable cue bypasses required work. Do not use symmetric bounds as the whole definition of hard or stack fractional amplitude, slope, awkward bounds and unlike radicals to create a label.

Use one derivation to render the final worked result and canonical answer. Show needed logarithm power/quotient conversions and integration-constant redefinitions. Normalize accepted aliases after transformation, deduplicating identical ones. For newly authored antiderivative blanks, prefer the existing external `$+C$` contract, with the key containing only the requested fragment. Check a valid expanded entry that absorbs a back-substitution constant, a wrong nonconstant term and a changed-domain response with the native grader.

Distractors should be plausible for the actual instance: omitted differential factors, normalization mistakes, primitive/endpoint errors or incorrect bound subtraction. A copied setup or arbitrary integer offset is not a sufficient fallback. A colliding source recipe requires a different valid bank before it requires excluding the mathematical case. Probe numerical rank, unique sign/shape and intersections of modal coefficients within recognizable families; preserve legitimate mathematical reasoning and use the principles’ evidence threshold.

## Equilibrium candidates

For a task assessing constant equilibrium candidates after rearrangement, preserve useful factors/shifts and the original domain, form one consistent residual, and test candidates against it. Avoid gratuitous expansion, repeated equivalent work or adding the same irrelevant expression to both sides just to cancel. Preserve a different source-supported method if it serves the same target and workload.

If a bank is meant to require both domain checking and equilibrium testing, include valid candidates that still require the equilibrium test; domain exclusions alone must not answer that task. Do not turn that example into a universal rule for a different domain-recognition objective. Keep the equilibrium criterion and required conversions in the solution when recalling them is assessed.

## Matrix calculations and classification

A source-supported partial output matrix can reduce repeated calculations while preserving the learner’s row/column choices for missing entries. In ordinary multiplication practice, put expanded dot products and procedural reminders in the solution, not the easy prompt. Preserve the meaning of supplied noninput cells: for KP 17592186115226, actual remaining values must not become asterisks with an invented “unrequested cell” convention.

Change a matrix’s relevant entries or zero structure and recompute properties and keys; do not preserve the source truth vector merely to reuse prose. When several objects can qualify independently, repairing an always-qualifying label must still permit legitimate combinations such as A+C. For offered-operation tasks, assess correctness among the offered operations; do not add global minimality unless the target explicitly asks for it.

## Tables, graphs and diagrams

For table-to-graph recognition, distinguish unmarked answer curves from worked graphs with calculated table points. Derive a common scale from the whole bank independently of which graph is correct. Choose a grid that makes required coordinates and candidate differences readable at the actual display size; automatic ticks and unique mathematical loci are insufficient. Do not mark the correct table points or add construction guides to choices to compensate for poor readability. Preserve source-supported overlays in the solution. Rebuild or resample visually unresolved banks.

Use [plot extraction](5-plot-extraction.md) and [plot generation](7-plot-generator.md). Inspect the companion table/formulas on narrow layouts as well as the curves; split long tables into small panels with repeated headings while preserving each cell’s parameter association.

## Focused calculator and rounding tasks

A numerical progression may retain the same formula and reasoning: convenient whole-number arithmetic, less automatic products, then one simple decimal measurement. This is an authored design example, not empirical calibration or a requirement to use those exact number forms. Keep calculator access and rounding conventions consistent; do not supply an intermediate area or replace the target formula with a right-angle shortcut to create easy, or add unrelated skills to create hard.

If calculator change of base is the target, log_a(a), log_a(1), common powers and base 10 can eliminate that operation. Include such cases only when identity recognition belongs to the objective; do not insert them to balance regions. For rounding, test equivalent numeral spellings such as 1 and 1.000, nearby wrong values and explicit significant-figure contracts through the real grader. An intended form requirement is different from an unintended formatting penalty.

For a rounded final result, keep full precision until the final step. Compare bank spacing with complete imported banks in units of the required precision. Do not manufacture tiny offsets or adjacent cents/integers merely to fill a bank when rounding is incidental. Prefer plausible calculation mistakes or a supported independently sampled bank, and inspect all choices after rounding for collisions. Repair the bank or resample when distinct meaningful choices cannot be produced; do not claim that exact bank size or separation has a universal pedagogical value outside its response contract.
