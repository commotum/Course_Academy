## Easy

Consider the following differential equation.

$$
{y}^{′}=\frac{y}{{x}^{2}},\,x\ne 0
$$

 

Let $f(x)=-\frac{1}{{x}^{2}}.$ Which of the following statements are true?

Select all that apply.

- **I.** ${f}^{′}(x)=-\frac{2}{{x}^{3}}$
- **II.** Substituting $y=f(x)$ and ${y}^{′}={f}^{′}(x)$ into the differential equation gives a true statement
- **III.** The function $y=f(x)$ is a solution of the differential equation

Author key:
- I: false
- II: false
- III: false

For a function $f(x)$ to be a solution to a differential equation, it must satisfy the equation for all values of $x$ in an interval where the function is defined and the equation is valid.

With that in mind, let's check each statement in turn.

- Statement I is false. Differentiating $f(x)=-\frac{1}{{x}^{2}},$ we get 

$$
\begin{aligned}{f}^{′}(x) & ={(-\frac{1}{{x}^{2}})}^{′} \\  & =\frac{2}{{x}^{3}} \\  & \ne -\frac{2}{{x}^{3}}.\end{aligned}
$$

- Statement II is false. Substituting $y=-\frac{1}{{x}^{2}}$ and ${y}^{′}=\frac{2}{{x}^{3}}$ into the original equation, we get a false statement:

$$
\begin{aligned}{y}^{′} & \overset{?}{=}\frac{y}{{x}^{2}} \\ \frac{2}{{x}^{3}} & \overset{?}{=}\frac{(-\frac{1}{{x}^{2}})}{{x}^{2}} \\ \frac{2}{{x}^{3}} & \ne -\frac{1}{{x}^{4}}\,\times \end{aligned}
$$

- Statement III is false. Since statement II is false, the function $y=-\frac{1}{{x}^{2}}$ is not a solution to our differential equation.

Therefore, the correct answer is "None."

- **I.** ${f}^{′}(x)=2{e}^{2x}$
- **II.** Substituting $y=f(x)$ and ${y}^{′}={f}^{′}(x)$ into the differential equation gives a true statement
- **III.** The function $y=f(x)$ is a solution of the differential equation

Author key:

- II: true
- III: true

With that in mind, let's check each statement in turn.

- Statement I is true. Differentiating $f(x)={e}^{2x},$ we get 

$$

## Hard

Consider the following differential equation.

$$
{y}^{′}=-12{e}^{-y/(3)}
$$

Let $f(x)=3 \ln{\left(4 x - 4 \right)},$ on $4 x - 4>0.$ Which of the following statements are true?

Select all that apply.

- **I.** ${f}^{′}(x)=\frac{12}{4 x - 4}$
- **II.** Substituting $y=f(x)$ and ${y}^{′}={f}^{′}(x)$ into the differential equation gives a true statement
- **III.** The function $y=f(x)$ is a solution of the differential equation

Author key:
- I: true
- II: false
- III: false

For a function $f(x)$ to be a solution to a differential equation, it must satisfy the equation for all values of $x$ in an interval where the function is defined and the equation is valid.

With that in mind, let's check each statement in turn.

- Statement I is true. Differentiating $f(x)=3 \ln{\left(4 x - 4 \right)},$ we get 

$$
\begin{aligned}{f}^{′}(x) & ={(3 \ln{\left(4 x - 4 \right)})}^{′} \\ & =3\cdot \frac{4}{4 x - 4} \\ & =\frac{12}{4 x - 4}.\end{aligned}
$$

- Statement II is false. Substituting $y=3 \ln{\left(4 x - 4 \right)}$ and ${y}^{′}=\frac{12}{4 x - 4}$ into the original equation, we get a false statement:

$$
\begin{aligned}{y}^{′} & \overset{?}{=}-12{e}^{-y/(3)} \\ \frac{12}{4 x - 4} & \overset{?}{=}-12{e}^{-(3 \ln{\left(4 x - 4 \right)})/(3)} \\ \frac{12}{4 x - 4} & \overset{?}{=}-12{e}^{-\ln(4 x - 4)} \\ \frac{12}{4 x - 4} & \ne - \frac{12}{4 x - 4}\,\times \end{aligned}
$$

- Statement III is false. Since statement II is false, the function $y=3 \ln{\left(4 x - 4 \right)}$ is not a solution to our differential equation.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only."
