Consider the following differential equation.

$$
{y}^{′}=\frac{4y}{x},\,x\ne 0
$$

 

Let $f(x)={x}^{4}.$ Which of the following statements are true?

Select all that apply.

- **I.** ${f}^{′}(x)=5{x}^{5}.$
- **II.** Substituting $y=f(x)$ and ${y}^{′}={f}^{′}(x)$ into the differential equation gives a true statement.
- **III.** The function $y=f(x)$ is a solution of the differential equation.

Author key:
- I: false
- II: true
- III: true

For a function $f(x)$ to be a solution to a differential equation, it must satisfy the equation for all values of $x$ in the equation's domain.

With that in mind, let's check each statement in turn.

- Statement I is false. Differentiating $f(x)={x}^{4},$ we get 

$$
\begin{aligned}{f}^{′}(x) & =({x}^{4})^{′} \\  & =4{x}^{3} \\  & \ne 5{x}^{5}.\end{aligned}
$$

- Statement II is true. Substituting $y={x}^{4}$ and ${y}^{′}=4{x}^{3}$ into the original equation, we get a true statement:

$$
\begin{aligned}{y}^{′} & \overset{?}{=}\frac{4y}{x} \\ (4{x}^{3}) & \overset{?}{=}\frac{4({x}^{4})}{x} \\ 4{x}^{3} & =4{x}^{3}\,✓\end{aligned}
$$

- Statement III is true. Since statement II is true, the function $y={x}^{4}$ is a solution to our differential equation.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II and III only."
