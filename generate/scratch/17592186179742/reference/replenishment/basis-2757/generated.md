Consider the vector field $F(x,y)$ shown above. Let $C$ be the path where the elliptic arc is traversed from $A$ to $B.$ Which of the following statements are true?

- ${\int }_{C}F\cdot \text{d}r<0$

- ${\int }_{C}F\cdot \text{d}r=0$

- ${\int }_{-C}F\cdot \text{d}r<0$

Select all that apply.

Remember that ${\int }_{C}F\cdot \text{d}r$ gives the total work done by a force $F$ in moving a particle along $C:$

- If the force works *with* the motion of the particle (i.e., the force helps to push the particle along the path), then ${\int }_{C}F\cdot \text{d}r>0.$

- If the force works *against* the motion  of the particle (i.e., the force impedes the motion of the particle as it moves along the path), then ${\int }_{C}F\cdot \text{d}r<0.$

With that in mind, let's check each statement.

- Statement I is false, while statement II is true. Since the force $F$ acts perpendicular to the motion of the particle as it moves from $A$ to $B,$ it neither helps nor hinders its motion.

![](images/dc/dcc2f364085ccca85e6e6ddd8a5affac51c84f774281b817d4852bd2322bcb1b.png)

So, ${\int }_{C}F\cdot \text{d}r=0$.

- Statement III is false. If we reverse the orientation of $C,$ the force is still perpendicular to the motion.

![](images/50/508e0ac5f67d7b119ad689ccaede8894268eefe31d9b00f148ddd6bf851de6b4.png)

So, ${\int }_{-C}F\cdot \text{d}r=0$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II only."
