# Circular-cap polar integral extraction

The exact per-question descriptions and original parameter values are in `classification.md` and `reference/initial/source.json`; all source option values and complete recipes are retained in `components.json`. Each source's original scale is 1. The typed `DATA` and `BANKS` sections of `../17592186157298.py` are the executable extraction: they contain every circle center, radius, line normal/offset, angular interval, Cartesian integrand and five full integral tuples. Values are exact SymPy expressions, never rounded angles.

The shared instructional purpose is to express an integral over a depicted circular cap in polar coordinates. Each template supplies a circle, a cutting line, the selected shaded portion and a fixed integrand. It asks for one complete iterated integral, preserving a five-choice radio response. The ordered stages are the change-of-variables formula, disk center/radius, coordinate equations, circle substitution (including factoring r for a circle through the origin), line substitution/isolation, angular endpoints, region inequalities, substituted integrand with Jacobian, and final integral. One concrete solution is rendered independently of learner choice. Source typos identified in classification are corrected in generated instances, supported by the original equations and final keys.

Only the **positive geometric homothety** is variable. It scales the circle center and radius, cutting-line offset, Cartesian coordinates on plots and radial limits together. The integrand, circle/line mathematical roles, angular sector, response contract, option count and ordered solution stages are fixed per template. Scaling does not change which cap or which half-cap is requested. This conservative generalization preserves the method. Source scales are observed; integer multipliers 2 through 12 are a narrower authored sampling policy, not a claim that all source measurements are integers. Scale 1 is retained for replay. The bounded package contains 132 mathematical tasks (11 sources × 12 scales), including the 11 source-equivalent replays; normal sampling exposes 121 new candidates. Larger scales or geometric rotations require new validation.

Represent each choice as `(theta_low, theta_high, radial_low, radial_high, angular_factor, radial_power)` and render

`integral(theta_low..theta_high) angular_factor * integral(radial_low..radial_high) r^radial_power dr dtheta`.

This preserves whole formal integral objects, including source mistakes that swap radial and angular bounds, alter both the Jacobian and angular factor, discard the line boundary, change the selected quadrant, or replace reciprocal trigonometric bounds. Compound errors remain exact tuple recipes with their diagnostic interpretation unresolved. A changed upper angular limit equal to a radius in q-83980 and swapped bounds in q-85065 are parameterized from the corresponding scaled geometric measurements. No recipe is replaced by a plausible alternative. Every original bank is replayed, with a bijection of all five full objects and its designated correct answer. Notation normalization is separate from equivalence. The assessed response must represent the stated region and transformed density; equality of accidentally cancelling definite integral values over different regions does not establish setup equivalence.

The correct radial lower bound is `d/(N·(cosθ,sinθ))`. The upper bound is the radius for origin-centered disks, or `2 C·(cosθ,sinθ)` for disks through the origin. The latter follows from `|C|²=R²`. On each declared angular interval the radial limits are positive and ordered. Integrands with x in a denominator remain away from x=0 throughout their declared regions. The square-root density uses r≥0 to reduce √(r²) to r. The Jacobian is r. All dependent values derive from the same template and scale.

No geometric target position is declared variable. Orientation, selected quadrant, and integrand are instructional constraints within a template; arbitrary target movement could change the response bank and solution stages. Validation therefore covers the entire scalar slot domain and controlled changed-scale examples, rather than claiming positional variation. Distinctness compares the actual circle, cutting line, angular sector and integrand, independent of random seed, UUID and option ordering. Geometry changes with scale; no identity-only or choice-order variant is counted.
