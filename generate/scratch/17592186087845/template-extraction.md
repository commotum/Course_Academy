### q-113547 (:question.difficulty/moderate)

Find all of the vertical asymptotes of the function $f(x)=\frac{x+1}{{x}^{3}+{x}^{2}+x+1}.$

Fields: 0; worked solution present.

To find the vertical asymptotes of a rational function, we factor the numerator and denominator and cancel any common factors. Then, we set the denominator equal to zero and solve for $x.$

Factoring the numerator and denominator and canceling any common factors, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}f(x) & =\frac{x+1}{{x}^{3}+{x}^{2}+x+1} \\  & =\frac{x+1}{{x}^{2}(x+1)+(x+1)} \\  & =\frac{x+1}{(x+1)({x}^{2}+1)} \\  & =\frac{\cancel{x+1}}{\cancel{(x+1)}({x}^{2}+1)} \\  & =\frac{1}{{x}^{2}+1}\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we set the denominator equal to zero:

$$
{x}^{2}+1=0
$$

This equation does not have any real solutions. Therefore, $f(x)$ does not have any vertical asymptotes.
