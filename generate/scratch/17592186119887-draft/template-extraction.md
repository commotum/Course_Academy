## 1. Original-specific components

The exact immutable originals are in `source.json`; `source-components.json` assigns every original field and option its exact captured value, source identity, complete-bank profile and public parameterized recipe. Review includes each original and its changed-parameter evidence bank. The table in classification records each specific surface, prompt input syntax, fields and absence. All variables are real. Source functions include sin, cos, exponential, bilinear products and powers two/three; these operators and exponents remain fixed. Coordinate offsets `+1` in q-109911 remain fixed since their derivatives vanish and they supply no meaningful practice variation.

Source q-167648 uses `x∈[0,1]`, `θ∈[0,2π)` and an already computed product. Its source full solution establishes that product as `<8x³,-2x² cos θ,-2x² sin θ>`. The surface is singular at x=0; the algebraic norm formula remains valid there. No regularity condition is silently invented for the norm calculation. Other surfaces have no captured parameter restrictions; their cross expressions are algebraically valid everywhere and geometric regularity holds precisely where the product is nonzero.

## 2. Generalized mathematical backbone

For the six vector families, multiply the original complete coordinate functions by positive integer coefficients `(a,b,c)`. Derive both three-coordinate partial vectors with the other independent variable held fixed, then compute `r_u × r_v`, in that order. Preserve the source's formal coordinate partial row, substituted complete-coordinate row, evaluated partial row, 3×3 determinant, signed 2×2 cofactor vector, and the source-specific final vector. q-105607 is absent-source authoring based on the canonical example; it also displays unsimplified determinant products and the outside negative middle-cofactor sign before collection.

For the existing norm family the surface scales are `(a,b,b)`, preserving circular radial coefficients. Its supplied product is `<8b²x³,-2abx² cos θ,-2abx² sin θ>`. Compute its scalar Euclidean norm. The norm stages preserve source squaring, expanded squares, trigonometric common factor, identity reduction, and collected square-root result `2b x² sqrt(16b² x²+a²)`. Positive b and real x make the outside `x²` nonnegative even for negative x. Source raw replay retains all prior full-solution stages; normal output retains only the norm stages because the product was already found in the previous step.

Each source bank is a complete profile, selected before parameters. A source vector option `<P,Q,R>` maps to `<bcP,acQ,abR>` under diagonal coordinate scaling. No arbitrary fit or source-only literal payload branch occurs: every option is a complete scalar-slot template under the public generate API, and changed coefficients are tested. All original banks, including unkeyed/duplicate banks, are retained.

## Native grading boundary

The application's memory-only `learning::grade_field` accepts canonical and reordered factored scalar norms and rejects scalar zero. Its supported equivalence rules leave `sqrt(64x^6+4x^4)` unresolved despite exact real mathematics. This is a documented grading-form restriction, not an algebraic source error. The normal blank retains the original expression shape and meaningful accepted reordered forms; no answer equation or vector fragment is substituted, and the engine is unchanged. All six actually keyed original fields are separately graded; the two missing original refs are never fabricated for raw native fixtures.

Changed evaluated tangent/cross rows collect the actual coefficient products. Expanded cross sums remain expanded, and the norm cross common-factor row retains its grouped trigonometric sum with its changed leading coefficient. Anchor spelling remains exact; changed evaluated scalar expressions use conventional SymPy TeX notation. Determinant cells and substituted partial arguments retain explicitly grouped factors until their evaluated stages.
