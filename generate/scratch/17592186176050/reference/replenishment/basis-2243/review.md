If $\tan x = -16$, for some value of $x$, find the value of $\tan (x + 1π)$.

A. $16$
B. $-\frac{1}{16}$
C. $-16$
D. $-16 + \pi$
E. $\frac{1}{16}$

The period of $y=\tan x$ is $\pi .$ Therefore, for integer $n$ we have

$$
\tan x=\tan (x+n\pi ).
$$

Substituting $n=1$ into the right-hand-side of the above gives

$$
\begin{aligned}\tan x=\tan (x+1\cdot \pi )=\tan (x+1\pi ).\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, if $\tan x=-16,$ we get that $\tan (x+1\pi )=-16.$

If $\tan x=-\frac{1}{2},$ for some $x$, find the value of $\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)\tan (x+1\pi )-1.$

A. $\frac{5}{4}$
B. $\frac{1}{4}$
C. $-\frac{3}{4}$
D. $-\frac{\pi}{2} -\frac{3}{4}$
E. $0$

The period of $y=\tan x$ is $\pi .$ Therefore, for any integer $n,$ we have

$$
\tan x=\tan (x+n\pi ).
$$

Substituting $n=1$ into the above gives

$$
\begin{aligned}\tan x=\tan (x+1\cdot \pi )=\tan (x+1\pi ).\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\tan x=-\frac{1}{2},$ we get that $\tan (x+1\pi )=-\frac{1}{2}.$ Therefore

$$
\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)\tan (x+1\pi )-1=-\frac{1}{2}(-\frac{1}{2})-1=\frac{1}{4}-1=-\frac{3}{4}.
$$
