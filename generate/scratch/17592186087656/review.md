What is the center and radius of the circle with equation $(x-2)^{2}+(y-3)^{2}=6?$

- The center is $(2,3)$ and radius is $6$
- The center is $(-2,-3)$ and radius is $6$
- The center is $(0,0)$ and radius is $\sqrt{6}$
- The center is $(-2,-3)$ and radius is $\sqrt{6}$
- The center is $(2,3)$ and radius is $\sqrt{6}$

**Observed correct:** The center is $(2,3)$ and radius is $\sqrt{6}$

The general equation of a circle with radius $r$ centered at the point $(a,b)$ is 

$$
(x-a)^{2}+(y-b)^{2}={r}^{2}.
$$

Let's compare the general equation to our equation side-by-side as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}(x-a)^{2}+(y-b)^{2} & ={r}^{2} \\ (x-2)^{2}+(y-3)^{2} & =6 \\ (x-2)^{2}+(y-3)^{2} & =(\sqrt{6})^{2}.\end{aligned}
$$

We see that $a=2,$ $b=3,$ and $r=\sqrt{6}.$ 

Therefore, the circle is centered at $(2,3)$ and its radius is $\sqrt{6}.$
