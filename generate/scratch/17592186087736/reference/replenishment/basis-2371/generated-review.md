$$

**Worked solution**

Let's consider each variable in turn.

- To find the range of $x,$ notice that for $0\le \theta <2\pi,$ we have $-1\le \operatorname{cos}\theta\le 1.$ Consequently,

$$
\begin{aligned}-1 & \le \operatorname{cos}\theta \le 1 \\ \frac{3}{4} & \text{ }\ge -\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{cos}\theta \text{ }\ge -\frac{3}{4} \\ -\frac{3}{4} & \le -\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{cos}\theta \le \frac{3}{4} \\ -\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{4} & \le -\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{cos}\theta +\frac{1}{4}\le \frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{4} \\ -\frac{1}{2} & \le x\le 1.\end{aligned}
$$

So, we can conclude that $-\frac{1}{2}\le x\le 1.$

- To find the range of $y,$ notice that for $0\le \theta <2\pi,$ we have $-1\le \operatorname{sin}\theta\le 1.$ Consequently,

$$
\begin{aligned}-1 & \le \operatorname{sin}\theta \le 1 \\ \frac{1}{2} & \text{ }\ge -\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sin}\theta \text{ }\ge -\frac{1}{2} \\ -\frac{1}{2} & \le -\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sin}\theta \le \frac{1}{2} \\ -\frac{1}{2}-\frac{7}{3} & \le -\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sin}\theta -\frac{7}{3}\le \frac{1}{2}-\frac{7}{3} \\ -\frac{17}{6} & \le y\le -\frac{11}{6}.\end{aligned}
$$

So, we can conclude that $-\frac{17}{6}\le y\le -\frac{11}{6}.$
