## raw original evidence — raw:q-124608

The point $A$ is located in the first quadrant and lies on the unit circle. The $y$-coordinate of $A$ is $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}.$ What is the $x$-coordinate of $A?$

- $\frac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$
- $\frac{\sqrt{7}}{3}$
- $-\frac{\sqrt{11}}{9}$
- $\frac{\sqrt{11}}{3}$
- $-\frac{\sqrt{6}}{9}$

Since the point $A$ lies on the unit circle, it must satisfy the Pythagorean theorem: 

$$
{x}^{2}+{y}^{2}=1
$$

Substituting $y=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}$ into the above and solving for $x$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}{x}^{2}+{y}^{2} & =1 \\ {x}^{2}+{(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3})}^{2} & =1 \\ {x}^{2}+(\frac{2}{9}) & =1 \\ {x}^{2} & =1-\frac{2}{9} \\ {x}^{2} & =\frac{7}{9} \\ x & =\sqrt{\frac{7}{9}} \\ x & =\pm \frac{\sqrt{7}}{3}.\end{aligned}
$$

Since $A$ lies in the first quadrant, $x$ must be positive, so we select the positive answer. Therefore, our final answer is $x=\frac{\sqrt{7}}{3}.$
