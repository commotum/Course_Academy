If a curve $C$ is given by $y=(- 3 x)\cdot (\ln{\left(x \right)})$, calculate the slope of the tangent line to the curve at the point where $x=e$.

Slope $=$ {{field-1}}

- $-6$

We differentiate using the product rule. Let $u(x)=- 3 x$ and $v(x)=\ln{\left(x \right)}.$ Then, we have

$$
u'(x)=-3,\quad v'(x)=\frac{1}{x}.
$$

Applying the product rule gives

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx} &=uv'(x)+vu'(x) \\ &=(- 3 x)\cdot (\frac{1}{x})+(\ln{\left(x \right)})\cdot (-3) \\ &=- 3 \left(\ln{\left(x \right)} + 1\right).\end{aligned}
$$

To find the slope of the tangent line, we now substitute $x=e$ into the formula and get

$$
\begin{aligned}\left.\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\right|_{x=e} &=(- 3 (e))\cdot (\frac{1}{(e)})+(\ln{\left((e) \right)})\cdot (-3) \\ &=(- 3 e)\cdot (e^{-1})+(1)\cdot (-3) \\ &=-6.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the slope of the tangent line is $-6.$

$$

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx} &=uv'(x)+vu'(x) \\ &=(e^{x})\cdot (1)+(x + 4)\cdot (e^{x}) \\ &=\left(x + 5\right) e^{x}.\end{aligned}

$$

If a curve $C$ is given by $y=(x^{3})\cdot (\sin{\left(x \right)})$, calculate the slope of the tangent line to the curve at the point where $x=\frac{3 \pi}{2}$.

- $- \frac{81 \pi^{3}}{8}$
- $- \frac{9 \pi^{2}}{4}$
- $\frac{27 \pi^{2}}{4}$
- $- \frac{27 \pi^{2}}{4}$
- $- \frac{27 \pi^{3}}{8}$

We differentiate using the product rule. Let $u(x)=x^{3}$ and $v(x)=\sin{\left(x \right)}.$ Then, we have

$$
u'(x)=3 x^{2},\quad v'(x)=\cos{\left(x \right)}.
$$

Applying the product rule gives

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx} &=uv'(x)+vu'(x) \\ &=(x^{3})\cdot (\cos{\left(x \right)})+(\sin{\left(x \right)})\cdot (3 x^{2}) \\ &=x^{2} \left(x \cos{\left(x \right)} + 3 \sin{\left(x \right)}\right).\end{aligned}
$$

To find the slope of the tangent line, we now substitute $x=\frac{3 \pi}{2}$ into the formula and get

$$
\begin{aligned}\left.\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\right|_{x=\frac{3 \pi}{2}} &=((\frac{3 \pi}{2})^{3})\cdot (\cos{\left((\frac{3 \pi}{2}) \right)})+(\sin{\left((\frac{3 \pi}{2}) \right)})\cdot (3 (\frac{3 \pi}{2})^{2}) \\ &=(\frac{27 \pi^{3}}{8})\cdot (0)+(-1)\cdot (\frac{27 \pi^{2}}{4}) \\ &=- \frac{27 \pi^{2}}{4}.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the slope of the tangent line is $- \frac{27 \pi^{2}}{4}.$

If a curve $C$ is given by $y=(- 4 x^{2} - x - 1)\cdot (\sin{\left(x \right)})$, calculate the slope of the tangent line to the curve at the point where $x=\frac{3 \pi}{4}$.

Slope $=$ {{field-1}}

- $\frac{3 \sqrt{2} \pi \left(-7 + 3 \pi\right)}{8}$

We differentiate using the product rule. Let $u(x)=- 4 x^{2} - x - 1$ and $v(x)=\sin{\left(x \right)}.$ Then, we have

$$
u'(x)=- 8 x - 1,\quad v'(x)=\cos{\left(x \right)}.
$$

Applying the product rule gives

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx} &=uv'(x)+vu'(x) \\ &=(- 4 x^{2} - x - 1)\cdot (\cos{\left(x \right)})+(\sin{\left(x \right)})\cdot (- 8 x - 1) \\ &=- 4 x^{2} \cos{\left(x \right)} - 8 x \sin{\left(x \right)} - x \cos{\left(x \right)} - \sin{\left(x \right)} - \cos{\left(x \right)}.\end{aligned}
$$

To find the slope of the tangent line, we now substitute $x=\frac{3 \pi}{4}$ into the formula and get

$$
\begin{aligned}\left.\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\right|_{x=\frac{3 \pi}{4}} &=(- 4 (\frac{3 \pi}{4})^{2} - (\frac{3 \pi}{4}) - 1)\cdot (\cos{\left((\frac{3 \pi}{4}) \right)})+(\sin{\left((\frac{3 \pi}{4}) \right)})\cdot (- 8 (\frac{3 \pi}{4}) - 1) \\ &=(- \frac{9 \pi^{2}}{4} - \frac{3 \pi}{4} - 1)\cdot (- \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2})+(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2})\cdot (- 6 \pi - 1) \\ &=\frac{3 \sqrt{2} \pi \left(-7 + 3 \pi\right)}{8}.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the slope of the tangent line is $\frac{3 \sqrt{2} \pi \left(-7 + 3 \pi\right)}{8}.$

If a curve $C$ is given by $y=(x^{2} - 2 x + 4)\cdot (\sin{\left(x \right)})$, calculate the slope of the tangent line to the curve at the point where $x=\frac{5 \pi}{4}$.

- $\frac{\sqrt{2} \left(- 25 \pi^{2} - 32\right)}{32}$

We differentiate using the product rule. Let $u(x)=x^{2} - 2 x + 4$ and $v(x)=\sin{\left(x \right)}.$ Then, we have

$$
u'(x)=2 x - 2,\quad v'(x)=\cos{\left(x \right)}.
$$

Applying the product rule gives

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx} &=uv'(x)+vu'(x) \\ &=(x^{2} - 2 x + 4)\cdot (\cos{\left(x \right)})+(\sin{\left(x \right)})\cdot (2 x - 2) \\ &=x^{2} \cos{\left(x \right)} + 2 x \sin{\left(x \right)} - 2 x \cos{\left(x \right)} - 2 \sin{\left(x \right)} + 4 \cos{\left(x \right)}.\end{aligned}
$$

To find the slope of the tangent line, we now substitute $x=\frac{5 \pi}{4}$ into the formula and get

$$
\begin{aligned}\left.\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\right|_{x=\frac{5 \pi}{4}} &=((\frac{5 \pi}{4})^{2} - 2 (\frac{5 \pi}{4}) + 4)\cdot (\cos{\left((\frac{5 \pi}{4}) \right)})+(\sin{\left((\frac{5 \pi}{4}) \right)})\cdot (2 (\frac{5 \pi}{4}) - 2) \\ &=(- \frac{5 \pi}{2} + 4 + \frac{25 \pi^{2}}{16})\cdot (- \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2})+(- \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2})\cdot (-2 + \frac{5 \pi}{2}) \\ &=\frac{\sqrt{2} \left(- 25 \pi^{2} - 32\right)}{32}.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the slope of the tangent line is $\frac{\sqrt{2} \left(- 25 \pi^{2} - 32\right)}{32}.$
