$\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}(4cos⁡x+2sin⁡x)=$

- $4cos⁡x+2sin⁡x$
- $2cos⁡x+4sin⁡x$
- $2cos⁡x-4sin⁡x$
- $-4cos⁡x-2sin⁡x$
- $4cos⁡x-2sin⁡x$

We know that $\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}(sin⁡x)=cos⁡x,$ and that $\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}(cos⁡x)=-sin⁡x.$ Applying these formulas, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}(4cos⁡x+2sin⁡x) & =4(-sin⁡x)+2(cos⁡x) \\  & =2cos⁡x-4sin⁡x.\end{aligned}
$$

## q-1900

What is the slope of the tangent line to the curve $y=2x-5cos⁡x$ at the point $x=\frac{\pi }{6}?$

- $\frac{11}{2}$
- $\frac{21}{5}$
- $-\frac{21}{6}$
- $\frac{9}{2}$
- $\frac{9}{4}$

The slope at $x=\frac{\pi }{6}$ is given by the value of the derivative ${y}^{′}$ at $x=\frac{\pi }{6}.$ 

First, we compute the derivative, using the fact that $\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}(cos⁡x)=-sin⁡x.$ We get

$$
\begin{aligned}{y}^{′}(x) & =\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}(2x-5cos⁡x) \\  & =2-5(-sin⁡x) \\  & =2+5sin⁡x.\end{aligned}
$$

Evaluating at $x=\frac{\pi }{6},$ we get

$$
\begin{aligned}{y}^{′}(\frac{\pi }{6}) & =2+5sin⁡(\frac{\pi }{6}) \\  & =2+5(\frac{1}{2}) \\  & =2+\frac{5}{2} \\  & =\frac{9}{2}.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,  the instantaneous rate of change is $\frac{9}{2}.$

## independent-moderate

What is the slope of the tangent line to the curve $y=6 x - 3 \cos{\left(x \right)}$ at the point $x=\frac{\pi}{3}$?

Slope:
{{field-1}}

- $\frac{3 \sqrt{3}}{2} + 6$

Use $\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}(\sin x)=\cos x$ and $\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}(\cos x)=-\sin x$, with the sum, constant multiple, and power rules.

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} &= \frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}(6 x - 3 \cos{\left(x \right)}) \\ &= (3 \sin{\left(x \right)})+(6) \\ &= 3 \sin{\left(x \right)} + 6.\end{aligned}
$$

Evaluating at $x=\frac{\pi}{3},$

$$
\begin{aligned}y'(\frac{\pi}{3}) &= \frac{3 \sqrt{3}}{2} + 6 \\ &= 6+(3)(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}) \\ &= \frac{3 \sqrt{3}}{2} + 6.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the slope is $\frac{3 \sqrt{3}}{2} + 6.$
