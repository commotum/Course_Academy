Which of the following equations is a Bernoulli differential equation?

Work on intervals where $x>0$ and $y>0$, and where the displayed coefficients are defined.

- **I.** $y' + \frac{y^{3}}{x^{2}} = 5 \cdot \sin{\left(y \right)}$
- **II.** $y' - 2 \cdot y \cdot e^{x} = 2 \cdot x^{2} \cdot y^{5}$
- **III.** $y' + 3 \cdot y^{4} \cdot \log{\left(x \right)} = - x \cdot y^{3}$

Select all that apply. If none apply, leave all boxes unchecked.

A Bernoulli differential equation has the form

$$
y'+p(x)y=q(x)y^n.
$$

With that in mind, let's examine the given options. Work on the stated positive domain, so dividing by powers of $y$ is valid.

- Equation I is *not* a Bernoulli differential equation. The normalized equation contains a function of $y$ that is not a single power of $y$.

- Equation II is a Bernoulli differential equation. In this case, we have

$$
p(x)=- 2 \cdot e^{x},\quad q(x)=2 \cdot x^{2},\quad n=5.
$$

- Equation III is *not* a Bernoulli differential equation. The normalized equation contains two different powers of $y$, neither with exponent 1.

Therefore, select II.
