Which of the following lines is perpendicular to $y=-\frac{5}{7}x+6?$

- $y=-\frac{7}{5}x-4$
- $y=\frac{7}{5}x-9$ **Correct**
- $y=3$
- $y=-\frac{5}{7}x+12$
- $y=\frac{5}{7}x-10$

**Worked solution**

For the two lines to be perpendicular, their slopes must multiply to $-1.$

The line $y=-\frac{5}{7}x+6$ is in slope-intercept form, and its slope is $-\frac{5}{7}.$

Looking at the answer choices, the line $y=\frac{7}{5}x-9$ has a slope of $\frac{7}{5}.$

Multiplying the slopes together, we have

$$
-\frac{5}{7}\cdot \frac{7}{5}=-1.
$$

Since the slopes multiply to $-1,$ we conclude that the line $y=\frac{7}{5}x-9$ is perpendicular to the given line.
