## q-10871 scale 1

Find where the normal line to the curve $f(x)=2{x}^{2}+x$ at the point $(1,3)$ intersects the $x$-axis.

Taking the derivative, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}{f}^{′}(x)=\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}(2{x}^{2}+x)=4x+1.\end{aligned}
$$

So the slope of the tangent line is

$$
m={f}^{′}(1)=4(1)+1=5,
$$

and therefore the slope of the normal line is

$$
{m}^{′}=-\frac{1}{m}=-\frac{1}{5}.
$$

Finally, the equation of the normal line is

$$
\begin{aligned}y-3 & =-\frac{1}{5}(x-1) \\ y & =-\frac{1}{5}x+\frac{1}{5}+3 \\ y & =-\frac{1}{5}x+\frac{16}{5}.\end{aligned}
$$

Lastly, we find the point of intersection with the $x$-axis by setting $y=0$ and solving the resulting equation. We get

$$
\begin{aligned}-\frac{1}{5}x+\frac{16}{5} & =0 \\ x & =16.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the normal line intersects the $x$-axis at $(16,0).$
