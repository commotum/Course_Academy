### Easy — power_blank (method_bank)

The least common multiple of $9(x+8)^{3}$ and $5(x+8)$ is {{field-1}}.

- $45(x+8)^{3}$ **correct**

**Worked solution**

To find the least common multiple ($\text{LCM}$) of two polynomials that are made up of products of binomials, we find the least common multiple of the constants and the least common multiple of the variable part. Then, we multiply all of the results together.

So first, we find $\text{LCM}(9,5):$

- Multiples of $9:$ $\,9,\,18,\,27,\,36,\,45,\,54,…$

- Multiples of $5:$ $\,5,\,10,\,15,\,20,\,25,\,30,\,35,\,40,\,45,\,50,…$

So, $\text{LCM}(9,5)=45.$

To find the $\text{LCM}$ of $(x+8)^{3}$ and $(x+8),$ we raise $(x+8)$ to the largest power that appears in the expressions, which is $3.$ Therefore,

$$
\text{LCM}((x+8)^{3},\,(x+8))=(x+8)^{3}.
$$

Finally, we multiply all of our results together:

$$
\text{LCM}(9(x+8)^{3},\,5(x+8))=45\cdot (x+8)^{3}=45(x+8)^{3}.
$$
