## Original q-119308

The point $(2,3)$ lies on the curve $y=f(x)-1.$ Which of the following points **must** lie on $y=-f(x)+4?$

- $(2,-1)$
- $(2,0)$
- $(2,2)$
- $(2,1)$
- $(2,-3)$

**Method 1**

To plot $y=-f(x)+4,$ we apply the following steps:

- First, take the graph $y=f(x)-1.$

- Then, we shift the graph vertically by $1$ unit up and get the graph of $y=f(x).$ After this transformation, the point $(2,3)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(2,3+1)=(2,4).
$$

- Next, we reflect the result across the $x$-axis and get the graph of $y=-f(x).$ After this transformation, the point $(2,4)$ will be mapped  to the point

$$
(2,-(4))=(2,-4).
$$

- Finally, shift the result vertically by $4$ units up and obtain the graph of $y=-f(x)+4.$ After this transformation, the point $(2,-4)$ will be mapped to the point 

$$
(2,-4+4)=(2,0).
$$

**Method 2**

Since the point $(2,3)$ lies on the graph of $y=f(x)-1$, we have that 

$$
f(2)-1=3\,⟹\,f(2)=4.
$$

Now consider the graph of $y=-f(x)+4.$ Substituting $x=2$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}y & =-f(2)+4 \\  & =-4+4 \\  & =0.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the point $(2,0)$ must lie on $y=-f(x)+4.$
