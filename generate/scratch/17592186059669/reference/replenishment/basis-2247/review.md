The point $(-8,7)$ lies on the curve $y=4f(x).$ Which of the following points **must** lie on $y=-f(x)-5?$

- $(-8,- \frac{27}{4})$
- $(8,- \frac{27}{4})$
- $(-8,\frac{27}{2})$
- $(8,- \frac{27}{2})$
- $(-8,- \frac{27}{8})$

**Method 1**

We first find a point corresponding to $(-8,7)$ on $y=f(x).$ Then, we find a corresponding point on $y=-f(x)-5:$

- First, take the graph $y=4f(x).$

- Then, we stretch the graph by a scale factor of $\frac{1}{4}$ parallel to the $y$-axis and get the graph of $y=f(x).$ After this transformation, the point $(-8,7)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(-8,\frac{1}{4}\cdot (7))=(-8,\frac{7}{4}).
$$

- Next, we reflect the result across the $x$-axis and get the graph of $y=-f(x).$ After this transformation, the point $(-8,\frac{7}{4})$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(-8,-(\frac{7}{4}))=(-8,- \frac{7}{4}).
$$

- Next, we shift the result vertically by $5$ units down and obtain the graph of $y=-f(x)-5.$ After this transformation, the point $(-8,- \frac{7}{4})$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(-8,- \frac{7}{4}-5)=(-8,- \frac{27}{4}).
$$

**Method 2**

Since the point $(-8,7)$ lies on the graph of $y=4f(x),$ we have that

$$
4f(-8)=7\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(-8)=\frac{7}{4}.
$$

Now consider the graph of $y=-f(x)-5.$ Substituting $x=-8,$ we get

$$
\begin{aligned}y&=-f(-8)-5\\&=-(\frac{7}{4})-5\\&=- \frac{27}{4}.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the point $(-8,- \frac{27}{4})$ must lie on $y=-f(x)-5.$

The point $(-7,8)$ lies on the curve $y=2f(x).$ Which of the following points **must** lie on $y=-f(x)-4?$

- $(-7,16)$
- $(7,-8)$
- $(-7,-8)$
- $(-7,-4)$
- $(7,-16)$

**Method 1**

We first find a point corresponding to $(-7,8)$ on $y=f(x).$ Then, we find a corresponding point on $y=-f(x)-4:$

- First, take the graph $y=2f(x).$

- Then, we stretch the graph by a scale factor of $\frac{1}{2}$ parallel to the $y$-axis and get the graph of $y=f(x).$ After this transformation, the point $(-7,8)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(-7,\frac{1}{2}\cdot (8))=(-7,4).
$$

- Next, we reflect the result across the $x$-axis and get the graph of $y=-f(x).$ After this transformation, the point $(-7,4)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(-7,-(4))=(-7,-4).
$$

- Next, we shift the result vertically by $4$ units down and obtain the graph of $y=-f(x)-4.$ After this transformation, the point $(-7,-4)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(-7,-4-4)=(-7,-8).
$$

**Method 2**

Since the point $(-7,8)$ lies on the graph of $y=2f(x),$ we have that

$$
2f(-7)=8\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(-7)=4.
$$

Now consider the graph of $y=-f(x)-4.$ Substituting $x=-7,$ we get

$$
\begin{aligned}y&=-f(-7)-4\\&=-(4)-4\\&=-8.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the point $(-7,-8)$ must lie on $y=-f(x)-4.$

The point $(1,2)$ lies on the curve $y=\frac{1}{3}f(x).$ Which of the following points **must** lie on $y=-f(x)-4?$

- $(1,10)$
- $(1,-10)$
- $(1,\frac{1}{3})$
- $(1,-4)$
- $(1,6)$

**Method 1**

We first find a point corresponding to $(1,2)$ on $y=f(x).$ Then, we find a corresponding point on $y=-f(x)-4:$

- First, take the graph $y=\frac{1}{3}f(x).$

- Then, we stretch the graph by a scale factor of $3$ parallel to the $y$-axis and get the graph of $y=f(x).$ After this transformation, the point $(1,2)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(1,3\cdot (2))=(1,6).
$$

- Next, we reflect the result across the $x$-axis and get the graph of $y=-f(x).$ After this transformation, the point $(1,6)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(1,-(6))=(1,-6).
$$

- Next, we shift the result vertically by $4$ units down and obtain the graph of $y=-f(x)-4.$ After this transformation, the point $(1,-6)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(1,-6-4)=(1,-10).
$$

**Method 2**

Since the point $(1,2)$ lies on the graph of $y=\frac{1}{3}f(x),$ we have that

$$
\frac{1}{3}f(1)=2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(1)=6.
$$

Now consider the graph of $y=-f(x)-4.$ Substituting $x=1,$ we get

$$
\begin{aligned}y&=-f(1)-4\\&=-(6)-4\\&=-10.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the point $(1,-10)$ must lie on $y=-f(x)-4.$
