Simplify the trigonometric expression $\cos \theta(1-\cos \theta)-\sin^{2}\theta.$

- $- \cos \theta - 1$

- $\cos \theta$

- $\cos \theta - 1$ **correct**

- $\cos \theta + 1$

- $- \cos \theta$



First, we expand the parentheses:

$$
\cos \theta(1-\cos \theta)-\sin^{2}\theta = \cos \theta-\cos^{2}\theta-\sin^{2}\theta
$$

Next, we group the $\sin^2 \theta$ and $\cos^2 \theta$ terms.

$$
\cos \theta-\cos^{2}\theta-\sin^{2}\theta = \cos \theta-1(\cos^{2}\theta+\sin^{2}\theta)
$$

Then, we use $\sin^2{\theta}+\cos^2{\theta}=1$ to simplify the expression in the parentheses:

$$
\cos \theta-1(\cos^{2}\theta+\sin^{2}\theta) = \cos \theta - 1
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\cos \theta(1-\cos \theta)-\sin^{2}\theta = \cos \theta - 1.
$$

- $18$ **correct**

- $-18$

- $-9$

- $0$

$$

Then, we use $\sin^2{x}+\cos^2{x}=1$ to simplify the expression in the parentheses:

$$
18(\sin^{2}x+\cos^{2}x) = 18
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
(3\sin x-3\cos x)^{2}+(3\sin x+3\cos x)^{2} = 18.
$$
