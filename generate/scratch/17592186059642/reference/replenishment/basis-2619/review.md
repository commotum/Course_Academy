**E — normal/authored**

Simplify the trigonometric expression $\frac{10\cdot\left(\sin^{2} \phi\right)+10\cdot\left(\cos^{2} \phi\right)}{\sin \phi}.$

1. $2(10\cdot\left(\csc \phi\right))$

2. $(10\cdot\left(\csc \phi\right))\sin \phi$

3. $10\cdot\left(\sin \phi\right)$

4. $-(10\cdot\left(\csc \phi\right))$

5. $10\cdot\left(\csc \phi\right)$ **✓ Correct**

Worked solution

First, we factor the numerator:

$$
\frac{10\cdot\left(\sin^{2} \phi\right)+10\cdot\left(\cos^{2} \phi\right)}{\sin \phi}=\frac{10\cdot(\sin^{2} \phi+\cos^{2} \phi)}{\sin \phi}
$$

Next, we use the Pythagorean identity to rewrite the expression in the parentheses, and then we simplify the resulting expression as much as possible.

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{10\cdot(\sin^{2} \phi+\cos^{2} \phi)}{\sin \phi} & =\frac{10\cdot 1}{\sin \phi} \\ & =\frac{10}{\sin \phi} \\ & =10\cdot\left(\csc \phi\right)\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, we conclude that

$$
\frac{10\cdot\left(\sin^{2} \phi\right)+10\cdot\left(\cos^{2} \phi\right)}{\sin \phi}=10\cdot\left(\csc \phi\right).
$$
