Let $R$ be a simple solid region bounded by a closed surface $S$ with positive (outward) orientation. If a vector field $F$ has continuous partial derivatives for all its components in an open region in space that contains $R,$ then the divergence theorem states that

$$
{∬}_{S}F\cdot dS={∭}_{R}\text{div}\,F\,\text{d}V.
$$

Computing the divergence of $F,$ we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}\text{div}\,F & =\frac{∂}{∂x}(x)+\frac{∂}{∂y}(xz)+\frac{∂}{∂z}(3z) \\  & =1+0+3 \\  & =4.\end{aligned}
$$

Let $R$ be the solid sphere of radius $5$ enclosed by $S.$ Then, using the divergence theorem, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}{∬}_{S}F\cdot dS & ={∭}_{R}4\,\text{d}V \\  & =4{∭}_{R}\,\text{d}V \\  & =4\cdot \text{Volume}(R) \\  & =4\cdot \frac{4}{3}\pi \cdot {5}^{3} \\  & =\frac{2\,000\pi }{3}.\end{aligned}
$$

\begin{aligned}{∬}_{S}F\cdot dS & ={∭}_{R}2\,\text{d}V \\  & =2{∭}_{R}\,\text{d}V \\  & =2\cdot \text{Volume}(R) \\  & =2\cdot \frac{4}{3}\pi \cdot {5}^{3} \\  & =\frac{1000 \pi}{3}.\end{aligned}
$$
