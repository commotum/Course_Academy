# Replenishment variants

A curve is defined by the parametric equations

$$
x={e}^{5t},\,y=4{t}^{2}-8t,\,0\le t\le 2.
$$

Determine the value of the parameter $t$ at which the normal line to the curve is vertical.

selection
- t=1
- t=\sqrt{2}
- t=2
- t=-1
- t=\frac{1}{4}

Worked solution
The normal line is vertical when the tangent to the curve is horizontal. Therefore, we require ${y}^{′}(t)=0$ while ${x}^{′}(t)\ne 0.$

So, we first find the derivatives of $x$ and $y:$

$$
{x}^{′}(t)=5{e}^{5t},\,{y}^{′}(t)=8t-8
$$

Solving ${y}^{′}(t)=0$ gives

$$
8t-8=0\,⟹\,t=1.
$$

Now, we note that

$$
{x}^{′}(1)=5{e}^{5}=5 e^{5}\ne 0,\,\,
$$

which confirms that the normal to the curve is indeed vertical when $t=1.$

A curve is defined by the parametric equations

$$
x=\operatorname{ln}⁡(\pi t)-8t,\,y={e}^{1t},\,0<t<\infty .
$$

Determine the value of the parameter $t$ at which the normal line to the curve is horizontal.

selection
- t=\frac{\pi}{8}
- t=\frac{1}{8\pi}
- t=8
- t=\frac{1}{8}
- t=-\frac{1}{8}

Worked solution
The normal line is horizontal when the tangent line to the curve is vertical. Therefore, we require ${x}^{′}(t)=0$ while ${y}^{′}(t)\ne 0.$

So, we first find the derivatives of $x$ and $y:$

$$
{x}^{′}(t)=\frac{1}{t}-8,\,{y}^{′}(t)=1{e}^{1t}
$$

Solving ${x}^{′}(t)=0$ gives

$$
\frac{1}{t}-8=0\,⟹\,t=\frac{1}{8}.
$$

Now, we note that

$$
{y}^{′}(\frac{1}{8})=1{e}^{1\times (1/8)}=e^{\frac{1}{8}}\ne 0,\,\,
$$

which confirms that the normal to the curve is indeed horizontal when $t=\frac{1}{8}.$

A curve is defined by the parametric equations

$$
x=7(t-4),\,y=3{t}^{3}-18t,\,-\infty <t<\infty .
$$

Determine the values of $t$ at which the normal to the curve is vertical.

selection
- t=0
- t=\pm \sqrt{2}
- t=\pm \sqrt{6}
- t=-\sqrt{2}
- t=\sqrt{2}

Worked solution
The normal is vertical if the tangent is horizontal. Therefore, we require ${y}^{′}(t)=0$ while ${x}^{′}(t)\ne 0.$

So, we first find the derivatives of $x$ and $y:$

$$
\begin{aligned}{x}^{′}(t)=7,\,{y}^{′}(t)=9{t}^{2}-18.\end{aligned}
$$

Solving ${y}^{′}(t)=0$ gives

$$
9({t}^{2}-2)=0\,⟹\,t=\pm \sqrt{2}.
$$

Now, we note that

$$
\begin{aligned}{x}^{′}(-\sqrt{2}) & =7\ne 0, \\ {x}^{′}(\sqrt{2}) & =7\ne 0,\end{aligned}
$$

which confirms that the normal to the curve is indeed vertical when $t=\pm \sqrt{2}.$
