## exponential — original q-21207

A curve is defined by the parametric equations

$$
x={e}^{2t},\,y={t}^{2},\,0\le t\le 2.
$$

Determine the value of the parameter $t$ at which the normal line to the curve is vertical.

selection
- t=2
- t=\frac{1}{3}
- t=1
- t=0
- t=\frac{1}{2}

Worked solution
The normal line is vertical when the tangent to the curve is horizontal. Therefore, we require ${y}^{′}(t)=0$ while ${x}^{′}(t)\ne 0.$

So, we first find the derivatives of $x$ and $y:$

$$
{x}^{′}(t)=2{e}^{2t},\,{y}^{′}(t)=2t
$$

Solving ${y}^{′}(t)=0$ gives

$$
2t=0\,⟹\,t=0.
$$

Now, we note that

$$
{x}^{′}(0)=2{e}^{0}=2\ne 0,\,\,
$$

which confirms that the normal to the curve is indeed vertical when $t=0.$

Worked solution
The normal is vertical if the tangent is horizontal. Therefore, we require ${y}^{′}(t)=0$ while ${x}^{′}(t)\ne 0.$

So, we first find the derivatives of $x$ and $y:$

$$
{x}^{′}(t)=-8\sin(2t-\frac{2\pi}{3}),\,{y}^{′}(t)=8\cos(2t-\frac{2\pi}{3})
$$

Solving ${y}^{′}(t)=0$ gives

$$
8\cos(2t-\frac{2\pi}{3})=0\,⟹\,t=\frac{7\pi}{12}, \frac{13\pi}{12}, \frac{19\pi}{12}, \frac{25\pi}{12}.
$$

Now, we note that

$$
\begin{aligned}{x}^{′}(\frac{7\pi}{12}) & =-8\ne 0, \\ {x}^{′}(\frac{13\pi}{12}) & =8\ne 0, \\ {x}^{′}(\frac{19\pi}{12}) & =-8\ne 0, \\ {x}^{′}(\frac{25\pi}{12}) & =8\ne 0,\end{aligned}
$$

which confirms that the normal to the curve is indeed vertical when $t=\frac{7\pi}{12}, \frac{13\pi}{12}, \frac{19\pi}{12}, \frac{25\pi}{12}.$
