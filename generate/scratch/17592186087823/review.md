## Original source replay — moderate
Profile: `intercept_base`; raw source evidence.

For the curve $y=5({6}^{x})+2,$ find the point of intersection with the $y$-axis.

**A.** **Correct** $(0,7)$

**B.** $(7,0)$

**C.** $(0,12)$

**D.** $(11,0)$

**E.** $(0,11)$

**Worked solution**

**Method 1**

- For the curve $y={6}^{x},$ the point of intersection with the $y$-axis is $(0,1).$

- For the scaled function $y=5({6}^{x}),$ the $y$-coordinate becomes

$$
y=5\cdot 1=5.
$$

- Finally, after a vertical shift of $2$ units up, we get a point with the $y$-coordinate

$$
y=5+2=7.
$$

**Method 2** 

To find the $y$-intercept, we substitute $x=0$ into the equation of the curve:

$$
\begin{aligned}y & =5({6}^{0})+2 \\  & =5\cdot 1+2 \\  & =5+2 \\  & =7\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $y=5({6}^{x})+2$ intercepts the $y$-axis at $(0,7).$
