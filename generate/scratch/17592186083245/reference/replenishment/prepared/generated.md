A curve is defined by the parametric equations

$$
x=4 t + 4,\,y=t^{2} + 4,\,-\infty<t<\infty.
$$

Let $C$ be the point that corresponds to $t=1$ on the curve. Find the equation of the normal to the curve at $C.$

selection:
- $y=21 - 2 x$
- $y=\frac{x}{2} + 1$
- $y=2 x - 11$
- $y=5 - 2 x$
- $y=11 - 2 x$

Worked solution:
First, we find the coordinates of the point $C$ by substituting $t=1$ into the parametric equations:

$$
\begin{aligned}x & =4(1)+4=8, \\ y & =1(1)^2+4=5.\end{aligned}
$$

So we're looking to find the equation of the normal at the point $(8,5).$ For this, we need to find $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x},$ which means we first need to find the derivatives of $x$ and $y,$ as follows:

$$
x'(t)=4,\quad y\prime(t)=2 t.
$$

So, we have

$$
\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{y\prime(t)}{x\prime(t)}=\frac{2 t}{4}.
$$

At the point where $t=1$, the slope $m$ of the tangent line is given by

$$
m=\frac{2(1)}{4}=\frac{1}{2}.
$$

To find the slope $m_n$ of the normal line, we take the negative reciprocal of $m$:

$$
m_n=-\frac{1}{m}=-\frac{1}{(\frac{1}{2})}=-2.
$$

To calculate the equation of the normal, we use point-slope form:

$$
\begin{aligned}y-(5) & =-2(x-(8)) \\ y & =21 - 2 x.\end{aligned}
$$
