Find the **positive** $x$-coordinate of the point on the curve $y=- x^{3} + 42 x - 2$ whose tangent has a slope of $-6.$

$x=$
{{field-1}}

$4$

Let $f(x)=- x^{3} + 42 x - 2.$ The slope of the tangent at the point $x$ is equal to $f^{\prime}(x).$ Computing the derivative, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{\prime}(x) &= \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(- x^{3} + 42 x - 2\right) \\
&= (-1)\cdot 3\cdot x^{2} + (42)\cdot 1\cdot 1 \\
&= 42 - 3 x^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

We now set $f^{\prime}(x)=-6$ and solve for $x:$

$$
\begin{aligned}
42 - 3 x^{2} &= -6 \\
48 - 3 x^{2} &= 0 \\
- 3 \left(x - 4\right) \left(x + 4\right) &= 0 \\
x &= -4,\,4
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $x>0$, the required coordinate is $x=4.$
