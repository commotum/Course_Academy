Consider the differential equation

$$
\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}=y.
$$

 Which of the following is a solution to this equation?

Select all that apply.

- **I.** $y=3 e^{x} + 1$
- **II.** $y=- 2 e^{- x}$
- **III.** $y=- 3 e^{x} - 2$

**Correct checkbox values:** I=false, II=false, III=false

For a function $f(x)$ to be a solution to a differential equation, it must satisfy the equation for all values of $x$ in the stated domain.

We check each proposed solution by differentiating and substituting it into the original equation. 

- Differentiating $y=3 e^{x} + 1,$ we get $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}=3 e^{x}.$ Substituting into the original equation, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} & =y \\ 3 e^{x} & \overset{?}{=}1\cdot \left(3 e^{x} + 1\right) \\ 3 e^{x} & \overset{?}{=}3 e^{x} + 1 \\ -1 & \not\equiv 0\end{aligned}
$$

This statement is *not true* for all $x.$ Therefore, the function $y=3 e^{x} + 1$ is *not* a solution to our differential equation.

- Differentiating $y=- 2 e^{- x},$ we get $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}=2 e^{- x}.$ Substituting into the original equation, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} & =y \\ 2 e^{- x} & \overset{?}{=}1\cdot \left(- 2 e^{- x}\right) \\ 2 e^{- x} & \overset{?}{=}- 2 e^{- x} \\ 4 e^{- x} & \not\equiv 0\end{aligned}
$$

This statement is *not true* for all $x.$ Therefore, the function $y=- 2 e^{- x}$ is *not* a solution to our differential equation.

- Differentiating $y=- 3 e^{x} - 2,$ we get $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}=- 3 e^{x}.$ Substituting into the original equation, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} & =y \\ - 3 e^{x} & \overset{?}{=}1\cdot \left(- 3 e^{x} - 2\right) \\ - 3 e^{x} & \overset{?}{=}- 3 e^{x} - 2 \\ 2 & \not\equiv 0\end{aligned}

\begin{aligned}\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} & =2 e^{- \frac{y}{2}} \\ \frac{1}{x} & \overset{?}{=}2e^{-\frac{1}{2}\cdot \left(\ln{\left(x \right)}\right)} \\ \frac{1}{x} & \overset{?}{=}2e^{0}\cdot e^{\ln\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\right)} \\ \frac{1}{x} & \overset{?}{=}\frac{2}{\sqrt{x}} \\ \frac{1}{x} - \frac{2}{\sqrt{x}} & \not\equiv 0\end{aligned}
$$

This statement is *not true* for all $x.$ Therefore, the function $y=\ln{\left(x \right)}$ is *not* a solution to our differential equation.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only."
