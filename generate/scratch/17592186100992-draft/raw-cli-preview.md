Find $K+L-(K+J)$, if

$$
J=\begin{bmatrix}3 & 4 \\ -8 & -1\end{bmatrix},\,K=\begin{bmatrix}7 & 6 \\ 3 & 0\end{bmatrix},\,L=\begin{bmatrix}6 & 2 \\ 9 & -3\end{bmatrix}.
$$

First, we simplify the expression by distributing the subtraction over the matrices in the parentheses:

$$
\begin{aligned}K+L-(K+J) & =K+L-K-J \\  & =\cancel{K}+L-\cancel{K}-J \\  & =L-J\end{aligned}
$$

We are left with a single subtraction. So, we subtract the corresponding entries of the two matrices:

$$
\begin{aligned}L-J & =\begin{bmatrix}6 & 2 \\ 9 & -3\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}3 & 4 \\ -8 & -1\end{bmatrix} \\  & =\begin{bmatrix}6-3 & 2-4 \\ 9-(-8) & -3-(-1)\end{bmatrix} \\  & =\begin{bmatrix}3 & -2 \\ 17 & -2\end{bmatrix}\end{aligned}
$$
