# Replenishment variants

Find a vector equation of the normal line to the level surface $x^{2}+2\left(y - 1\right)^{2}-\left(-z\right)=0$ at the point $(1,2,-3).$

- $r=⟨1,2,-3⟩+t\,⟨2,4,1⟩,\,t\in (-\infty ,\infty )$
- $r=⟨1,2,-3⟩+t\,⟨2,4,0⟩,\,t\in (-\infty ,\infty )$
- $r=⟨1,2,-3⟩+t\,⟨0,4,1⟩,\,t\in (-\infty ,\infty )$
- $r=⟨1,2,-3⟩+t\,⟨2,0,1⟩,\,t\in (-\infty ,\infty )$
- $r=⟨2,2,-3⟩+t\,⟨2,4,1⟩,\,t\in (-\infty ,\infty )$

Let's set $f(x,y,z)=x^{2}+2\left(y - 1\right)^{2}-\left(-z\right).$ Then, our surface can be viewed as the level surface $f(x,y,z)=0.$

A normal vector to the level surface that passes through the point $(1,2,-3)$ can be found by computing $∇f(1,2,-3).$

So, we find the gradient and evaluate it at $(1,2,-3):$

$$
\begin{aligned}∇f(x,y,z)&={f}_{x}(x,y,z)i+{f}_{y}(x,y,z)j+{f}_{z}(x,y,z)k\\&=\frac{∂}{∂x}(x^{2}+2\left(y - 1\right)^{2}-\left(-z\right))i+\frac{∂}{∂y}(x^{2}+2\left(y - 1\right)^{2}-\left(-z\right))j+\frac{∂}{∂z}(x^{2}+2\left(y - 1\right)^{2}-\left(-z\right))k\\&=\left(2 x\right)i+\left(4 y - 4\right)j+\left(1\right)k\\∇f(1,2,-3)&=\left(2\right)i+\left(4\right)j+\left(1\right)k\\&=⟨2,4,1⟩\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, the vector equation of the line that is parallel to $∇f(1,2,-3)$ and passes through the point $(1,2,-3)$ can be written as

$$r=⟨1,2,-3⟩+t\,∇f(1,2,-3)=⟨1,2,-3⟩+t\,⟨2,4,1⟩.$$

where $t\in (-\infty,\infty).$
