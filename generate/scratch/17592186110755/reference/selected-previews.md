Any point $(x,y)$ on the unit circle is related to the central angle $\theta $ as follows:

$$
x=cos⁡\theta ,\,y=sin⁡\theta 
$$

We're given that $x=\frac{7}{8}$ at the point $P.$ Therefore, we have

$$
cos⁡\theta =\frac{7}{8}.
$$

We can use the Pythagorean identity to find $sin⁡\theta :$

$$
\begin{aligned}{sin}^{2}⁡\theta +{cos}^{2}⁡\theta  & =1 \\ {sin}^{2}⁡\theta  & =1-{cos}^{2}⁡\theta  \\ sin⁡\theta  & =\pm \sqrt{1-{cos}^{2}⁡\theta }\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $cos⁡\theta =\frac{7}{8}$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}sin⁡\theta  & =\pm \sqrt{1-{(\frac{7}{8})}^{2}} \\  & =\pm \sqrt{1-\frac{49}{64}} \\  & =\pm \sqrt{\frac{15}{64}} \\  & =\pm \frac{\sqrt{15}}{8}.\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we select the positive answer since $sin⁡\theta $ is positive in the first quadrant. So our final answer is $sin⁡\theta =\frac{\sqrt{15}}{8}.$
