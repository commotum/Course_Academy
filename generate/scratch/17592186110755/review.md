Any point $(x,y)$ on the unit circle is related to the central angle $\theta $ as follows:

$$
x=cos⁡\theta ,\,y=sin⁡\theta 
$$

We're given that $x=\frac{6}{7}$ at the point $P.$ Therefore, we have

$$
cos⁡\theta =\frac{6}{7}.
$$

We can use the Pythagorean identity to find $sin⁡\theta :$

$$
\begin{aligned}{sin}^{2}⁡\theta +{cos}^{2}⁡\theta  & =1 \\ {sin}^{2}⁡\theta  & =1-{cos}^{2}⁡\theta  \\ sin⁡\theta  & =\pm \sqrt{1-{cos}^{2}⁡\theta }\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $cos⁡\theta =\frac{6}{7}$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}sin⁡\theta  & =\pm \sqrt{1-{(\frac{6}{7})}^{2}} \\  & =\pm \sqrt{1-\frac{36}{49}} \\  & =\pm \sqrt{\frac{13}{49}} \\  & =\pm \frac{\sqrt{13}}{7}.\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we select the positive answer since $sin⁡\theta $ is positive in the first quadrant. So our final answer is $sin⁡\theta =\frac{\sqrt{13}}{7}.$

Any point $(x,y)$ on the unit circle is related to the central angle $\theta $ as follows:

$$
x=cos⁡\theta ,\,y=sin⁡\theta 
$$

We're given that $y=\frac{\sqrt{7}}{4}$ at the point $P.$ Therefore, we have

$$
sin⁡\theta =\frac{\sqrt{7}}{4}.
$$

We can use the Pythagorean identity to find $2{cos}^{2}⁡\theta :$

$$
\begin{aligned}{sin}^{2}⁡\theta +{cos}^{2}⁡\theta  & =1 \\ {cos}^{2}⁡\theta  & =1-{sin}^{2}⁡\theta  \\ 2{cos}^{2}⁡\theta  & =2-2{sin}^{2}⁡\theta \end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $sin⁡\theta =\frac{\sqrt{7}}{4}$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}2{cos}^{2}⁡\theta  & =2-2{(\frac{\sqrt{7}}{4})}^{2} \\  & =2-2(\frac{7}{16}) \\  & =2-\frac{14}{16} \\  & =\frac{9}{8}.\end{aligned}
$$

So our final answer is $2{cos}^{2}⁡\theta =\frac{9}{8}.$
