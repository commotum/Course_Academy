For the function $f(x,y)=2 x^{2} y^{2}+x y,$ evaluate $\frac{∂f}{∂x}$ at the point $(2,1).$

- $8$
- $7$
- $-7$
- $9$ **(correct)**
- $1$

We differentiate the given function with respect to $x,$ treating all other variables as constants:

Additionally, we use the sum and constant multiple rules for differentiation, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{∂f}{∂x} & =\frac{∂}{∂x}(2 x^{2} y^{2}+x y) \\  & =\frac{∂}{∂x}(2 x^{2} y^{2})+\frac{∂}{∂x}(x y) \\  & =2 y^{2}\cdot \frac{∂}{∂x}(x^{2})+y\cdot \frac{∂}{∂x}(x) \\  & =2 y^{2}\cdot (2 x)+y\cdot (1) \\  & =4 x y^{2}+y\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we evaluate the partial derivative at the point $(2,1):$

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{∂f}{∂x}{|}_{(2,1)} & =4 (2) (1)^{2}+(1) \\  & =8+(1) \\  & =9\end{aligned}
$$
