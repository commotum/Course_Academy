For the function $f(x,y)=3 y^{2} x^{2}+2 y x,$ evaluate $\frac{∂f}{∂y}$ at the point $(1,1).$

- $4$
- $8$ **(correct)**
- $-4$
- $2$
- $6$

We differentiate the given function with respect to $y,$ treating all other variables as constants:

Additionally, we use the sum and constant multiple rules for differentiation, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{∂f}{∂y} & =\frac{∂}{∂y}(3 y^{2} x^{2}+2 y x) \\  & =\frac{∂}{∂y}(3 y^{2} x^{2})+\frac{∂}{∂y}(2 y x) \\  & =3 x^{2}\cdot \frac{∂}{∂y}(y^{2})+2 x\cdot \frac{∂}{∂y}(y) \\  & =3 x^{2}\cdot (2 y)+2 x\cdot (1) \\  & =6 y x^{2}+2 x\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we evaluate the partial derivative at the point $(1,1):$

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{∂f}{∂y}{|}_{(1,1)} & =6 (1) (1)^{2}+2 (1) \\  & =6+(2) \\  & =8\end{aligned}
$$
