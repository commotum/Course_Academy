Find the slope $m$ of the tangent to the curve $y=e^{{x}^{3}-2{x}^{2}-1}$ at the point where $x=2.$

$m=$
{{field-1}}

$\frac{4}{e}$

First, we express $y$ as the composite function

$$
y=e^{u},\,u={x}^{3}-2{x}^{2}-1.
$$

Differentiating gives

$$
\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}u}=e^{u},\,\frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x}=3{x}^{2}-4x.
$$

We now apply the chain rule and get

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} & =\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}u}\cdot \frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x} \\ & =e^{u}\cdot (3{x}^{2}-4x) \\ & =(3{x}^{2}-4x)e^{u} \\ & =\left(3 x^{2} - 4 x\right) e^{x^{3} - 2 x^{2} - 1}.\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, to find the slope $m$ of the tangent to the curve at $x=2$, we have to evaluate $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}$ at that point as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}m=\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}|_{x=2} & =(3{x}^{2}-4x)e^{{x}^{3}-2{x}^{2}-1}|_{x=2} \\ & =(3(2)^{2}-4(2))e^{(2)^{3}-2(2)^{2}-1} \\ & =4\cdot e^{-1} \\ & =\frac{4}{e}.\end{aligned}
$$
