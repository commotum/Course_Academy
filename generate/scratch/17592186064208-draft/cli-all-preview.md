Calculate $\int (\frac{1}{x+7}+\frac{1}{x-6})\,\text{d}x.$ Assume that $K$ is an arbitrary positive constant.

- $\ln(K|(x+7)(x-6)|)$
- $-\ln(K|(x+7)(x-6)|)$
- $2\ln(K|(x+7)(x-6)|)$
- $\ln(K|\frac{x+7}{x-6}|)$
- $-\ln(K|\frac{x+7}{x-6}|)$

Let's solve this using the general formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}\int (\frac{1}{x+7}+\frac{1}{x-6})\,\text{d}x & =\int \frac{1}{x+7}\,\text{d}x+\int \frac{1}{x-6}\,\text{d}x \\  & =\operatorname{ln}⁡|x+7|+\operatorname{ln}⁡|x-6|+C \\  & =\operatorname{ln}⁡|(x+7)(x-6)|+\operatorname{ln}⁡K \\  & =\operatorname{ln}⁡(K|(x+7)(x-6)|)\end{aligned}
$$

Notice that we used the laws of logarithms to write the result as a single logarithm.
