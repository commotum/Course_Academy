# Original hard source

Find the equation of the tangent to the curve $y =\cos \left(\sin x \right)$ at the point $\left(0,1 \right).$

First, the slope of $y = f(x)$ at $x = 0$ is given by $f'(0).$

We will calculate $f'(x)$ using the chain rule. First, we express $f(x)$ as the composite function

\[
f = \cos{u}, \qquad u = \sin x.
\]

Differentiating gives

\[
\dfrac{\textrm d f}{\textrm d u} = -\sin{u}, \qquad \dfrac{\textrm d u}{\textrm d x} =\cos x.
\]

We now apply the chain rule, and get

\begin{align*}
 \dfrac{\textrm d f}{\textrm d x} &= \dfrac{\textrm d f}{\textrm d u} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm d u}{\textrm d x}\\[5pt]
&=-\sin u \cdot (\cos x)\\[5pt]
&=-\cos x \sin (\sin x).
\end{align*}

Evaluating at $x=0,$ we find
\[
	\eqalign{
	f'(0)&=-\cos (0) \sin (\sin (0))\\[3pt]
	&=-1 \cdot 0 \\[3pt]
        &=0.
}
\]

Finally, we find the equation of the tangent at the point $(0,1) \mathbin{:}$

\begin{align}
y-y_1 &= m(x-x_1)\\[3pt]
y-1 &= (0)\cdot\left(x-0\right)\\[3pt]
y&=1.
\end{align}

Find the equation of the tangent to the curve $y=\cos{\left(\sin{\left(x \right)} + \frac{\pi}{6} \right)}$ at the point $\left(0,\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$.

Write the right-hand side of the equation in terms of $x$.

$y=$ {{field-1}}

Answer: $- \frac{x}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

First, the slope of $y=f(x)$ at $x=0$ is given by $f'(0)$.

We calculate $f'(x)$ using the chain rule. First, express $f(x)$ as the composite function

$$f=\cos u,\qquad u=\sin{\left(x \right)} + \frac{\pi}{6}.$$

Differentiating gives

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}u}=-\sin u,\qquad \frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}=\cos x.$$

We now apply the chain rule:

$$f'(x)=\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}u}\cdot \frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}=-\sin(\sin{\left(x \right)} + \frac{\pi}{6})\cos x.$$

Evaluating at $x=0$ gives $f'(0)=-\sin(\frac{\pi}{6})=- \frac{1}{2}$.

Finally, use the point-slope equation:

$$y-\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)=\left(- \frac{1}{2}\right)(x-0),\qquad y=- \frac{x}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$
