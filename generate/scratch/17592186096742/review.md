## q-48569

Original:

Find $f^{′}(x)$ when $f(x) = \cos^{3} 3x$.

[No source solution]

Find $f^{′}(x)$ when $f(x) = \cos^{3} 2x$.

- -6\cos^{2} 2x\sin 2x (correct)
- -6\cos^{2} x\sin x
- -3\sin^{2} 2x
- 6\sin^{2} 2x\cos 2x
- 3\cos^{2} 2x\sin 2x

First, we express $f$ as the composite function

$$
f=u^3,\,u=\cos v,\,v=2x.
$$

Differentiating gives

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}u}=3u^2,\quad \frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}v}=-\sin v,\quad \frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}=2.
$$

Applying now the chain rule twice, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}f'(x)&=\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}u}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}v}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}\\
&=3u^2\cdot (-\sin v)\cdot 2\\
&=-6\cos^{2} 2x\sin 2x.\end{aligned}
$$
