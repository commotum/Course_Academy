Let's pick two distinct points on the line. For example, $A$ and $B$ with coordinates $(0,6)$ and $(2,0)$, respectively.

![](images/d6/d6cb6d026c181d79bda5c809729eb070c554c2025a287577df0330d1dd8a03e7.png)

Now, we substitute the coordinates of both points in our equations. If both points satisfy an equation, then we can conclude that the equation represents the line in the graph. Otherwise, if any point does *not* satisfy the equation, then we can conclude that the equation does *not* represent the line in the graph.

- Equation I: $y+3x=6.$
Substituting the coordinates of $A(0,6)$ and $B(2,0)$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}(6)+3(0) & =6 \\ 6 & =6\,✓ \\ (0)+3(2) & =6 \\ 6 & =6\,✓\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $y+3x=6$ is an equation of the line in the graph.

- Equation II: $\frac{1}{3}y+x=2.$
Substituting the coordinates of $A(0,6)$ and $B(2,0)$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{3}(6)+(0) & =2 \\ 2+0 & =2 \\ 2 & =2\,✓ \\ \frac{1}{3}(0)+(2) & =2 \\ 0+2 & =2 \\ 2 & =2\,✓\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,$\frac{1}{3}y+x=2$ is an equation of the line in the graph.

- Equation III: $y=-3x+6.$
Substituting the coordinates of $A(0,6)$ and $B(2,0)$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}(6) & =-3(0)+6 \\ 6 & =6\,✓ \\ (0) & =-3(2)+6 \\ 0 & =-6+6 \\ 0 & =0\,✓\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $y=-3x+6$ is  an equation of the line in the graph.

All the given equations represented the line in the graph. Therefore, the correct answer is "I, II and III."
