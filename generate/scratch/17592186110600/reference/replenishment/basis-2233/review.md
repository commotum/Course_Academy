Which of the following lines is perpendicular to $-5x+2y=4?$

- $y=-\frac{5}{2}x+2$
- $y=\frac{5}{2}x+10$
- $y=\frac{2}{5}x-7$
- $y=-\frac{2}{5}x-7$ **correct**
- $y=-3$

For the two lines to be perpendicular, their slopes must multiply to $-1.$

To find the slope of the given line $-5x+2y=4,$ we convert to slope-intercept form:

$$
\begin{aligned}-5x+2y & =4 \\ 2y & =5x+4 \\ y & =\frac{5}{2}x+2\end{aligned}
$$

In slope-intercept form, the slope is the $x$-coefficient. So, the slope of the given line is $\frac{5}{2}.$

Looking at the answer choices, the line $y=-\frac{2}{5}x-7$ has a slope of $-\frac{2}{5}.$

Multiplying the slopes together, we have

$$
\frac{5}{2}\cdot (-\frac{2}{5})=-1.
$$

Since the slopes multiply to $-1,$ we conclude that the line $y=-\frac{2}{5}x-7$ is perpendicular to the given line.
