## Original q-44404

Which of the following lines is perpendicular to $x+5y=10?$

- $y=\frac{1}{5}x-6$
- $y=-x+1$
- $y=5x-4$ **correct**
- $y=-5x-1$
- $y=-\frac{1}{5}x+15$

For the two lines to be perpendicular, their slopes must multiply to $-1.$

To find the slope of the given line $x+5y=10,$ we convert to slope-intercept form:

$$
\begin{aligned}x+5y & =10 \\ 5y & =-x+10 \\ y & =-\frac{1}{5}x+2\end{aligned}
$$

In slope-intercept form, the slope is the $x$-coefficient. So, the slope of the given line is $-\frac{1}{5}.$

Looking at the answer choices, the line $y=5x-4$ has a slope of $5.$

Multiplying the slopes together, we have

$$
(-\frac{1}{5})\cdot 5=-1.
$$

Since the slopes multiply to $-1,$ we conclude that the line $y=5x-4$ is perpendicular to the given line.
