What is the antiderivative of $-\frac{4}{x}?$

- $-4\ln(|x|+K)$
- $4\ln(K|x|)$
- $\ln(K|x|)$
- $-4\ln(K|x|)$
- $\frac{1}{-4}\ln(K|x|)$

First, we apply the constant factor rule and take the coefficient $-4$ out of the integral. Then we compute the integral of $\frac{1}{x},$ and get

$$
\begin{aligned}\int -\frac{4}{x}\,\text{d}x & =-4\int \frac{1}{x}\,\text{d}x \\ & =-4\ln|x|+C.\end{aligned}
$$

Lastly, let's rewrite the constant so that we can combine it with the natural logarithm. For any constant $C,$ we can always write $C=-4\ln K$ where $K$ is some other positive constant. Doing this allows us to combine the constant with $-4\ln|x|$ term, and we get

$$
\begin{aligned}\int -\frac{4}{x}\,\text{d}x & =-4\ln|x|+C \\ & =-4\ln|x|+(-4)\ln K \\ & =-4(\ln|x|+\ln K) \\ & =-4\ln(K|x|).\end{aligned}
$$
