- $\frac{2\sqrt{5}}{15}$
- $\frac{\sqrt{45}}{4}$
- $\frac{3\sqrt{5}}{2}$
- $\frac{45}{4}$
- $\frac{2}{7}$

![](images/21/21170c11aa5ff0ab39b86f94588e5e8a0fa178d0c34b9aa1774ee9f07a4d0812.png)

Any point $(x,y)$ on the unit circle is related to the central angle $\theta $ as follows:

$$
x=cos⁡\theta ,\,y=sin⁡\theta 
$$

We're given that $y=\frac{3\sqrt{5}}{7}$ at the point $P.$ Therefore, we have

$$
sin⁡\theta =\frac{3\sqrt{5}}{7}.
$$

We can use the Pythagorean identity to find $cos⁡\theta :$

$$
\begin{aligned}{sin}^{2}⁡\theta +{cos}^{2}⁡\theta  & =1 \\ {cos}^{2}⁡\theta  & =1-{sin}^{2}⁡\theta  \\ cos⁡\theta  & =\pm \sqrt{1-{sin}^{2}⁡\theta }\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $sin⁡\theta =\frac{3\sqrt{5}}{7}$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}cos⁡\theta  & =\pm \sqrt{1-{(\frac{3\sqrt{5}}{7})}^{2}} \\  & =\pm \sqrt{1-\frac{45}{49}} \\  & =\pm \sqrt{\frac{4}{49}} \\  & =\pm \frac{2}{7}.\end{aligned}
$$

We select the positive answer since $cos⁡\theta $ is positive in the first quadrant. So, $cos⁡\theta =\frac{2}{7}.$

Finally, knowing that $tan⁡\theta =\frac{sin⁡\theta }{cos⁡\theta },$ we have

$$
tan⁡\theta =\frac{(\frac{3\sqrt{5}}{7})}{(\frac{2}{7})}=\frac{3\sqrt{5}}{2}.
$$

![](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/generate/scratch/17592186110758/assets/407f26a2e6fec424f5d4aa9567d14185ceb5e1d91a1bfde1f9680529e523f677.png)

Any point $(x,y)$ on the unit circle is related to the central angle $\theta $ as follows:

$$
x=cos⁡\theta ,\,y=sin⁡\theta 
$$

We're given that $y=\frac{\sqrt{53}}{8}$ at the point $P.$ Therefore, we have

$$
sin⁡\theta =\frac{\sqrt{53}}{8}.
$$

We can use the Pythagorean identity to find $cos⁡\theta :$

$$
\begin{aligned}{sin}^{2}⁡\theta +{cos}^{2}⁡\theta  & =1 \\ {cos}^{2}⁡\theta  & =1-{sin}^{2}⁡\theta  \\ cos⁡\theta  & =\pm \sqrt{1-{sin}^{2}⁡\theta }\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $sin⁡\theta =\frac{\sqrt{53}}{8}$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}cos⁡\theta  & =\pm \sqrt{1-{(\frac{\sqrt{53}}{8})}^{2}} \\  & =\pm \sqrt{1-\frac{53}{64}} \\  & =\pm \sqrt{\frac{11}{64}} \\  & =\pm \frac{\sqrt{11}}{8}.\end{aligned}
$$

We select the positive answer since $cos⁡\theta $ is positive in the first quadrant. So, $cos⁡\theta =\frac{\sqrt{11}}{8}.$

Knowing that $tan⁡\theta =\frac{sin⁡\theta }{cos⁡\theta },$ we have

$$
tan⁡\theta =\frac{(\frac{\sqrt{53}}{8})}{(\frac{\sqrt{11}}{8})}=\frac{\sqrt{53}}{\sqrt{11}}.
$$

Finally, we rationalize the denominator.

$$
\begin{aligned}tan⁡\theta  & =\frac{\sqrt{53}}{\sqrt{11}} \\  & =\frac{\sqrt{53}}{\sqrt{11}}\cdot \frac{\sqrt{11}}{\sqrt{11}} \\  & =\frac{\sqrt{53\cdot 11}}{11} \\  & =\frac{\sqrt{583}}{11}\end{aligned}
$$
