Given that $f(x,y)= (y + 1)\ln (x + 4),$ find the points $(x, y)$ where $\nabla f(x,y) = \mathbf 0.$

- $(-5,-1)$
- $(-3,0)$
- $(-3,1)$
- $(-3,-1)$
- $(-4,-1)$

Correct: (-3,-1)

First, we find the partial derivatives: 

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{∂f}{∂x} & =\frac{∂}{∂x}((y + 1)\ln (x + 4)) \\  & =\frac{y + 1}{x + 4} \\ \frac{∂f}{∂y} & =\frac{∂}{∂y}((y + 1)\ln (x + 4)) \\  & =\ln(x + 4)\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have

$$
\nabla f(x,y) = \dfrac {y + 1}{x + 4} \, \mathbf i + \ln(x + 4) \, \mathbf j.
$$

Now, we solve $\nabla f(x,y) = \mathbf 0\mathbin{:}$

$$
\dfrac {y + 1}{x + 4} \, \mathbf i + \ln(x + 4) \, \mathbf j = 0 \mathbf i + 0\mathbf j
$$

By equating the coefficients of $\mathbf{i}$ on both sides of the equation (and similarly with $\mathbf{j}$), we obtain

$$
\{\begin{aligned}\frac{y + 1}{x + 4}=0 \\ \ln(x + 4)=0.\end{aligned}
$$

From the first equation, we get $y + 1=0,$ which means $y=-1.$ From the second equation, we obtain $\ln(x + 4)=0,$ which means that $x=-3.$

Therefore, $\nabla f(x,y) =\mathbf 0$ at the point $(-3,-1).$
