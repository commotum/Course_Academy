# Difficulty and variety review

The lesson starts with the origin-centered formula and wide canonical example (5,3), followed by a separate tall KP and then axis-length interpretation. Those later skills are excluded. Every generated problem reads four explicitly labeled intercepts, identifies a>b>0, substitutes the same two radii and squares them. Grid increments, unlabeled endpoints and a new graph-reading skill are not introduced.

Easy: two positive integer squares, values 1..9, ratio≤6. Moderate: at least one odd half-unit radius, values 1..9.5; the decimal-radio templates require the appropriate axis to be fractional while the other is integral. Fraction blanks can use one or two fractional radii, as observed. Hard: both radii have reduced denominator 4, odd numerators 3..23, ratio≤6; two rational squares with denominator 16. All hard examples have the same workload rather than a progression across examples. Bounds apply after Rational reduction and reject integer/half inputs that erase the quarter demand.

Finite domains checked from actual displayed equations and solution rows: 509 complete bank instances across template/profile combinations. The union of meaningful radius pairs excludes layout, bank and seed aliases: easy 33, moderate 112, hard 52. The same counts apply to the union of normal default layouts; individual radio domains can be smaller because complete-bank collisions are rejected, while a blank has no distractor collisions. `validation.json` exposes per-template/per-profile eligible counts. Graph sizes and option order are not additional tasks.

Review contains two distinct radius pairs for every one of the seven supported templates and two changed instances of each five observed bank profiles: 24 records total (10 easy, 10 moderate, 4 hard). Radio/blank examples with identical radii are presentation variants, counted once mathematically. Opaque profiles are shown to make observed evidence reviewable; they are never selected by default. Native grading tests every selected radio option and blank canonical/combined-denominator/incorrect entries. Hard classification remains authored; there is no observed hard denominator to report.
