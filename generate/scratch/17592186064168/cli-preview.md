### easy / power / authored

Find the antiderivative of ${x}^{1/2}.$

Work on $x>0$.

The antiderivative is {{field-1}} $+C$. Enter only the term before $+C$, with no additive constant.

- $\frac{2{x}^{3/2}}{3}$ [correct]

We can apply the power rule with $n=\frac{1}{2}$ and get

$$
\begin{aligned}\int {x}^{1/2}\,\text{d}x & =\frac{{x}^{1/2+1}}{(\frac{1}{2}+1)}+C \\  & =\frac{{x}^{3/2}}{(\frac{3}{2})}+C \\  & =\frac{2{x}^{3/2}}{3}+C.\end{aligned}
$$

Again, let's check by differentiating:

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}(\frac{2{x}^{3/2}}{3}+C) & = \\ \frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}(\frac{2{x}^{3/2}}{3})+\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}(C) & = \\ \frac{2}{3}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}({x}^{3/2})+0 & = \\ \frac{2}{3}\cdot \frac{3}{2}{x}^{3/2-1} & = \\ {x}^{1/2}\,✓\end{aligned}
$$
