Let $T(t)$ be the temperature of the liquid in degrees Celsius, and let $t>0$ be the time in minutes. If the temperature of the surroundings is ${25}^{∘}\text{C},$ which differential equation could be used to model the temperature of the liquid? Assume that $k>0$ is a constant of proportionality.

- ${T}^{′}(t)=-k(T-25)$ **[correct]**

- ${T}^{′}(t)=kT(T+25)$

Newton's law of cooling states that the rate at which the temperature $T(t)$ of a body changes is proportional to the difference between its temperature and its surroundings. Therefore, we have the differential equation

$$
\frac{\text{d}T}{\text{d}t}=-k(T-{T}_{S}),
$$

where ${T}_{S}$ is the temperature of the surroundings, and $k>0$ is a constant of proportionality.

In our case, ${T}_{S}={25}^{∘}\text{C},$ and we have

$$
\frac{\text{d}T}{\text{d}t}=-k(T-25).
$$
