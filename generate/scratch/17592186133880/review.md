Consider the vector field $F$ shown above. Which of the following statements are true everywhere in the domain of $F?$

- $\text{div}\,F=0$
- $\text{div}\,F>0$
- $\text{div}\,F<0$
- $F$ is solenoidal

**Original correct designation absent.**

Field 2: :answer-field.type/radio, key selection

- I only

- III and IV only

- III only **CORRECT**

- II only

Worked solution:

Recall that $\text{div}\,F=∇\cdot F$ denotes the divergence of the vector field $F.$

Intuitively, the divergence of $F$ at a point $P$ measures how much $F$ acts like a source or a sink at $P:$

- If $∇\cdot F>0$ at $P,$ then $F$ acts like a source at $P.$

- If $∇\cdot F<0$ at $P,$ then $F$ acts like a sink at $P.$

- If $∇\cdot F=0$ at $P,$ then $F$ acts like neither a source nor a sink at $P.$

![](/media/jake/SSD/EDB/math/images/6c/6c747149618d4c5ef35e727af15cd001365bd9c2d5e064689baf24c6ee23cff9.png)

If $∇\cdot F=0$ everywhere in the domain of $F,$ we say that $F$ is solenoidal.

With that in mind, let's examine each statement:

- Statement III is true, whereas statements I and II are false. Notice that the lengths of the vectors decrease as we move in the $y$-direction. Therefore, the vector field $F$ acts like a sink at every point.

- Statement IV is false. Since $∇\cdot F\ne 0,$ the vector field $F$ is not solenoidal.

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only."

Consider the vector field $H$ shown above. Which of the following statements are true everywhere in the domain of $H?$

I. $∇\cdot H=0$

II. $∇\cdot H>0$

III. $∇\cdot H<0$

IV. $H$ is solenoidal

Field 1: :answer-field.type/radio, key selection

- III only **CORRECT**

- II only

- I only

- I and IV only

- IV only

Worked solution:

Recall that $\text{div}\,H=∇\cdot H$ denotes the divergence of the vector field $H.$

Intuitively, the divergence of $H$ at a point $P$ measures how much $H$ acts like a source or a sink at $P:$

- If $H$ acts like a source at $P,$ then $∇\cdot H>0$ at $P.$

- If $H$ acts like a sink at $P,$ then $∇\cdot H<0$ at $P.$

- If $H$ acts like neither a source nor a sink at $P,$ then $∇\cdot H=0$ at $P.$

![](images/6c/6c747149618d4c5ef35e727af15cd001365bd9c2d5e064689baf24c6ee23cff9.png)

If $∇\cdot H=0$ everywhere in the domain of $H,$ we say that $H$ is solenoidal.

With that in mind, let's examine each statement:

- Statement III is true, whereas statements I and II are false. Notice that the lengths of the vector's $x$-components decrease as we move horizontally towards the origin. Therefore, the vector field $H$ acts like a sink at every point.

- Statement IV is false. Since $∇\cdot H\ne 0,$ the vector field $H$ is not solenoidal.

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only."
