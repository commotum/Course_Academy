## minimum_sin_degree — :question.difficulty/easy

What is the minimum value of $f(x)=6\sin(x-45^{\circ})+4$?

- $-2$ **correct**
- $10$
- $-6$
- $4$
- $6$

The range of $\sin x$ is

$$
-1\le \sin x\le 1.
$$

Horizontal shifts and stretches have no effect on the range. Therefore,

$$
-1\le \sin(x-45^{\circ})\le 1.
$$

Multiplying the above inequality by $6$ and then adding $4$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned} & -1\le \sin(x-45^{\circ})\le 1 \\  & -6\le 6\sin(x-45^{\circ})\le 6 \\  & -2\le 6\sin(x-45^{\circ})+4\le 10 \\  & -2\le f(x)\le 10.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the minimum value is $-2.$

What is the minimum value of $f(x)=- \frac{2}{3}\sin(x+15^{\circ})+\frac{13}{4}$?

- $\frac{31}{12}$ **correct**
- $\frac{47}{12}$
- $- \frac{2}{3}$
- $\frac{13}{4}$
- $\frac{2}{3}$

$$
-1\le \sin x\le 1.
$$

$$
-1\le \sin(x+15^{\circ})\le 1.

$$
\begin{aligned} & -1\le \sin(x+15^{\circ})\le 1 \\  & \frac{2}{3}\ge - \frac{2}{3}\sin(x+15^{\circ})\ge - \frac{2}{3} \\  & \frac{47}{12}\ge - \frac{2}{3}\sin(x+15^{\circ})+\frac{13}{4}\ge \frac{31}{12} \\  & \frac{31}{12}\le f(x)\le \frac{47}{12}.\end{aligned}

## range_cos_degree — :question.difficulty/hard

What is the range of the function $y=- \frac{11}{4}\cos(x-30^{\circ})+\frac{11}{4}$?

- $y\in (0,\frac{11}{2})$
- $y\in [0,\frac{11}{2}]$ **correct**
- $y\in [0,\frac{11}{2})$
- $y\in (-\infty,\infty)$
- $y\in (0,\frac{11}{2}]$

The range of $\cos x$ is

$$
-1\le \cos x\le 1.
$$

Horizontal shifts and stretches have no effect on the range. Therefore,

$$
-1\le \cos(x-30^{\circ})\le 1.
$$

Multiplying the above inequality by $- \frac{11}{4}$ and then adding $\frac{11}{4}$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned} & -1\le \cos(x-30^{\circ})\le 1 \\  & \frac{11}{4}\ge - \frac{11}{4}\cos(x-30^{\circ})\ge - \frac{11}{4} \\  & \frac{11}{2}\ge - \frac{11}{4}\cos(x-30^{\circ})+\frac{11}{4}\ge 0 \\  & 0\le y\le \frac{11}{2}.\end{aligned}
