$$
\frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x}=2\,⟹\,\text{d}x=\frac{1}{2}\text{d}u.
$$

We can now rewrite the integral in terms of $u,$ solve it, and write the final answer in terms of $x$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}\int \frac{1}{2x+3}\,\text{d}x & =\int \frac{1}{u}\cdot \frac{1}{2}\,\text{d}u \\  & =\frac{1}{2}\int \frac{1}{u}\,\text{d}u \\  & =\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{ln}⁡|u|+C \\  & =\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{ln}⁡|2x+3|+C\end{aligned}
$$

We can further simplify the above by writing $C=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{ln}⁡K$ for another arbitrary constant $K,$ and then we can combine the two terms using the laws of logarithms, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{ln}⁡|2x+3|+C & =\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{ln}⁡|2x+3|+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{ln}⁡K \\  & =\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{ln}⁡|2x+3|+\operatorname{ln}⁡K) \\  & =\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{ln}⁡(K|2x+3|)\end{aligned}
$$

So, we finally conclude that

$$
\int \frac{1}{2x+3}\,\text{d}x=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{ln}⁡(K|2x+3|).
