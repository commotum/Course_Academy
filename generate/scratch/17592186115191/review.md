## Original hard source

Find ${f}^{'}(\frac{\pi }{6})$ if $f(x)=-6\operatorname{cot}x.$

First, we just take the constant out of the derivative and use the result for the derivative of $\operatorname{cot}x,$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}\begin{aligned}{f}^{'}(x) & =\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}(-6\operatorname{cot}x) \\  & =-6(-{csc}^{2}x) \\  & =6{csc}^{2}x\end{aligned}\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we substitute $x=\frac{\pi }{6}$ in the derivative and get

$$
\begin{aligned}{f}^{'}(\frac{\pi }{6}) & =6{csc}^{2}(\frac{\pi }{6}) \\  & =6{(2)}^{2} \\  & =24.\end{aligned}
$$
