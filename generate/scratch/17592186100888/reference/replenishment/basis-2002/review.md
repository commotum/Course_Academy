## Moderate

Consider the following differential equation.

$$
{y}^{′}=\frac{2y}{x},\,x\ne 0
$$

 

Let $f(x)=-4{x}^{2}.$ Which of the following statements are true?

Select all that apply.

- **I.** ${f}^{′}(x)=-6x$
- **II.** Substituting $y=f(x)$ and ${y}^{′}={f}^{′}(x)$ into the differential equation gives a true statement
- **III.** The function $y=f(x)$ is a solution of the differential equation

Author key:
- I: false
- II: true
- III: true

For a function $f(x)$ to be a solution to a differential equation, it must satisfy the equation for all values of $x$ in an interval where the function is defined and the equation is valid.

With that in mind, let's check each statement in turn.

- Statement I is false. Differentiating $f(x)=-4{x}^{2},$ we get 

$$
\begin{aligned}{f}^{′}(x) & ={(-4{x}^{2})}^{′} \\  & =-4\cdot 2x \\  & =-8x \\  & \ne -6x.\end{aligned}
$$

- Statement II is true. Substituting $y=-4{x}^{2}$ and ${y}^{′}=-8x$ into the original equation, we get a true statement:

$$
\begin{aligned}{y}^{′} & \overset{?}{=}\frac{2y}{x} \\ -8x & \overset{?}{=}\frac{2(-4{x}^{2})}{x} \\ -8x & =-8x\,✓\end{aligned}
$$

- Statement III is true. Since statement II is true, the function $y=-4{x}^{2}$ is a solution to our differential equation.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II and III only."
