## Moderate

Consider the following differential equation.

$$
{y}^{′}=\frac{4y}{x},\,x\ne 0
$$

 

Let $f(x)=-2{x}^{4}.$ Which of the following statements are true?

Select all that apply.

- **I.** ${f}^{′}(x)=-6{x}^{3}$
- **II.** Substituting $y=f(x)$ and ${y}^{′}={f}^{′}(x)$ into the differential equation gives a true statement
- **III.** The function $y=f(x)$ is a solution of the differential equation

Author key:
- I: false
- II: true
- III: true

For a function $f(x)$ to be a solution to a differential equation, it must satisfy the equation for all values of $x$ in an interval where the function is defined and the equation is valid.

With that in mind, let's check each statement in turn.

- Statement I is false. Differentiating $f(x)=-2{x}^{4},$ we get 

$$
\begin{aligned}{f}^{′}(x) & ={(-2{x}^{4})}^{′} \\  & =-2\cdot 4{x}^{3} \\  & =-8{x}^{3} \\  & \ne -6{x}^{3}.\end{aligned}
$$

- Statement II is true. Substituting $y=-2{x}^{4}$ and ${y}^{′}=-8{x}^{3}$ into the original equation, we get a true statement:

$$
\begin{aligned}{y}^{′} & \overset{?}{=}\frac{4y}{x} \\ -8{x}^{3} & \overset{?}{=}\frac{4(-2{x}^{4})}{x} \\ -8{x}^{3} & =-8{x}^{3}\,✓\end{aligned}
$$

- Statement III is true. Since statement II is true, the function $y=-2{x}^{4}$ is a solution to our differential equation.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II and III only."
