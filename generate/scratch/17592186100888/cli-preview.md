## Easy

Consider the following differential equation.

$$
{y}^{′}=\frac{1}{{e}^{y}}
$$

 

Let $f(x)=\operatorname{ln}⁡(x-3).$ On the interval $x-3>0,$ which of the following statements are true?

- ${f}^{′}(x)=\frac{1}{x-3}$
- Substituting $y=f(x)$ and ${y}^{′}={f}^{′}(x)$ into the differential equation gives a true statement
- The function $y=f(x)$ is a solution of the differential equation

- I and II only
- I, II, and III **(correct)**
- I only
- II and III only
- III only

For a function $f(x)$ to be a solution to a differential equation, it must satisfy the equation for all values of $x$ in an interval where the function is defined and the equation is valid.

With that in mind, let's check each statement in turn.

- Statement I is true. Differentiating $f(x)=\operatorname{ln}⁡(x-3),$ we get 

$$
\begin{aligned}{f}^{′}(x) & ={(\operatorname{ln}⁡(x-3))}^{′} \\  & =\frac{1}{x-3}.\end{aligned}
$$

- Statement II is true. Substituting $y=\operatorname{ln}⁡(x-3)$ and ${y}^{′}=\frac{1}{x-3}$ into the original equation, we get a true statement:

$$
\begin{aligned}{y}^{′} & \overset{?}{=}\frac{1}{{e}^{y}} \\ \frac{1}{x-3} & \overset{?}{=}\frac{1}{{e}^{\operatorname{ln}⁡(x-3)}} \\ \frac{1}{x-3} & =\frac{1}{x-3}\,✓\end{aligned}
$$

- Statement III is true. Since statement II is true, the function $y=\operatorname{ln}⁡(x-3)$ is a solution to our differential equation.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I, II, and III."
