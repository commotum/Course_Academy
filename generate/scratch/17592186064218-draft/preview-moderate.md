Calculate $\int \frac{2}{\sqrt{144-{x}^{2}}}\,\text{d}x.$

{{0}}

- $2\operatorname{arcsin}⁡(12x)+C$
- $\operatorname{arcsin}⁡(\frac{x}{12})+C$
- $\frac{2}{144}\operatorname{arcsin}⁡(\frac{x}{12})+C$
- $144\operatorname{arcsin}⁡(12x)+C$
- $2\operatorname{arcsin}⁡(\frac{x}{12})+C$ [correct]

Let's rewrite our integral as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}\int \frac{2}{\sqrt{144-{x}^{2}}}\,\text{d}x & =2\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{144\cdot (1-{x}^{2}/144)}}\,\text{d}x \\  & =\frac{2}{12}\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-{x}^{2}/144}}\,\text{d}x \\  & =\frac{2}{12}\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-(x/12)^{2}}}\,\text{d}x\end{aligned}
$$

This integral resembles the basic integral for inverse sine,

$$
\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-{u}^{2}}}\,\text{d}u=\operatorname{arcsin}⁡(u)+C.
$$

The only difference is that instead of ${u}^{2}$ as the variable, we have ${(\frac{x}{12})}^{2}.$ 

So, let's substitute $u=\frac{x}{12}.$ Differentiating, we get  

$$
\frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x}=\frac{1}{12}\,⟹\,\text{d}x=12\text{d}u.
$$

Using the above, we can write the integral in terms of $u$ and evaluate:

$$
\begin{aligned}\begin{aligned}\int \frac{2}{\sqrt{144-{x}^{2}}}\,\text{d}x & =\frac{2}{12}\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-(x/12)^{2}}}\,\text{d}x \\  & =\frac{2}{12}\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-{u}^{2}}}\cdot (12\,\text{d}u) \\  & =2\int \frac{\text{d}u}{\sqrt{1-{u}^{2}}} \\  & =2\operatorname{arcsin}⁡u+C \\  & =2\operatorname{arcsin}⁡(\frac{x}{12})+C\end{aligned}\end{aligned}
$$
