Calculate $\int \frac{7}{5\sqrt{36-3x^{2}}}\,\text{d}x.$

{{0}}

- $\frac{7 \sqrt{3} \operatorname{arcsin}{\left(\frac{\sqrt{3} x}{6} \right)}}{15}+C$ [correct]
- $\frac{7 \operatorname{arcsin}{\left(\frac{\sqrt{3} x}{6} \right)}}{30}+C$
- $\frac{7 \operatorname{arcsin}{\left(\frac{\sqrt{3} x}{6} \right)}}{15}+C$
- $\frac{7 \sqrt{3} \operatorname{arcsin}{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}}{15}+C$
- $\frac{7 \sqrt{3} \operatorname{arcsin}{\left(\frac{x}{6} \right)}}{15}+C$

Let's rewrite our integral as follows:

$$
\int \frac{7}{5\sqrt{36-3x^{2}}}\,\text{d}x=\frac{7}{30}\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-(\frac{\sqrt{3} x}{6})^{2}}}\,\text{d}x
$$

This integral resembles the basic integral for inverse sine,

$$
\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-u^{2}}}\,\text{d}u=\operatorname{arcsin}(u)+C.
$$

So, let's substitute $u=\frac{\sqrt{3} x}{6}.$ Differentiating, we get

$$
\frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{6}\,⟹\,\text{d}x=2 \sqrt{3}\,\text{d}u.
$$

Using the above, we can write the integral in terms of $u$ and evaluate:

$$
\begin{aligned}\int \frac{7}{5\sqrt{36-3x^{2}}}\,\text{d}x & =\frac{7}{30}\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-(\frac{\sqrt{3} x}{6})^{2}}}\,\text{d}x \\ & =\frac{7}{30}\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-u^{2}}}\cdot (2 \sqrt{3}\,\text{d}u) \\ & =\frac{7 \sqrt{3}}{15}\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-u^{2}}}\,\text{d}u \\ & =\frac{7 \sqrt{3} \operatorname{arcsin}{\left(u \right)}}{15}+C \\ & =\frac{7 \sqrt{3} \operatorname{arcsin}{\left(\frac{\sqrt{3} x}{6} \right)}}{15}+C\end{aligned}
$$
