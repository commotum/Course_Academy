Calculate $\int \frac{1}{5\sqrt{1-25{x}^{2}}}\,\text{d}x.$

{{0}}

- $\frac{1}{5}\operatorname{arcsin}⁡(x)+C$
- $5\operatorname{arcsin}⁡(5x)+C$
- $\frac{1}{25}\operatorname{arcsin}⁡(5x)+C$ [correct]
- $25\operatorname{arcsin}⁡(5x)+C$
- $\frac{1}{5}\operatorname{arcsin}⁡(5x)+C$

This integral resembles the basic integral for inverse sine,

$$
\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-{u}^{2}}}\,\text{d}u=\operatorname{arcsin}⁡(u)+C.
$$

The only difference is that instead of ${u}^{2}$ as the variable, we have $25{x}^{2}=(5x)^{2}.$

So, let's substitute $u=5x.$ Differentiating, we get

$$
\frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x}=5\,⟹\,\text{d}x=\frac{1}{5}\text{d}u.
$$

Using the above, we can write the integral in terms of $u$ and evaluate:

$$
\begin{aligned}\begin{aligned}\int \frac{1}{5\sqrt{1-25{x}^{2}}}\,\text{d}x & =\frac{1}{5}\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-(5x)^{2}}}\,\text{d}x \\  & =\frac{1}{5}\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-{u}^{2}}}\,\cdot (\frac{1}{5}\text{d}u) \\  & =\frac{1}{25}\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-{u}^{2}}}\,\text{d}u \\  & =\frac{1}{25}\operatorname{arcsin}⁡u+C \\  & =\frac{1}{25}\operatorname{arcsin}⁡(5x)+C\end{aligned}\end{aligned}
$$
