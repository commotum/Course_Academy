Find ${f}^{′}(x)$ when $f(x)=9{sin}^{2}⁡(x).$

- 18\operatorname{sin}⁡x\operatorname{cos}⁡x
- -9{cos}^{2}⁡x
- 18\operatorname{sin}⁡x{cos}^{2}⁡x
- 18\operatorname{sin}⁡x

First, we express

$$
f(x)=9(\operatorname{sin}⁡x)^{2}.
$$

Now, we see that $f(x)$ can be expressed as the composite function

$$
f=9{u}^{2},\,u=\operatorname{sin}⁡x.
$$

Differentiating, we get

$$
\frac{\text{d}f}{\text{d}u}=18u\,\text{and}\,\frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x}=\operatorname{cos}⁡x.
$$

We now apply the chain rule, and get

$$
\begin{aligned}{f}^{′}(x) & =\frac{\text{d}f}{\text{d}u}\cdot \frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x} \\  & =18u\cdot \operatorname{cos}⁡x \\  & =18\operatorname{sin}⁡x\operatorname{cos}⁡x.\end{aligned}
$$
