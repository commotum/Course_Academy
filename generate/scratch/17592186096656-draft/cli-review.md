\frac{8^{5 x}}{\ln{(8 )}}+C
\frac{8^{5 x}}{5}+C
\frac{8^{5 x}}{5 \ln{(8 )}}+C
\frac{8^{x}}{\ln{(8 )}}+C
- \frac{8^{5 x}}{5 \ln{(8 )}}+C

If we let $u=5x$, then 

$$
\frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x}=5\,⟹\,\frac{1}{5}\text{d}u=\text{d}x.
$$

Then, we can write the integral in terms of $u$ and evaluate:

$$
\begin{aligned}\int {8}^{5x}\,\text{d}x & =\int {8}^{u}\cdot \frac{1}{5}\,\text{d}u \\  & =\frac{1}{5}\int {8}^{u}\,\text{d}u \\  & =\frac{1}{5}\frac{{8}^{u}}{\operatorname{ln}⁡8}+C \\  & =\frac{{8}^{5x}}{5\operatorname{ln}⁡8}+C\end{aligned}
$$

$\int_{1}^{2} 2\cdot {4}^{x - 3}\,\text{d}x=$

\frac{3}{8\ln 4}

If we let $u=x - 3,$ then

$$
\frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x}=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 1\,\text{d}u=\text{d}x.
$$

Computing our limits of integration, we get the following:

| $x$ | $1$ | $2$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $u$ | $-2$ | $-1$ |

Then, we can write the integral in terms of $u$ and evaluate:

$$
\begin{aligned}\int_{1}^{2} 2\cdot {4}^{x - 3}\,\text{d}x & =\int_{-2}^{-1} 2\cdot {4}^{u}\cdot (1)\,\text{d}u \\  & =(2)\int_{-2}^{-1} {4}^{u}\,\text{d}u \\  & =(2)(\frac{{4}^{u}}{\ln 4})|_{-2}^{-1} \\  & =(\frac{2}{\ln{(4 )}})({4}^{-1}-{4}^{-2}) \\  & =(\frac{2}{\ln{(4 )}})(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{16}) \\  & =\frac{3}{8\ln 4}\end{aligned}

$\int_{0}^{3} 3\cdot {4}^{2 x - 2}\,\text{d}x=$

$$
\frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x}=2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{1}{2}\,\text{d}u=\text{d}x.
$$

| $x$ | $0$ | $3$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $u$ | $-2$ | $4$ |

$$
