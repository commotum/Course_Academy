- First, we take the graph $y={x}^{2}.$

- Then, we shift the graph horizontally by $1$ unit to the right and get the graph of $y=(x-1{)}^{2}.$ After this transformation, the point $(a,b)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(a+1,b).
$$

- Finally, we shift the result vertically by $2$ units up and obtain the graph of $y=(x-1{)}^{2}+2.$ After this transformation, the point $(a+1,b)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(a+1,b+2).
$$

**Method 2**

Since the point $(a,b)$ lies on the graph of $y={x}^{2}$, we have that ${a}^{2}=b.$

$$
\begin{aligned}y & =(a+1-1{)}^{2}+2 \\  & ={a}^{2}+2 \\  & =b+2.\end{aligned}
