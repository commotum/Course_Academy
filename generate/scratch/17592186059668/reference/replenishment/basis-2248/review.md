The point $(a,b)$ lies on the curve $y=3^{x}.$ Which of the following points **must** lie on $y=3^{x/2}-2?$

- $(\frac{a}{2},b+2)$
- $(a+2,b-2)$
- $(a^{\frac{3}{2}},b-2)$
- $(2a,b-2)$ (correct)
- $(\frac{a}{2},b-2)$

**Method 1**

To plot $y=3^{x/2}-2,$ we apply the following steps:

- First, we take the graph $y=3^{x}.$

- Then, we stretch the graph by a scale factor of $2$ parallel to the $x$-axis and get the graph of $y=3^{x/2}.$ After this transformation, the point $(a,b)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(2a,b).
$$

- Finally, we shift the result vertically by $2$ units down and obtain the graph of $y=3^{x/2}-2.$ After this transformation, the point $(2a,b)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(2a,b-2).
$$

**Method 2**

Since the point $(a,b)$ lies on the graph of $y=3^{x}$, we have that $3^{a}=b.$

Now consider the graph of $y=3^{x/2}-2.$ Substituting $x=2a$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}y &=3^{\frac{2a}{2}}-2 \\ &=3^{a}-2 \\ &=b-2.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the point $(2a,b-2)$ must lie on $y=3^{x/2}-2.$

The point $(-1,-2)$ lies on the curve $y=f(x).$ Which of the following points **must** lie on $y=-f(x)-5?$

- $(-1,-3)$ (correct)
- $(1,-3)$
- $(-1,3)$
- $(1,-8)$
- $(-1,-8)$

**Method 1**

To plot $y=-f(x)-5,$ we apply the following steps:

- First, we take the graph $y=f(x).$

- Then, we reflect the graph across the $x$-axis and get the graph of $y=-f(x).$ After this transformation, the point $(-1,-2)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(-1,2).
$$

- Finally, we shift the result vertically by $5$ units down and obtain the graph of $y=-f(x)-5.$ After this transformation, the point $(-1,2)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(-1,-3).
$$

**Method 2**

Since the point $(-1,-2)$ lies on the graph of $y=f(x)$, we have that $f(-1)=-2.$

Now consider the graph of $y=-f(x)-5.$ Substituting $x=-1$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}y &=-f(-1)-5 \\ &=-( -2 )-5 \\ &=2-5 \\ &=-3.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the point $(-1,-3)$ must lie on $y=-f(x)-5.$

The point $(-1,2)$ lies on the curve $y=f(x).$ Which of the following points **must** lie on $y=-f(x)+7?$

- $(-1,5)$ (correct)
- $(1,5)$
- $(-1,-5)$
- $(1,12)$
- $(-1,12)$

**Method 1**

To plot $y=-f(x)+7,$ we apply the following steps:

- First, we take the graph $y=f(x).$

- Then, we reflect the graph across the $x$-axis and get the graph of $y=-f(x).$ After this transformation, the point $(-1,2)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(-1,-2).
$$

- Finally, we shift the result vertically by $7$ units up and obtain the graph of $y=-f(x)+7.$ After this transformation, the point $(-1,-2)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(-1,5).
$$

**Method 2**

Since the point $(-1,2)$ lies on the graph of $y=f(x)$, we have that $f(-1)=2.$

Now consider the graph of $y=-f(x)+7.$ Substituting $x=-1$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}y &=-f(-1)+7 \\ &=-( 2 )+7 \\ &=-2+7 \\ &=5.\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the point $(-1,5)$ must lie on $y=-f(x)+7.$
