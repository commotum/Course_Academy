Calculate $\int \frac{5}{3x+3}\,\text{d}x.$ Assume that $K$ is an arbitrary positive constant.

- $3\operatorname{ln}(K|3x+3|)$
- $\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{ln}(K|3x+3|)$
- $\frac{3}{5}\operatorname{ln}(K|3x+3|)$
- $\operatorname{ln}(K|3x+3|)$
- $\frac{5}{3}\operatorname{ln}(K|3x+3|)$

First, let $u=3x+3.$ Then differentiating with respect to $x$ gives

$$
\frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x}=3\,⟹\,\text{d}x=\frac{1}{3}\text{d}u.
$$

We can now rewrite the integral in terms of $u,$ solve it, and write the final answer in terms of $x$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}\int \frac{5}{3x+3}\,\text{d}x & =5\int \frac{1}{3x+3}\,\text{d}x \\ & =5\int \frac{1}{u}\cdot (\frac{1}{3})\,\text{d}u \\ & =\frac{5}{3}\int \frac{1}{u}\,\text{d}u \\ & =\frac{5}{3}\operatorname{ln}|u|+C \\ & =\frac{5}{3}\operatorname{ln}|3x+3|+C\end{aligned}
$$

We can further simplify the above by writing $C=\frac{5}{3}\operatorname{ln}K$ for another arbitrary positive constant $K,$ and then we can combine the two terms using the laws of logarithms, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{5}{3}\operatorname{ln}|3x+3|+C & =\frac{5}{3}\operatorname{ln}|3x+3|+\frac{5}{3}\operatorname{ln}K \\ & =\frac{5}{3}(\operatorname{ln}|3x+3|+\operatorname{ln}K) \\ & =\frac{5}{3}\operatorname{ln}(K|3x+3|)\end{aligned}
$$

So, we finally conclude that

$$
\int \frac{5}{3x+3}\,\text{d}x=\frac{5}{3}\operatorname{ln}(K|3x+3|).
$$

Calculate $\int \frac{6}{3x+7}\,\text{d}x.$ Assume that $K$ is an arbitrary positive constant.

- $3\operatorname{ln}(K|3x+7|)$
- $\frac{1}{7}\operatorname{ln}(K|3x+7|)$
- $\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{ln}(K|3x+7|)$
- $\frac{7}{3}\operatorname{ln}(K|3x+7|)$
- $2\operatorname{ln}(K|3x+7|)$

First, let $u=3x+7.$ Then differentiating with respect to $x$ gives

$$
\frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x}=3\,⟹\,\text{d}x=\frac{1}{3}\text{d}u.
$$

We can now rewrite the integral in terms of $u,$ solve it, and write the final answer in terms of $x$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}\int \frac{6}{3x+7}\,\text{d}x & =6\int \frac{1}{3x+7}\,\text{d}x \\ & =6\int \frac{1}{u}\cdot (\frac{1}{3})\,\text{d}u \\ & =2\int \frac{1}{u}\,\text{d}u \\ & =2\operatorname{ln}|u|+C \\ & =2\operatorname{ln}|3x+7|+C\end{aligned}
$$

We can further simplify the above by writing $C=2\operatorname{ln}K$ for another arbitrary positive constant $K,$ and then we can combine the two terms using the laws of logarithms, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}2\operatorname{ln}|3x+7|+C & =2\operatorname{ln}|3x+7|+2\operatorname{ln}K \\ & =2(\operatorname{ln}|3x+7|+\operatorname{ln}K) \\ & =2\operatorname{ln}(K|3x+7|)\end{aligned}
$$

So, we finally conclude that

$$
\int \frac{6}{3x+7}\,\text{d}x=2\operatorname{ln}(K|3x+7|).
$$

Calculate $\int \frac{3}{4x-3}\,\text{d}x.$ Assume that $K$ is an arbitrary positive constant.

- $4\operatorname{ln}(K|4x-3|)$
- $-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{ln}(K|4x-3|)$
- $\frac{4}{3}\operatorname{ln}(K|4x-3|)$
- $-\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{ln}(K|4x-3|)$
- $\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{ln}(K|4x-3|)$

First, let $u=4x-3.$ Then differentiating with respect to $x$ gives

$$
\frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x}=4\,⟹\,\text{d}x=\frac{1}{4}\text{d}u.
$$

We can now rewrite the integral in terms of $u,$ solve it, and write the final answer in terms of $x$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}\int \frac{3}{4x-3}\,\text{d}x & =3\int \frac{1}{4x-3}\,\text{d}x \\ & =3\int \frac{1}{u}\cdot (\frac{1}{4})\,\text{d}u \\ & =\frac{3}{4}\int \frac{1}{u}\,\text{d}u \\ & =\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{ln}|u|+C \\ & =\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{ln}|4x-3|+C\end{aligned}
$$

We can further simplify the above by writing $C=\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{ln}K$ for another arbitrary positive constant $K,$ and then we can combine the two terms using the laws of logarithms, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{ln}|4x-3|+C & =\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{ln}|4x-3|+\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{ln}K \\ & =\frac{3}{4}(\operatorname{ln}|4x-3|+\operatorname{ln}K) \\ & =\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{ln}(K|4x-3|)\end{aligned}
$$

So, we finally conclude that

$$
\int \frac{3}{4x-3}\,\text{d}x=\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{ln}(K|4x-3|).
$$
