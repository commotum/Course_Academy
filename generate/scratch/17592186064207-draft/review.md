\begin{aligned}\int \frac{12}{-5-8x}\,\text{d}x & =12\int \frac{1}{-5-8x}\,\text{d}x \\ & =12\int \frac{1}{u}\cdot (-\frac{1}{8})\,\text{d}u \\ & =-\frac{3}{2}\int \frac{1}{u}\,\text{d}u \\ & =-\frac{3}{2}\operatorname{ln}|u|+C \\ & =-\frac{3}{2}\operatorname{ln}|-5-8x|+C\end{aligned}
$$

We can further simplify the above by writing $C=-\frac{3}{2}\operatorname{ln}K$ for another arbitrary positive constant $K,$ and then we can combine the two terms using the laws of logarithms, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}-\frac{3}{2}\operatorname{ln}|-5-8x|+C & =-\frac{3}{2}\operatorname{ln}|-5-8x|-\frac{3}{2}\operatorname{ln}K \\ & =-\frac{3}{2}(\operatorname{ln}|-5-8x|+\operatorname{ln}K) \\ & =-\frac{3}{2}\operatorname{ln}(K|-5-8x|)\end{aligned}
$$

So, we finally conclude that

$$
\int \frac{12}{-5-8x}\,\text{d}x=-\frac{3}{2}\operatorname{ln}(K|-5-8x|).
$$
