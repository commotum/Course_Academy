Calculate $\int \frac{9}{11-14x}\,\text{d}x.$ Assume that $K$ is an arbitrary positive constant.

- $9\operatorname{ln}(K|11-14x|)$
- $-\frac{1}{14}\operatorname{ln}(K|11-14x|)$
- $\frac{9}{14}\operatorname{ln}(K|11-14x|)$
- $-\frac{14}{9}\operatorname{ln}(K|11-14x|)$
- $-\frac{9}{14}\operatorname{ln}(K|11-14x|)$

First, let $u=11-14x.$ Then differentiating with respect to $x$ gives

$$
\frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x}=-14\,⟹\,\text{d}x=-\frac{1}{14}\text{d}u.
$$

We can now rewrite the integral in terms of $u,$ solve it, and write the final answer in terms of $x$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}\int \frac{9}{11-14x}\,\text{d}x & =9\int \frac{1}{11-14x}\,\text{d}x \\ & =9\int \frac{1}{u}\cdot (-\frac{1}{14})\,\text{d}u \\ & =-\frac{9}{14}\int \frac{1}{u}\,\text{d}u \\ & =-\frac{9}{14}\operatorname{ln}|u|+C \\ & =-\frac{9}{14}\operatorname{ln}|11-14x|+C\end{aligned}
$$

We can further simplify the above by writing $C=-\frac{9}{14}\operatorname{ln}K$ for another arbitrary positive constant $K,$ and then we can combine the two terms using the laws of logarithms, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}-\frac{9}{14}\operatorname{ln}|11-14x|+C & =-\frac{9}{14}\operatorname{ln}|11-14x|-\frac{9}{14}\operatorname{ln}K \\ & =-\frac{9}{14}(\operatorname{ln}|11-14x|+\operatorname{ln}K) \\ & =-\frac{9}{14}\operatorname{ln}(K|11-14x|)\end{aligned}
$$

So, we finally conclude that

$$
\int \frac{9}{11-14x}\,\text{d}x=-\frac{9}{14}\operatorname{ln}(K|11-14x|).
$$
