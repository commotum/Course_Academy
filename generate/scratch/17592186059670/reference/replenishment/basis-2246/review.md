Notice that the graph of $y=4f(x-5)$ can be obtained from the graph of $y=f(x)$ by

- translating it by $5$ units to the right.

- stretching it by a scale factor of $4$ parallel to the $y$-axis.

Since these transformations do not flip the graph across the $x$-axis, the combined transformation maps the global maximum of $y=f(x)$ to the global maximum of $y=4f(x-5).$ So, we need to find the image of $(2,2),$ the global maximum of $y=f(x),$ under the transformation.

To plot $y=4f(x-5),$ we apply the following steps:

- First, we take the graph $y=f(x)$ that passes through $(2,2).$

- Then, we apply the transformation by translating the graph by $5$ units to the right and get the graph of $y=f(x-5).$ After this transformation, the point $(2,2)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(2+5,2)=(7,2).
$$

- Then, we apply the transformation by stretching the graph by a scale factor of $4$ parallel to the $y$-axis and get the graph of $y=4f(x-5).$ After this transformation, the point $(7,2)$ will be mapped to the point

$$
(7,4\cdot (2))=(7,8).
$$

Therefore, the global maximum of $y=4f(x-5)$ is $(7,8).$
