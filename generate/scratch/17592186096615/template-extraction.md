# Template extraction

source-cases.json retains every specific prompt, value, field, worked solution, and source identity; source.json and reference/source.edn preserve the captured database values.

Domain: x > 0; bases positive and unequal to 1. Observed bases are 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10; source exponential bases 2, 3, 7. Sampling extends integer coefficients/bases to 2..12, retains fixed points 1 and 3 and common base 10, and excludes exponential base 4 in the altered-base bank and coefficient equal to base in the scaled-log bank to prevent equivalent choices. Negative signs, polynomial exponents, and fixed term coefficients remain authored source structure.

The renderer makes simultaneous replacements of role-bearing numbers/fragments. Cubic source 3 has three roles and 6 has two roles, so complete expression fragments are parameterized instead of replacing all equal literals. Prompt, every solution step and complete answer bank share parameters. At original parameters strings replay exactly, including source spacing and nested aligned environments. No option feedback is added.

Radio recipes are recorded per original option; the source altered exponential base 4 is retained as a fixed observed expression, with its mistake interpretation unresolved. No inferred distractor bank is mixed in. Blanks retain field-1 and canonical owned answers, without distractors. Correct mathematical objects are computed through SymPy before rendering, then independently validated from the displayed expressions and worked rows.

No source has a plot reference. Plot extraction and plot generation have no assets to process. Mathematical LaTeX is text, not a plot.

Source solution and field layouts remain separate templates. Complete bank replay must cover 3 radio banks (12 distractors), plus 6 blank canonical banks; the other three source records remain excluded with evidence.

Hard sampling additionally requires c >= b because the native grader could not reject zero for one mixed-sign transcendental rate. This explicit narrower policy includes the observed source (c=5, b=4) and guarantees a positive rate. The original failure is retained in grading-limitations.json; it is not called a passing test.
