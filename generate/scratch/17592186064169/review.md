## Captured original q-113705

Given that $f(x)=\frac{1}{{x}^{4/3}}$ and that ${F}^{'}(x)=f(x)$, calculate $F(x).$

Remember that integration is the opposite of differentiation. So, if ${F}^{'}(x)=f(x),$ then integrating both sides of this equation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}{F}^{'}(x) & =f(x) \\ \int {F}^{'}(x)\,\text{d}x & =\int f(x)\,\text{d}x \\ F(x) & =\int f(x)\,\text{d}x.\end{aligned}
$$

Note that $\frac{1}{{x}^{4/3}}={x}^{-4/3}.$ So, we can use the power rule with $n=-\frac{4}{3}$ and get

$$
\begin{aligned}F(x) & =\int f(x)\,\text{d}x \\  & =\int {x}^{-4/3}\,\text{d}x \\  & =\frac{{x}^{-4/3+1}}{(-\frac{4}{3}+1)}+C \\  & =\frac{{x}^{-1/3}}{(-\frac{1}{3})}+C \\  & =-3{x}^{-1/3}+C \\  & =-\frac{3}{{x}^{1/3}}+C.\end{aligned}
$$
