The gradient of the function $f(x,y)$ is defined as

$$
∇f(x,y)=\frac{∂f}{∂x}\,i+\frac{∂f}{∂y}\,j.
$$

Since $∇f(x,y)=F(x,y),$ the function $f(x,y)$ must satisfy the following system:

$$
\frac{∂f}{∂x}=9 x^{2} - 3 y^{2},\qquad \frac{∂f}{∂y}=- 6 x y + 12.
$$

First, we integrate $\frac{∂f}{∂x}$ with respect to $x.$ Note that the solution should contain an arbitrary function of $y:$

$$
\begin{aligned}f(x,y)&=\int \frac{∂f}{∂x}\,\text{d}x\\&=\int (9 x^{2} - 3 y^{2})\,\text{d}x\\&=3 x^{3} - 3 x y^{2}+g(y)\end{aligned}
$$

Now, differentiating the above expression with respect to $y,$ we obtain

$$
\frac{∂f}{∂y}=- 6 x y+g'(y).
$$

Taking $\frac{∂f}{∂y}=- 6 x y + 12$ into account, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}- 6 x y+g'(y)&=- 6 x y + 12\\g'(y)&=12\\g(y)&=12 y+C,\end{aligned}
$$

where $C$ is a constant.

Finally, substituting $g(y)=12 y+C$ into our expression for $f(x,y),$ we get

$$
f(x,y)=3 x^{3} - 3 x y^{2} + 12 y+C.
