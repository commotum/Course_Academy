- $15(u-7)^{4}(z+4)^{3}$

- $15(u-7)^{2}(z+4)^{3}$

- $15(u-7)^{3}(z+4)^{4}$

- $(u-7)^{3}(z+4)^{4}$

- $15(u-7)^{5}(z+4)^{7}$

To find the least common multiple ($\text{LCM}$) of two polynomials that are made up of products of binomials, we find the least common multiple of the constants and the least common multiple of each variable part. Then, we multiply all of the results together.

So first, we find $\text{LCM}(5,3):$

- Multiples of $5:$ $\,5,\,10,\,15,\,20,…$

- Multiples of $3:$ $\,3,\,6,\,9,\,12,\,15,\,18,…$

So, $\text{LCM}(5,3)=15.$

To find the $\text{LCM}$ of $(u-7)^{3}(z+4)^{3}$ and $(u-7)^{2}(z+4)^{4},$ we find the largest powers for $(u-7)$ and $(z+4)$ that appear in the expressions:

- For $(u-7)^{3}$ and $(u-7)^{2},$ the largest power is $3.$

- For $(z+4)^{3}$ and $(z+4)^{4},$ the largest power is $4.$

Now, we raise $(u-7)$ and $(z+4)$ to the corresponding largest powers. Therefore,

$$
\text{LCM}((u-7)^{3}(z+4)^{3},\,(u-7)^{2}(z+4)^{4})=(u-7)^{3}\cdot (z+4)^{4}.
$$

Finally, we multiply all of our results together:

$$
\text{LCM}(5(u-7)^{3}(z+4)^{3},\,3(u-7)^{2}(z+4)^{4})=15(u-7)^{3}(z+4)^{4}.
$$
