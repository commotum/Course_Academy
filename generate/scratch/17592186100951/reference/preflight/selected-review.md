Consider the following initial value problem

$$
\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}=4y,\,y(- \frac{3}{2})=-1.
$$

You're given that $y=A{e}^{4x}$ is the solution to this initial value problem. What is the value of the constant $A?$

$A=${{field-1}}

- $- e^{6}$ **[correct]**

We're given that $y=A{e}^{4x}$ is the solution to the initial value problem. Therefore, it must satisfy the differential equation *and* the initial condition $y(- \frac{3}{2})=-1.$

Substituting the initial condition $y(- \frac{3}{2})=-1$ into our solution and solving for $A,$ we get

$$
\begin{aligned}y & =A{e}^{4x} \\ -1 & =A{e}^{4\cdot (- \frac{3}{2})} \\ -1 & =A{e}^{-6} \\ \frac{-1}{{e}^{-6}} & =A \\ A & =\frac{-1}{{e}^{-6}}.\end{aligned}
$$
