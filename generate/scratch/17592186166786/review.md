## q-95537 n=2

Calculate $\int x\cosh (2x^{2})dx$.

$\frac{1}{2}\sinh (x^{2}) + C$

$\frac{1}{4}\sinh (2x^{2}) + C$

$-\frac{1}{4}\sinh (2x^{2}) + C$

$-\frac{1}{2}\sinh (x^{2}) + C$

$\frac{1}{2}\sinh (4x^{2}) + C$

Let $u=2x^2$. Differentiating gives $\mathrm{d}u=4x\,\mathrm{d}x$, so $x\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{d}u$.

Using this substitution,

$$\begin{aligned}
\int x\cosh(2x^2)\,\mathrm{d}x
&=\frac{1}{4}\int\cosh u\,\mathrm{d}u \\
&=\frac{1}{4}\sinh u+C \\
&=\frac{1}{4}\sinh(2x^2)+C.
\end{aligned}$$

This antiderivative is defined for every real $x$.

## q-95543 n=1

Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle \int_{1}^{e}\frac{1}{x}\operatorname{sech}^{2} (\ln x)dx$.

$\frac{1 - 2e}{e^{2} + 1}$

$\frac{e - 1}{2e + 1}$

$\frac{e + 1}{e - 1}$

$\frac{e^{2} - 1}{e^{2} + 1}$

$\frac{e^{2} + 1}{e^{2} - 1}$

Let $u=\ln x$. Differentiating gives $\mathrm{d}u=\frac{1}{x}\mathrm{d}x$.

The limits change from $x=1$ to $u=0$, and from $x=e^{1}$ to $u=1$. Therefore,

$$\begin{aligned}
\int_1^{e^{1}}\frac{\operatorname{sech}^2(\ln x)}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x
&=\int_0^{1}\operatorname{sech}^2 u\,\mathrm{d}u \\
&=\left[\tanh u\right]_0^{1} \\
&=\tanh 1-\tanh 0 \\
&=\frac{e^{2}-1}{e^{2}+1}.
\end{aligned}$$

The original interval has $x>0$, so $\ln x$ and the substitution are defined throughout.
