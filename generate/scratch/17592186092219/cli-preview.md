The point $A$ has polar coordinates $(r,0)$ and the point $B$ has polar coordinates $(2r,\frac{4\pi}{3})$. Find the Cartesian coordinates of $B$ given that the Cartesian coordinates of $A$ are $(2,0)$.

selection: $(2,-2\sqrt{3})$, $(-2,-2\sqrt{3})$, $(-2,2\sqrt{3})$, $(-1,-\sqrt{3})$, $(-2\sqrt{3},-2)$

First, we use the Cartesian coordinates of $A$ to find $r,$ the distance between $A$ and the origin:

$$
\begin{aligned}r & =\sqrt{{x}^{2}+{y}^{2}} \\   & =\sqrt{(2)^{2}+(0)^{2}} \\   & =\sqrt{4} \\   & =2\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently, the polar coordinates of $B$ are

$$
(2\cdot(2),\frac{4\pi}{3})=(4,\frac{4\pi}{3}).
$$

Now, we convert $B$ to Cartesian coordinates. To convert from polar coordinates to Cartesian coordinates, we use

$$
x=r\operatorname{cos}⁡\theta ,\,y=r\operatorname{sin}⁡\theta .
$$

First, we calculate the $x$-coordinate of $B:$

$$
\begin{aligned}x & =r\operatorname{cos}⁡\theta  \\   & =4\cdot\operatorname{cos}(\frac{4\pi}{3}) \\   & =4\cdot(-\frac{1}{2}) \\   & =-2\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we calculate the $y$-coordinate of $B:$

$$
\begin{aligned}y & =r\operatorname{sin}⁡\theta  \\   & =4\cdot\operatorname{sin}(\frac{4\pi}{3}) \\   & =4\cdot(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}) \\   & =-2\sqrt{3}\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the Cartesian coordinates of $B$ are $(-2,-2\sqrt{3}).$

$$
x=r\operatorname{cos}⁡\theta ,\,y=r\operatorname{sin}⁡\theta .

The point $A$ has polar coordinates $(6, θ)$ and the point $B$ has polar coordinates $(3, θ - \frac{π}{4})$. Find the Cartesian coordinates of $B$ given that the Cartesian coordinates of $A$ are $(\sqrt{18},-\sqrt{18})$.

selection: $(0,3)$, $(\frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2},-\frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2})$, $(-3,0)$, $(3,0)$, $(0,-3)$

First, we use the Cartesian coordinates of $A$ to find its reference angle ${\theta }_{R}:$

$$
\begin{aligned}{\theta }_{R} & =\operatorname{arctan}(\frac{3\sqrt{2}}{3\sqrt{2}}) \\   & =\operatorname{arctan}(1) \\   & =\frac{\pi}{4}\end{aligned}
$$

Since the point $A$ lies in the fourth quadrant, we have $\theta=\frac{7\pi}{4}.$

Consequently, the polar coordinates of $B$ are

$$
(3,\frac{7\pi}{4}+(-\frac{\pi}{4}))=(3,\frac{3\pi}{2}).
$$

Now, we convert $B$ to Cartesian coordinates. To convert from polar coordinates to Cartesian coordinates, we use

$$
x=r\operatorname{cos}⁡\theta ,\,y=r\operatorname{sin}⁡\theta .
$$

First, we calculate the $x$-coordinate of $B:$

$$
\begin{aligned}x & =r\operatorname{cos}⁡\theta  \\   & =3\cdot\operatorname{cos}(\frac{3\pi}{2}) \\   & =3\cdot(0) \\   & =0\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we calculate the $y$-coordinate of $B:$

$$
\begin{aligned}y & =r\operatorname{sin}⁡\theta  \\   & =3\cdot\operatorname{sin}(\frac{3\pi}{2}) \\   & =3\cdot(-1) \\   & =-3\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the Cartesian coordinates of $B$ are $(0,-3).$
