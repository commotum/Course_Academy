**Status correction: the implementation is partial. The earlier completion claim is withdrawn.**

The completion criterion was wrong: implemented edits and passing boundary tests were treated as sufficient evidence that the identified instructional failure mechanisms had been addressed. The exemplar omission also starts in the proposal: section 4 allowed local drafts to reach the author before independent review. That fixes early publication, but leaves the acknowledged risk of building a generator around a flawed interpretation. The manager/integration review did not catch this distinction. Responsibility for that claim rests with the primary agent.

This November 5, 2026 audit compares the original recurring-failure analysis and ten-part proposal with the resulting code, prompts and tests. Three agents reviewed distinct areas; the primary agent inspected the supporting code and reran the exemplar-flow and canonical-fallback tests. No production code or database content was changed during this audit. The following distinguishes missing behavior from improvements whose pedagogical effect remains unmeasured.

1. **Missing: independent acceptance of exemplars before construction.** `Pipeline.investigate()` checks three records, difficulty labels, question structure and assets, then advances directly to `generate`. It does not require passing grading/rendering reports or independent pedagogical acceptance. The lifecycle test supplies `evidence_paths: []` and explicitly expects investigation, authoring, then review. That test passes today. Permanent local storage prevents publication; it does not prevent a flawed exemplar from shaping the generator. The missing sequence is exemplar creation, validation and independent verification, repair to acceptance, then generator construction and verification of generated output. [Runner](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/scripts/generator_pipeline.py:590), [test](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/tests/test_generator_three_phase.py:49).

2. **Missing: a required, explicit handoff of the instructional account.** The investigator is told to write `workspace/investigation.md`, but `investigation_file` actually points to the machine-produced `investigation-input.json`. The author is told to read that context field. The substantive investigation and author accounts are hashed only if they exist; their absence does not block the handoff. Thus complete examples can become the effective specification while the account of required learner work is missing. [Context path](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/scripts/generator_investigation.py:261), [author instructions](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/generate/pipeline/author.md:8), [optional hashing](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/scripts/generator_pipeline.py:339).

The intended order is prompt advice, not a separate boundary. A runtime correction still tells the investigator to construct examples “using the supplied reference,” contradicting the optional-donor rule. Likewise, the verifier's initial account is not captured independently by the runner before implementation inspection. A temporary fixture probe overwrote both the initial and final reasoning together, without an amendment, and `validate_review()` accepted it. The amendment checks establish consistency with the current file, not preservation of the original interpretation. [Donor setup](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/scripts/generator_investigation.py:195), [correction instruction](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/scripts/generator_pipeline.py:586), [account validation](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/scripts/generator_review.py:180).
