# Recurring generator failures and loss of instructional intent

The strongest recurring failure is a loss of the learning objective when it is translated into templates, difficulty rules, answer banks, and acceptance checks. Several authors correctly describe what the learner should do, then implement a question that supplies that decision, replaces it with another task, or permits an unrelated shortcut. Reviewers sometimes explicitly approve the same interpretation. This supports the concern about semantic loss, with an important refinement: failure to preserve an understood objective is better evidenced than a universal failure to consider the objective at all.

The review covers the selected 35 one-off cases first, followed by the 64 campaign cases: 99 distinct knowledge points. These are selected modification and repair records, not a random sample of all generators. The one-off set includes preventive and narrow changes; membership does not establish a pedagogical failure. Publication history is pinned to EDB basis 13008. The analysis uses source questions, retained old generators, original investigations and accepted reviews where available, repair histories, and previously matched publication receipts. It does not claim to have rerun every historical test.

The [one-off review group](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/generate/reports/generator-modification-cohort-2026-11-05/one-off-review-group.json) identifies the initial cases. [Campaign finding counts](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/generate/reports/generator-modification-cohort-2026-11-05/campaign-recorded-finding-counts.json) show that 50 of the 64 have recorded decision-shortcut findings, 38 source-fidelity findings, and 20 restriction findings. Categories overlap. These are historical finding labels, not counts of independently proven instruction-following causes.

**1. “Make it easy” becomes “do the important part for the learner.”**

The clearest example spans twelve integration KPs. A difficulty redesign explicitly borrowed supplied-substitution exercises from a different KP. Easy questions gave \(u\) and asked for the transformed integral; full calculation was reserved for other bands. That preserved related mathematics while removing substitution selection and completion of the integral from the easy task. The target sources required both. This is a directly documented design decision, not a motive inferred from the repaired code. [Original redesign](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/reference/edb-math/repairs/2026-10-24-trig-scaffolding/README.md:21), [objective-restoration diagnosis](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/reference/edb-math/repairs/2026-10-25-restore-substitution-recognition/README.md:7).

The recent trig cases independently show the same reasoning. One original investigation explains that easy support was added because no complete easy import existed. Its generated question supplies the cosine difference formula and the required exact values. The reviewer accepts this as an explicit authored progression. The question still contains an expansion, but selecting and recalling the tools have been performed for the learner. Another generator supplies both substitutions for repeated double-angle application. [Original investigation](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/.local/learning/inventory/math/59d5cf13-351c-4114-be19-4c3bb64ee051/0d8f6dfe058f421f3bdc922660dde5be0c8dd6858c0783ea3f66db6fb718d0a6.pipeline/2/workspace/investigation.md:27), [accepted review](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/.local/learning/inventory/math/59d5cf13-351c-4114-be19-4c3bb64ee051/0d8f6dfe058f421f3bdc922660dde5be0c8dd6858c0783ea3f66db6fb718d0a6.pipeline/2/review-1-policy-6-c1dd4be41873cbc7/result.json:135).

Matrix multiplication, implicit tangent/normal intercepts, triangle area, and equilibrium identification repeat this pattern. The matrix author explicitly identifies locating and pairing a row and column as the target, then supplies one requested dot-product setup in easy. The accepted review counts the remaining pairings and support removal as the progression. The task description was understood; its implications were not consistently enforced. [Author account](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/.local/learning/inventory/math/59d5cf13-351c-4114-be19-4c3bb64ee051/354d30bae91b7bed522089f5cb5643d91b2c1c4d4034b42c8ddfc3809a8b4dc3.pipeline/2/workspace/instructional-account.md:3), [easy design](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/.local/learning/inventory/math/59d5cf13-351c-4114-be19-4c3bb64ee051/354d30bae91b7bed522089f5cb5643d91b2c1c4d4034b42c8ddfc3809a8b4dc3.pipeline/2/workspace/instructional-account.md:17).

The missing distinction is between reducing incidental effort and supplying the knowledge being assessed. A worked teaching stage can legitimately provide a substitution. Independent practice of choosing that substitution cannot be justified merely by borrowing the teaching stage.

**2. “Make it hard” becomes “add more work or another skill.”**

Several generators manufacture progression through compounded demands: radical endpoint arithmetic, enforced noncancellation, fourth-degree trig regrouping, repeated identities, or new recognition stages. Some old integration contracts explicitly require these features. The printed complexity becomes the difficulty criterion even when simplification removes it or the extra work belongs elsewhere.

The triangle-area history shows both sides of the mistake. The initial question supplied the areas, leaving subtraction. A repair restored an area calculation, but easy and moderate used right angles, bypassing the intended general-triangle sine calculation. The later repair retained sine-area calculation and subtraction across all bands and varied manageable numerical demand. [Repair history](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/reference/edb-math/repairs/2026-10-27-triangle-area-progression/README.md:7).

This does not make every additional step inappropriate. In the trig group, selecting an angle decomposition, factoring a ratio, and exposing a reciprocal have genuine source support in particular KPs. Some new hard extensions are plausible but uncalibrated, rather than demonstrably outside the skill. The relevant question is whether the added demand belongs to this learning objective and stage, not whether the solution is longer.

**3. “Preserve the template and vary the parameters” preserves accidental answers.**

This is especially prominent in the 64-case campaign. Translating or positively scaling an entire source bank changes every number while preserving which answer is smallest, second-largest, uniquely negative, shortest, or recognizable by a repeated component. Shuffling options does not remove those relationships.

The rank-nullity extraction provides an unusually explicit trace. It correctly describes using columns and the rank-nullity theorem, but represents each complete source bank as the correct answer plus five fixed offsets. It labels those offsets opaque recipes and requires their preservation. The resulting bank shape lets learners infer the answer without using the supplied dimensions or nullity. The extraction protected an incidental numerical arrangement as though it were part of the teaching method. [Original extraction](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/generate/repairs/2026-10-20-early-cohort/jobs/17592186184219/runtime-0/17592186184219/template-extraction.md:5), [shortcut finding](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/generate/repairs/2026-10-20-early-cohort/jobs/17592186184219/repair-history.json:8).

Similar failures appear in Gaussian elimination, vector combinations, matrix classification, integrating factors, growth/decay, and dilution. Other generators preserve the same qualifying labels, consistency branch, or resonant-term position. They reproduce the surface of the examples without preserving the need to apply the rule anew.

One original matrix-classification implementation makes the reasoning explicit: preserving the complete source bank and shared proof prose requires preserving the source truth profile. Its validator rejects changing a lower-triangular matrix into a diagonal matrix, although deciding which statements become true is precisely the skill. A constraint introduced to reuse the representation prevents the meaningful variation. [Original comment and guard](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/generate/repairs/2026-10-20-early-cohort/jobs/17592186119784/runtime-0/17592186119784.py:122).

Source replay is useful for establishing what an example contains. It cannot by itself establish which features should remain fixed across future practice. The invariant should usually be the mathematical rule, with the answer determined by the instance.

**4. Tooling and validation rules become restrictions on the curriculum.**

Some generators reject mathematically legitimate inputs because their chosen distractor recipes collide, because a preferred variation method cannot change the instance, or because the grader cannot recognize a valid answer form. That reverses the intended dependency: the implementation begins defining which mathematics is allowed.

Examples include excluding full-row-rank matrices because the “rows” and “rank” distractors coincide; excluding valid zero coefficients because a bank contains both \(t\) and \(-t\); dropping an autonomous ODE because translation cannot vary it; and narrowing response wording to exclude equivalent answers the engine fails to grade. The source task remains defensible; the implementation needs a different bank, sampler, or grader rule.

A row-echelon generator goes further: it adds global minimization absent from the source, rejecting the only correct offered operation because a smaller unoffered operation exists. “Choose the valid offered operation” has become a different optimization problem. [Recorded restriction and drift](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/generate/repairs/2026-10-20-early-cohort/jobs/17592186129115/repair-history.json:8).

Not all omitted families show this mechanism. Many campaign omissions come from stale source snapshots, missing replay profiles, or incomplete refreshes. Those are operational coverage defects and should remain separate from claims about literal interpretation.

**5. A successful technical check stands in for the learner experience it was meant to protect.**

Several different checks exhibit the same substitution:

- Mathematical equivalence replaces a supported solution path. In the chain-rule case, SymPy reversed a trig argument, so connecting the keyed answer to the worked solution required an unexplained parity identity. Derivative checks, source replay, and grading passed. [Chain-rule diagnosis](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/reference/edb-math/repairs/2026-10-23-trig-chain-rule/README.md:5).
- Distinct, well-distributed choices replace useful alternatives. The initial-amount generator used rank-conditioned random offsets around the key, producing adjacent cents. This balanced answer ranks but made incidental rounding decide the choice. [Spacing repair](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/reference/edb-math/repairs/2026-10-26-compound-interest-answer-spacing/README.md:10).
- Correct curves and integer tick labels replace readable discrimination. A parametric bank required distinguishing a one-unit translation on a three-unit grid. The renderer solved fitting and layout while omitting the resolution required by the task. [Visual audit](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/reference/edb-math/audits/2026-10-30-parametric-plot-readability/README.md:25).
- A supported constructor replaces delivered coverage. The repeated-double-angle generator could construct cancellation cases, but ordinary moderate generation scheduled only product cases. Technical availability did not mean learners received the intended family.

These failures require different code repairs, but share a semantic error: a measurable property of the artifact is treated as sufficient evidence of the underlying educational purpose.

**6. Local repairs satisfy the latest finding while retaining or creating a wrong assumption.**

The integration sequence is the clearest example. The first redesign introduces given-\(u\) transformation tasks. The next hint-removal repair preserves those as legitimate guided exercises. The later repair revisits the target objective and removes them. The intermediate repair was explicitly scoped and user-approved; this is evidence that a mistaken design premise survived a local correction, not proof that the correcting agent should have exceeded its authorization. [Hint-removal scope](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/reference/edb-math/repairs/2026-10-24-remove-blanket-integration-hints/README.md:5).

The campaign has a parallel example. A rank-classification batch always qualified A. A repair introduced contrasting A-only and C-only cases, but then enforced mutual exclusivity. Learners could inspect A and infer C, and the legitimate A+C case disappeared. The repair varied the previously fixed label while failing to preserve independent classification of every matrix. [Successive findings](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/generate/repairs/2026-10-20-early-cohort/jobs/17592186119848/repair-history.json:8).

This is the most direct general lesson for repair work: the latest counterexample identifies a defect, but passing its narrow regression does not establish that the changed rule still serves the original objective.

**Where the pipeline contributes**

The historical instructions already contain substantial intent language. The October 13 principles require the same instructional purpose and comparable scope. The October 16 author prompt asks what the learner must inspect, distinguish, infer, calculate, or construct; the reviewer is told to reconstruct that independently. It would be inaccurate to explain every failure as the absence of such instructions, or to attribute later wording retroactively to earlier agents. [Historical principles](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/generate/reports/generator-modification-cohort-2026-11-05/evidence/pipeline-history/36b53b294ef-principles.md:13), [historical author prompt](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/generate/reports/generator-modification-cohort-2026-11-05/evidence/pipeline-history/f3aa91619f7-author.md:10).

The evidence instead points to three vulnerable translations. Investigation turns incomplete examples and a reference KP into an authored progression. Extraction turns source observations into fixed template constraints. Verification turns those constraints into checks that can pass while the learner's required reasoning has changed. In the strongest one-off cases, original investigations and accepted reviews repeat the same mistaken progression. Independent sessions did not guarantee an independent interpretation.

The all-band workflow makes the first translation consequential: it asks for three complete exemplars when source levels are incomplete, while the reference KP supplies a model of support and progression. The prompt also requires adaptation to the actual target skill. Several saved investigations satisfy the visible three-band shape by adding support or new stages without adequately preserving that skill. This is a documented interpretation problem, not a direct instruction to give away answers. [Historical investigator prompt](/home/jake/Developer/Course_Academy/generate/reports/generator-modification-cohort-2026-11-05/evidence/pipeline-history/7720ac1b45d-investigator.md:8).

**What the evidence supports changing next**

The useful unit of review is the learner decision that must remain necessary before feedback. For each proposed template or repair, compare that decision with what the actual prompt, choices, diagram, and accepted responses require. A statement of the objective is useful only when the design is checked against it.

That suggests a small set of substantive questions for later pipeline work:

1. What must the learner determine independently, and where does each delivered question still require it?
2. Which source features express the method, and which are merely one example's values, labels, support, or answer arrangement?
3. Can someone bypass the target skill using the prompt support, the bank alone, a recognizable template, or the delivered batch?
4. Does the proposed repair preserve legitimate cases and the original objective while removing the demonstrated defect?
5. Which observation would disprove the design account, including a superficially similar source example where the proposed restriction would be wrong?

These questions should guide the implementation and review of concrete instances; adding another report field without testing its claim would repeat the observed failure.

The counterexamples matter. The final-amount compound-interest generator's existing banks already passed; that modification added a preventive guard. Some trig factoring and decomposition demands are source-supported. Coarse grids are valid for broad shape contrasts but not necessarily for fine coordinate discrimination. Some campaign shortcuts are demonstrated only within a particular delivered batch, and one is a statistical advantage rather than a universal answer rule. Package defects, stale snapshots, grading limitations, and evidence-path errors also occur without establishing a pedagogical misinterpretation.

An incidental rule found after inspecting two or five questions is weaker evidence than an invariant in the construction code or a controlled comparison. Its success in that batch does not establish a stable strategy across future practice. Treating every such coincidence as grounds for a mandatory balance quota could itself repeat the mistake of optimizing a review metric instead of the task.

The records establish observable behavior, some explicit design rationales, and repeated acceptance of weakened objectives. They do not recover every original user request or every agent's reasoning. The strongest supported conclusion is that the system often checks compliance with a chosen interpretation more thoroughly than it checks whether that interpretation still requires the intended learning.
