# Instructional specification

The learner identifies the triangular notch of a concave quadrilateral, finds the enclosing triangle's area, and subtracts the quadrilateral area. The subtraction direction is fixed by containment, not by a source label. Before parameters: select acute/obtuse included-angle case on hard, low/high notch proportion, response shape and complete error profile. Then choose compatible small side lengths, area and presentation. All generated quadrilaterals are concave, inside an enclosing triangle, with positive quadrilateral and notch areas. Polygon construction uses a point on the ray from the enclosing angle to the midpoint of the opposite edge; shoelace independently verifies area. A dashed edge completes that enclosing triangle. Cyclic relabeling and quarter rotations vary the identities and placement of the enclosing corner and target. Labels must agree in text, diagram, key and solution. No fixed target label is necessary. Proportions span both sides of one half so the given quadrilateral is not always larger than the requested area. The operation itself remains subtraction because this KP specifically asks for a missing triangle outside a concave quadrilateral; adding an internal target would change the activity.

Easy retains both area calculation and subtraction. It supplies perpendicular sides from 4–10 and a specific hint identifying the enclosing triangle and the two operations; it never supplies that triangle's area. Moderate uses the same two operations without the hint and allows sides through 18. Hard requires the general sine-area formula with an acute or obtuse included angle, degree-mode calculation and final rounding. Radio is retained in easy and moderate; hard includes both source radio and numerical-entry demands. The easy/moderate distinction is removal of task-specific support, not larger coefficients. A matched comparison holds all geometry and values fixed and removes only the hint. This authored progression corrects the earlier easy subtraction-only design; imported coverage remains hard only.

Small integer sides (4–18) and integer quadrilateral areas control incidental arithmetic. Nonright angles 35–130 degrees include acute and obtuse cases without new identities. Moderate uses even sides to make the enclosing area integral. Units are cm or in, a same-method variation. Source fractional and right-angle hard examples remain available for source replay, not taken as evidence that awkward arithmetic alone creates authored hard difficulty. Simple cancellations and small positive differences are legitimate. No parameter may remove area subtraction. Hard avoids exact sine special angles so ordinary calculator practice is retained.

Radio banks are newly authored profiles of specific area mistakes: omit subtraction; return the supplied quadrilateral; add areas; omit half; halve the final difference; halve the quadrilateral; subtract the quadrilateral twice; omit sine (hard only). Select a whole four-error profile first, then reject colliding, nonpositive or poorly separated rounded banks. The profiles vary rather than always placing the key below every error or giving it a unique double/half counterpart. There are no tiny random offsets or adjacent rounding distractors. Source opaque numeric banks are retained exactly for replay but are not extrapolated as misconceptions. The existing script's always-large quadrilateral range and fixed method bank are deliberately replaced. Merely shuffling choices would not fix their conditional rank cues.

The learner derivation is ordered: identify enclosing/target regions; calculate the enclosing area with the stated operator inputs; subtract the given area; round once if required. The final solution expression and field value share this derivation. No supplementary-angle rewrite, reversed trigonometric argument or other silent conversion is permitted.
